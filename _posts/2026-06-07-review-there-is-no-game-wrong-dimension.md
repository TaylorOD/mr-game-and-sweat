A game that keeps telling you not to click anything is a funny companion to a treadmill, because the whole joke depends on you clicking anyway. I think it can work for an easy workout, though I’d save it for a pace where you can stop and think without feeling like your legs are waiting on you.

## Gameplay

The narrator’s warnings are the invitation, and the fun comes from poking at the screen to see what the game lets you get away with. You’re solving puzzles by clicking and dragging things around, and the game keeps messing with the rules it has just taught you. I like that the joke is part of how you play, rather than something that happens in a cut scene while you wait to get back to the game.

The narrator is a big reason this works. He’s trying to keep the game on track, while you’re encouraged to ignore him, and the back and forth gives even small interactions a bit of personality. I think some of the jokes are more amusing than laugh out loud funny, but there’s enough going on in the narration that I’d keep listening instead of treating it as background noise. He does have a lot to say.

The shifts into different kinds of games keep the puzzle format from feeling like the same screen over and over. You get to watch the game imitate familiar styles and then find ways to mess with those styles, which is a fun setup for an adventure game. I don’t know whether every joke will land for you, but the changes in presentation give you something new to look at while you work out what the puzzle wants.

There are stretches where you need to slow down, inspect what’s on screen and try a few ideas, and that’s where this could interrupt a run if you’re expecting constant action. I’d call that a fair trade for the kind of game it is, but it does mean you can’t just tune out and mash buttons. You’re still doing the puzzle, even when the narrator is doing most of the talking.

The puzzles are generally the reason to play, not an obstacle between you and some bigger action game. I’d expect the odd solution to make me stare at the screen and wonder what I’m missing, which is not ideal while jogging, but at least the game isn’t demanding a perfect reaction while I’m trying to think. If I hit a wall, I’d slow down or pause rather than turn a workout into a meeting with a cartoon narrator.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The pace is forgiving, but your attention still has to be on the screen. This isn’t a game where you can keep a steady rhythm by steering down a track, because the important part is noticing an object or figuring out what the narrator has just said. I’d choose an easy walk or a relaxed bike ride, not intervals where I’m already struggling to make a sensible decision about anything.

It supports a controller, which makes it a much more practical option than trying to reach for a mouse while moving. The analog stick can take a little more care for cursor movement than a mouse would, though, and I’d expect some clicks or drags to take an extra try when the belt is bouncing me around. That’s manageable. It’s just not the kind of game where I’d want to be in a hurry.

A bike is probably the easier place to give the puzzles your attention, especially if you can sit upright and keep the controller steady. On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace low and be comfortable pausing when a puzzle needs more than a quick look. There’s no glory in trying to solve a screen while walking like a person carrying a full bowl of soup.

I wouldn’t pick it for a hard workout, and I’d probably skip playing during the parts of a session when I want to move without thinking. The game’s slow, deliberate interaction is a plus when you’re exercising, but you do have to look and reason your way through it. That makes it a better fit for a casual session than a distracted one.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest, with Intel HD 4000 integrated graphics listed, so you don’t need a recent gaming computer just to give it a try. I’d check that your graphics hardware is at least in that general range first, and the rest should be a fairly easy ask.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Worth picking up if you like clever puzzle adventures and want something you can pause while exercising. I’d pass if you need a game that keeps your legs moving through nonstop action.
