A workout with *Legend of Mortal* sounds like a better fit for a steady ride than a hard run, because a strategy RPG about deciding whether to get involved in a collapsing Tang world seems likely to ask for a little thought. I’d be curious whether the actual pace gives you room to think, because the store description doesn’t say.

## Gameplay

The choice at the center of the premise is a good one. You’re a nobody in a turbulent world where the Tang-Man is declining and old enemies keep coming, and the question is whether you stay out of it or fight for its future. I like a story that starts with a person who has no reason to be important, because that makes the decision feel more personal than being told you’re destined to save everything before the first cup of tea.

The genre mix makes me think there could be some slower stretches where you’re weighing decisions instead of reacting, which can be nice when you’re exercising, as long as the game doesn’t ask you to sit and read for too long. I don’t know how the story is delivered or how often it pauses for dialogue, so I wouldn’t promise that it’s an easy game to keep moving through. Strategy can be a welcome change from games that need your full attention every second, but it can also turn a jog into a meeting with yourself.

There’s also a lot of room in the premise for the journey to feel unpredictable, and that’s the part that makes me want to see more. The description doesn’t tell me what choices change, how the RPG systems work, or what fighting the old enemies actually asks you to do, so I can’t give you a confident opinion about the moment to moment play. That’s not a tiny detail when the genres include both adventure and strategy. I’d want to know whether the decisions are interesting or whether I’m mostly reading about interesting decisions.

I think the safest expectation is a game that may be engaging when you’re alert and a little less convenient when your attention is split. If its strategic parts give you time to consider things, that could suit a lower effort workout, and if the story keeps stopping to explain the world, you may end up doing more standing than sweating. The premise earns my interest, but it doesn’t tell me enough to call the gameplay a sure thing.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is a real plus here, and I’d start with a bike or an easy walking pace rather than trying to run hard while learning how its strategy and RPG parts work. A controller means you don’t need to reach for a keyboard, but it doesn’t tell us whether the game has precise timing or whether the menus are easy to use while you’re moving. Those details matter more than the box being checked.

I’d probably give it a try on a treadmill once I knew how much attention the combat takes. If the game lets you think between decisions, you can keep the belt moving and take your time, but if enemies demand quick reactions, the controller being supported won’t save you from trying to make a careful move while your hands are bouncing. I can’t tell from the store details which kind of game it is in practice.

A bike feels a little more forgiving if you want to stop pedaling briefly to work through a decision, and that seems useful for a strategy RPG. It’s still not something I’d start during a hard interval, because I’d rather have enough attention for the story’s choices than keep losing track of what the game is asking me to do. That’s a pretty ordinary problem.

I’d skip a fast treadmill pace until you know how the game handles its fights, and I wouldn’t treat the word “strategy” as proof that everything is slow or safe to play while distracted. Full controller support gives you a good starting point, but the store description doesn’t say whether you can pause whenever you want or how demanding the action gets. So I’d keep the first workout easy.
