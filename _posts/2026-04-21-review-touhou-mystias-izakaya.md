Collecting ingredients by day and running Mystia’s izakaya at night sounds like a nice way to keep your brain occupied on an easy workout, and I think the restaurant shift is the part that could make you forget you’re moving. The ingredient gathering is a little less demanding, which is probably useful when your legs are doing their own thing.

## Gameplay

The day and night split gives the game a good rhythm for exercise, because gathering ingredients and learning recipes feel like the calmer stretch, and then the customers arrive and you have to pay attention. I like that the two halves ask different things of you instead of making every minute feel like the same chore. Some days you’re mostly collecting what you need, and some nights you’re trying to remember what people want while keeping the place moving.

The restaurant service is where I think the game earns its keep. You’re matching dishes and drinks to customers, and the familiar Touhou characters have their own preferences, so serving them isn’t just clicking the first thing that looks edible. I’d expect to have a few orders in mind at once and make small decisions as the shift goes on, which is enough to take my attention without turning the game into a second job. It does sound like the kind of evening where I’d forget to make myself a snack.

I don’t know how much of the fun depends on already knowing the Touhou cast, but the restaurant setup seems like it can carry a lot of the appeal on its own. Meeting characters and learning recipes gives the collecting a reason beyond making a pile of ingredients, and I think that’s a good fit for a game about running a small place. If the references mean more to you, that’s a nice extra. If they don’t, you still have customers to feed.

There’s also a bit of planning tucked into the cozy pixel art, and I’d expect that to be the part that slows me down now and then. You need to think about what you’ve collected and what you’re serving, and I can see myself taking a moment to sort it out rather than moving through everything on instinct. That’s not a complaint, exactly. Sometimes you want a game that lets your brain do one small task at a time.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

This seems workable on a treadmill, but I’d keep the pace easy during the night shift, because juggling orders is more attention than I’d want while trying to run hard. Full controller support is a real plus here, and the game doesn’t sound like it depends on precise aiming or twitch reactions. Still, if several customers are waiting and I’m trying to read what each one wants, my attention is going to the screen. My running form can wait its turn.

The ingredient gathering by day sounds like the more relaxed part to do while moving, since you’re not also trying to serve a room full of customers. I’d use that stretch to settle into a steady pace, then be ready to slow down a little when the restaurant opens. There’s no reason to make a cozy cooking game feel like interval training, though I can see how I’d try to do that by accident.

On a bike, I think the menus and recipe choices would be easier to manage, since your hands can stay steady while you look over what you need. That doesn’t mean the treadmill is a bad fit, just that the busy service parts ask for a little more coordination when your body is moving up and down. I’d probably keep the controller grip simple and avoid turning the pace up while I’m learning how the orders fit together.

I don’t know whether I’d want to play through a whole busy night while running, because I can imagine missing a request and then trying to recover while the next one appears. But the game has a natural calmer stretch built into its day and night routine, so you can choose when to push and when to keep things casual. That’s a sensible arrangement.

## Conclusion and Score

Worth a try if you like cozy management games and want something that gives you a quieter stretch before asking for more attention. I’d play it on an easy treadmill pace or a bike, and save the busy restaurant shifts for when you can focus.
