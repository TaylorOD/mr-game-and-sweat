Crossout can work well for a workout because its vehicle battles keep you steering, aiming, and firing instead of staring through long story scenes. The catch is that multiplayer opponents will not slow down while you find your rhythm, and you cannot treat a match like a pause button with wheels.

## Gameplay

The main appeal is building a machine that feels like yours, then finding out if your design survives contact with other players. You assemble an armoured vehicle from parts such as a cabin, frames, wheels, weapons, and support modules. That gives you room to make a nimble car with guns, a lumbering machine with heavy firepower, or something built around a more unusual weapon setup. The game also supports aerial battles, so vehicle design is not limited to ground combat.

Your build choices matter in the fight. Weapons can be shot off, wheels can be destroyed, and losing parts can leave a once sturdy vehicle crawling across the map. You can aim at an opponent’s weapons or mobility instead of simply firing at the biggest target you see. That makes each clash feel more tactical than a simple race with guns strapped on. A fast build may be able to circle a heavier opponent, while a heavier one can punish a driver who charges in without a plan.

Crossout has multiplayer battles as well as cooperative PvE activities, giving you a change of pace from fighting other players. Matches are generally focused on action, with little in the way of long cut scenes or story interruptions. You spend more time driving and shooting than reading lore. There is still some downtime in menus, matchmaking, and the garage, especially if you want to tinker with a build before queuing again. That garage time can be part of the fun, but it can also turn a quick session into a surprisingly long debate about whether your vehicle needs one more gun.

The free to play structure brings a longer progression and parts economy into the picture. Collecting and trying new components gives you reasons to keep adjusting your machine, though it also means you will not immediately have every part you want. The basic loop is strong: build, battle, earn, and rebuild. It keeps your attention well, even if the menu and progression side is less exciting than the fights themselves.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Crossout has full controller support, and driving a vehicle feels like a natural fit for a gamepad. Steering and camera control are easy to understand, while firing and aiming keep your hands occupied. You do not need to manage a complicated set of character actions or read a lot of text in the middle of a battle.

That does not make it an effortless workout game. Battles are online and competitive, so you cannot pause when you need to adjust your pace or catch your breath. Enemies move quickly, and aiming at specific parts of a moving vehicle takes focus. A poorly timed turn can send you into a wall or expose your weapons to a player who knows exactly what to target. It is manageable on a bike or treadmill at a steady pace, but hard intervals may make precise driving and aiming a chore.

The best approach is to keep your exercise effort moderate during matches and use the garage or matchmaking time for a brief reset. Co op activities can be a less stressful option than facing human opponents, though you still need to steer and fight. If you want a game that lets you zone out and pedal, look elsewhere. If you like active combat and can keep your workout pace consistent, Crossout is a decent match.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements call for a 64 bit version of Windows 10 or 11, a four core Intel Core i5 2310 or AMD FX 4100 processor, 4 GB of RAM, and a GeForce GTX 950 or Radeon RX 460. You also need DirectX 12, a broadband internet connection, and 24 GB of available storage.

Those graphics cards are older, but they are dedicated gaming cards rather than basic integrated graphics. A normal mid range computer with a graphics card from the last several years should be in a reasonable position to run the game. The minimum RAM requirement is modest, though the online nature of the game makes a reliable internet connection part of the deal. This is not a game that demands a top end machine, but it is also not one to count on for a very old office laptop.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Crossout gets a lot of mileage from letting you build the vehicle you want, then watching that design get battered apart in a multiplayer fight. The parts system gives battles personality, and the action stays busy without making you sit through a long story first. You will spend some time in menus and progression systems, but the actual fights are the reason to stick around.

For exercise, full controller support is a plus, and vehicle controls suit a gamepad well. The main limitation is that aiming and reacting to opponents takes attention, while online matches cannot be paused. It is better for a steady workout than a frantic sprint interval. If you want a free to play action game with creative vehicle building and you can keep your pace under control, Crossout is worth a try.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
