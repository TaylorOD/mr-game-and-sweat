A slow search through abandoned places sounds better for a workout than another game asking you to react to everything at once, but Stay Out also has shooting and a shared online world, so I’d keep the pace easy and my expectations fairly modest. It might be a nice way to make a steady session less boring, as long as you’re okay with stopping to focus now and then.

## Gameplay

The urban exploration angle is what gets my attention. Searching through places that have been left behind by people gives you a reason to look around instead of just charging toward the next marker, and I think that kind of slower attention can pair well with an easy workout. You’d have something to do with your hands and your eyes without needing the game to throw a fresh emergency at you every few seconds. At least, that’s what I’d hope for.

The shooting changes the mood. I like the idea of wandering through a strange, abandoned area, but once the game asks you to aim and react, you’re not really taking a casual stroll anymore. I don’t know how often those moments come up or how demanding they are, so I wouldn’t promise this is a relaxed game all the way through. It sounds like a game that could let you settle into a rhythm and then ask you to pay attention.

There’s also the MMORPG part, which makes me a little less sure about how cleanly the workout fits. I enjoy games with a world to spend time in, but online games can put you in situations where you can’t simply pause and take a breather while the rest of the world waits politely. That’s not a complaint about the idea, it’s just a thing I’d factor in before stepping onto a moving belt.

Stay Out is free to play and in Early Access, which makes it easy to try, and also means I’d treat my first session as a test rather than a commitment. If the exploring is the part you enjoy, you may find enough to keep your mind occupied; if you need every minute to be active and predictable, the slower searching could feel like standing in a room trying to remember why you walked in. I’ve done that without a game helping.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d start with a slow walking pace, not a run, because shooting and moving at the same time asks more of your hands than the exploration description does. On a bike, keeping your legs moving while you look around may be easier, but aiming still needs your attention, and I wouldn’t count on being able to play carelessly just because you’re seated. The abandoned places sound like the calmer stretches. The shooting sounds like the part where you stop trying to multitask.

I can’t tell from the information here whether full controller support is available, so I wouldn’t buy or install it expecting a controller to work without checking first. That matters a lot on a treadmill, where reaching for a keyboard and mouse is less comfortable than it is at a desk, and it’s not a detail I’d want to guess about. If you already know your setup works, I’d still keep the pace low until you know how much aiming the game asks of you.

I also wouldn’t start a session while planning to pause whenever you feel like it. Stay Out is an MMORPG, so I’d assume the online world may keep moving around you, and I’d choose a workout where slowing down or stepping off is easy. You don’t need a heroic plan here. Just give it a try at a pace where a surprise fight doesn’t turn your treadmill into a small administrative problem.

For the workout itself, I’d skip running until you know how comfortably you can handle the controls, and I’d avoid treating every shooting moment like it needs a perfect response. That may mean the game fits an easy bike ride better than a hard run, but I don’t think it’s automatically a bad match. The unknown controller support keeps me from being more confident.

## Computer Requirements

This needs a fairly capable midrange gaming computer, not just something that can display a desktop and open a browser. I’d look first at whether your graphics card meets the listed minimum, since an older or integrated one may leave you short.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Stay Out sounds like a reasonable try for an easy workout if the slow exploration appeals to you and your controls are sorted out. I’d skip it for fast treadmill sessions or anyone who wants a game that can be safely ignored while moving.
