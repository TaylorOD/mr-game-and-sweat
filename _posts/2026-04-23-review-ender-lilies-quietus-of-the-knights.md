I’d save ENDER LILIES: Quietus of the Knights for a walk or an easy bike ride, because its fights ask you to watch an enemy closely and one distracted dodge can send you back to a safe spot. The ruined kingdom is a good place to spend a workout, but I wouldn’t pair a hard run with a boss fight.

## Gameplay

The combat is what keeps this moving for me. Lily doesn’t do the fighting herself, so you call on fallen knights to attack while you dodge and try to read what’s coming. That gives you a nice mix of managing your own movement and choosing how to deal with a threat, and I think that’s more interesting than hitting the same button until the screen clears. There’s usually something to pay attention to.

But this isn’t the kind of action game where you can keep jogging and let your hands sort it out. Enemies can punish a moment of inattention, and bosses ask you to learn their patterns instead of just charging in. I like the tension, but I’d be lying if I said I could keep a steady workout pace and focus on every attack at the same time. Sometimes my brain only has room for one moving thing.

Exploring the kingdom gives the fights some breathing room, and I like the feeling that there’s another path to check instead of a long stretch of story that makes you stop playing. There are quiet moments, though, and you may end up walking back through places you’ve already seen while looking for where to go next. I don’t know that every bit of backtracking earns its place, but it’s easier to manage than sitting through a long cutscene while the treadmill keeps going.

The mood is very somber, which fits the world and gives the exploring a bit of weight. It can also make a workout feel like a long walk through a place where nobody has had a good week in quite a while. I like that the story doesn’t constantly interrupt the action, and I’d take its quiet sadness over an endless parade of speeches.

The fallen knights make the combat feel flexible without turning it into a pile of menus I need to study before I can start moving. Still, switching attacks and keeping track of enemy timing takes more attention than I’d want during a fast run. I think the game is best when you’re awake enough to enjoy learning a fight, but not so tired that a dodge feels like a math problem.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A slow walk is probably the safest way to start, because the game’s fights rely on timing and it’s easy to imagine a thumb bouncing at the exact moment you need to dodge. Full controller support makes it straightforward to play from a treadmill or bike, but that doesn’t make the combat hands off. When a boss starts attacking, I’d slow down or stop pedaling for a bit rather than try to prove I can do both at once.

The exploration is friendlier to exercise than the boss fights. Moving through a familiar area and checking another path should leave you more room to settle into a pace, while a new enemy or a tricky encounter is likely to pull your attention back to the screen. I’d take those sections as they come and avoid treating the treadmill speed as something I have to defend. The machine will keep going. It doesn’t need me to win the argument.

On a bike, I think an easy pace makes sense, especially once the enemies get quicker and the fights ask you to react right away. A stationary bike also lets you pause your legs without needing to step off, which is useful when you want to focus on a boss. On a treadmill I’d stay at a walk, and I’d lower the pace before a fight instead of waiting until I’m already trying to dodge and breathe at the same time.

I wouldn’t skip the game’s exploration, but I would save the most demanding fights for when you’re comfortable easing off the workout. If you’re trying to keep a fast pace the whole time, the game may turn into a choice between playing carefully and getting the exercise you planned. I don’t know how people make that feel automatic. I’d rather take it slow.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest enough that an older computer with a basic graphics setup should be able to run it, and the game only needs a small amount of storage. I’d check that the computer can handle the required 64 bit system and DirectX 11, then worry less about building a machine that can also launch a spaceship.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is a thoughtful action RPG for someone who wants exploration and tense fights during an easy workout, not a game to pair with an all out run. I’d give it a try on a walk or relaxed bike ride, and save the tougher fights for when you can give them your attention.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
