I’d start with the bike events on a treadmill, because Riders Republic gives you a lot to do without asking you to read a story between every run. Save the Mass Races for when you’re ready to give the controller your whole attention.

## Gameplay

The open world is the part I like most, because you can switch from racing to messing around on a bike, skis, a snowboard, or a wingsuit without the game making you settle into one thing for very long. There are events to chase, but you can also just pick a direction and ride, and I think that freedom is useful when you’re exercising and your brain isn’t looking to manage a complicated plan. You can make a little route for yourself, or you can just head toward whatever looks fun.

The events keep things moving, which is probably why I’d stay interested for a longer workout than I would in a game that keeps stopping to explain its world. Bike races give you a clear job, and trick events let you focus on getting a line right instead of worrying about finishing first. I like that the sports feel different enough that switching between them changes the kind of attention you need. The wingsuit asks for more steering precision than I’d want when my feet are bouncing around, though. I’d save that for a slower part of the workout.

Mass Races sound like a great idea when you’re sitting still, because throwing a lot of riders into one event makes everything feel busy and ridiculous. On a treadmill, that same crowd could turn a simple race into a lot of last second steering, and I don’t know that I’d want to spend my run getting jostled around while I’m trying to make a turn. I’d try one when I was fresh, but I wouldn’t make it the default.

There’s enough going on that I’d expect to spend some time choosing where to go and what to do, and that’s the part that keeps this from being a completely effortless workout game. Still, the actual sports are the point, and you can get back to riding without sitting through a long story scene. For me, that balance works. You’re mostly out there moving.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The controller makes this much more practical than trying to steer with a keyboard while your hands are occupied, and the store page specifically recommends one. I’d keep the controller setup simple and stick with the bike races or trick events first, because those give you a clear direction without needing quite as much precision as a wingsuit run. A bike is probably the easier place to start, since you can focus on turning and staying on the course instead of making tiny corrections through the air.

The tricky part is that these are sports games, so your input matters. You’re steering around terrain, choosing when to jump, and trying not to turn one small mistake into a long detour, and while you’re on a moving treadmill that can take more focus than it sounds like. I’d avoid trying to be perfect about every landing. You don’t need to ride like you’re auditioning for a highlight reel. You need to keep going.

Free riding looks like the sensible option when you’re tired, because there’s no event finish line pushing you to react quickly and you can just explore at your own pace. Then, when you feel more alert, pick an event and give it a go. I don’t know how much I’d enjoy a crowded race while jogging, but having the open world available means you can change the mood without quitting the game.

On a bike, I’d be more comfortable with the faster events because the seat keeps your body steadier while you’re steering. I’d still skip the Mass Races if I was trying to keep my effort even, since the scramble of a multiplayer race is likely to pull my attention away from the workout. The game needs an online connection, too, so this isn’t a choice for a setup where the internet drops in and out. That could get old.

## Computer Requirements

This needs a dedicated graphics card around the level of an older gaming PC, so I wouldn’t count on integrated graphics to handle it. I’d check the graphics card first, and then make sure the computer can stay online while you play. It asks for some muscle, but not a machine built to launch a satellite.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Riders Republic makes exercise feel less repetitive by giving you a lot of sports and places to move around in, but the fast steering and crowded races won’t suit every workout. I’d recommend it to someone who wants an active game with room to roam, and I’d tell anyone who wants a calmer ride to stick with free riding and the bike events.
