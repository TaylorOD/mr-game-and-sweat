I’d put Maximum Action on a bike or an easy treadmill walk, because its slow motion gunfights sound fun until you’re trying to aim while your hands are bouncing around. It’s built to keep your attention, but it asks for steadier thumbs than a hard run usually gives you.

## Gameplay

The old school shooter setup makes sense for exercise because you’re spending your attention on the action instead of waiting through long conversations, and I think that’s a real advantage here. Maximum Action is about moving through fights and shooting, with slow motion giving you room to make those fights feel like action movie scenes. You don’t need to study a pile of systems before you can get going. That’s nice.

I’m going to be a little controversial here, slow motion is probably the part that makes this work for me, not just the flashy part of it. When the action gets fast, slowing it down gives you a chance to choose a target and make a plan, which is much better than trying to aim while your whole body is bouncing. It also means the game can keep the pace high without making every second feel like a tiny panic attack.

The physics based shooting sounds like it should make every fight feel loose and unpredictable, and that can be fun, but it also means I wouldn’t expect every shot to behave like a clean little point and click. I like the idea of a shooter that leans into messy action rather than asking you to stand still and line up perfect shots. Still, if a fight starts going sideways, I’d be tempted to pause my exercise and sort it out. My attention has limits.

There’s no big story pitch here to pull you away from the action, which suits this kind of game. I don’t know how much the early access status changes from one version to the next, so I wouldn’t buy it expecting every edge to be polished. But if you want a shooter where the point is to get into fights and make them look dramatic, it seems like a pretty direct use of your time. The premise is easy to understand.

I’d probably enjoy the action more when I’m not already trying to manage a tough workout, because aiming is still aiming, even when time slows down. The game can keep you mentally busy, but it’s not the sort of busy where you can forget your hands are holding a controller. If you like action movies and old school shooters, that tradeoff makes sense. If you mostly want something you can play on autopilot, this may not be it.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The bike seems like the more comfortable place to play this, since your upper body can stay steadier while you work through the fights. A treadmill walk could be fine too, especially if you’re willing to keep the pace easy, but I’d be careful about trying to run hard while making quick turns and lining up shots. It sounds like a lot of little corrections, and I already have enough trouble keeping my feet where I expect them.

It has controller support, which is important, because playing a first person shooter on a keyboard while moving would be a lot to ask of yourself. I’d use the controller and keep the workout gentle enough that aiming doesn’t turn into a negotiation between my thumbs and the belt. The game’s slow motion helps, but it doesn’t make the aiming disappear.

I’d skip the idea of treating every fight like a speed run while you’re exercising. When you’re moving, there’s a difference between having a fun action scene and suddenly needing to make a very specific turn before someone shoots you. I don’t know how consistently you’ll be able to stay calm once the action ramps up, but I’d rather play it at a pace where a missed shot is mildly annoying instead of a reason to grab the rails.

This isn’t a game where you can just pedal along and let your hands do almost nothing. The shooting gives you a reason to keep watching, and the slow motion can buy a little breathing room, but you still have to aim and react. That’s a reasonable compromise for a bike session, and probably a tougher one for a run.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are low enough that an older gaming PC should have a chance, and you don’t need to start by shopping for a new graphics card. I’d check that your machine can handle the game smoothly, because a shooter feels worse when the action stutters than when the room looks a little plain. The computer can be old. Your aim has enough to deal with.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Take Maximum Action to a bike ride or a gentle walk if you want a fast shooter that keeps you looking at the screen. I’d skip it for a hard run, but action movie fans who enjoy aiming and quick reactions should give it a try.
