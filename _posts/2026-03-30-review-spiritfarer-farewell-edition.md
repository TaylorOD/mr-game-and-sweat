The boat gives you little jobs to think about while you move, which sounds like a pretty good treadmill arrangement until a spirit needs a conversation and the game asks you to stand still for a bit. I think Spiritfarer: Farewell Edition is lovely, but it’s not exactly a steady stream of things to do.

## Gameplay

Most of your time is split between sailing, taking care of your boat, and helping the spirits who live aboard. You build rooms and work areas, grow food, cook meals, and handle requests, which gives you a pleasant routine without turning the whole game into a list of chores. I like having a few small goals to bounce between. It feels more like tidying up a kitchen than trying to run one.

The people you meet are the reason I’d keep going. They have their own needs and conversations, and eventually you have to help them leave for the afterlife. That gives even a simple task like cooking dinner a bit more weight, because you’re doing it for someone who matters to the story. I don’t know that every conversation will land the same way for everybody, but the game gives you room to care about these characters without rushing you.

Sailing is where the pace can get a little sleepy. You choose where to go, then the boat travels while you can do chores, and I think that can be relaxing if you’re happy to putter around. It can also feel like waiting for the next part of the game to start, especially when you’ve finished the things you wanted to do on board. There’s only so much sweeping a boat needs.

The platforming and little activities add some change of pace, and I’d be glad to have them so the whole game isn’t feeding people and checking on plants. But they also mean you sometimes need to pay closer attention than the gentle look of the game suggests. Some moments ask you to move precisely or react to what’s happening, and that can make a slow trip across the water feel surprisingly busy. I wouldn’t call it a constant distraction, though.

There’s a lot of reading and conversation, and you can’t really skip past the emotional parts without losing what makes the game work. I think that’s a good choice for playing on the couch and a less convenient choice when your legs are already doing something. If you want a game that keeps giving you immediate action, this probably isn’t it. If you like having a calm little project between story moments, it may be exactly your speed.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

You’ll probably spend stretches doing simple boat chores, then stop moving around the boat to talk to a spirit or decide where to sail next. That on and off rhythm seems manageable on a treadmill at an easy pace, but I’d expect it to interrupt a harder run. It’s a game that occasionally asks for your attention and then gives you a quiet stretch to recover it.

Full controller support is a big help here, since you don’t have to reach for a keyboard while you’re moving. I’d keep the controls in your hands and the pace comfortable, because climbing around the boat and handling platforming sections could get awkward if you’re bouncing along. Nothing about the basic routine sounds like it needs sharp aim or constant fast reactions, but there are enough moments of movement that I wouldn’t treat every section as background play.

On a bike, I think the stop and start pacing would be easier to live with, since you can keep pedaling through a conversation without worrying about your stride. That doesn’t mean the game suddenly turns into nonstop activity, though. You may be cruising along, tending a garden, then watching a conversation unfold while your character waits. It’s a little like taking a long ride with someone who occasionally asks you to pull over and help carry a table.

I’d play with the difficulty of the workout in mind, rather than trying to push hard through the trickier movement sections. If a spirit’s request or a conversation matters to you, stop and give it your attention instead of trying to read while you’re moving. I don’t know how much of the quieter sailing time you’ll enjoy as exercise time, but at least the game doesn’t demand that you be quick every minute.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest enough that I’d look first at whether your computer has a compatible graphics card, rather than worrying about needing a new gaming machine. The listed graphics requirement is an older, basic one, so this should be within reach for plenty of older computers.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is a gentle, emotional game for you if you want a cozy routine and don’t mind pausing your workout focus for conversations and platforming. I’d skip it if you need constant action to keep your legs moving, but for an easy ride or walk with a thoughtful game, I’d give it a try.
