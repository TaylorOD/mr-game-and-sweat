A starship is a pretty good thing to have on screen while you’re walking, because a lot of the time you can steer the fight instead of trying to hit a tiny target while your hands bounce around. Star Trek Online has full controller support, though I’d treat the ground missions as a different workout than the space missions.

## Gameplay

I think Star Trek Online is a good game with a lot of different speeds, and that variety is useful when you’re trying to keep your legs going but it also means you won’t be doing space combat every minute. You take charge of a ship and its crew, then move between exploring, talking to people, and fighting. When the game gives you a fight, I’d expect it to pull your attention in a way that makes the treadmill feel less like the only thing happening. Some of the conversations are a slower gear.

The ship combat sounds like the part I’d be most excited to have during a workout. You’re managing a starship rather than trying to make a tiny character do perfect little movements, and that feels like a better match for a moving controller. There’s room to think about what you’re doing without every second demanding a fast reaction. I’d probably be much more relaxed steering a ship through a fight than trying to aim while my thumbs are doing their own thing.

Ground missions change that calculation a little. Your character and crew are down on a planet, and now you’re moving around and aiming instead of commanding a ship from a distance. I don’t know how much I’d enjoy that part while jogging, because even ordinary aiming gets fussy when your hands aren’t still, and a group of enemies makes it easier to lose track of what you meant to do. On a bike, I’d be more comfortable with it.

The story and exploration are part of the appeal, but they’re also why this isn’t a constant action game. You’re going to have stretches of dialogue and getting from one activity to the next, and I think those can be nice at an easy pace, especially when you want a break from focusing on combat. If you’re hoping the game will keep throwing fights at you without asking you to listen, that’s probably not what you’re getting. The quiet parts are part of the trip.

There’s also a lot of game wrapped around the individual missions, because this is a free to play massively multiplayer RPG and not a short action game you finish in one tidy sitting. That can be great if you like having a universe to return to and enjoy setting your own pace, but I’d expect menus and decisions to take up some of your attention too. I’d start with the story missions and leave the more complicated ship and character choices for when you’re stopped. There’s plenty to sort through.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The space fights are where I’d put the treadmill to work. They sound like a better fit for steady walking or an easy run than combat built around precise aiming, and the controller means you’re not stuck reaching for a keyboard while trying to keep your stride. I’d keep the pace comfortable enough that I could follow what was happening. The game still gets your attention.

On a bike, I’d feel more willing to mix in the ground missions, since your upper body can stay steadier while your legs keep moving. I’d still expect aiming to take more concentration than steering a ship, so I’d save the more active stretches for a pace where I can use the controller without fighting my own balance. It isn’t a game I’d want to turn into a sprint workout.

Because it’s an online game, I wouldn’t plan on treating a mission like a video I can pause whenever I feel like it. I’d choose a point where I have enough time to finish what I’m doing, and I’d avoid starting a workout with the expectation that I can step away in the middle of a fight. I don’t know that every moment needs intense focus, but the game is still happening while you’re playing it.

I’d also be picky about what I do while moving. Ship missions and exploration seem like the sensible starting point, while long stretches of dialogue are better at a slow pace or when you’re off the machine. If you want one game that turns every minute of exercise into frantic action, this would probably leave you waiting around sometimes. That isn’t always bad.

Full controller support matters a lot here, and it keeps the game in the running for exercise, but it doesn’t make every part equally comfortable. I’d give it a try on a walk or an easy ride first, then see whether ground combat feels manageable for you. There’s no need to make your workout harder just because an enemy showed up.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are unusually friendly, with support for older integrated graphics as well as old graphics cards, so this doesn’t look like a game that demands a new gaming computer. I’d check that your graphics hardware can handle DirectX 11 and that you have room for the 21GB install, then make sure your internet connection is up to it.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Star Trek Online makes sense for you if you want a big, ongoing Star Trek game that can fill an easy workout with ship combat and exploration, and you’re fine with dialogue and setup between the action. I’d start on the treadmill with the space missions and save ground combat for the bike or a slower pace.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
