I’d play ShellShock Live on a steady walk, not a hard run, because lining up a tank shot takes just enough attention that I’d want my feet doing something predictable. The turns give you time to breathe, but they also give you time to start caring a little too much about where that shot lands.

## Gameplay

The aiming is the part that makes this work for me. You pick an angle and power, account for the wind, and send a weapon across the map, which is simple to understand but still gives you a small decision to chew on each turn. I like that the game can make one shot feel clever without asking you to learn a giant list of controls. Sometimes it’s just adjusting the angle a bit and hoping the other tank is still there.

The turn based pace is useful for exercising, but it isn’t constant action. You can be ready to shoot and then wait while the other players take their turns, and depending on the match that might feel like a nice breather or like standing around while your treadmill keeps going. I think team matches make that waiting easier to tolerate, since you’re watching the whole team’s situation instead of only your own tank. It’s still waiting, though.

There are hundreds of upgradable weapons, and earning XP to unlock more gives you a reason to keep coming back. That’s fun, and it also means there’s a lot of gear to sort through once you start unlocking things. I’d focus on finding a handful of weapons whose shots make sense to you instead of trying to remember every option, because I can barely remember what I meant to buy at the grocery store.

Free for all sounds like the easiest way to keep the match about your own shots, while team play gives you the nice feeling of helping somebody else land theirs. I’d probably pick based on how much attention I had that day, since making decisions for a team while bouncing along on a treadmill could get a little busy. There’s no story taking over the match, which is a big plus here. You get to play.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is a real reason to try it, and the turn based structure means you aren’t constantly steering, jumping, or reacting at the exact second something happens. I’d put the controller somewhere comfortable, keep the pace moderate, and use the turns to settle back into a steady stride before aiming. If your movement makes the shot drift around more than you like, you’ll notice pretty quickly.

A bike might be easier if you want to lean forward and concentrate on the angle and power, but I don’t think the treadmill is out of the question. The input is deliberate rather than twitchy, and missing a shot doesn’t ask you to make a frantic correction with your hands. Still, the online match keeps moving while you’re in it, so I wouldn’t choose this for the workout where you’re already tired and struggling to keep your attention on the screen.

I don’t know how much control precision matters until you’re trying to land a shot across uneven terrain, and that’s where the workout could start competing with the game. A small change in angle or power can be the difference between a hit and a shot that goes wandering off, and I’d find that annoying if my thumb was moving with every step. I’d keep the pace easy and give myself permission to take the less ambitious shot.

Playing with friends seems like the friendlier way to start, especially in a team match where one imperfect shot isn’t the whole story. I’d skip any match where you’re trying to prove something to strangers, because then every miss gets weirdly important and you’re suddenly doing math while sweating. I don’t know why I always think I can make a perfect shot when I’m in a hurry, but I’d try not to build a workout around that belief.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very modest, so an older computer or one using integrated graphics should be enough to get started. I’d check that your internet connection is steady first, since this is built around online multiplayer and a dropped connection would be more annoying than a low graphics setting. Your tank does not need a fancy computer.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

ShellShock Live makes sense for an easy treadmill walk or a steady bike ride, especially if you like taking a careful shot and can live with waiting between turns. I’d pass if you need nonstop action to keep your workout moving, but for a slower session, it’s an easy one to try.
