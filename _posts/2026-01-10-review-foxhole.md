A persistent war where your side needs supplies sounds like it could make a treadmill session disappear, but Foxhole also asks you to coordinate with strangers and aim while the belt keeps moving. I’d save it for sitting down.

## Gameplay

Foxhole has a big idea that I really like: the war isn’t just a row of matches that reset when you’re done, and the soldiers, supplies, defenses, and reconnaissance all come from players. That makes even the less glamorous jobs feel connected to the fight, which is unusual and pretty cool. You’re not just being told that your actions matter. The game is built around that idea.

The tradeoff is that a lot of the work is not a firefight. You can contribute through logistics or base building, and I think that’s a real strength when you want to feel useful without being the person at the front trying to win every duel. But it also means you might spend a chunk of a session gathering yourself, figuring out what the war needs, and doing a job that is important without being very exciting moment to moment. That’s a bit like going to the gym to put all the plates away.

Combat gives you the urgency the logistics side sometimes lacks, but it’s not the sort of action where you can switch your brain off and keep running. You need to pay attention to where you are, what you can see, and what other players are doing, and a careless move can make your contribution pretty short. I like that the scale makes a small fight feel connected to a larger effort, though I wouldn’t expect every stretch of play to feel like a dramatic push across the map. Some of it is just people trying to get organized.

There’s also a learning curve in working out how the different jobs fit together. I don’t know how quickly a new player will feel useful without someone pointing them toward a task, and that matters because the game seems much more rewarding when you understand what the group needs. You can still pick a direction and contribute, but I’d go in expecting to learn by watching and asking rather than assuming the game will explain the whole war neatly.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The movement of the treadmill is not the part I’d worry about. It’s trying to aim at a moving target, keep track of the fight, and make decisions for a team while your hands are doing their own small workout. Foxhole is a live multiplayer war, so I wouldn’t count on pausing when you need to slow down or look away, and I can picture myself stepping off the belt because I wanted to finish one more fight. That would defeat the point a little.

The game’s view from above helps you see more of the battlefield than a close camera would, but seeing a lot doesn’t mean the action is easy to manage while moving. I’d expect frontline combat to ask for careful aiming and quick reactions, and those are exactly the moments where a bouncing treadmill can turn a small adjustment into a very bad shot. On a bike the movement may be steadier, but you’re still trying to do a precision job while paying attention to your legs.

I can’t confirm native controller support from the information here, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming a controller will work the way you want. I’d check that before planning a workout around it, and I’d skip frontline fighting until I knew the controls felt reliable. A quieter logistics or building task sounds more manageable, but the war keeps going and other players are depending on the shared effort, so this isn’t a game that politely waits for your workout to catch up. I don’t know that I’d want that pressure while running.

If you do try it while exercising, I’d start at a slow pace and choose a task that doesn’t ask you to react to every little change on screen. That’s less exciting than joining the action, but it seems like the practical way to find out whether the controls and your setup can coexist. I’d keep the treadmill speed boring. Boring is useful here.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum asks for a fairly modest processor, but it also calls for a dedicated graphics card around the GTX 960 level, so I’d check the graphics first rather than assume a basic laptop will manage it. It needs a Windows computer with 8 GB of memory and 20 GB of storage, which is manageable, though the graphics requirement is doing the heavy lifting.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Save Foxhole for a focused session at a desk, especially if the idea of helping a huge player run war sounds more appealing than being in constant combat. For exercise, I’d pass unless you’ve checked the controls and are willing to keep the pace gentle. The war can manage without me jogging through it.
