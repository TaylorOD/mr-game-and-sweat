I think Abiotic Factor could make a good easy workout game, as long as you’re okay with some of the workout being spent looking for the right materials instead of fighting things. You’re a scientist trying to survive a research facility that has gone very wrong, which is a better reason to wander around than most.

## Gameplay

The survival crafting setup sounds like it fits the strange facility well, and I like that you’re not just dropped into a wilderness with trees and rocks and told to make a chair. You’re trying to get by inside a research complex while paranormal containment is failing, the military is making its own problems, and things from other realms are involved. That gives all the resource gathering a reason to be there, even if gathering resources is still gathering resources.

I’d expect the appeal to be the slow accumulation of useful stuff, where you explore a little, bring materials back, and make your situation less bad before heading farther in. That can be a nice pace for a workout because there’s usually a small practical goal sitting in front of you, and you don’t have to care about winning a close match. But if your idea of a good run is never stopping to think about what you need next, survival crafting can turn into a lot of standing still with a menu open. It happens.

The setting is doing a lot of work for me. An underground research facility full of containment problems gives the usual scavenging and crafting loop a little more personality, and the clash between scientists, military forces, and threats from other realms sounds like a fun mess to explore. I don’t know how much of that variety comes through moment to moment, but it’s a more appealing backdrop than another patch of woods where I’m punching a tree because apparently that’s what people do now.

There’s also room to play with up to six people, which could make the survival chores feel less solitary and let everyone have a job, assuming your group is the sort that can agree on a job. I could see that being the version I’d want with friends, but co op also makes it easier to lose track of what you were supposed to be doing, which is familiar from any group activity that involves a shared pile of supplies. Solo sounds more deliberate, and probably easier to fit around your own pace.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The parts that suit exercise are the stretches of exploring and collecting, where I’d expect to move through the facility at my own pace and decide what’s worth bringing back. Those are the bits I’d want during a relaxed walk or an easy bike session. I’d probably make a small goal before starting, like gathering a particular kind of material, because “I’ll just look around” can become twenty minutes of opening storage and forgetting why I went over there.

Combat is where I’d be more careful. The facility’s threats and the military conflict mean this isn’t only a calm scavenging game, and anything that asks you to aim and react quickly is harder when your hands are bouncing along with your legs. Full controller support is good, but it doesn’t make your thumb suddenly more accurate. I’d slow down, use a comfortable pace, and avoid turning a workout into a desperate attempt to hit something moving across the screen.

Inventory and crafting are the parts I’d skip while moving fast. I’d handle the fiddly decisions before I started, or pause the workout if I had to sort out what I was carrying, because crafting menus and a treadmill do not need to become a team project. I don’t know how much pausing or safe downtime the game gives you in each situation, so I wouldn’t count on being able to stop thinking whenever I wanted.

On a bike, I’d be more comfortable with the menu breaks, since there’s less need to keep a steady walking rhythm, but I still wouldn’t pick a hard effort for a first session. The game seems better suited to a pace where you can glance at what’s happening and react without making every enemy encounter into a little emergency. You can give it a try on an easy workout and see how often you end up reaching for the pause button.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This needs a reasonably capable gaming PC, and the graphics card is what I’d check first because the minimum asks for a GTX 1660 or RX 5600 XT. The game runs on Unreal Engine 5, so the art style is not a promise that an old computer will quietly handle it.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

I’d suggest Abiotic Factor to someone who likes survival crafting and wants a game with enough quiet exploring to fit an easy workout. Skip it if menus and resource gathering make you want to put the controller down.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
