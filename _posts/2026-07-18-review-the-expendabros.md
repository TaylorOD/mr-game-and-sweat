Those Eastern European forests are a pretty good place to get your legs moving, because The Expendabros keeps putting enemies and explosive scenery in front of you instead of asking you to study a map. I’d start at a walking pace, though, since the jumping and sudden firefights need a little more attention than a relaxed jog.

## Gameplay

The setup gets to the point quickly. The Expendabros are going after arms dealer Conrad Stonebanks, and I think that’s plenty of story for a game where the trees and the people standing near them can both turn into debris. I don’t need a long introduction before I can start firing.

The side scrolling action is busy in a way that works for exercise, because you’re usually moving, aiming, jumping, or trying to get out of a mess you helped make. The destructible scenery gives fights a loose, chaotic feel, and explosions can open up space or make the whole screen harder to read. I like that the game doesn’t ask me to stop and carefully plan every move, even if sometimes the plan seems to be “shoot near the problem.”

Rescuing characters and getting access to different weapons helps keep the action from feeling exactly the same all the way through. Some attacks are easier to use at a glance than others, and I’d probably enjoy the switch ups more than I’d enjoy trying to play every character perfectly. I don’t know why the chunky look makes the mess easier to follow, but I think it helps when the screen gets busy.

There’s a difference between chaotic and confusing, and The Expendabros occasionally wanders toward confusing when explosions, enemies, and a jump all arrive together. It’s still the kind of problem I’d rather have in a short action game than a long stretch of walking around waiting for the next fight. Conrad Stonebanks gives the campaign a clear target, and the game seems happiest when it’s getting you back into the action.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A controller is the right way to play this, and full controller support means you don’t have to reach for a keyboard while you’re moving. The basic actions make sense on a controller, but there’s enough quick aiming and jumping that I wouldn’t expect every input to land neatly while running. The game is not complicated, but your thumbs still have to keep up.

On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace easy enough that you can look at the screen and react without bouncing through a jump by accident. You’ll be moving constantly in the game, which is good for keeping your attention, but a run of enemies can make you lean forward and focus on the controller instead of your workout. I can see myself forgetting about my pace for a bit. That’s probably a sign to slow down.

A bike seems easier to pair with the sudden action, since your hands can stay steady while your legs keep going, but I wouldn’t call it effortless there either. When enemies fill the screen and you’re trying to jump out of the way, the game can take more concentration than a casual ride really needs. I still don’t know how much more the game could simplify those fights without making them dull, so I’d treat this as an easy workout game rather than something to play during a hard effort.

I’d skip jogging quickly with it, and I wouldn’t choose it for a workout where you need the game to wait patiently while you catch your breath. It’s more forgiving than a competitive game where every small mistake costs the match, but it does ask you to react. At a gentle pace, that feels manageable.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The listed requirements are light, so I’d check that an older computer’s graphics support meets the OpenGL requirement before worrying about raw power. It needs very little storage and doesn’t ask for a recent, expensive graphics card, which is nice for a game that mostly wants you to blow up a forest.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Take The Expendabros to an easy walk or steady bike ride if you want a free action game that keeps you busy without a lot of story pauses. I’d skip it for fast treadmill running, but it’s an easy recommendation for a relaxed workout and a short, noisy campaign.

### Overall Score: 2.67 out of 3
