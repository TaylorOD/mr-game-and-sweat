A treadmill can work for a trip through the Old Republic, but I’d keep the pace comfortable because this game asks you to stop and think pretty often. The lightsaber fights are fun, but the conversations are where you’ll want your eyes and attention.

## Gameplay

I think STAR WARS Knights of the Old Republic is still a great RPG, partly because it lets you decide what kind of person you’re going to be and then makes those choices matter to how the story feels. You can try to be a decent Jedi, lean into the dark side, or make choices based on what seems useful at the time, which is probably how a lot of us would behave if the galaxy suddenly got complicated. The game gives you room to think about it.

That also means there’s a lot of dialogue, and some of it takes a while. You’re talking to people, learning what’s going on, and picking responses, so this isn’t a game that keeps your attention on constant action. I don’t mind that when I’m sitting down, but while exercising it can mean you spend a stretch reading instead of getting swept up in a fight. Some conversations are easy to move through, and some make me want to put down the controller and get a snack.

Combat is less about quick reflexes than making choices and watching your party carry them out. I like that you can pause the action to decide what to do, because it feels less like a scramble and more like having a few seconds to get your head straight. There are still battles where a bad decision can make things go poorly, but you aren’t expected to aim a blaster with perfect timing while your hands are bouncing around.

The story has a strong pull once you’re moving through places like Taris and Dantooine, and getting access to the Force gives the adventure a different feel from the early stretch. I don’t know that every bit of wandering and conversation moves along at the same pace, but I’d rather have a game that lets me spend time with its characters than one that throws another fight at me every minute. There are definitely slower stretches. You can save those for a cool down or a day when your legs aren’t asking for anything complicated.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The main challenge here is not the combat, it’s reading while moving. A conversation can put you in front of a screen full of text, and keeping your eyes on it while jogging might be more work than the workout itself. I’d use a lower intensity and save the longer dialogue sections for when I’m walking or taking a break. Nobody needs to learn the fate of the Republic while trying to read at a sprint.

The slower combat should be manageable with a controller, especially because you can pause and think before choosing an action. I’d map the controls first and make sure moving between dialogue options feels comfortable, since fumbling through a conversation can be annoying even when you’re sitting still. If you’re on a bike, that may be easier because your upper body can stay more settled, but the same issue with reading is still there.

I’d skip trying to rush through every conversation just to keep your legs moving. The choices are a big part of the appeal, and I’d rather take a breather than accidentally tell a character something I didn’t mean because the controller moved a little. For a steady ride or walk, though, the game has a nice rhythm of talking, exploring and then getting into a fight. I think it can hold your attention without asking you to react instantly. Just don’t expect every minute to feel active.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very modest, so an older computer should be able to run it, and you don’t need a powerful graphics card for this one. I’d check that your current system can handle the game and that your preferred controller setup works, since hardware from the era it was made for is not exactly hard to find.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is a good choice for a relaxed walk or ride if you like story heavy RPGs and don’t mind slowing down to read. I’d skip it for a hard run where you want constant action, but the Old Republic is worth visiting when you have time to pay attention.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
