A mercenary firefight gives you a lot more to pay attention to than a quiet game of fishing, which is probably good for keeping a workout from dragging and not so good when you’re trying to keep your hands steady. I’d treat Hired Ops as a walking pace game first, because aiming at rival players while your whole body is bouncing sounds like a lot to ask of one thumb.

## Gameplay

The session based setup makes sense for an exercise game because you’re getting into a fight rather than waiting through a long story, and the premise is clear enough that I don’t need the game to stop and explain why these mercenary groups are shooting at each other. You can get into the action, and that’s what I want when I’m trying to keep moving. The plot can stay at work.

I think an online shooter has a useful advantage here, which is that the other players keep things from feeling too predictable, but that also means you’re not deciding the pace for yourself. If you’re having an unfocused minute, the people on the other team are still doing their thing, and the game is not going to politely wait while you remember which thumbstick aims. That’s a fairly active workout companion.

There’s also a difference between being busy and having a good time, and I think Hired Ops depends on whether you enjoy the particular kind of attention a shooter asks for. You’re watching for threats and trying to line up shots, so the game gives your brain a job. I’d rather have that than spend a run reading dialogue boxes. I can read those while sitting down.

The mercenary conflict gives it a practical setup for matches, but I don’t think the global scale part matters much once you’re focused on the person trying to shoot you. I don’t know how much variety the setting can give you by itself, and I wouldn’t buy the game just because the premise sounds bigger than one firefight. The fighting needs to carry it.

Since it’s free to play, giving it a try is easier to justify than paying full price for a game you might not want to play while exercising. Free still costs you time, though, and online shooters can be a hobby all by themselves if you let them. I’d go in expecting a focused action game, not something you can half ignore while your legs do all the work.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The part I’d be careful with is aiming. In a shooter, you can’t just point your character generally toward the action and hope it works out, and the movement from running or pedaling makes fine control harder. I’d start at a comfortable walking pace on the treadmill, or an easy pace on the bike, before deciding whether I wanted to go faster.

I also wouldn’t treat a match like a place to pause and catch up with your breathing. It’s an online game, so other players keep acting whether you’re ready or not, and the action is built around reacting to them. You might get a moment where nothing much is happening, then suddenly need to turn and aim, which is an awkward little transition when your hands are already busy.

Controller support isn’t something I’d assume either way based on the information here, so I’d test your controller before getting on a moving machine. If aiming feels comfortable, that’s a real step toward making this work, but a controller doesn’t make a fast firefight less demanding. I can tell you exactly how I’d like this to go, and then my thumb would probably have other plans.

A bike may be the easier place to try it because your upper body can stay more settled while you steer, but I wouldn’t call it an effortless ride. You still need to aim and react, and the minute you start leaning forward to concentrate, you’ve turned a casual spin into a meeting with your handlebars. Keep the resistance easy.

For the treadmill, I’d skip running at first. Aiming while jogging sounds like trying to write your name on a bus, and I don’t know that I’d enjoy losing fights because my reticle keeps wandering off the person I mean to shoot. Walking gives you a better chance of paying attention to the match without needing to stop every time something moves quickly.

This is probably a better fit for someone who already likes shooters and is willing to make the workout secondary for a while. If you’re looking for a game that waits for you, or one where loose inputs are part of the fun, I’d pick something else. Hired Ops asks you to care about what’s happening.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older gaming computer rather than a machine that needs a new graphics card, though I’d check the graphics card first because the game does require DirectX 11. It asks for a six year old class of processor and six gigabytes of memory, so a recent mid range PC should have room to handle it. The internet connection matters too, since this is an online shooter.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Hired Ops makes more sense as a focused shooter you happen to play while exercising than as a game built around exercising. I’d suggest it to someone who already likes online firefights and can keep their workout easy, but I’d skip it if you want loose, forgiving controls.
