Zombie survival sounds like a decent way to keep a treadmill run moving, but Infestation: Survivor Stories 2020 seems like the kind of game where a quiet stretch can turn into a fight before you’ve got your feet sorted out. I’d save it for an easy workout where you can pay attention, not the part of your run where you’re trying to hold a pace.

## Gameplay

The appeal here is the tension of trying to stay alive in a world full of zombies while other players are around too, and I think that can keep your attention in a way that a game with nothing but wandering wouldn’t. You’re looking for useful supplies and trying not to get caught out, so even moving through the world has a little bit of pressure attached to it. That’s fun, but it also means I wouldn’t expect this to be the relaxing kind of zombie game where you can stare at the TV and let your legs do all the work.

The slower stretches are part of the deal. You’re in a survival MMO, so I’d expect some time spent moving around and deciding where to go instead of constantly fighting, and that can make the minutes pass quickly when you’re into it. It could also leave you jogging along while your character jogs along, which is not the most exciting pairing. I don’t know, sometimes matching your real activity to the activity on screen is either motivating or a little too much like running errands.

Then a zombie or another player can make the trip more urgent, and that shift is where I think the game has a useful workout rhythm. You can settle into movement, but you still have to keep an eye out, and suddenly your attention is back on the screen. The other players make the world feel less predictable, which is good for keeping you engaged and bad for the part of your brain that wants to relax for a minute. There’s not really a quiet setting you can count on.

I’d probably enjoy the scavenging more than the shooting while exercising, because looking around for supplies gives you a reason to keep moving without asking you to land a perfect shot every few seconds. I’m not saying that’s the whole game, and I wouldn’t want to pretend the action is just decoration, but it’s the part I’d lean on during a workout. When aiming and reacting matter, the game starts asking for the kind of focus that can make you forget you’re exercising. It’s easy to do that.

There’s a particular kind of suspense in knowing another person might be nearby, and I like the idea of that more than I like having to handle it while bouncing on a treadmill. That’s not the game doing anything wrong. It’s just that my running form and my careful survival plan probably don’t improve together. I’d try it on an easy pace first and see if the constant alertness feels exciting or tiring.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The big question is whether you can keep your hands steady enough to deal with a game that involves looking around and aiming while the world keeps going. I can’t verify native controller support from the information here, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming a controller will work well. I’d check before starting, and if it turns out you need mouse and keyboard, I’d keep it off the treadmill. Tiny mouse movements and a moving belt are not a partnership I’m eager to test.

Even with a controller, I’d be careful about playing during a hard run. Movement through the world and scavenging sound manageable, but combat against zombies or other players could need quick aiming and fast decisions, and the other players aren’t going to wait while you get your balance back. I don’t know that I’d be able to enjoy that pressure at a pace where every thumb movement is wobbling around a little. I’d pick a relaxed pace, not intervals.

A stationary bike seems a little more forgiving here, since your upper body can stay still while your legs keep going, and that could make aiming less frustrating. I’d still start with a low effort setting and avoid turning the workout into a race, because a surprise fight is already enough to pay attention to. If you find yourself gripping the controller like it’s going to keep you upright, it’s probably time to slow down.

I’d skip anything that depends on reacting quickly until you know how the controls feel during exercise, and I’d stop moving for any part where you’re struggling to aim. That may sound like a strange way to exercise, but nobody gets bonus points for being distracted by a zombie and walking into the console. If you have confirmed controller support and you’re on a bike, give it a try at an easy effort. Otherwise, this one is better saved for sitting down.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum calls for an older dedicated graphics card, so you don’t need a recent high end gaming PC to get started. I’d check the graphics card first, since the GTX 460 or Radeon HD 5850 requirement is the part most likely to rule out a very basic computer. The rest is an older system by current standards.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This makes more sense as a focused survival game than as something to play while pushing hard on a treadmill. I’d suggest it to someone who likes tense zombie survival and has a bike or an easy pace, but I’d skip it if you need reliable controller support and loose, forgiving controls.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
