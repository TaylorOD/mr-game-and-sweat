I’d save Need for Speed Unbound for a workout where you want to pay attention, because threading through traffic while the cops are after you is not something your thumbs can do on autopilot. The bike sounds easier to manage than a run to me, but I’d still lower the difficulty before I tried to race at exercise pace.

## Gameplay

Unbound makes street racing feel busy in a way that mostly works for a workout. You’re picking races, trying to earn enough to keep improving your car, and watching the police become more interested in you as you spend time out on the streets. That gives the driving a reason to keep going beyond “there’s another road over there,” which I appreciate. I don’t need every game to give me a reason to care, but I’m more likely to keep pedaling when there’s a qualifier coming up.

The weekly qualifiers for The Grand give the story a clear destination, and I like having a goal that isn’t just endlessly upgrading cars until the garage looks expensive. There are characters and story scenes along the way, though, so this isn’t pure racing from menu to finish. I don’t know if I’d want to follow all the dialogue while running hard, but the scenes are easy enough to treat as a chance to slow down or take a break. A race is a better use of a moving belt than a conversation.

The driving itself asks you to make a lot of small decisions quickly. You’re steering around traffic, choosing when to use nitrous, and trying not to turn a corner into a very costly visit with a barrier. Drifting can feel good when you get it right, but it also asks for more control than I’d want to manage while bouncing around. I’d probably focus on clean, simple driving rather than trying to make every turn look stylish. My car doesn’t need to impress anybody.

Getting chased by the cops adds tension, and I think that’s a fun idea for a game but a questionable one for keeping a steady workout. A race ends and you may still have to deal with a pursuit, so the game can take a relaxed drive and suddenly make every intersection important. I don’t know that I’d want that surprise during a hard run, though I can see it being exactly the kind of extra pressure some people enjoy. The cops make the roads feel less like a warmup.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The bike is probably the more comfortable place to start, since the game asks for quick steering and braking but doesn’t need you to move your whole body around to play. A controller works well for driving, and I’d set the game to Casual before trying to manage races and exercise at once. You can still enjoy the speed without turning every mistake into a personal review of your thumb coordination.

On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace easy and use races where I can focus on the road instead of trying to run hard and hit perfect drifts. There’s a lot happening on screen, and traffic can appear right when you’re already steering through a turn, so a loose grip or a bouncing controller could make the driving messy. I’d skip chasing a perfect line. The car will survive a little ugly driving.

The open world gives you room to ride around and do things like speed traps, long jumps, and drift zones, which could be a nice change from lining up race after race. Still, the police can turn a casual drive into a chase, and that’s the part I’d be cautious about on a treadmill. I’d rather have the freedom to cruise than have to react quickly while my feet are doing their own thing. A treadmill is not where I want to discover that I’ve been staring at the screen too intensely.

Online racing is there for people who want it, but I’d stick to the single player side while exercising. Competing against other people would make me focus on the race even more, and there’s already plenty to react to in the regular game. If I were getting tired, I’d pause between events instead of convincing myself I could squeeze in one more chase. That plan never gets more sensible because a car is involved.

## Computer Requirements

This asks for a modest but real gaming computer, rather than something that relies on integrated graphics, and the listed graphics cards are older entry level gaming options. I’d check the graphics card first, then make sure there’s room for the 50 GB install, because deleting half your library is its own sort of workout.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Need for Speed Unbound is a fun choice for a steady bike workout or an easy treadmill session, especially if you enjoy racing and don’t mind staying alert. I’d lower the difficulty, keep the focus on single player, and skip it when you want a game that lets your brain wander.
