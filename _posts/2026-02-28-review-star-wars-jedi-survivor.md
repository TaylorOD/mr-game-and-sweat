Cal Kestis has a lot of conversations and dramatic pauses between the fights, so I’d play STAR WARS Jedi: Survivor on a walk or an easy bike ride rather than try to make every minute a hard run. The lightsaber fights are fun enough to hold your attention, but they ask for more precise timing than I want when my hands are bouncing around.

## Gameplay

The combat is the part that keeps pulling me back in, because you’re reading an enemy, choosing when to block or dodge, and trying to land your own hits without just mashing buttons until something works. That’s a nice change from games where you can half pay attention and still get through everything. It also means I’d probably lower the difficulty if I were exercising, since a fight that needs several clean parries is a pretty silly time to discover my coordination has left the building.

There’s a lot of exploring between fights, and I like that the environments give you places to climb, jump, and poke around rather than putting every interesting thing directly in front of Cal. But some of those sections slow down into platforming or puzzles, and I can see myself getting off the treadmill mentally while trying to figure out where a wall run is supposed to go. I don’t know that every stretch needs to be busy, but this game sometimes has me wandering around looking for the next door, which is not the most exciting part of a workout.

The story is a big piece of the experience, and I think that works if you’re here to follow Cal and the people around him, but long conversations and cut scenes are a real change of pace from the action. You can’t expect it to keep feeding you fights every few minutes. I’d treat those slower parts as an easy pace section, or just stop moving if you don’t want to miss the story while watching your feet. That’s a reasonable trade.

The lightsaber and Force abilities give the fights enough variety that I wouldn’t want to skip them, and coming across stormtroopers or droids is more fun than dealing with another stretch of scenery. I do think some enemies demand a lot more attention than others, so a relaxed workout can turn into me trying to time a dodge and forgetting that I’m meant to be moving my legs. That’s probably the most exciting distraction you could ask for, unless you count missing a turn because a robot swung at me.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A bike seems like the more comfortable place to handle the busy fights, since your upper body can stay a little steadier while you aim and use the controls. On a treadmill, I’d stick to walking or an easy jog, keep the controller close, and lower the difficulty before I start. Full controller support means you’re not fighting the setup, which is great, but it doesn’t make a precise parry easier when you’re moving.

The game has stretches where you can look around and explore at your own speed, and those should pair well with a gentle pace. Then an enemy rushes in, Cal has to dodge, and suddenly I’m asking my thumbs to do a small job with the steadiness of a shopping cart with one bad wheel. I’d slow down for combat instead of trying to keep a hard pace through every encounter.

I’d also give myself permission to pause when a puzzle starts taking too much attention. There’s no online match waiting on you, so you don’t have to pick between solving a room and letting strangers down, which is nice. I don’t know if I’d want to puzzle through a tricky platforming section while running, though, because missing a jump and missing my stride at the same time sounds like more coordination than I brought.

For a longer workout, I’d probably play in shorter stretches and let the story scenes be a breather rather than treating every minute as exercise time. You could also do the exploring at a steady pace and take the more demanding fights slower. It’s not a game I’d choose for interval training, but it can work well when the goal is to keep moving while paying attention to something else.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You’ll need a dedicated graphics card around the GTX 1070 or Radeon RX 580 level, so this doesn’t ask for a brand new high end computer, but integrated graphics won’t be enough. I’d check available storage first too, because 155 GB is a lot of room for one game, and internet is required for its patching.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is a fun choice for a walk or an easy ride if you want a story between your fights and don’t mind slowing down when the timing gets demanding. I’d skip it for a workout built around running hard or keeping a steady rhythm the whole time.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
