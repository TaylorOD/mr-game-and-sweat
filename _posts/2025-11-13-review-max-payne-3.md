Max Payne 3’s frequent shootouts can make a treadmill or bike session fly by, but its long cinematic stretches interrupt the action. Its precise, fast-paced gunplay is also a poor match for exercising while playing.

# Gameplay

The third-person shooting is engaging, with cover-based fights and slow-motion shootouts that give its action a distinctive style. But the game is heavily cinematic: extended cutscenes and story sequences regularly break up the combat, so you won’t be moving through nonstop action. The shootouts can hold your attention, while the quieter stretches are less suited to an exercise session.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

# Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Steam lists no controller support, and the action calls for quick reactions and accurate aiming—difficult demands when your movement is less steady on a treadmill or bike. Slow-motion can give you a little extra time to react, but it doesn’t make the aiming forgiving enough to turn this into a comfortable workout game.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

# Computer Requirements

The listed minimum requirements include Windows 10 64-bit, 2GB of RAM, a 35GB hard drive, and a dedicated graphics card such as an NVIDIA 8600 GT with 512MB of VRAM or a Radeon HD 3400 with 512MB of VRAM. Those are older, basic dedicated GPUs, rather than integrated graphics, so the game shouldn’t require a modern high-end PC at the minimum settings.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

# Conclusion and Score

Max Payne 3 offers gripping action and a memorable crime story, but its long cutscenes and demanding shootouts make it a tough fit for exercising while playing. It’s better suited to a focused session at your desk than to a workout on a treadmill or bike.

### Overall Score: 1.67 out of 3
