Chasing enemies across little islands sounds like a decent way to forget you’re exercising, and Crab Champions moves quickly enough that I’d probably stay interested. I’d use a bike before a fast treadmill, though, because aiming while your hands are bouncing around sounds like a small problem that gets bigger quickly.

## Gameplay

The runs have a nice, simple rhythm: land on an island, fight through enemies, grab what you can, and head toward the next fight. I like that the game keeps giving you something to do instead of stopping every few minutes to explain a new chapter, and a short stretch of clearing enemies seems like a reasonable thing to fit around a workout. Then you open a chest and start thinking about upgrades instead of your legs. That’s probably the idea.

Weapons and upgrades give each run room to turn into something different, which is where I think the roguelike setup earns its place. You can build toward a weapon that feels good to use, or collect upgrades that change how your damage works, and the decisions are small enough that you don’t need to sit down and study a guide. I’d be curious how often I’d take a sensible upgrade over one that just sounds fun, because those are not always the same choice.

The crab movement is more than just a way to get from one enemy to the next, and that gives the fights some energy. You can move quickly around the arena, so there’s a nice feeling of being in control when you’re cutting across an island and getting out of the way of incoming attacks. I don’t know if I’d want every run to be a blur of dashing and firing, but that’s a better problem for an action game than having long stretches where nothing happens.

Boss fights sound like the point where a relaxed little shootout starts asking more of you. I like having those bigger moments at the end of a run, because they give the island hopping a reason to build toward something, but I’d expect to pay more attention to enemy attacks and my position when one shows up. If I’m already tired, I might be less thrilled about learning a boss pattern while trying not to drift toward the edge of the treadmill.

There’s also the usual roguelike deal where a run can go well because your upgrades fit together, or feel a little awkward because they don’t. That uncertainty makes it more fun than repeating the exact same route, though I wouldn’t want to spend my whole workout comparing upgrade choices. For the most part, I think the game gives you enough action to keep the pauses short. You get back to shooting.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d start on a bike, where a sudden enemy rushing in doesn’t also make my whole body bounce while I’m trying to aim. The game has full controller support, which is a big help for this kind of setup, and moving around with one stick while aiming with the other should feel familiar. Still, this isn’t one of those games where you can point in roughly the right direction and expect things to sort themselves out.

A treadmill could work if you’re walking or keeping the pace easy, but I think faster running would make the shooting part frustrating. The camera, movement, and aiming all ask for your hands at the same time, and enemies aren’t politely waiting for you to finish a thought. I’d lower the workout intensity before I tried to play at a hard pace. No need to turn a crab into a coordination exercise.

The less demanding stretches between fights should be easier to handle, and you can probably use them to settle your grip or take a drink. I’d try to avoid making big changes to the game while moving, since picking upgrades asks for more attention than running around and firing. I don’t know, maybe you’re great at reading choices while your feet are moving, but I would not bet a whole workout on that.

The quick movement is fun, but it also means there are moments when you’ll want to react fast and keep track of where enemies are coming from. That’s why I’d call this workable rather than automatic treadmill entertainment. On a bike, I’d be more comfortable pushing the pace a little, and on a treadmill I’d keep the speed gentle enough that missing a shot doesn’t turn into a whole event.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very modest, including Intel HD 4000 integrated graphics, so you don’t need a recent gaming computer just to give it a try. I’d look first at whether your computer meets that graphics baseline, and then check that it has the room for the game. Six gigabytes is not much, which is nice for once.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Crab Champions sounds like a fun workout companion if you want a lot of action and don’t mind aiming while you exercise. I’d suggest it for bike sessions or easy treadmill walks, but not for a hard run where every quick turn starts competing with your balance.
