Sailing in ATLAS sounds better for a steady treadmill pace than a hard bike session, because some stretches should give you room to settle in, but the shared world can ask for your attention at inconvenient times. I’d start with the goal of getting a ship moving, not with the goal of becoming a pirate legend before dinner.

## Gameplay

The idea of building a ship, bringing a crew together, and heading out across a huge world is genuinely appealing, and it gives you a reason to keep going beyond just watching the scenery pass by. I think the scale is exciting in theory, but a world with thousands of other players also sounds like a lot to learn and a lot of time spent getting yourself organized before you’re doing the fun part. That’s a fairly large warm up.

Building your own ship is the hook I’d be most interested in, because it gives the sailing some purpose and makes the trip feel like it belongs to you. The less appealing side is that building and preparing a ship sounds like work you want to do with your attention on the game, not while your legs are already handling another job. I don’t know how quickly you can get from the preparation to the open water, and that matters to me more than the size of the map.

Once you’re sailing, I can see this being a nice game to play at an easy pace, especially if you’re happy to spend time traveling and planning rather than getting constant bursts of action. The action and adventure parts are there, but the massively multiplayer setup means your plans are happening alongside other people, and that can make a quiet trip feel less quiet. I’d enjoy the idea of heading out somewhere with a crew. I’d also expect a handful of stretches where I’m mostly waiting to arrive.

The Early Access label makes me cautious, not because an unfinished game can’t be fun, but because this kind of game needs a lot of pieces to work together before the big pirate fantasy feels worth the time. I’d want to know that the basic rhythm of gathering, building, and sailing feels good before I committed to the larger world. You can look up the feature list, but that won’t tell you if you like spending your evening getting ready to have an evening.

For me, ATLAS sounds like a good fit if you enjoy making your own goals and don’t mind some slow setup between the exciting parts. If you want a game that keeps handing you a new thing to do every minute, I’d probably look elsewhere. I don’t know if the journey across that much world would hold my attention every time, but I understand why someone would want to claim a little piece of it.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is a real plus, and the sailing sounds like the sort of activity that could work while you’re moving at an easy, consistent pace. I’d use the controller and keep the workout comfortable enough that I can still pay attention when the game asks me to make a decision. You’re not going to get much from a huge pirate world if you’re staring at the floor the whole time.

The ship building and preparation sound less suited to a run, since that’s where I’d expect to need more focus and more deliberate inputs. I’d do those parts before getting on the treadmill, or save them for a bike ride where I’m not bouncing around as much. There’s a practical limit to how carefully I want to place things while my hands are moving. Mine is not especially high.

The shared world is also hard to treat like a relaxing background game, because other players are part of the experience and you may need to respond to what’s happening around you. If the action gets busy, I’d rather slow down or stop than try to make quick decisions while my footing and my thumbs are both doing their own thing. I don’t know how often that kind of moment comes up, but it’s enough that I wouldn’t pick this for a workout where I want to be completely checked out.

I’d give the open water a try on a treadmill walk or an easy ride, and I’d skip the more involved ship work until I was sitting still. That makes ATLAS workable, but it doesn’t sound like a game that will always meet you at the pace you want. Sometimes you’re exercising and sometimes you’re trying to run a pirate operation. That’s a lot of jobs.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum calls for a dedicated graphics card around the level of an older GTX 770, so I wouldn’t expect integrated graphics to be enough. I’d check your graphics card and whether you have room for the 120 GB install before anything else, because that is a meaningful amount of space for one game.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

ATLAS sounds worth a look if you like slow travel, ship building, and making your own plans in a big multiplayer world. For exercise, I’d save it for an easy session and keep the busy parts for when you can give them your full attention.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
