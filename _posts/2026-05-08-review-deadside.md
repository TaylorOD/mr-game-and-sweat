I’d keep Deadside for a stationary bike or a slow walk, not a run where your hands are bouncing around while somebody else is trying to shoot you. The safe zones sound like the part to start with, but the game’s whole mix of player fights and survival makes it hard to stay casual for long.

## Gameplay

Deadside has a setup I understand the appeal of: you head into an open world, try to secure loot, and decide how much trouble you’re willing to invite by getting into fights with other players or enemies. That gives you a reason to pay attention beyond watching a map fill up, and I think that can make the workout go by faster. It can also turn a relaxed ride into a long stretch of carefully checking where you’re going. Not exactly a scenic bike path.

The safe zones give you somewhere to focus on securing loot without immediately taking that same risk out into the wild, and I like that the game offers that kind of lower pressure activity alongside hardcore PVP and PVE. You could use those stretches to settle into your pace before taking on anything more demanding. I don’t know how much time you’ll want to spend there once you’re ready for action, but it gives you a sensible place to start.

Building a fortress sounds appealing if you like having something to work toward besides surviving the next gunfight. I’d probably enjoy having that longer term goal, because it gives the loot a purpose beyond collecting things until the next time I get shot. But there’s a practical tradeoff here, since gathering what you need and thinking through what to do next can pull your attention away from exercising. I’d keep the pace easy when you’re making those decisions.

The action is where the game could be fun and also where I’d be least comfortable exercising hard. Gunfights ask you to aim and react, and the more you care about keeping your gear, the more likely you are to stop thinking about your legs. I don’t know that I’d want a game where a moment of sloppy aim could cost me the things I’ve been working to secure while I’m trying to keep a steady run going. I’d play it when I’m ready to pay attention.

Overall, I think there’s enough to do here to keep the game from feeling empty, and the mix of PVP and PVE gives you a reason to treat the open world cautiously. That’s also why I wouldn’t call it easy background gaming. The slow stretches might be fine on an easy ride, but the action can make the workout secondary pretty quickly.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, the issue isn’t just whether your hands can reach the controls. Aiming during a fight while your body is moving sounds like the sort of thing that would make me drift toward the edge of the belt, and that’s not a clever way to discover I need to pay attention. I’d save running for a game that doesn’t ask for much precision.

A stationary bike seems more workable because you can keep your upper body steadier while you look around and aim. I’d still keep the effort moderate, especially when heading away from the safe zones, because getting surprised in a PVP fight is exactly when you’re likely to make quick corrections with the controls. Those corrections may be fine at a desk. On a moving treadmill, they sound less fun.

I can’t confirm full controller support, so I wouldn’t buy it on the assumption that you can pick up a controller and play comfortably. Check that before you set up your workout, and if you do use a controller, give it a try while stationary first. I don’t know how the aiming would feel for you, and finding out halfway through a tense fight seems like a poor time to learn.

I’d skip the more demanding sessions while exercising, and I wouldn’t count on being able to pause when a fight gets serious in an online game. You can make the workout easier and choose when to go looking for danger, but the other players aren’t going to match your pace. This is probably best for someone who likes a slow ride with occasional bursts of attention, not someone trying to keep a fast treadmill session going.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older or modest gaming PC, though you’ll still want a dedicated graphics card rather than assuming any computer will handle a large open world shooter. I’d look at the graphics card first, and remember you’ll also need a broadband connection and room for the game. The store page’s toaster warning is fair enough.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Deadside makes more sense for a cautious player on a stationary bike than for someone trying to run hard through every gunfight. I’d give it a look if the survival and PVP mix is what you want, but I’d verify controller support first and keep the treadmill session easy.
