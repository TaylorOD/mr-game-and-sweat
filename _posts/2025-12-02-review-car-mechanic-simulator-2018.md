A repair job that asks you to find a worn part is probably a better treadmill companion than a race where one missed turn sends you into a wall, and Car Mechanic Simulator 2018 is mostly patient work with cars. I’d keep the pace easy, because I can imagine getting more interested in the parts than in my feet.

## Gameplay

The satisfying thing here is figuring out what’s wrong with a car and then getting it back into working shape, and I think that gives you a nice little reason to keep paying attention without demanding you react every second. You take repair jobs, inspect cars, replace parts and get paid, which is the kind of work that sounds simple until you’re looking through a car thinking, okay, what did I forget to put back. There’s a lot of detail, and sometimes that detail is the fun.

It’s also a game where progress can mean spending a while looking at menus and parts instead of moving around. You’re not racing through a track just because the genre list says racing, and I don’t know if the occasional car work will hold your attention if you want constant action while you exercise. I’d probably enjoy the slower rhythm, but it’s not going to keep your heart rate up by itself.

There’s a small pleasure in turning a job into a little project, especially when you get to work out what needs replacing and then see the car come together. The business side gives you a reason to take jobs and build up your repair service, though I suspect I’d be more interested in the actual car than in growing an empire. I have never been especially good at pretending a spreadsheet is exciting, and this is at least a spreadsheet with wheels.

The Barn Find and Junkyard modules sound like a good change of pace from customer repairs, since they let you look for classic or unusual cars rather than only fixing whatever came through the door. I like the idea of taking a car that needs work and making it into something you want to keep, although I’d expect those sections to pull you into more deliberate browsing and decision making. The Car Editor is there too, which is nice if building your own car is the part you care about. I probably wouldn’t spend much time in it, but I can see why somebody would.

Overall, there’s enough of a steady task to make this appealing, but you should expect stretches of careful clicking, inspecting and sorting out what to do next. I’d play it when I wanted a quieter workout, not when I needed the game to keep me moving.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

This seems like a game that will wait while you catch your breath or check what you’re doing, which is a big advantage over anything that expects you to make a fast turn at the exact wrong moment. Controller support is available, and I’d use a controller rather than reach for a mouse while moving. Even then, there’s enough menu navigation and small, deliberate work that I’d expect to slow down now and then.

A bike is probably the easier fit if you’re planning to take on more involved repair work, since you can keep your hands steadier while you look through parts and options. On a treadmill, I’d stick to easier walking and avoid trying to do careful work at a pace where the controller is bouncing around. It’s not because the game is punishing, it’s because I would absolutely select the wrong thing and then stare at it like the car had done that on purpose.

I’d skip the fiddly jobs when you’re tired, or pause the game to finish them if that’s an option you’re comfortable using during a workout. The game’s slower pace is helpful, but the attention it asks for can still pull you away from exercising if you get absorbed in a repair. That may be exactly what you want. I don’t know that I’d want every workout to turn into a parts inventory, but for a relaxed session it sounds pretty workable.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum asks for an older dedicated graphics card, so this doesn’t look like a game that needs a new high end machine just to get started. I’d check the graphics card first, since the listed minimum is more demanding than integrated graphics.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Car Mechanic Simulator 2018 makes sense for someone who enjoys patient, hands on tinkering and wants a game that won’t punish every little interruption. I’d suggest it for an easy bike ride or a gentle treadmill walk, but skip it if you need fast action to keep you engaged.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
