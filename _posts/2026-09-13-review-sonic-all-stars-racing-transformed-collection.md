The boat and plane sections make this a more interesting workout than another race where you follow the same strip of road for three laps. I’d pick a solo event, keep the effort comfortable, and let the vehicle do its little midrace identity crisis.

## Gameplay

I think this works because the changing vehicle gives your attention something new to follow without turning the race into a long explanation of how to play. You’re steering, trying to keep up, and adjusting as the course shifts from land to water to air, so there’s a nice steady stream of things to notice. It’s busy in a useful way.

The transformations are the part I’d be most excited about, since the same race can ask you to handle the vehicle differently as the route changes. That sounds more fun than just memorizing where every corner is, and it gives your brain a small job while your legs are doing theirs. I’d probably be looking ahead for the next change instead of staring at the workout timer.

There are weapons and other racers to keep track of, so you’re not just driving a clean line around the course. I like that in a short race, because it gives you a reason to stay engaged even when you’re not in first place, but I can also see a busy stretch making me forget I was supposed to be exercising. The game does not need a story break to remind you of that.

World Tour and Grand Prix give you solo ways to work through races, which is what I’d choose for a workout. I don’t know how much time you’ll want to spend moving through event menus between races, but at least the basic appeal is immediate: pick an event and get moving. I’d skip anything that makes you sit and plan for a while when you’re already warmed up.

This looks like a game where a mistake costs you a position, not the whole afternoon, and that’s a good fit for casual exercise. I wouldn’t expect every race to go perfectly, especially once the track changes and other racers start making things messy, but getting bumped around is part of the deal. It’s a racing game, so sometimes the traffic has opinions.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The main thing I’d plan around is the steering, because racing asks you to make small corrections at the same time you’re moving your body. Full controller support is a big plus, and the basic controls are familiar, but sharp turns and quick changes of direction could get a little clumsy if your hands are bouncing with your steps. I’d start at an easy pace and see how much control you have before trying to push harder.

On a treadmill, I’d keep the speed low enough that I can look at the screen and steer without turning every corner into a negotiation with my feet. The vehicle transformations should help keep my attention, but they don’t make steering any less important. I’d rather finish a race calmly than win it while leaning over the console like I’m trying to read a street sign.

A stationary bike sounds a little easier for this one, since your upper body can stay more settled while you steer. Still, the quick turns and other racers mean I’d avoid trying to play at an effort where I’m already struggling to concentrate. You can probably get away with a few rough inputs, but I wouldn’t count on the game waiting patiently while you recover your thumbs.

I’d choose a solo event and lower the difficulty if the available options let you do that, then leave online competition for when you’re not trying to manage a workout at the same time. I don’t know whether every player will find the controls comfortable while moving, but full controller support and short races make this a reasonable one to try. If your steering gets sloppy, slow the workout down before blaming the plane.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are from a much older generation of computers, so this should be comfortable for a modest machine with basic graphics rather than something built for new games. I’d check that your current operating system can run it, since the store page’s compatibility note makes the old Windows support information less reassuring than the low requirements suggest.

## Conclusion and Score

A lively choice for someone who wants arcade racing to keep a workout from feeling repetitive, especially if you’re happy with solo races and a steady pace. Skip it if precise steering while moving sounds like more work than fun.
