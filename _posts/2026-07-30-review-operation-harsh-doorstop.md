A tactical shooter is a pretty awkward treadmill companion, because your legs can keep moving while your eyes are trying to spot somebody across a map. I’d try Operation: Harsh Doorstop on an easy walk or a stationary bike first, not start out jogging and hope my aim sorts itself out.

## Gameplay

The free sandbox idea is appealing, and also a little hard to judge because a big pile of community made maps, weapons, and vehicles can mean there’s always something different to try or that you spend the evening deciding what to try. I like that it has singleplayer, co-op, and multiplayer, since you can choose how much you want other people depending on whether you’re exercising or trying to pay close attention. A menu full of options can still turn into its own activity. Not the one you planned.

I think the tactical pace is what will decide whether this works for you. A shooter that asks you to watch sight lines and choose when to move gives your mind something to do, which is useful when a workout starts feeling like the same thirty seconds over and over, but careful aiming and checking your surroundings will compete with keeping your balance. I’d try it at a pace where I can still look at the screen without feeling like the treadmill is making an argument.

Singleplayer and co-op sound like the sensible places to begin, because you can take a little more time and get used to a map without feeling like every small delay is holding somebody else up. Multiplayer may be fun if you want the pressure, but I’d save it for when you’re sitting down or moving very gently, since a tactical shooter can turn one distracted glance into a bad time. I don’t know how much the different modes vary in pace, so I wouldn’t promise every session will feel equally busy.

The Workshop support is a big reason to be curious. Thousands of community created maps, weapons, and vehicles is a lot to explore, and I like the idea of being able to change things up when a familiar setup stops holding my attention. I’d still pick one map and mode before starting the workout instead of browsing around while the belt moves. I can make a very ordinary decision take a while.

Early Access is part of the deal, so I’d go in expecting an unfinished sandbox rather than a polished, carefully paced campaign. The developers came from the cancelled Project Reality 2 project, which is an interesting bit of background, but it doesn’t tell me how smooth or consistent the current experience will feel. The free price makes it easier to give it a try, as long as you’re comfortable with a game that’s still in development.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The safest version of this workout is probably a stationary bike or a slow treadmill walk, because you’re still going to be aiming and making quick decisions rather than just steering through a relaxed world. I wouldn’t choose a pace that makes your hands bounce around, especially if you’re playing a mode where other people are relying on you. I’ve got no desire to make a stranger’s match harder because I’m trying to get my steps in.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information here, so I’d test your controller while sitting down before building a workout around it. If it works comfortably, I’d use it for singleplayer or co-op and start with the easiest pace you can keep without losing control of the camera. If you end up needing a mouse and keyboard, I’d keep this one for sitting down, because reaching around while moving sounds like the sort of clever plan that lasts about four minutes.

A bike gives you a little more room to concentrate on the screen, but it doesn’t remove the need to aim or react, so I’d still start gently and avoid treating a multiplayer match like a place to test your limits. I don’t know whether every community map will feel equally readable or busy, and that uncertainty makes me less interested in promising this as a reliable workout game. Try one short session in a forgiving mode first.

The good fit is someone who already likes tactical shooters and wants a free game to experiment with while moving at an easy pace. If you mostly want something you can play on autopilot, or you’re planning to jog hard while trying to land precise shots, I’d skip it for that workout. There are easier games to keep going when your attention wanders.

## Computer Requirements

This asks for a dedicated graphics card in the minimum requirements, so I wouldn’t count on a basic laptop with integrated graphics to handle it well. I’d check your graphics card first, then make sure you have the storage space, since forty gigabytes is a decent amount for a free game.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Operation: Harsh Doorstop is worth a try if you want a free tactical shooter and can take the workout slowly, especially in singleplayer or co-op. For a fast treadmill session or a game you can play without much focus, I’d pick something else.
