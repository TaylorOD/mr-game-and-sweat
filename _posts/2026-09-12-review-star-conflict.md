Space battles sound like a pretty good excuse to keep moving, but Star Conflict asks you to aim and steer while other people are trying to do the same thing to you. I’d start with PvE missions, not the busiest player battles, and see how much attention the flying takes.

## Gameplay

I think the appeal is pretty clear: you’re piloting a combat starship, fighting in big battles, and building a fleet as you go. That gives you a reason to keep playing beyond one fight, which is nice, but I’d expect the fleet building side to make the game feel more like a hobby than a quick workout game. There’s always another ship to think about.

The action itself sounds like the part that would keep me engaged, because you’re steering around in space and trying to line up attacks instead of watching a character run down a corridor. That’s a lot more active than a game where the interesting thing happens in a cut scene. I’d probably have to pay attention.

PvE missions seem like the sensible place to get comfortable with the flying and fighting before taking on other players. I like having that option, because a match with people who know exactly what they’re doing can turn into a long lesson in how little I understand about space combat. PvP could be fun once you know what you’re doing, but I wouldn’t make that the first thing you try while exercising.

Open Space gives you a different kind of activity from jumping straight into a battle, and I think that’s useful if you want a less intense session. I don’t know how much time you’ll spend just moving around versus fighting, so I wouldn’t count on every minute being equally engaging. A little quiet time is fine. A lot of it would have me checking whether my treadmill display had changed.

The free to play setup makes it easy to give it a try, and the fleet progression could keep you interested if you enjoy gradually building out your options. I’d still go in expecting menus and decisions between fights, because an online game with ships to collect isn’t going to be all action all the time. That’s not automatically a problem, but it’s not the same as a game you can pick up and understand in ten seconds.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Your biggest concern here is aiming while your body is moving. Space gives you room to steer, but you still need to face the right direction and line up attacks, and I think even a little bounce could make that feel fussier than it sounds sitting still. It’s not a game where I’d expect to get away with pressing one button now and then.

I can’t confirm controller support, so I’d check that before setting up a workout around it. If you can play comfortably with a controller, I’d try a low pressure PvE mission at a gentle pace first, with the camera and controls set up so you aren’t making big, fast corrections every few seconds. If it feels like you’re chasing the ship with the stick, that’s probably not going to improve once you’re jogging.

PvP is where I’d be cautious. You can’t assume the other players will wait while you get your footing, and I’d rather not be trying to keep up with a live fight and a treadmill at the same time. A bike might make the aiming easier to manage than running, but I’d still start with PvE and save the competitive battles for when you’re sitting down.

The fleet and menu parts could be a decent fit for a slower ride, since you can take your time deciding what you want to do next. I’d skip trying to manage everything at a run, though. I can picture myself moving through a menu with the focus of someone picking a cereal while the grocery store is closing.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are friendly to older computers, including older integrated graphics, so you don’t need a recent gaming machine just to try it. I’d check that your PC meets the Windows and memory requirements and has a stable broadband connection, since this is an online game.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Star Conflict makes the most sense for you if you want space combat and fleet building, and you’re willing to test the controls before exercising with it. I’d give the PvE missions a try on a bike or at an easy treadmill pace, but I wouldn’t pick it for a fast run or a competitive session while moving.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
