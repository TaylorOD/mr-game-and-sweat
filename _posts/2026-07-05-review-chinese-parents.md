Studying for the Gaokao while you walk sounds like a better fit than trying to play something that expects you to react before your feet land, but Chinese Parents has enough reading and decision making that I’d save it for an easy pace. It seems like the sort of game where your legs can keep moving while your brain takes a minute.

## Gameplay

I like the idea of following one ordinary kid from birth through the end of high school, because that makes the pressure around the Gaokao feel like part of a whole life instead of a single big exam scene. You’re balancing studying, having fun, and making friends, which is a more interesting set of priorities than “study all the time” would be. It also sounds like the game is asking you to decide what kind of childhood you’re willing to give this kid, and I think that’s a good thing to sit with.

The catch, for me, is that this kind of sim asks you to stop and consider what you’re doing, and that’s not always what I want while I’m exercising. You might be picking between another round of studying and time to have fun, then thinking about how that choice fits into the longer stretch toward the Gaokao. I can see myself getting invested in those little decisions, but I’d also want to pause the belt sometimes and actually read instead of pretending I absorbed a paragraph while my eyes were bouncing around. That’s a very normal thing for me to do.

I don’t know whether every part of the journey gives you equally interesting decisions, and I wouldn’t expect the story to move at the speed of an action game. The childhood to high school setup sounds like it has room for quiet stretches, and the point seems to be watching the consequences build rather than constantly throwing a new crisis at you. That can be nice when you want a calmer game, but it’s not going to keep your attention by making you dodge something every few seconds. There’s time to think.

The future parenting question is the detail that makes the premise stick with me. After you’ve gone through the kid’s life, you’re asked to consider whether you’d become a Tiger Parent yourself, which is a pretty direct way to make the whole experience look back at your choices. I like that the game doesn’t stop at the exam and treat it as the only thing worth caring about. Still, I’d go in wanting a thoughtful life sim, not a game that’s always busy. Those are different things.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A slow, choice driven game is easier to fit around exercise than something that needs fast aim or precise timing, and I don’t expect Chinese Parents to punish you for taking a moment to look at the screen. The trouble is that looking closely and reading are part of the appeal, so I’d keep the pace comfortable enough that I can follow what’s going on without leaning forward like I’m trying to read a tiny restaurant menu from across the room.

I’d play in shorter stretches of attention, making a decision and then settling back into the walk or ride while the game moves along. If you’re jogging hard, the reading could get old quickly, and I’d probably be happier biking gently or walking rather than trying to keep up with a workout that demands more from my eyes. There’s no need for twitch reactions here, which is a plus. You still need to pay attention.

Controller support isn’t something I can confirm from the information here, so I’d check that before buying if a controller is important to your setup. If you’re playing at a desk beside your treadmill or bike, mouse and keyboard may be an option, but reaching over while moving is the kind of plan that sounds fine until I try to do it. I’d get the controls sorted before starting, and I’d skip this for a workout where I want to look away from the screen most of the time.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very modest, so an older basic computer should be able to run it, and you don’t need a recent graphics card for this one. I’d check that the screen is comfortable to read from where you exercise, because the game asks more of your attention than your computer.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Chinese Parents sounds like a thoughtful, slower game for an easy walk or bike ride, especially if you enjoy following a character’s life and weighing school against everything else. I’d skip it for hard workouts or if you want a game that keeps your eyes moving all the time.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
