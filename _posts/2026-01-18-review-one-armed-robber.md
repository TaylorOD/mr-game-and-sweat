A one armed robbery sounds like a pretty funny way to spend an easy workout, but I think the game’s chaotic first person heists are going to ask more of your hands than your legs will enjoy. I’d try it sitting down before bringing it onto a moving belt.

## Gameplay

The one arm premise gives this heist game a reason to be awkward, which I like more than a game that’s chaotic just because it can be. You’re trying to pull off robberies in banks, shops, and other places, and I think that makes the whole thing sound fun in a way that’s easy to understand. You have a job, you have a strange limitation, and something is probably going to get messy. That’s a decent setup.

I’m less sure about how well that premise holds up over time. A single arm is funny as an idea, but if the challenge mostly means doing ordinary heist tasks with less control, I can see that turning from amusing to fussy pretty quickly. I don’t know how much variety the different establishments bring, so I wouldn’t buy it expecting a deep strategy game just because Strategy is one of its genres. I’d expect the moment to moment trouble to be the reason to play.

The first person view and the action focus should keep you involved, and I think that’s a real advantage for exercise. You’re not sitting through a long story just to get back to doing something, at least based on the game’s premise and description. But heists ask you to pay attention to what you’re doing, and the game’s own word for it is chaotic, so I’d expect the fun to come with a little bit of mental traffic. My brain sometimes has room for one problem at a time. That’s enough.

I’d give it a try if you like games where things can go wrong in a silly way, and I wouldn’t expect every robbery to feel clever or polished. The one armed hook is specific enough that it could make simple actions entertaining, but it also has to carry a lot of weight. If it’s funny to you when a game makes basic robbery business difficult, that sounds promising. If you want a careful planning game where every move feels smooth, maybe not the best.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A moving treadmill is where I’d be cautious, because first person action usually means you need to aim and make quick adjustments, and the whole point here is that the robber has one arm. I can imagine trying to keep your balance while also steering a character through a chaotic heist, and that sounds like a lot of small corrections at once. I’d probably spend more time thinking about my hands than my pace.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information here, so I wouldn’t assume the game is ready for a controller just because that would be useful. Check that before setting it up for exercise. Even if you can play with a controller, I’d start on a stationary bike or at a very easy treadmill pace, because being able to make a quick aim adjustment matters more here than it would in a slower game. I don’t know whether the game gives you time to stop and think during a robbery, so I’d rather test it before trusting my feet to keep up.

The bike seems like the less awkward place to begin, mostly because you can keep your upper body steadier while you figure out how the game feels. That’s not me saying the game is impossible on a treadmill, just that a first person heist is asking for more precision than I’d want during a run. I’d skip anything that starts making me rush to keep up, and I’d treat a calm pace as the point rather than trying to match the game’s chaos. The belt can wait.

If the controls work for you and the action feels manageable, I can see this being a fun short workout game, especially for somebody who enjoys a bit of disorder. But I’d want the controller question answered first. Otherwise you might be balancing, reaching for a mouse, and attempting to rob a bank at the same time, which is a lot of jobs for one person.

The minimum requirements point to an older computer with a dedicated graphics card, rather than a machine that’s only meant for basic everyday use. I’d look at the graphics card first, since the listed minimum is a GTX 760, and the game doesn’t need much storage.

## Conclusion and Score

One-armed robber sounds like a reasonable free game to try if you enjoy chaotic first person action and don’t mind a heist that may be more awkward than strategic. I’d start off the treadmill and verify the controls before making it part of a workout.
