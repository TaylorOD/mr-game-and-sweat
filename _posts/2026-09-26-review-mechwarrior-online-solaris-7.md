A MechWarrior fight looks like a great way to forget you’re running, right up until you need to aim carefully while your feet are making the floor move. I’d save Solaris 7 for when you’re sitting down, though I can see why you’d want a giant robot to make the miles go by.

## Gameplay

The mech combat has a nice weight to it, and I like that winning isn’t only about turning fast and pointing at somebody. You’re managing heat while choosing when to fire, and aiming at specific parts of an enemy mech gives each fight a little more to think about than just emptying every weapon at once. That’s cool, but it also means I’d want my attention on the screen and not on whether I’m drifting toward the edge of the treadmill.

Solaris 7 makes that attention feel especially important because the arena fights put you up against other players, not a set of enemies who politely wait while you sort yourself out. I think that makes a win feel earned, but it also gives the combat a sharper edge than I’d want on an easy workout. You can’t really have half your brain in the match and expect the other half to keep the mech pointed where it needs to go. I’d lose track of my legs first.

There’s also a lot to learn before the fighting feels natural, and building a mech asks you to think about what you want it to do instead of just picking the biggest machine and hoping. I enjoy that kind of choice when I’m in the mood for it, but it makes the menus feel like a real part of the game rather than a short stop before the action. I don’t know how much time you’ll want to spend comparing equipment before a workout, but I’d make those choices while stationary and get into a match afterward.

The game’s slower movement is a nice change from shooters where every second is a sprint and a jump. You’re piloting a heavy machine, so positioning matters, and I think that gives the fights their own rhythm. It’s still a competitive online game, though, so a slower mech doesn’t mean you can wander off mentally. I’d give Gameplay a two because the fighting can hold your attention, but the learning and setup take a little patience.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d avoid Solaris 7 while you’re moving, even though mech combat sounds like it should be a comfortable fit for a steady pace. The aiming needs care, the fights are against other people, and you can’t pause an online match because your footing needs attention. I think a bike would be less awkward than a treadmill here, but I still wouldn’t want to be making careful target adjustments while bouncing around.

The arena format doesn’t really give you a quiet stretch where you can stop watching and then casually pick things back up. Even a larger team fight can turn quickly, and the player across from you isn’t going to wait while you get your rhythm back. I don’t know that changing the game settings would solve that, since the pressure comes from the live match itself. That’s a lot to ask of your balance and your aim at the same time.

If you’re set on trying it, I’d sort out the controls while you’re sitting down first, then see whether your setup feels natural before bringing exercise into it. I’m not going to assume controller support from the store details, and this is the kind of game where awkward aiming would get old quickly. For a workout, I’d pick something that lets you use loose inputs or pause without putting a competitive match on hold. This one asks for more focus than I want to spend while moving.

## Computer Requirements

The listed graphics requirement is old enough that the game doesn’t look like it needs a recent, powerful gaming card, so I’d start by checking that your computer can handle the game comfortably rather than worrying about having the newest hardware. The installation temporarily needs more space than the finished game, which is a slightly odd amount of room to clear out. My computer desk has seen less planning than that.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Solaris 7 makes sense if you want competitive mech fights and enjoy spending time tuning a machine before you use it. I’d play it seated, not as a treadmill or bike game, because the live arena battles need more precise attention than I’d want to manage while exercising. For the workout part, I’d keep looking.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
