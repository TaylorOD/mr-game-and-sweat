A workshop game in the middle of the desert sounds like a nice way to keep your legs moving without having to react to something every second, and I think My Time at Sandrock mostly delivers that. I’d save it for an easy workout, though, because the story and crafting menus can pull you right off the treadmill and into a very thoughtful discussion with a pile of scrap.

## Gameplay

The part I like is that your workshop gives the day some direction without making every minute feel urgent. You gather materials, build machines, and take commissions for the people of Sandrock, and I think that works well when you want something to focus on while you exercise. You can usually keep a small goal in mind, like collecting what you need for a machine, instead of trying to remember a dozen plot details. It’s nice to have a job.

That steady work does turn into a fair amount of sorting and planning, and I don’t think that’s automatically bad, but it does make the game more stop and start than the desert setting might suggest. You’ll spend time checking what a commission needs, seeing whether you have enough materials, and deciding what to make next. If that kind of workshop planning relaxes you, great. If you’d rather your game keep moving while you move, it can feel like you’ve brought a little desk job to the treadmill.

The story gives the town a reason to need your help, and I like that Sandrock feels like a community you’re gradually contributing to instead of a blank crafting menu with people standing around it. Conversations and cutscenes are part of the pace, though, so a workout session can go quiet while you listen or read. I’d be fine with that on a walk or an easy ride, but it’s not what I’d choose when I’m trying to keep a steady running rhythm. There’s a lot going on in town.

Ruins and the occasional fight with Geeglers break up the building, which is welcome because I wouldn’t want to spend every session making machines and checking supplies. Still, the action isn’t what I’d come to Sandrock for, and I’d expect combat to take more attention than gathering does. I don’t know how much I’d enjoy handling a fight while jogging, especially when I’m already trying to keep my hands steady. That’s probably a reason to slow down, not a reason to skip the whole game.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support gives you a real option here, and most of the workshop play seems like a reasonable fit for a controller while your hands are moving. Gathering and making your way around town don’t ask for the kind of split second precision that makes me want to stop the belt. I’d keep the pace comfortable, because even a forgiving game gets harder when your thumbs are bouncing around.

The menus are where I’d expect to lose my rhythm. Choosing materials and keeping track of commissions asks you to read and think, and there are stretches where Sandrock wants your attention more than your legs do. I’d slow down for that, or use those stretches on a bike where sitting still to check a crafting menu feels less awkward. I don’t know whether I’d want to do a hard run while planning a workshop expansion. I’d probably forget one of those plans.

Combat is another place I’d be careful. Geeglers aren’t the reason to avoid exercising with the game, but a fight asks for more direct control than wandering around town, and that’s when imprecise movement could get annoying. I’d play at an easy pace and let the calmer gathering and workshop tasks carry most of the workout. If you’re looking for a game that keeps you pressing buttons constantly, this might not be it.

For a bike, the slower sections are easier to live with because you can take a moment to read and decide what to build without feeling like you’re about to step off your stride. That doesn’t make every part effortless, but it makes the game’s pauses feel less like a distraction. On a treadmill, I’d keep the speed low enough that a menu doesn’t turn into an unexpected coordination exercise. I’d rather make a chair than fall over.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum asks for a dedicated graphics card around the level of an older GTX 760 or Radeon 7950, so I wouldn’t count on a basic integrated graphics setup. A mid range gaming computer from the last several years should be a much safer place to start, and I’d check that you have the 50 GB free before installing. The SSD recommendation is worth paying attention to if you have the choice, because this game takes up a bit of room.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Sandrock is a good choice for an easy walk or bike ride if you enjoy crafting and don’t mind pausing to plan. I’d skip it for an intense run where you want the game to keep your attention without asking you to read a menu.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
