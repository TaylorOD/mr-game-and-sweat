A wall mounted TV is the best option if you can drill into your wall. If you cant, a heavy rolling stand works well too. Just dont hang a screen off the treadmill unless the maker says you can. It shakes a lot, and that is a lot of weight in a bad spot.

## Your options

**Wall mount.** This is the sturdiest and keeps the floor clear. It needs solid wall behind it, and that usually means screwing into studs.

**Rolling TV stand.** No drilling and you can move it out of the way. Get one with a wide base and lock the wheels. Treadmills shake the floor, so test it before you trust it.

**Regular TV stand or dresser.** The cheap option. It works if the height is right and the TV is strapped down. Most TVs come with a way to attach a strap, or you can buy an anti tip kit for a few dollars.

**Monitor arm on a desk.** This is good for a treadmill desk where the desk isnt attached to the machine. Keep your speed slow, since the arm will wobble more than a wall mount.

## Find the right height

Stand on the treadmill and look straight ahead. Have someone mark that spot on the wall, or use a level and a tape measure. The middle of the screen should be close to that height. Remember the deck adds a few inches, and that some treadmills fold up, so check that nothing hits the screen.

## Wall mounting steps

1. **Check the back of your TV.** Look up its VESA size and weight in the manual.
2. **Buy a mount rated for it.** Make sure it matches the VESA size and holds more than the TV weighs.
3. **Find your studs.** Use a stud finder and confirm with a small test hole if you are unsure.
4. **Check for wires and pipes.** Never drill blind. If you dont know what is in the wall, ask someone who does.
5. **Follow the mount's instructions.** Use the hardware it came with and get a level out.
6. **Get a second person to lift it.** TVs are awkward and easy to drop.
7. **Shake test it.** Pull on it gently before you let go, and check it again after a few workouts.

If your wall is brick, concrete or something you arent sure about, or you rent, check first or hire a pro. It is cheaper than a broken TV.

## Handle the cables

Keep every cable away from the belt and the rollers. Run them along the wall or inside a cable cover. A wireless controller means you only have to deal with power and video.

## Set it up for gaming

Turn on your TV's game mode to cut down on input lag. If your PC is far away, a long HDMI cable or Steam's Remote Play can get the picture to your screen. Then run through the rest of [our treadmill setup guide](/how-to-set-up-your-treadmill-for-gaming/).

#### Warning: A falling screen can cause serious injury. If you arent sure your setup is secure, have a professional install it.
