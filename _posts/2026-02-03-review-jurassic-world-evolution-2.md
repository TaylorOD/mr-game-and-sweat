Building a dinosaur park is a pretty good way to keep your brain busy on an easy workout, because there’s always another enclosure to adjust or another dinosaur making a choice you didn’t plan for. I’d play Jurassic World Evolution 2 at a pace where I can stop and think, not while trying to run hard.

## Gameplay

The part I enjoy most is that the park feels like a bunch of small decisions that keep affecting each other, and you can spend a while changing one enclosure because a dinosaur isn’t comfortable, then realize the guests have their own complaints too. You’re balancing animal welfare with the practical business of keeping a park going, which is a nice problem to have in a game and a less nice one if you were hoping to just look at dinosaurs.

There’s a satisfying rhythm to sending teams on expeditions, getting fossils back, and working toward new dinosaurs, but it also means the game asks you to spend time in menus and wait for things to finish. I think that makes it a good game for a steady workout where you don’t mind the occasional quiet stretch, although it isn’t going to keep your hands occupied every second. Sometimes the big event is a research project moving along.

The dinosaurs are the reason I’d keep coming back. They have needs, they interact with their surroundings, and when an enclosure isn’t working you usually have to think through what to change rather than just click a button and move on. I like having to consider the animals as more than decorations, even if I would probably spend too long adjusting a fence and then wonder why the guests are standing around.

The storms and breakouts add a little urgency, and that helps keep the park from feeling like a spreadsheet with large reptiles in it. It can be stressful when several problems show up together, but this isn’t a game that demands quick aiming or perfect reactions, so I don’t think those moments ruin the pace. I’d pause and sort things out rather than make a bad decision because I’m also trying to keep my feet moving.

Chaos Theory is a nice place to go when you want a more directed goal, since it puts you in situations based on the films instead of leaving every decision to you. The campaign has story and objectives, but I’d still expect plenty of time managing the park between the more scripted moments. Sandbox is probably where I’d settle in when I want to make things at my own pace. The dinosaurs are doing enough.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

This is much more suited to a steady pace than a hard run, because you’ll be looking around the park, choosing buildings, and making small adjustments with a controller. Full controller support is a real plus here, and most of the time I’d expect to have room to think before committing to a change. A bike might feel a little easier when you want to take your hands off the controls briefly, but I don’t think you need one to enjoy it.

I’d start in Sandbox, get the park moving, and pause the simulation while placing buildings or changing an enclosure. That lets you focus on the part that needs precision without the rest of the park continuing to generate new problems, and then you can get back to pedaling while the game runs. I don’t know if I’d want to learn every system while jogging, because the menus and park layout take some attention, but a familiar park should be manageable.

The trickiest moments are when a dinosaur escapes or a storm creates several problems at once, because now you’re trying to read what’s happening and choose what to deal with first. It’s not twitch gameplay, but I can picture reaching for the wrong option with a bouncing hand and sending a team somewhere unhelpful. There’s no need to make the workout harder than the park already is.

I’d skip a fast treadmill pace and save the more involved Challenge or Chaos Theory sessions for when you’re comfortable stopping to plan. On a bike, I’d use a relaxed pace and let the simulation run while I handle the quieter management work. You don’t have to keep moving the camera every second. That helps.

## Computer Requirements

You’ll want a dedicated graphics card, and I’d check that first since the minimum calls for a card with 4 GB of video memory. It doesn’t look like a game that demands a top of the line machine, but an older basic laptop may have a harder time keeping up with all the dinosaurs.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Jurassic World Evolution 2 is worth playing if you like building and managing a park, and you’re happy with a workout game that gives you quiet stretches between decisions. I’d pick it for an easy treadmill or bike session, not a hard run where the park gets whatever attention is left.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
