Keep the treadmill at a pace where you can turn your attention to a firefight without stepping like you’re trying to dodge a chair. BRAIN / OUT sounds like a better workout distraction than a game with long story breaks, but I’d be careful about expecting your aim to stay tidy.

## Gameplay

The constant action is the appeal here, and also the reason I wouldn’t call it an easy background game. BRAIN / OUT is a multiplayer shooter built around dynamic battles, so there’s likely to be plenty happening rather than long stretches of waiting for the next bit of the game to begin. That’s useful when you’re trying to stay engaged on a treadmill, because a match gives your brain something immediate to follow. It also means your attention is going to get pulled toward the screen. Your legs may be doing their own thing.

A large arsenal of modern weapons gives the game room to make choosing your approach part of the fun, and I like that the setting isn’t the usual shiny future battlefield. The nostalgic post Soviet feel gives it a specific flavor without needing a long explanation, which is nice when you’re trying to keep moving and don’t want a game to stop for a history lesson. I don’t know how much that look changes the feel of a match, but I’d rather have a shooter with a point of view than one that looks like every other room with a gun in it.

The multiplayer part is what makes me hesitate a little. When other players are involved, a missed shot or a slow reaction can mean you’re out of the action, and that’s a different kind of distraction from a game that lets you pause and take a drink. I think the pace could keep a workout from feeling long, but the match may also turn into a very serious conversation between you and one small target on the screen. The target probably has a better afternoon planned.

Free to play makes it easy to see if the general feel works for you before you put money into it, which matters because multiplayer shooters can be very personal. Some people enjoy having to stay alert and aim carefully, and some people, including me in this particular setup, would rather not make every jog feel like a tryout for a job that involves shooting. There’s enough here to sound worth trying at your desk first, but I wouldn’t promise you’ll find the action relaxing.

I’d put gameplay at a two. It sounds like the fights keep you occupied, and there’s no story heavy setup in the description that would make you stand around waiting, but the focus needed to aim and react could be a lot while you’re exercising. If you want a game that keeps your mind busy, that’s a plus. If you’re hoping to half watch while you run, probably not.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Your feet can keep a steady rhythm through a match, but your hands may have a harder time keeping the same rhythm as the people you’re aiming at. A multiplayer shooter asks you to look, turn and react, and those inputs get less precise when your body is moving. On a bike I’d expect it to be easier to hold a stable position, though I’d still start with a gentle pace rather than trying to win a firefight and pedal like a machine.

Controller support is something I can’t confirm from the information available, so I wouldn’t buy or set up the game on the assumption that a controller will work. I don’t know whether the controls offer any aim assistance either, and I’m not going to invent a setting that would make this sound easier than it is. Test the controls sitting down first, then try a very easy workout if they feel comfortable. It’s not the sort of game I’d want to troubleshoot while standing on a moving belt.

The multiplayer format is also a reason to avoid treating this like a pause whenever you feel like it game. Other players won’t necessarily stop because you need to adjust your grip or look down at the treadmill, and if you lose track of the action you could spend the rest of the match trying to catch up. I’d skip it for a fast run, and give it a try on a slow walk or an easy bike ride where you can keep your hands steady.

There’s a version of this that could work well, especially if you like shooters enough that aiming keeps you interested through a workout. But I think it asks for more accuracy than I’d want to promise from a moving treadmill, and I wouldn’t make a hard workout depend on landing shots. Let the belt do the exercise part. Your mouse hand or controller hand has enough going on.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very modest, so an older computer with basic graphics should be enough to run it, and the small storage requirement is unlikely to be the thing that stops you. I’d check that your internet connection is steady first, since this is a multiplayer game and a workout is already enough moving around.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

BRAIN / OUT sounds worth a try for someone who wants a busy shooter and is happy to exercise at an easy pace, especially on a bike. I’d skip it for a hard treadmill session, and I’d check the controls before planning a workout around it.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
