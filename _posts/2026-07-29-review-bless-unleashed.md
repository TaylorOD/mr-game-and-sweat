The open world and treacherous dungeons make Bless Unleashed sound like a better fit for an easy ride than a hard run, because I’d rather have a little room to react when the intense combat starts. It’s free to play, so you can try it without buying the whole workout plan up front.

## Gameplay

The thing I like about an open world MMO for exercise is that it gives you a place to move around without every minute being a tightly timed challenge, and Bless Unleashed has that kind of room to explore. The combat sounds like it can pull your attention in quickly, though, which is good when you want the game to keep you engaged and less good when you’re trying to keep your stride relaxed.

I think the strongest fit is exploring the regions at a pace where you can look around and decide what you want to take on, rather than treating every stretch like a race to the next fight. There’s a lot of space in that idea, and I like games that let you set the intensity yourself. I’d probably be tempted to run straight toward the next bit of action, which is exactly how I turn a calm workout into a small argument with my own legs.

The dungeons sound like the part I’d save for when I’m sitting down. They’re described as treacherous, and the game’s combat as intense, so I wouldn’t want to be splitting my attention between a demanding fight and keeping my feet on a moving belt. On a bike, I’d be more comfortable trying one, but I’d still expect it to take more focus than casual exploring.

Playing with friends or alongside other people could make the world feel less like a long stretch of exercise with some scenery around it, and that’s a real point in its favor. I don’t know how much of the fun depends on coordinating with other players, so I wouldn’t plan a workout around a group activity unless everyone’s comfortable with me occasionally being distracted by pedaling. That is not an especially heroic role.

There’s also a useful difference between an MMO that keeps handing you reasons to keep going and one that needs you to stop and think through every moment. Bless Unleashed sounds closer to the first one when you’re out exploring, and closer to the second when you’re in a dungeon or a serious fight. I’d enjoy the change of pace, but I wouldn’t assume the whole game stays equally easy to play while moving.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is a big help here, because you can keep your hands on a controller instead of reaching for a keyboard while you’re moving. I’d still start on a treadmill at a pace where I can make small corrections without thinking about it too much. Combat that’s described as intense is a reason to be cautious, not a reason to turn the speed up and prove something to a game.

For a treadmill, I’d stick to exploring and lighter stretches of play, then slow down or stop before heading into a dungeon. The world may be broad, but it’s still an online game with other people in it, and I wouldn’t count on being able to pause whenever I want. That makes it a little less forgiving than a game that waits patiently while you catch your breath.

A bike seems like the easier place to deal with sudden fights, since your balance isn’t changing with every step and your hands can stay on the controller. But the combat could still ask for more attention than I’d want during a hard interval, so I’d keep the effort moderate. If you’re trying to push your pace, the game may end up getting the leftover part of your brain.

I’d also be selective about when I play with friends. Exploring together sounds like a nice way to keep a workout from feeling repetitive, but I wouldn’t volunteer for a complicated dungeon while I’m concentrating on staying steady. If everyone knows you may need to take things slowly, that sounds workable. If they’re expecting you to respond instantly, I’d play another time.

The practical plan is pretty simple: use the controller, keep the workout easy enough that you can pay attention, and treat the dungeons as a stop moving moment. I don’t know that I’d want every session to involve combat, but the open world gives you a reason to stay with it when you’re not in the mood to focus hard.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You’ll need a dedicated graphics card around the level of the GTX 960 or Radeon R7 370, so this isn’t a game I’d count on running well with basic integrated graphics. I’d check the graphics card first, then make sure there’s room for the 50 GB install, because that is a fair amount of space to give one game.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Bless Unleashed makes sense for an easy workout if you enjoy exploring an online world and are willing to save the intense fights for when you can focus on them. I’d give it a try if you want a free game with room to roam, but I wouldn’t choose it for a workout built around speed or hard intervals.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
