A beaver heist sounds like a much better treadmill distraction than watching the seconds crawl by, but I’d only set this up when another person is ready to play beside you. Two players stealing wood from a sawmill under time pressure sounds fun, and a little hard to coordinate while you’re bouncing around.

## Gameplay

The premise is wonderfully direct. You and another beaver are trying to steal wood from a sawmill while things go sideways, and I like that it gives you something immediate to do instead of asking you to read a bunch of story before the game gets moving. I’m not sure how much variety there is from one heist to another, so I wouldn’t promise that the idea stays fresh for hours, but it’s an easy setup to understand.

The time pressure is the part that makes me think this could be a good game to play with someone who likes a little chaos. You have a shared job, a clock pushing you along, and presumably plenty of chances to get in each other’s way without needing a complicated plan. I say presumably because the store description doesn’t give me the details of how the heists play, and I’d rather leave a blank space than pretend I know what the sawmill is throwing at you.

Local co op also means the person beside you is part of the game, not just a witness to your workout. That can make a simple game much more entertaining, especially if you both find the pressure funny instead of stressful. It can also turn a small mistake into a whole discussion about who was supposed to grab the wood. I’ve seen that kind of thing happen in games. Usually both people were sure they were helping.

I don’t know how much time you spend waiting or figuring out what to do, and that matters for a treadmill game because a pause in the action is when I start paying attention to the belt again. The basic pitch sounds active enough to keep you engaged, but the game is in Early Access, so I’d treat this as a promising little co op idea rather than a sure bet for a long workout routine. That’s a fair amount of uncertainty.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The big question is whether you have another player with you, because this is a two player local co op game and it’s built around doing the heist together. Full controller support is helpful here, since I’d much rather hold a controller than try to manage a keyboard while exercising, but it doesn’t solve the practical issue of needing a second person who also wants to play.

On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace comfortable and avoid treating the timer like a reason to sprint. The whole point is that the heist is under pressure, and that pressure may get you leaning into the game when your feet should be doing their usual thing. I think it could work well at an easy pace, where you have room to react without every little moment feeling urgent.

A bike may be the simpler option if you want to put more attention into coordinating with the person beside you. You still have to pedal and play, but you’re not managing each step at the same time, and I’d expect that to make the time pressure easier to handle. I don’t know whether the controls demand quick, precise movements, so I wouldn’t start with a hard workout and assume the game will politely wait for you. It might not.

I’d skip this for a workout where you’re trying to stay focused on a particular pace or intensity. The premise sounds like it wants you to react to what’s happening, and having a partner there probably makes that more fun, but it also gives you another reason to get distracted. If you’re exercising casually and the person next to you is in on the plan, that sounds like a good time. If you’re alone, the two player setup makes it a less obvious choice.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimums are extremely modest, so I’d expect this to run on an older computer or one using integrated graphics. I’d check that the machine can launch the game and that your controllers work, not worry about buying a new graphics card for a beaver heist.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This sounds like a cheerful choice for two people who want a casual, slightly chaotic workout game and don’t mind that it’s still in Early Access. I’d give it a try for an easy bike session or a relaxed treadmill pace, but I’d skip it if you’re exercising alone or need a game that never asks for your attention.
