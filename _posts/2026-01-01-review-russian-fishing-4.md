A fishing game can make a treadmill feel less like exercise and more like standing near a lake while your legs do something useful, and Russian Fishing 4 is unusually well suited to that slow pace. I’d play it for a gentle workout, not for a session where I’m trying to keep my heart rate up.

## Gameplay

The lack of a storyline is a plus here, because I don’t want to be jogging through a quiet stretch and suddenly realize I missed a conversation that explains why I’m supposed to care about a king or a cursed sword. Russian Fishing 4 is built around going where you want and fishing, and that gives you room to settle into your own pace. It’s pretty much the opposite of a game that keeps shouting for your attention.

Waiting is part of the experience, and I think that’s either the reason this will work for you or the reason it won’t. There are stretches where you cast and watch for a bite, and those stretches can be peaceful if you’re in the mood to let your brain wander a little. They can also feel like being asked to watch a kettle boil while you’re already standing on a moving belt.

The fishing itself gives you something to pay attention to once a fish takes interest, so it isn’t all staring at water. I like the idea of that change in pace, because you can settle into the game and then have a small reason to focus without the whole thing becoming frantic. Still, I wouldn’t expect the steady stream of decisions you get from an action game. Sometimes the main activity is waiting for the main activity.

The RPG side gives the open ended fishing a reason to keep going, and I think that suits the game better than a story would. You can make a little progress without needing to finish a chapter or remember a bunch of plot details, which is handy when you’re also trying to remember whether you started walking faster. I don’t know if that kind of gradual progression will be enough for everyone, but it makes sense for what this is.

There’s a lot of freedom in a game with no set route, and I like that it doesn’t seem to be in a hurry to move you along. But freedom can also mean you have to supply some of your own goals. If you need every session to hand you a dramatic new thing to do, this may feel like a long afternoon where the fish are being polite and not much else.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A slower fishing game is a much better match for exercise than something that expects you to aim quickly while the floor is bouncing under you. I’d start at an easy pace, cast, and let the waiting parts do their job, then slow down a bit whenever I needed to pay closer attention to a fish. The game has room for that kind of stop and start.

The part I’d be cautious about is controlling it comfortably. I can’t confirm controller support, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming you can pick up a controller and have everything work the way you want. Check the controls on your setup before you plan a workout around it. If you’re using a mouse and keyboard, a stationary bike seems easier to manage than a treadmill, because your hands don’t have to stay steady while your whole body is moving.

When a fish is on the line, I’d expect to give the screen more attention than during the quiet stretches, and I wouldn’t try to make that part fit a fast run. There’s no need to turn every bite into a race. Take the pace down, deal with the fish, and carry on afterward. That’s less exciting than sprinting through a boss fight, but I think it’s a more sensible deal for your balance.

I’d also skip it on a workout where I wanted constant action. You can’t make a fishing simulator keep producing bites just because you’re getting bored, and that’s probably fine for the game but not for every exercise plan. If you like a steady, low effort ride and a game that gives your mind somewhere to go, this could fit. If you need to watch the road in your game every second, you may spend more time waiting than playing.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You’ll need a dedicated graphics card around the level of a GTX 760, so this isn’t a game I’d count on running well on basic integrated graphics. I’d check the graphics card first, then make sure you have room for the substantial install; 40 GB is a lot of virtual shoreline.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Russian Fishing 4 makes the most sense for you if you want a calm game to pair with an easy workout and don’t mind stretches of waiting. I’d skip it if you want constant action, and I’d verify your controls before buying.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
