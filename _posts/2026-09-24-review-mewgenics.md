The cat breeding and turn based fights make this a better workout game than its busy planning screen might suggest, because you can take your time once a fight starts. I’d keep the pace comfortable and let the cats handle the urgent part.

## Gameplay

I think Mewgenics has a genuinely good idea at the center of it: you put together a cat crew through breeding, then send them into tactical adventures where abilities and items shape how a run goes. That gives the choices a little weight, because you’re not only picking what seems useful right now, you’re also thinking about what kind of cat you want to send out. It’s a lot more interesting than staring at a character screen and deciding which hat looks strongest.

The turn based combat should be the part that holds your attention while you’re moving. You can consider a turn instead of trying to hit a target while your hand bounces, and that matters a lot on a treadmill. Drafting abilities and collecting items also give each outing some variation, so I wouldn’t expect every trip to feel like the same small chore. There’s enough going on to keep your brain occupied.

The breeding side is also the thing I’d be most likely to enjoy and the thing I’d be most likely to get bogged down in. Manipulating genetics across generations sounds compelling, and it gives the cats a connection to the larger plan rather than making them feel like disposable pieces. But I can see myself lingering over the choices and trying to make the perfect team, which is not ideal when you’re supposed to be keeping a steady rhythm. I have made harder decisions over less important things.

I don’t know how much time the game asks you to spend between adventures, but the simulation and strategy parts mean there will be stretches where you’re thinking more than reacting. That’s not a dealbreaker for me. On an easy workout, I’d rather have a game that lets me slow down and make a choice than one that expects my thumbs to perform while my legs are doing their own thing.

The roguelike structure sounds like a good fit for short sessions, since you can focus on an adventure rather than needing to hold a long story in your head. Still, I’d expect some runs to take more attention than others as the tactical choices stack up, and I wouldn’t pick this when I’m trying to do a hard interval workout. It asks for thought, but it doesn’t sound like it asks for twitch reflexes.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, I’d play this at a walk or an easy jog, because the battles wait for you and the controller support is full. That takes away the part that usually makes a game stressful while moving, which is having to react in the same second something happens. You can make a decision, take a breath, and then keep going.

The controller is a real advantage here. I’d rather hold one than reach toward a keyboard while trying to stay balanced, and turn based combat is forgiving of a little imprecision. I don’t know if I’d want to make detailed breeding decisions at a fast running pace, though, because reading and weighing options can pull your attention away from your stride. I’d save the fiddly planning for an easier section of the workout.

A bike should work too, especially if you’re comfortable glancing down at the screen and taking your time between turns. I wouldn’t call it a game that needs a bike, since the lack of rapid inputs makes a treadmill reasonable, but a steady ride gives you more freedom to focus on the cats when you’re picking abilities or sorting out a team. Either way, I’d avoid trying to turn it into a sprint session.

There’s a practical split here. Play the adventures while you’re moving, and don’t feel like you have to finish every management choice at the same pace; if you catch yourself stopping just to think about genetics, that’s a sign to ease off for a bit. I like that the game’s turn structure gives you that option without making you feel behind.

## Computer Requirements

This doesn’t look like a game that needs a new graphics card, but I’d check that your card can handle the required graphics support before assuming an older computer will run it well. A midrange card from the last several years should be plenty, and the minimum specs don’t sound demanding overall.

## Conclusion and Score

My bottom line is that I’d suggest Mewgenics to you if you want a thoughtful game that can sit alongside a steady workout, rather than one that keeps demanding quick reactions. The breeding and planning may slow you down, but the turn based fights make it practical to play while moving. It’s for people who don’t mind stopping to think.
