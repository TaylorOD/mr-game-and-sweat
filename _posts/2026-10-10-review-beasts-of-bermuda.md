Growing into a huge dinosaur sounds like a decent treadmill distraction, but Beasts of Bermuda also sounds like a game where you can spend a lot of your workout trying to stay alive. I’d give it a try at an easy pace, not when I’m trying to set a personal record.

## Gameplay

The player made ecosystem is the part that makes me curious. You aren’t just wandering around a dinosaur themed map by yourself, you’re sharing the world with other players who are also trying to survive and grow, and that gives the whole thing a little more unpredictability than a regular survival game. I like the idea of a world that can feel different because of who else is in it. I also know that unpredictable people can turn a quiet plan into a mess pretty fast.

Growing to monstrous sizes sounds like a good long term goal, but it probably means the game asks you to stick with one dinosaur and put time into keeping it going. I’m fine with games that take their time, but I need something to do while they’re doing it. I don’t know how much moment to moment activity there is between the big survival moments, so I’d go in expecting some stretches where the world is doing more than I am.

The storms and fearsome foes sound like the parts that could wake the game up when things have gotten quiet. I think that contrast could work well, because surviving a sudden threat matters more when you’ve been trying to build up your dinosaur instead of just charging from fight to fight. But I wouldn’t want every calm stretch to feel like waiting for weather. That’s a lot of staring at a very large lizard.

There’s also a nice bit of tension in being part of a player created ecosystem. You’re not just collecting progress in a vacuum, and the other animals around you might change how safe or dangerous the world feels. I can see that being compelling for someone who likes a slower multiplayer survival game, though I’m not sure it gives you the steady activity I’d want on every workout. Sometimes the interesting thing is just what someone else is doing.

Early Access makes me a little more cautious, mostly because I’d want to know what the current experience feels like before committing to a long growth focused game. I don’t mean that as a knock, it’s just easier to enjoy a slow game when you know what kind of pace you’re signing up for. This seems like one I’d try for the ecosystem and survival, not because I need a constant stream of action.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is a good start, and it means you don’t have to build your workout around reaching for a keyboard. I’d use a controller and keep the pace comfortable, because even a game with simple movement can get awkward when your hands are bouncing and you’re trying to pay attention to other players.

The multiplayer part gives me pause for exercising. I’d expect to care about what’s happening around my dinosaur, especially if I’ve spent time growing it, and I don’t know whether I’d be relaxed enough to keep a steady pace when a storm or a foe turns up. That might be a fun kind of pressure on a bike, where you can ease off the effort without also trying to stay balanced on a moving belt.

On a treadmill, I’d start slow and choose a pace where looking around and steering don’t feel like a thumb wrestling match. I wouldn’t make this the game for a hard run, since trying to react quickly while your whole body is moving sounds like a reliable way to miss both the game and your stride. Not ideal.

If the world is quiet, the slower survival side could be a nice fit for an easy workout, because you can pay attention without needing to land perfect inputs every few seconds. When danger shows up, I’d expect to slow down or even pause the workout until I’m comfortable again. I’d rather keep the dinosaur alive than pretend I’m good at multitasking.

This isn’t an automatic treadmill game just because the controller works, but I do think it could suit someone who likes a slower pace and doesn’t mind multiplayer interruptions. I’d probably pick a gentle bike session first, then see if the movement feels manageable on a treadmill. The controller helps. The rest depends on the moment.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You’ll want a dedicated graphics card from an older gaming PC, rather than relying on basic integrated graphics, and the game also needs a broadband connection for its multiplayer world. I’d check the graphics card first, because the minimum calls for a GTX 680, GTX 770, GTX 960 or a similar Radeon card, which is more than a computer bought just for email may have hiding inside it.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Beasts of Bermuda sounds best for you if you want a slower multiplayer survival game and like the idea of growing into a big dinosaur in a shared world. For workouts, I’d keep the pace easy and be ready to slow down when the world gets dangerous. If you need constant action, I’d look elsewhere.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
