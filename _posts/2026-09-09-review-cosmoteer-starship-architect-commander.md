A steady bike ride is probably the best place for Cosmoteer, because you can take your time making a ship and then watch your choices matter when a fight starts. On a treadmill, I think I’d spend too much time trying to click tiny parts of the ship while also remembering to move my legs.

## Gameplay

The ship building is the part I’d keep coming back to, and also the part that can quietly eat your whole workout. You’re fitting rooms, weapons, engines and crew into a ship that has to function, not just look like something a space captain would put on a poster, and I like that the choices have practical consequences. A weapon can be powerful, but if the crew can’t get to it or the ship can’t keep it supplied, the design starts to look a little less clever.

Then you take that design into a real time fight, and suddenly the arrangement of those rooms matters in a way that’s easy to understand even when you’re not an expert. I think that’s really cool. You can adjust your ship after seeing what went wrong, which makes losing feel more like finding a bad shelf in a kitchen than being told you’re bad at the whole game.

The pace does move between building, managing and fighting, so it isn’t one continuous stream of action. There are moments where I’d be stopped, staring at the layout and deciding whether a new weapon is worth changing half the ship around for, and that’s an interesting decision when I’m sitting down but not necessarily while I’m trying to keep a jogging rhythm. I’d probably enjoy that thinking time more on a bike.

Playing alone gives you room to tinker without a friend waiting on you, while co op and online PvP offer very different kinds of company. PvP sounds like the mode where I’d care a lot more about every decision, which is good if that’s what you want and not what I’d pick for a casual workout. You can take your time with ship design. You may not feel like taking your time once somebody else is trying to turn your ship into spare parts.

It’s Early Access, so I’d go in expecting a game that’s still being developed rather than a finished package with every rough edge polished away. The basic idea has enough going on to hold my attention, but I’d want to be in the mood for planning and rearranging rather than looking for a quick match that starts immediately. There’s a lot to fiddle with.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The interface is the part I’d worry about before starting a run. Designing a ship means placing parts and checking how everything fits, and that kind of cursor work is much easier when you aren’t bouncing around on a treadmill. I can’t confirm full controller support, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming you can put the keyboard and mouse away.

A bike seems more forgiving, especially if you can sit upright and keep your hands steady, though I’d still expect the building sections to ask for more attention than a simple racing game. For a treadmill, I’d keep the pace easy and stop the belt before making detailed changes, then get moving again for the fights or simpler stretches. That does mean the workout gets broken up a bit. So does having to rescue a very ambitious ship design.

I’d skip online PvP while exercising. Other players aren’t going to wait while you sort out a layout, and even if you’re good at the game, managing a live fight while moving sounds like a pretty reliable way to forget what your feet are doing. Playing alone is the more sensible option, especially since you can focus on your own ship and pause when you need to think.

I don’t know how comfortable the controls would feel on a controller, and I wouldn’t want to pretend otherwise. If you already play PC games with a mouse and keyboard while biking, give it a try at an easy pace; if you need hands free or very simple inputs, this probably isn’t the game to start with.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum asks for a dual core processor, 4 GB of memory and a DirectX 10 graphics card with 1 GB of memory, so this doesn’t look like a game that needs a recent high end machine. I’d check the graphics card first if your computer is older, but the listed requirements are pretty modest.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Cosmoteer makes sense for you if building a ship is as appealing as fighting with it, and you’re happy to pause your workout for the fiddly parts. I’d choose a relaxed bike session, play alone, and leave PvP for when I can give it my full attention.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
