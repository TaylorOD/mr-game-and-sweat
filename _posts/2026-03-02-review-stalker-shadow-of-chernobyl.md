A Zone full of mutants, bandits, and things that bend the air is a pretty good way to forget you’re jogging, but I’d keep this for a slow treadmill session where you can stop without feeling like you’ve lost the whole workout. The aiming and the constant threat of something coming at you make it a rough fit for a bike.

## Gameplay

The Zone has the kind of danger that makes me pay attention, and that’s a real plus when you’re trying to stay occupied during exercise. Radiation, anomalies, creatures, and other stalkers all give you reasons to look around instead of just following a marker, and I think that tension works better than a steady stream of enemies would. You’re not just mowing through everything in front of you. Sometimes you need to decide that the strange patch of air is probably not a shortcut.

The quieter stretches are part of the appeal, but they’re also where the game can ask more of your attention in a less exciting way. You’ll be moving between objectives, checking what you’ve picked up, and talking to people who have their own plans for the Zone. I like that the world feels dangerous even when nothing is actively attacking you, but I’d expect those slower moments to make a run feel stop and start. It’s not all firefights.

And when a fight does happen, I think the game gets its hooks into you pretty quickly. Enemies can make you take cover and line up shots instead of charging ahead, which is compelling when you’re sitting still and maybe not what your legs were asking for. A pack of dogs appearing while you’re trying to focus on a conversation would be a pretty effective way to forget your pace. It’s not exactly calming.

I don’t know how much story you’ll want to follow while moving, because the game’s setting and your place in it matter, but it doesn’t sound like a game that makes you sit through one long movie before you can play. The story is there, and so are the people you meet, but the danger and wandering seem like the parts that would keep me engaged. If you’re looking for a game you can half watch while your brain handles the workout, this probably asks too much.

The survival angle also gives the walking around some purpose. You’re not just clearing rooms because the game needs another room cleared, and that makes exploring the Zone feel like a decent use of exercise time. I’d still expect to pause now and then to sort out what I’m carrying or think through where to go next. Sometimes the workout is going to be waiting for you.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, I’d start at a walk and leave myself room to stop, because aiming while your hands are bouncing is the part I’m least confident about. The game is a first person shooter, and even if you can move through the Zone at your own pace, enemies don’t politely wait for your feet to settle. A slow pace might be manageable. A hard run seems like a lot to ask from your thumbs.

I don’t believe the PC version has native controller support, so I’d check your setup before making this the game you plan a workout around. You may be able to use a controller mapping tool, but I wouldn’t assume that makes aiming feel comfortable. Mouse and keyboard are a much safer expectation here, and that can be awkward when your hands are busy keeping you upright.

The bike sounds a little less forgiving to me, since you’re still likely to need quick aiming and movement when something hostile shows up, and leaning over the bars to manage that is not an arrangement I’d trust. A stationary bike with a controller setup you’ve tested might be possible, but I’d keep the pace easy and avoid treating this like a game you can play by feel. The Zone is already doing enough.

If you do give it a try, I’d use a walk, keep the workout short at first, and stop the belt when you need to deal with an unfamiliar fight or sort through your gear. I know that sounds less convenient than just starting a run and playing, but I’d rather have a game interrupt the workout than have the workout interrupt my aim. A few careful minutes are still minutes.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This is an old PC game, and the minimum requirements are low enough that I’d look at whether your computer can run it comfortably before worrying about a graphics card upgrade. A very old graphics card is enough by the listed requirements, so you shouldn’t need a modern gaming machine to get into the Zone. Your computer is probably less intimidating than the things in it.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

S.T.A.L.K.E.R.: Shadow of Chernobyl sounds like a good game to play during a careful walk, but I wouldn’t buy it mainly as a workout game unless you’re comfortable with your control setup and the stop and go pace. If you want an atmospheric shooter that gives you plenty to pay attention to, it makes sense. If you want loose controls and easy exercise multitasking, I’d pick something else.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
