I’d save Dark Deception for an easy ride or a steady walk, because chasing down soul shards while a pack of murder monkeys closes in is a lot to ask from your feet and your thumbs at the same time. It looks like a horror game that can keep your attention, and it probably can, but I wouldn’t start it at a pace where missing a turn feels like a personal failure.

## Gameplay

The maze idea works for me because you’re moving most of the time, and the enemies make all that movement feel like it has a point. You’re trying to collect soul shards while things hunt you, so it isn’t the kind of game where you can drift off and let the screen do its thing. That’s useful for exercise, since I want something to keep my brain occupied, but it also means I’d be watching the game closely. The treadmill can wait a little.

The first maze’s murder monkeys are a good example of why this is more stressful than a casual collectathon. I think the chase gives every wrong turn some weight, and trying to remember where you’ve already been while something is coming after you sounds like a fun kind of panic. It also sounds like the kind of panic where my shoulders start creeping up toward my ears. I’d keep the pace comfortable.

Powerups like the stun orb and speed boost give you ways to deal with trouble instead of just hoping it goes away, and I like that the game lets you make a decision under pressure. Do I use something now, or save it in case the next corner is worse? That’s a nice little problem to have, although I can picture myself using the stun orb because I saw a monkey and then immediately wondering what I was saving it for. Probably nothing.

The story and the mysterious woman give the mazes a reason to connect, which is more appealing to me than just walking through spooky rooms because the lights are red. Still, story moments are where I’d want the game to let me settle down a bit, and a chase game can’t really promise that for long. I don’t know how much I’d care about the story while I’m trying to keep track of enemies, but I like having it there between the stressful parts.

Since it’s free to play, giving it a try doesn’t ask much of you besides some time and a willingness to be chased around a maze. I think the main appeal is clear pretty quickly, and the game doesn’t need a complicated setup to get you moving. Horror isn’t always what I want during exercise, though. If I’m already tired, I’d rather not add imaginary monkeys to the list of things demanding my attention.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A bike sounds easier to me than a treadmill for the parts where you have to turn quickly, because your legs can keep doing their thing while your hands deal with the maze. On a treadmill, I’d keep the speed low enough that I can look at the screen and steer without feeling like the belt is trying to take part in the chase. I wouldn’t use this for a hard run. I’m already asking myself to move in two directions at once.

The game works with a controller, which makes it a reasonable option for either setup, but the controls still need attention. You’re steering through corridors, looking for shards, and reacting when enemies get close. That’s not impossible while moving, but the fast turns and sudden danger could make a treadmill session feel less relaxed than the word exercise usually suggests. I’d be more comfortable on a bike, where a missed turn doesn’t also involve a moving belt.

I’d lower my workout intensity before I’d try to play faster, because there’s no benefit to turning a tense maze into a test of how quickly I can correct a mistake. I don’t know if I’d want this during a long stretch of running, since the chase sections ask you to keep looking and reacting, but it could fit a short, easy session. If the game starts getting under my skin, I’d pause and take a breather. That’s allowed.

The slower story parts are a useful chance to relax your hands and get back into the workout, but I wouldn’t count on being able to ignore the screen for long. You can’t really set the controller down when something is chasing you, and trying to do that while running sounds like a bad way to turn a game into a coordination exercise. I’d play a level at a time and stop when I notice I’m leaning toward the screen.

## Computer Requirements

You’ll want a computer with a dedicated graphics card around the level of a GTX 1060, so this isn’t a safe bet for an older laptop with only built in graphics. I’d check the graphics card first, then make sure the computer has enough memory, because the listed minimum is more demanding than the small storage requirement makes it sound. It should be fine on a mid range gaming computer from a few years back.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Dark Deception is a fun choice for an easy bike ride or a cautious walk, especially if you like horror games that keep you moving. I’d skip it for a fast treadmill run, but the free to play start makes it an easy one to try.
