I’d put Automobilista 2 on a stationary bike before I tried it on a treadmill, because keeping a car on a racing line while your hands bounce with your steps sounds like a lot of tiny corrections. The range of cars and tracks is appealing, though, and I think it could make a steady ride go by quickly.

## Gameplay

There’s a lot here for someone who likes racing itself more than a story about racing, and I think that’s a good fit for exercise because you’re usually paying attention to the road instead of waiting for the next long scene. The variety of cars and tracks gives you plenty to change when you want a different kind of drive, and the changing weather and track conditions mean the same circuit can ask a little more of you later. I like that there’s room to make your own kind of session instead of having to follow one long campaign.

The driving is the whole point, which is also why I wouldn’t call this a casual game to half watch while you jog. You’re steering, braking, and trying to keep a racing line, and a small mistake can turn into a bigger one if you’re already distracted by your feet. I’d probably start with a familiar car and track rather than choosing from the full collection like I’m picking a snack and somehow making it a project.

Dynamic weather sounds great for keeping races from feeling identical, but it also means you may need to pay closer attention when conditions change. I don’t know that I’d want that surprise on a run where I’m already tired, because I can barely make a decision about whether I want a drink when I’m jogging. For a bike session, though, the added variation could be fun.

There’s not much here to interrupt you with story or reading, which is a point in its favor. A race asks you to stay involved, and a quiet stretch of track doesn’t usually turn into a long break where you’re just sitting there. You can focus on the next corner instead of wondering how much more dialogue there is. That’s useful.

I’d keep the competition at a level where mistakes don’t ruin the whole session, especially while exercising. Racing against computer opponents can add some pressure, but I wouldn’t turn that pressure up just to prove I can handle it. I don’t know why I’m so tempted to make every game into a personal challenge, but that usually makes the game less relaxing.

The simulation side is a real plus if you want the driving to feel deliberate, and it’s also what makes this a little demanding. It isn’t a game where you can steer roughly and expect the car to sort things out for you. If you enjoy learning how a particular car behaves, that depth gives you something to work on. If you only want a loose arcade race while you sweat, this may feel like a lot of attention for a workout.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The bike seems like the easier place to start, since your hands can stay steadier while you steer and you can keep your effort consistent through a race. On a treadmill, I’d walk rather than run, because a racing game rewards small adjustments and those are harder when your whole body is moving. You might still manage it, but I’d expect more missed corners and less attention on the belt.

Full controller support helps a lot. You don’t need to set up a racing wheel just to play, and I’d use the controller’s analog sticks for steering and acceleration rather than trying to make every input sharp. That doesn’t make the driving simple, but it does make it easier to fit into a workout without having extra equipment taking over the room.

I’d start with a solo race or a practice session, keep the difficulty comfortable, and skip online competition while you’re moving. Having other people depend on your driving would make it very easy to care more about the race than your pace, and then you’re leaning forward like the bike is going to help you brake. It won’t.

I’d also stick with one car and one track until the controls feel familiar, then change things when you want a little more variety. The weather and track conditions can add interest, but I wouldn’t stack a bunch of extra challenge onto the same session right away. This game asks for attention, and your legs are already doing something.

If you want to run hard and barely look at the screen, I’d choose something else. If you’re walking or cycling at a steady pace and you enjoy concentrating on driving, Automobilista 2 has a good chance of keeping your mind occupied. That’s probably enough.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This needs a dedicated graphics card around the level of a GTX 680, so I wouldn’t assume an everyday laptop with integrated graphics will handle it well. I’d check the graphics card before anything else, and make sure there’s room for the large installation. The 120 GB storage requirement is not subtle.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Automobilista 2 makes the most sense for someone who wants a focused racing game during a bike ride or an easy walk, and who enjoys learning a car rather than casually pushing buttons. I’d skip it for a fast treadmill run, but I’d give it a try for steady exercise.
