I’d put Superflight on an easy workout, not the kind where you’re trying to set a personal record on the treadmill, because steering a wingsuit through mountains asks for a little attention even when the controls are simple. The endless generated maps make it easy to start a run and see where you end up.

## Gameplay

The appeal here is that you’re mostly doing one thing, flying, and the game doesn’t keep pulling you away from it with a story or a long list of jobs to finish. You steer through these generated landscapes and try to get a higher score, with points for flying close to the terrain, so the obvious move is to skim the mountain instead of taking the safe, wide route. I like that the game gives you a reason to take a little risk without turning every run into a complicated plan.

That proximity scoring is also where the game gets its tension. You can aim for open air and have a calmer flight, or cut close to the ground and try to squeeze out more points, and I think that choice keeps the basic flying from feeling completely automatic. Of course, the route that looks easy on the screen can turn into a rock in your face pretty quickly. That’s the deal.

The maps being procedurally generated is a good fit for this kind of short session, because you’re not waiting around for the game to get to the interesting part or trying to remember a long route. You can chase a new high score, start again, and get a different landscape to fly through. I don’t know that I’d want a game like this to have a lot more going on, honestly, because then I’d be trying to read instructions while already pointed toward a cliff.

There is a limit to how much variety the simple goal can give you, and if you need a new mechanic every few minutes, this probably won’t hold your attention for a long workout. But if you enjoy getting a little better at steering and taking a slightly riskier line, the repetition has a purpose. I’d be happy with that on a shorter session.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The loose, flowing movement sounds like a better match for a bike or a relaxed treadmill pace than a hard run, because you need enough attention to steer without needing the kind of sharp reactions that make you forget you’re exercising. Superflight supports a controller, which is what I’d use, and the basic idea is easy to understand. You’re not managing a bunch of buttons while also trying to keep your feet moving.

Still, flying close to the terrain for points means your hands will be making small corrections, and I think that’s where a treadmill can get awkward. If you’re bouncing with every step, a narrow gap on screen may feel like trying to thread a needle on a bus. I’d keep the treadmill at a pace where you can stay steady, and I wouldn’t make this the game you choose for intervals.

A bike gives you a little more freedom to focus on the screen, especially during the riskier parts of a flight, but it isn’t required. On either machine, I’d treat the score as a bonus and take the safer path when I can feel my attention drifting. You can always start another run, which is much less annoying than trying to rescue a flight while your legs are doing something else.

I’d skip it if you want a game that plays itself while you exercise, because the close passes are the whole reason to care about improving your score. But the controls are straightforward, and the short runs make it easy to stop after a flight without losing your place in a big campaign. It’s manageable. Just don’t expect to ignore the screen.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This is an unusually modest game by current standards, with a minimum graphics card that’s old enough that the computer running it probably has other stories. I’d check that your machine meets the listed GeForce 650 level first, but you don’t need a recent gaming computer for this.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Superflight is an easy one to suggest for a shorter workout if you like steering through a landscape and chasing a score, and I’d leave it off the list for anyone who needs a game that demands no attention. Try it on a bike or an easy treadmill pace, and give the mountains some room.

### Overall Score: 2.67 out of 3
