I’d use World of Guns: Gun Disassembly for an easy spin where I want something to look at, not something that needs me to react every second. Taking apart tiny gun parts sounds more like a careful bench task than a workout game, and that can be nice when you want the belt or pedals to do most of the work.

## Gameplay

The appeal is pretty specific, which I think helps it. You’re looking into how guns fit together, from larger parts down to small screws, and the fun is paying attention to what you’re looking at rather than rushing through a level. I like that kind of focused curiosity, but I also think it can turn into staring at a little part and wondering if I’ve missed something.

There isn’t a story pulling you forward or a stream of enemies to keep your hands busy. That makes this a calmer game than most of what we cover here, and it also means you have to actually enjoy the subject. If you’re interested in how mechanical things are assembled, I can see the appeal. If you mostly want a game to distract you from the treadmill, this might not give you enough to hold onto.

The careful pace is a mixed bag. You can take your time examining what’s on screen, which is a better fit for exercise than a game that expects sharp reactions, but the attention involved can make a jog feel like a bad time to do detailed work. I’d start on an easy walk or gentle ride and see if the small interactions stay comfortable. There’s no reason to turn a relaxing little investigation into a coordination exercise.

I don’t know that the act of taking things apart will hold everyone’s attention for long, because it’s a narrow activity and the game isn’t disguising that with a big adventure around it. But if the subject itself is interesting to you, the detail is the point, and there’s something nice about being able to take a closer look rather than just seeing a gun as an object in a game. It’s a patient sort of game.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, I’d begin at a walking pace, because the fiddly work asks for more accuracy than I’d want to give it while jogging. A slow walk might be fine if you can keep your eyes on the screen and your hands steady, but this isn’t something I’d pick for a run where I’m already bouncing around. I can manage plenty of things badly at once, but I don’t need to add small screws to the list.

On a bike, it seems more workable, especially if you can ride at a comfortable pace and keep your upper body settled. The game doesn’t sound like it’s pushing you to move quickly, so you can stop to think without losing a match or missing a timed moment. I’d still expect to pause pedaling or ease off when I’m trying to select a tiny part. That’s not a big tragedy. It’s just not a game where I’d expect to pedal hard and do precise work at the same time.

I can’t confirm how well it handles a controller, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming that’s sorted. The interaction looks like it calls for careful selection and looking closely at parts, and I’d test the controls while seated before trying to use it in motion. If you already have a setup that lets you interact comfortably, great, but I wouldn’t count on loose inputs being forgiving here.

The quiet pace is a plus, but the focus is still real. You’re not dodging attacks or trying to keep up with a timer, which is why I think a stationary bike is the safer fit than running. If I were using it on a treadmill, I’d keep the speed low and treat it as something to try on a walk, not a workout that depends on staying in motion the whole time. Sometimes that’s enough.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimums are modest, and the small storage requirement makes this sound easy to fit on an older or basic computer. I’d check that the machine can handle DirectX 11 and has an internet connection, since those are the requirements here. It doesn’t look like you need to buy a new graphics card just to inspect a screw.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

World of Guns: Gun Disassembly makes sense if you’re curious about how guns are put together and want a slow, focused thing to do while exercising gently. I’d try it on a bike or an easy walk after checking the controls, but I wouldn’t choose it for a run where I want the game to keep pulling my attention along.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
