The giant war machines are the reason I’d try this for a slow workout, but they’re also why I wouldn’t start it at a running pace. You’re trying to scavenge in the middle of a war, and staying focused on where you’re going matters more than keeping your legs moving quickly.

## Gameplay

I like that The Forever Winter makes the battlefield feel like a place you’re trying to survive, not a shooting gallery that happens to have giant robots in it. You and your squad are looking for loot among the dead while AI automata and cybernetically enhanced soldiers keep fighting around you, and that gives the basic scavenging a useful bit of tension. Sometimes the smart move is to keep going instead of trying to turn every nearby enemy into a problem you can solve with bullets. I’d probably need to remind myself of that.

The gargantuan war machines do a lot for the mood, because they make your little squad feel very small without making the whole thing sound like a joke. I think the pressure of being surrounded by a conflict that doesn’t care about you is a strong idea for a shooter, and it gives ordinary decisions like where to move and what to risk a little more weight. I’m much more interested in sneaking through a fight than trying to win the entire war one rifle at a time.

There’s a lot of looking, weighing the risk, and deciding whether whatever you came across is worth staying in a dangerous place for, so this isn’t the kind of action game that keeps your attention by throwing a new target at you every few seconds. That slower pace can be a nice change, especially when the battlefield itself is doing the loud and alarming part, but it also means there can be stretches where you’re thinking more than shooting. I don’t know how much patience I’d have for that on a day when I just want action right away.

Early Access matters here. The idea is appealing, but you’re buying into a game that’s still being worked on, and I wouldn’t expect every part of the experience to feel finished and carefully polished. I’d be curious to see how the game keeps changing, but if you want a complete, dependable campaign you can settle into without wondering what may shift, I’d wait. The war can keep going without me for a bit.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A slow walk is probably the right place to start, because the game asks you to pay attention to threats while you move through a battlefield full of things much larger and better armed than you are. On a bike, I’d expect it to be easier to look around and aim without the extra bounce, but neither setup makes careful shooting automatic. If you want to run hard, the visual chaos and the need to judge when to move or stay put sound like a pretty good way to forget the belt is moving.

I’d keep the workout easy enough that I could actually look for enemies and make a deliberate choice, instead of trying to sprint through every risky stretch because I’m bored. If you’re looting and the fight around you shifts, that feels like a moment where you might want to slow down or pause the workout, not just keep your pace and hope your aim sorts itself out. I can’t tell you how much easier it would be to manage all of that with a controller until you’ve checked the current controls on your setup, so I’d do that before planning a long session.

The big machines and the constant fighting could make a run feel lively, but I don’t think that means the game is friendly to sloppy inputs. Aiming and moving through danger are still the parts where your attention matters, and a treadmill adds just enough wobble to make a careful shot feel like trying to thread a needle while somebody bumps your elbow. Maybe not the best time to show off.

For a bike ride, I’d use a lower effort setting and treat the quieter scavenging as the workout’s steady part, with the dangerous moments as a reason to pay closer attention rather than pedal faster. On a treadmill, walking seems much more sensible than jogging, and I’d skip any attempt to make this an intense interval game. It’s not a game where I’d want my hands and my legs both making ambitious decisions at once.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This needs a recent, strong graphics card, so I wouldn’t count on an older laptop or basic integrated graphics to handle it well. The first thing I’d check is whether your GPU is in the same general range as the recommended minimum, and I’d make sure you have room on an SSD too, since one is recommended and the install takes 60 GB.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Worth a look if you want a slower, tense shooter and you’re comfortable with an Early Access game that’s still developing. For exercising, I’d keep it to a walk or an easy bike ride, because watching the battlefield is part of the work.
