Put Unpacking on an easy walk and let the boxes set the pace, because I think arranging somebody’s life one lamp and sock at a time is a much better treadmill activity than it sounds. It’s quiet enough that you might notice your breathing, which is kind of the point.

## Gameplay

The fun is in the little decisions, and I mean little in the very literal sense: where does this mug go, does this book belong on a shelf, and why am I suddenly treating a pile of belongings like a serious design problem. The block fitting puzzle part gives each room a clear job, but I like that you’re also making the place feel lived in instead of just clearing a board.

Moving from one home to another gives the objects a bit of context, and I think that’s where the game becomes more interesting than its tidy rooms suggest. A childhood bedroom and a later home don’t feel like the same person’s space, and you start to get clues about the life behind the boxes without the game needing to stop and explain everything. I’d be curious what people notice first, because I would probably be looking at the bookshelves.

It’s also not a game that keeps you busy every second. Some objects make you stop and think about where they belong, and if you’re trying to finish each room quickly, you can turn a calming puzzle into a small argument with a bedside table. I don’t know that rushing would make it more fun. There’s no reason to race the boxes.

That slower pace is a real part of the appeal, but it does mean this isn’t the kind of game that will pull your attention away from a hard workout. I’d expect to make a few placements, look at the room, and then stand there considering whether I’ve made a home or a very organized storage unit. It’s pleasant, just not busy.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The game waits while you decide where things go, which is exactly what you want when your hands are moving along with your legs. Full controller support means you don’t need to reach for a keyboard, and the gentle pace should be forgiving if your thumb lands a little off from where you meant. That makes the basic setup easy.

On a treadmill, I’d keep the speed comfortable enough that looking down at the controller doesn’t turn into a balance exercise, and I’d treat each room as a little stretch of play rather than something to rush through. You can unpack for a bit, think about the next object, and keep walking; I don’t think this asks for the sort of quick response that would have you stepping off the belt to recover your dignity.

A bike would work too, especially if you like being able to sit still while you look over a room and decide what belongs where. I wouldn’t call it a reason to choose the bike over the treadmill, though. The game isn’t asking you to aim or react fast, so you can pick whichever one you were going to use anyway.

The one thing I’d skip is trying to make this a hard interval workout. The game’s slow, thoughtful rhythm and a sprint interval don’t really help each other, and you’d probably spend the rest period deciding where to put a box of books. That’s a very manageable problem.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This should run on a pretty old computer, and the small amount of storage it needs is another sign that you don’t need a gaming tower to sort out a room. I’d check that your graphics can handle the game’s basic requirements, but this isn’t asking for a recent high end card.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Unpacking is a calm, thoughtful choice for an easy workout, especially if you enjoy making small decisions without a timer pushing you along. I’d suggest it to someone who wants a gentle game to keep nearby while walking or cycling, but not to someone looking for constant action.
