A fly trying to climb into the sky is a pretty strange thing to have on screen while you jog, and I think The Plan works better as a short moving break than as the game you build a whole workout around. It has a quiet, odd little idea, and then it’s over.

## Gameplay

The basic premise is enough to make me curious. You guide a fly upward while the game asks you to sit with how pointless its brief existence might be, which is a lot of weight to put on a bug that probably just wants to keep going. I like that it doesn’t sound like it needs a pile of systems or a long explanation to get that across.

I’d expect this to be more of a small mood than a steady stream of things to react to, and that’s both what I like about it and why it won’t suit every workout. A game that lets your mind wander can be nice on a gentle walk, but if you’re trying to stay engaged through a long run, you may find yourself thinking about literally anything else. There’s a limit to how much a fly can carry.

The shortness sounds like a strength here. I don’t need every game to turn into a forty hour commitment, and a brief, focused idea can be a much better use of your time than something that keeps adding errands after you’ve got the point. I’d rather have a small game that knows what it is than a big one that makes me collect twelve feathers for reasons.

I also think the premise could land a little differently depending on your mood. If you’re in the mood for something strange and reflective, the fly’s climb has a nice simplicity to it. If you want a game that keeps your attention busy while your legs do their thing, this may feel like staring at a very determined insect and waiting for the next thought to arrive. I don’t know, that might be exactly what somebody needs after a noisy day.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

This seems better suited to a relaxed pace than a hard effort, because I wouldn’t want a game asking me to think about the meaning of existence while I’m also trying to keep my feet in the right place at speed. The premise sounds low pressure, though, and I like games that don’t make every missed input feel like I’ve ruined the evening.

I can’t confirm native controller support, so I’d check that before setting up a treadmill or bike session around it. If your setup lets you control the game comfortably, I’d give it a try on an easy walk or ride, where a little less focus from the game is a feature and not a problem. I would not make it the plan for a hard workout. The fly can take its time.

The page says it’s optimal with headphones, and I’d follow that suggestion if you can do so safely with your exercise setup. The quiet mood seems like a big part of the appeal, and playing with sound could help you settle into it instead of treating it like a tiny task you have to finish. I wouldn’t want to be adjusting audio or reaching for settings once I was moving, though, so I’d set things up first.

There isn’t much here that sounds like it would demand quick reactions, which is nice, but that doesn’t automatically make it a perfect workout companion. The bigger question for me is whether you’re comfortable with the controls you have and whether you want a short, reflective game during exercise at all. Some days that’s appealing. Some days I’d rather have something that asks a little more of me.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very modest, and integrated graphics are enough, so this should run on an older or basic computer rather than asking you to buy a gaming machine. I’d check that your system can run the game and that you have a comfortable way to control it before you start sweating.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

The Plan sounds like a thoughtful little detour, not a workout companion with enough going on to fill a long session. I’d suggest it to you if you want a brief, unusual game and you can confirm your controls work, but I’d pick something else to carry a full run.
