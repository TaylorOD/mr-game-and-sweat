Turn based martial arts combat is a much better treadmill companion than a game that expects you to react before you’ve even found the right button. I’d start with the turn based option and let the pugilistic world take its time.

## Gameplay

Wandering Sword has a good setup for an RPG, with a young swordsman caught in a feud, nearly killed, and sent off to pursue martial arts and become a hero. I like that the story gives you a reason to keep moving through the world, though I’d expect plenty of conversations and reading along the way. That’s a tradeoff, not a dealbreaker.

The combat lets you choose between turn based and real time play, and I’d pick turn based even when I’m not exercising. You get a moment to think about your moves instead of having to keep up with a fight at the exact pace it’s happening. That helps the game feel more like a martial arts adventure and less like I’m trying to press the right button while my legs are doing something else.

I think the martial arts progression is where I’d get pulled in, since getting stronger is a natural reason to explore and take on more of the world. But RPGs can turn into a lot of menus and choices between the parts where you’re actually doing something, and I’d expect that here too. I don’t know how much patience I’d have for checking every option on a treadmill, so I’d keep the build simple and get back to exploring.

The feuding factions and heroic destination give the story a familiar shape, which can be nice when you’re trying to settle into a workout. I’d rather have a clear reason to go somewhere than spend the whole session wondering what the game wants from me. Still, if a scene gets talky, that’s a good time to slow down or pause instead of pretending I’m absorbing every line while jogging.

There’s a lot here that sounds appealing if you want an RPG to occupy your attention for a while, and it isn’t built around a constant stream of fast action. I don’t know whether the story will keep me interested through every slower stretch, but the option to play combat at my own pace counts for a lot. It’s a long sort of adventure.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d use the turn based combat mode and keep the real time option for when I’m sitting down. Turn based fights let you choose an action without racing the game, which is useful when your hands are moving a little more than you’d like. I can imagine spending one turn looking at the options and another remembering which one I meant to pick.

Wandering Sword supports a controller, and that makes it much easier to settle in than a game that expects you to reach for a keyboard and mouse. Even so, an RPG has menus, dialogue, and a lot of decisions, so I’d treat this as a steady workout rather than something to play when I’m pushing hard. You can take your time. The game can wait.

On a bike, I’d expect the menus and deliberate fights to be manageable, especially if you’re riding at an easy pace. On a treadmill, I’d keep the speed comfortable and avoid trying to read every bit of dialogue while bouncing along. The game isn’t asking for quick aim, but making a careful choice while your screen is moving is still a small chore.

I’d skip the real time combat while exercising, and I’d pause if I needed to read something important instead of turning every conversation into a moving reading test. The nice part is that you can make the game fit the workout instead of having to match its pace. That’s a practical setup.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You’ll need a dedicated graphics card, since integrated graphics aren’t supported, but the minimum points to an older entry level gaming card rather than a recent powerhouse. I’d check the graphics card first, because the game asks for more than a computer that only handles everyday tasks.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Wandering Sword makes sense for a steady treadmill or bike session if you enjoy story driven RPGs and don’t mind pausing to read or think. I’d suggest it to someone who wants a martial arts adventure at their own pace, not someone looking for constant action.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
