A motorcycle race sounds like a nice way to make a treadmill session feel less like staring at the wall, but RIDE 3 asks for more thumb control than I’d want during a hard run. I’d keep the pace comfortable and let the bikes do the exciting part.

## Gameplay

The racing is the reason to play this, and I think it should keep you focused once you’re out on a track. You’re choosing when to brake, how much speed to carry into a corner, and where to pass, which gives your attention something concrete to do besides counting down the minutes. On a long straight, you get a little room to breathe. Then the next corner shows up.

I like that the bikes aren’t just different paint jobs on the same ride. Picking one you enjoy and working through the game’s events gives you a reason to try different kinds of racing, and the game has enough bikes and tracks to keep that from feeling like one race repeated forever. I can see myself spending too long deciding which bike looks right, though. The livery editor is a very generous place to lose workout time without moving much.

The career is organized around race events, so you can get back to the part where you’re actually riding without sitting through a long story. That works well for exercise. I don’t need a dramatic scene to explain why I’m going around a corner at speed, and I’m happy to skip past the menus when I’ve picked my next event.

There is still a learning curve in the handling. A motorcycle leans and turns differently from a car, and slowing down before a corner matters more than it does in a game where you can just aim at the inside and hope. I think that’s fun when you have some attention to spare, but it can turn an easy run into a little motorcycle riding lesson. I’ve had enough of those in school.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The controller support is full, which gets you started without awkward keyboard controls, but the races still ask you for precise inputs. You’re balancing throttle, braking, and steering while the road bends, and a small overcorrection can send you wide. I’d expect that to be manageable on a steady bike ride and more distracting on a treadmill, where your hands are moving around while you’re trying to keep your feet in a rhythm.

I’d lower the difficulty and use the riding aids rather than treating the settings like a test of character. The point here is to keep moving, not to prove you can take every corner with the assists switched off. Automatic transmission would also let you put your attention on steering and braking instead of adding gear changes to the list. I don’t know that I’d want to chase perfect lap times while running, but you can still enjoy the race without doing that.

There are stretches where the track gives you space to settle in, and then a tight turn arrives and asks for a quick decision. That changing pace is more interesting than a game that keeps you pressing one button for twenty minutes, but it does mean I wouldn’t pair it with an interval where you’re already pushing hard. If your workout gets intense, the bike in the game may get less graceful. It happens.

The menus and bike customization are better for a warmup or a slower part of your session than for the middle of a race. I’d pick the next event before increasing your pace, then leave the livery editor alone until you’re cooling down. It’s easy to tell yourself you’re just changing one thing and then spend a while adjusting a color nobody else is going to see.

I think this can work on either machine, but I’d be more relaxed playing it on a stationary bike because the sharper corners need some attention. On a treadmill I’d choose an easier setting, keep the pace steady, and treat finishing the race as the goal. There’s no need to win every corner.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You’ll want a dedicated graphics card around the level of a GTX 1050 or Radeon HD 7950, so this is more than an integrated graphics job. I’d check the graphics card first, because the bike can wait while your computer tries to catch up.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

RIDE 3 makes sense for you if you like motorcycle racing and want a game that keeps your attention between corners, especially on a bike workout. For a treadmill session, I’d play on an easier setting and keep the pace reasonable, since the precise steering can pull your focus away from running.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
