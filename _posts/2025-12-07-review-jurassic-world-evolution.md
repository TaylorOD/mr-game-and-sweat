Building a dinosaur park sounds like a nice way to keep a workout moving, until a storm knocks out the fences and you’re trying to decide which problem needs attention first. I think Jurassic World Evolution is good for steady exercise, as long as you’re happy to manage a park instead of chasing dinosaurs around yourself.

## Gameplay

The fun is in making a park that looks like it might actually work, then watching your neat plans get complicated by animals that have opinions about their habitat. You build enclosures, hatch dinosaurs, keep an eye on what they need, and try to make the park earn enough to pay for the next round of decisions. I like the push and pull between making a place for visitors and making sure the dinosaurs aren’t about to break out. A zoo job with a much larger fence budget.

There’s a lot of pausing to think through where things should go, and then a lot of looking at alerts when the park starts objecting to your choices. A dinosaur that’s uncomfortable can turn a quiet stretch into a job for the ranger team, and a storm can damage structures just when everything looked settled. I think that’s a good source of tension, because it comes from managing the park rather than needing fast hands. The bad news is that sometimes your big plan is putting the fence back where it was.

The campaign gives you missions and contracts that send you between park management tasks, which helps when I’d otherwise spend too long deciding what to build next. But there’s also enough reading menus and checking information that the pace can feel stop and start. You might be thinking about a new enclosure, then spend a while sorting out what the park needs before you can get back to it. I don’t know how much of that would feel relaxing on every workout, but it does give you things to do besides stare at a moving belt.

Dinosaurs are the reason I’d keep coming back. Seeing a new one arrive is exciting, and watching it move around its enclosure makes the work feel connected to something, rather than just placing another building because a number went down. I also enjoy that you’re balancing what guests want with what the animals need, even when those goals are not especially compatible. Sometimes the dinosaurs are the part of the park with the clearest boundaries.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

This is much easier to manage while moving than a game that needs constant aiming or quick reactions, because a lot of the time you’re making choices through menus and watching the park respond. Full controller support is a big help here, and I’d expect the controller to be comfortable for the basic management. You can take a moment to read what’s going on instead of needing to react to every little change right away.

I’d play the campaign at a comfortable pace and pause whenever I needed to sort out a decision, rather than trying to build a perfect park while jogging. The storms and breakouts can still make things hectic, especially if several problems arrive together, but they’re not the whole game. If you’re on a bike, that slower planning rhythm seems like a natural fit. On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace easy enough that I can look at the menus without wobbling around like a dinosaur learning a new enclosure.

The bit I’d skip during a harder workout is trying to fix every issue the second it appears. I’d let the park wait while I get my footing, then deal with the urgent stuff when I’m ready to focus. I don’t know if I’d want to handle a big park during a fast run, because I’d probably start clicking through decisions just to get back to watching the dinosaurs. That’s not really the game’s fault. It’s just a lot of small choices.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You’ll want a computer with a dedicated graphics card, though the listed minimum is within reach for an older gaming setup and doesn’t call for a top end machine. I’d check the graphics card first, since the minimum asks for an older GeForce or Radeon class card rather than integrated graphics. The dinosaurs still need somewhere to live.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Jurassic World Evolution is for you if managing a park sounds like a pleasant way to keep a workout moving, with dinosaurs as the payoff for all the planning. I’d skip it if you want nonstop action or don’t want to stop and think between bursts of activity.
