A mining game that keeps sending a drill deeper sounds like a pretty reasonable treadmill companion, because I’d rather make small upgrade decisions than steer through traffic while my feet are doing their own thing. Mr.Mine probably won’t make the workout disappear, but it might give you something to check between stretches of steady pedaling.

## Gameplay

The slow, gradual progress is what I like about the idea, and also what could wear thin. You’re digging down, improving the drill, and looking for gems and relics, so there’s a clear little reason to keep going, but I don’t think every stretch of mining will feel like a new discovery. Sometimes the big event is that a number got bigger. That counts for something.

Upgrades are the part I’d pay attention to. Hundreds of them sounds like a lot, and I think there’s fun in deciding whether to spend what you’ve earned now or save up for a better improvement, but I also know how easily I can turn a game like this into checking menus every few minutes. You can make a tiny decision, go back to your workout, and come back to another tiny decision. That’s a decent rhythm if you like gradual progress. It is not much of a rhythm if you want the game to keep surprising you.

The treasures and relics give the digging a bit more character than just watching a drill go down, and discovering something unusual seems like the sort of thing that could make a slow run feel less empty. I don’t know how much the relics change the way you play, so I wouldn’t buy it expecting every find to turn the whole game around. The appeal, to me, is building up your mining operation bit by bit and seeing what turns up.

There’s also an odd mismatch in the pitch that I kind of appreciate. The game calls itself an idle mining game, but it gives you upgrades and things to uncover, so you’re not only watching a progress bar and hoping it develops a personality. Still, the waiting is part of the deal. If you need constant action to stay interested, this could feel like standing beside a shovel while someone else uses it.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

For a steady workout, I think the pace is a better fit than a game that asks you to aim quickly or react the instant something moves. You can make a decision about the drill, let the mining continue, and settle back into your stride instead of trying to make a precise turn while your hands bounce around. I’d give it a try on an easy pace first, because even a game with simple decisions can get fiddly when the screen is moving a little.

The part I’d be cautious about is how you interact with it. I can’t confirm full controller support from the information available here, and the mining and upgrade setup sounds like it may be more comfortable with a mouse than a controller. I wouldn’t assume you can sit back with a gamepad and have everything fall neatly into place. That uncertainty matters more on a treadmill, where reaching for a mouse or trackpad while walking can become its own little coordination exercise.

On a bike, I’d expect the pauses between decisions to be easier to live with, since your hands can stay steadier while you’re pedaling. But I still wouldn’t want to spend the whole workout hunting through menus, so I’d make a few upgrades when they’re convenient and then let the mine run while I keep moving. If you’re the sort of person who likes checking a game every so often, that makes sense. If you want to be pressing buttons constantly, this may feel like a long wait with a screen attached.

I’d skip trying to play it at a pace where you’re already struggling to stay balanced or focused. The game sounds forgiving about timing, but the controls still need to be reachable, and I wouldn’t want the workout to turn into a repeated negotiation with my keyboard. That’s not a problem with mining. It’s just me being bad at doing two fiddly things at once.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimums are extremely modest, so I’d expect an old computer with integrated graphics to be enough for this. I’d check that the machine can run a supported version of Windows and has a little storage free, then stop worrying about the hardware. The drill probably isn’t asking your computer to break a sweat.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Mr.Mine sounds best for you if you enjoy slow progress, small upgrade choices, and the chance of uncovering gems or relics while you keep moving. I’d skip it if you need nonstop action, and I’d check the controls before planning a treadmill session around it. For a relaxed workout with a little mining to look in on, I think it’s worth a try.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
