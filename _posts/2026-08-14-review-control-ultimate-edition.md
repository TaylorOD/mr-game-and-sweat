Telekinesis makes CONTROL a much better workout game than all the time you spend looking for the next door. I’d play it at a steady walk or on a bike, because Jesse can throw a filing cabinet across a room, but aiming that throw while jogging is a different job.

## Gameplay

The Oldest House has a great setup for keeping your attention, since the rooms keep changing shape and the Hiss are usually interrupting whatever you were trying to figure out. I think the fights do a good job of pulling you back in after a slower stretch of exploring, and throwing whatever’s nearby at an enemy feels much better than standing still and trading shots. There’s always another thing to aim at. Sometimes several.

That frantic pace is also why I wouldn’t call this a game to play on autopilot. You’re moving Jesse, aiming, dodging and deciding whether to use the Service Weapon or fling a desk at someone, and when enemies come from different directions you can’t really look away to check your pace. I’d be paying attention to the screen more than my legs in those rooms, which is not ideal if you’re trying to settle into an easy workout.

Between fights, there’s a lot of wandering through the Bureau and reading documents that explain the strange stuff going on. I like the odd details, but I don’t need every scrap of paper to understand what’s happening, and I’d skip most of the optional reading on a treadmill. Some of it is fun in a weird government office way, but stopping every few minutes to read another note makes the workout feel like I brought a very unusual filing cabinet to the gym.

The story and side missions give you reasons to explore, and the setting is strange enough that I’d want to see what’s around the next corner. A few sections slow down while you work out where to go or how to handle a particular fight, though, so this isn’t as steady as a game where you’re always moving forward. I don’t know that the story would hold my attention through every document, but the combat would get me back to it.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is a big help here, and the controls can be remapped, but the game still asks for accurate aiming and quick reactions. On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace at a walk and save the more demanding fights for when I’m feeling steady, because missing a throw is one thing and trying to correct it while your hands are bouncing is another. You can pause, which matters. You don’t have to turn a workout into a boss fight with your own balance.

A bike seems like the easier place to play, mostly because you can keep your upper body still while you aim. I’d use a comfortable pace rather than trying to sprint through every encounter, and I’d expect some fights to take longer when I’m not making precise shots. That’s okay. The Hiss can wait while I try to get my thumb to point at the right person.

I’d also look at the Assist Mode options before starting, especially the aiming and damage settings, and adjust them until combat feels manageable while moving. There’s no shame in making a game fit the workout you’re actually doing, and I’d rather keep pedaling than restart a fight because I tried to play like I was sitting perfectly still. For a run, I’d save the toughest encounters for a slower pace or play those sections off the treadmill. The game is a little demanding on the hands.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum graphics card is an older dedicated card, so you don’t need a new high end gaming computer, but integrated graphics probably aren’t what you want here. I’d check your graphics card first, since the shifting rooms and effects are a lot to ask of a very basic machine.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

CONTROL Ultimate Edition is for you if you want action that keeps your mind busy and you’re willing to slow down for the fights that need better aim. I’d play it on a bike or at a walking pace, use the assist options, and leave the careful combat for when I’m not bouncing around.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
