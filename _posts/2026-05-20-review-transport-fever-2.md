I think Transport Fever 2 could make a treadmill session disappear, but only if you’re happy to spend some of it staring at a map and deciding whether a train line is worth all the track it needs. I’d start with a campaign mission instead of an open ended map, because having a goal gives all that planning somewhere to go.

## Gameplay

The fun here is getting a transport network to make sense, and I like that you can connect places by land, water, and air instead of solving the same train problem over and over. A route that looks obvious on the map can turn into a whole chain of choices about where people need to go and what goods need to move, so you’re usually thinking ahead rather than reacting every few seconds. That’s nice for a workout, though you may find yourself jogging slowly while deciding where a station belongs.

Campaign missions give you a reason to keep building, which I think helps a lot. An open ended map can be relaxing, but without a goal I’d probably spend ten minutes choosing a route and then wonder what I was trying to accomplish. I know myself well enough to know that a map with no deadline can become an excuse to fiddle with the map.

The passenger and cargo networks are the part I’d be most interested in. You’re trying to get connections working well enough that towns and industries can use them, and there’s a good feeling in the idea of watching a distant place become part of a network you’ve been slowly piecing together. I don’t know that every route decision will feel important, but when a line has a clear job, it sounds like the kind of small problem I can happily turn over in my head.

The slow pace is also the thing that keeps me from calling this an easy recommendation for every workout. A lot of the game is planning, checking how your setup is working, and adjusting it, so it isn’t going to keep your attention the way a racing game or something with constant action might. You can get absorbed in it, but sometimes the most exciting development is that a new route seems like a better idea than the old route.

I’d choose a campaign mission when I want some direction and save the open ended game for a workout where I’m fine making my own goals. I don’t know, I think the appeal is less “look at this huge transport empire” and more “I have made the bus and train situation slightly less confusing.” That’s enough for me.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

This is a planning game, so your legs don’t have to match what your hands are doing. Full controller support is a big plus, and the game isn’t asking you to aim at a moving target or hit a perfect button at just the right moment. I’d expect it to work best on an easy treadmill pace or a steady bike ride, where you can keep your attention on the map without feeling like you need to rush.

I’d use the pause and speed controls whenever I was building or changing a route. Pausing gives you room to think through the layout, and then you can let the simulation run while you watch how your choices are working out. It’s not exciting in the same way as landing a difficult jump, but it’s also less likely to make a slightly wobbly step turn into a train line in a place you didn’t mean to put one.

Controller support doesn’t automatically make every menu pleasant, and I’d expect setting up routes and checking details to take more focus than just moving around a game world. If you’re doing a faster run, I’d keep the planning simple and stick with a mission goal you already understand rather than trying to redesign the whole network while bouncing along. I can be talked into complicated projects, and then I forget what I was doing.

A bike may feel easier for the stretches where you’re taking your time over a route, but I don’t think a treadmill is a bad fit. You can let the game wait while you get settled, and the slower moments are part of the game rather than a loading screen you’re trapped in. I’d skip any urge to turn every workout into a perfect network. There will still be another route to fix.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum graphics card is old enough that you don’t need a recent gaming computer just to get started, though I’d check that your machine meets the listed graphics requirements before buying. The game still has a lot to show on screen as your network grows, so the graphics card is the part I’d look at first. Your computer does not need to be a train station.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Save Transport Fever 2 for a steady workout where you’d enjoy planning more than reacting. I’d point you toward it if building routes and watching a network take shape sounds relaxing, but I’d pick something else if you need constant action to keep your legs moving.
