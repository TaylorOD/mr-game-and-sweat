I’d save War Robots for a gentle treadmill walk, not a run, because a six on six robot fight is going to ask for your eyes and thumbs at the same time. On a bike you can keep your legs moving, but I think the aiming will still pull most of your attention upstairs.

## Gameplay

The basic idea sounds good to me because it gets to the part I want from an action game pretty quickly: you’re in a team fight, there are other players to deal with, and your robot and weapons give you something to think about besides just pointing at the nearest target. The robot theme gives the shooter a little personality without asking me to care about a long story first. I’d rather spend the time fighting than reading about why the fight matters.

The choice of robot and weapons seems like it could keep the matches from feeling exactly the same, especially with hundreds of weapon combinations listed in the description. That’s a lot of options, though, and I don’t know how much of that is fun decision making versus staring at a setup screen trying to guess what will work. I can lose a decent chunk of a workout to menu indecision without much effort. I’m very capable of doing that.

Six players on each side is a nice size for this kind of game, since there should be enough happening to keep you involved without every single fight becoming a giant blur. I think that’s the appeal here. You’re not just wandering through a quiet map waiting for the action to show up, and you don’t have a story scene stopping the match so everyone can discuss a robot’s feelings. That part sounds pretty easy to get into.

The online PvP setup does make the action feel like it could get intense quickly, and that’s both the fun and the part I’d watch. Against other people, the next few seconds can matter more than whatever plan you thought you had when you started moving, so the game should keep you engaged. I also know myself well enough to think I’d sometimes chase the fight even when that wasn’t the smart move. My robot and I would have the same plan, which is encouraging.

Clan battles are an appealing idea if you want the game to feel like more than a pile of separate matches, but I’d only bother with that side if I actually wanted to make it part of my gaming routine. Other people depending on you can make a multiplayer game feel more lively, and it can also turn a quick session into something you think about after you’ve put the controller down. I don’t know how much commitment the clan side asks for, so I’d treat it as a bonus until I knew I wanted more.

I’d expect the weapon and robot choices to be the part that keeps me curious, while the repeated team fights are what would decide whether I stick around. That’s a fair trade for a free to play action game, because I can give the central idea a try before deciding it fits me. If the matches keep me busy and the options feel like useful choices instead of a second job, that sounds pretty good. It is still a shooter, though.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is a big plus, and I wouldn’t want to try managing a shooter like this with controls that make me reach for a keyboard. But controller support only gets you partway there, because War Robots is still an online fight where aiming and reacting to other players matter. You can’t count on the action politely waiting while your hands find their rhythm.

I’d start with a slow walk and see whether I could keep my attention on both things, rather than trying to run at a pace where every step makes the controller less steady. The fights sound like they’ll ask for quick adjustments, and a thumb that’s bouncing around is not a great tool for careful aiming. I’m not saying you need to sit completely still, but I would give myself an easy pace to work with.

A stationary bike sounds a little more forgiving if your upper body stays steady while you pedal, though I’d still expect the game to pull my focus toward the match. You’re responding to other players, not following a slow moving story, so I wouldn’t assume I could zone out and keep up. My legs can do their part while my brain acts like the whole team meeting is happening in my hands.

I’d skip the harder workout pace, and I’d also skip clan play while I was exercising if I knew other people were counting on me to be sharp. That kind of pressure can make you forget you’re supposed to be working out, and then your exercise plan is just you getting mad at a robot with a controller. Not my ideal use of a treadmill.

I don’t know how forgiving the aiming feels in practice, so I’d give it a careful try at a pace where I can stop or slow down without fuss. If I’m spending the whole time fighting my own movement, I’d save War Robots for when I’m sitting down. The full controller support makes it possible to try. The live PvP makes it a less relaxed workout game.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements look modest, and an older PC with a graphics card that supports DirectX 11 and has the required video memory should be enough to consider it. I’d check the graphics card first, since that’s more likely to be the part that makes an older computer unsuitable than the small memory requirement. It doesn’t read like a game asking you to buy a new computer just to watch robots shoot at each other.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

War Robots sounds like a fun fit for a slow walk if you enjoy competitive shooters and want to stay busy, but I wouldn’t choose it for a run or a workout where quick reactions are already hard to manage. The controller support and free to play setup make it easy to try, and the live matches are why I’d keep the pace gentle. If you want a game that lets you pay half attention, I’d look elsewhere.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
