I’d put Kingdoms and Castles on an easy treadmill walk, because deciding whether your little kingdom needs more farms or another wall gives your brain something to do without asking your hands to keep up. I wouldn’t start it on a hard run, though, unless you’re comfortable placing a castle while your whole body is making a small earthquake.

## Gameplay

The slow, deliberate pace is a feature here, and also the reason I wouldn’t call it a great game for every workout. You’re growing a hamlet into a city, and every choice pulls on another one: more villagers need more food, and bigger plans need more resources, so it’s easy to get absorbed in making the town work. I like that your people and resources are simulated individually, because it makes the place feel like something you’re managing rather than a pretty map you’re decorating. It also means the quiet parts ask you to stop and think. There are a lot of those.

Building a satisfying little kingdom sounds relaxing, and it often seems like it would be, but then you’re also thinking about protection, trade, and what might happen when the neighboring kingdoms decide they want to be less neighborly. I think that tension gives the planning a point beyond making tidy rows of buildings, which matters to me. I don’t know why I’m so willing to spend a workout worrying about whether a fictional village has enough food, but apparently it’s not hard to get invested.

Trade agreements and alliances give you ways to deal with the other AI kingdoms, and you can also go to war with them. I’d probably enjoy having those options, because being able to choose between trying to get along and picking a fight makes your kingdom feel like it has a direction. But I’d expect diplomacy and planning to be the parts that hold your attention, not fast action, and if you’re looking for a game that keeps your hands busy every second, this isn’t it. Sometimes your big strategic contribution is waiting to see how the plan works out.

Viking attacks and dragons can turn a peaceful building session into a scramble to protect what you’ve made. That sounds like a good change of pace, and I’d be more interested in responding to a threat than staring at the next empty patch of land wondering what should go there. Still, this isn’t a game where every moment is an emergency, and I think the gaps between dramatic problems are part of its appeal. You get time to think. You also get time to think about thinking.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A walking pace seems like the right place to start, since the game gives you room to make choices without making every choice urgent. I’d use the controller and keep the workout easy enough that I could look at the town and place buildings carefully, rather than trying to do that while bouncing along at a pace that makes the controller feel slippery. Full controller support is a big help here, though I’d still expect a city builder to ask for a bit more deliberate input than a game where you only need to steer and press one button.

On a bike, I’d expect the slower stretches to be even easier to manage, since you can keep your hands settled and take a moment to plan a bigger building project. The threat of Vikings or a dragon could pull your focus toward the screen, so I’d keep the effort moderate when the kingdom is in trouble and pause if I needed to sort out what was happening. There’s no shame in stopping the game for a minute. Your villagers can wait.

I’d probably skip trying to make every decision while moving quickly, because setting up a town is a lot of small choices, and some of them are easier when you can focus on the screen. You can let the game’s pace breathe instead of treating every quiet minute like a reason to increase the treadmill speed. I think that makes this a comfortable fit for a steady workout, even if it’s not the sort of game that turns a hard interval into a thrilling little story.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements include Intel HD Graphics 4600, so this doesn’t ask you to bring a new gaming computer just to build a small kingdom. I’d check that your machine has a compatible graphics setup and enough memory first, but the listed graphics requirement is pretty modest. Your computer probably has more demanding responsibilities than making sure the villagers get dinner.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is a calm strategy game for you if you like planning a town, tending to its needs, and having the occasional invasion interrupt your day. I’d play it on a walk or an easy bike ride, and I’d pass if I wanted constant action or something that needs little attention.

### Overall Score: 2.67 out of 3
