A naval fight sounds like a pretty good treadmill distraction, but Maelstrom can also make you forget you were trying to keep a steady pace. I’d save it for an easy workout, not one where you’re trying to hit a particular number.

## Gameplay

The ship combat has enough going on that I think it can keep your attention without asking you to read a bunch of story text between fights. You’re trying to steer into a useful position and bring your weapons to bear, and that makes the open water feel less like a backdrop and more like a place you’re constantly making small decisions. I like having to think about where the ship is pointed, even though I can imagine getting turned around and spending a little too long looking for a clean shot.

The sea monsters are a good fit for this kind of game, because another warship is one thing and a huge creature sharing the water gives the whole fight a different feel. I don’t know how much they change from battle to battle, so I wouldn’t buy it expecting a deep monster hunting game, but they give the naval combat a fun extra complication. Sometimes the ocean needs another problem.

There’s also fleet customization, which is cool if you like adjusting your approach before you head back into a fight. I’d be interested in trying different ships and seeing what suits me, but I can see that part taking you out of the action if you get stuck comparing choices while your treadmill keeps rolling. I’d keep the setup simple at first and spend more time learning how the fighting feels.

Since it’s multiplayer, the other ships aren’t going to wait politely while you finish thinking through a decision. That makes the fights more lively, and it also means this isn’t a game I’d pick when I’m already tired and barely paying attention. I think the mix of action and strategy works, but it asks you to stay involved. You can’t just drift through it.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The main thing I’d expect is that your hands and your attention both have to stay with the fight, especially when you’re trying to line up against another ship. That’s manageable at a relaxed walking pace, and maybe on a bike where your upper body is steadier, but I wouldn’t expect to run hard and make careful choices at the same time. I can barely decide what to eat when I’m tired, so commanding a fleet might be ambitious.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information I have, so I wouldn’t assume that part will work without checking before you set everything up. If it does, I’d give it a try while seated first and see whether steering and aiming feel manageable without a mouse. The difference matters here, because a naval fight is less forgiving when your hands are bouncing around than when you’re sitting still.

I’d avoid treating the multiplayer battles like background entertainment. You can’t assume a match will pause because you need to adjust your pace, and reacting to another player while steering your ship sounds like a lot when you’re moving quickly. Maybe you’ll be fine at an easy pace, but I’d start slow and see whether you can keep your attention on both the screen and the workout. No need to prove anything to a boat.

A bike might be a little easier if you’re sitting upright and can keep your hands steady, but I don’t think that makes the game effortless. You still have to keep track of the fight and make decisions on the fly, and that gets harder when the workout gets harder. I’d skip it for interval training, where you’re changing effort often and the game is asking you to react at the same time.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older dedicated graphics card rather than integrated graphics, but they don’t call for a new high end gaming computer. I’d check the graphics card first, since that’s the part most likely to decide whether an older machine can handle the sea battles comfortably.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Maelstrom makes sense for someone who wants competitive ship fights and can keep the workout easy while paying attention. I’d give it a try if you’re comfortable checking the controls first, but I’d skip it for a hard run or any workout where you need the game to wait for you.

### Overall Score: 1.67 out of 3
