I think DiRT Rally 2.0 is one of those games that can make a steady workout feel like a series of small emergencies, because the road keeps changing and a little steering correction can decide whether you stay on it. That’s fun, but I’d start on a bike or keep the treadmill pace easy until the driving stops taking all your attention.

## Gameplay

The rally stages are the reason I’d keep coming back. You’re racing against the clock through narrow roads, and the navigator’s calls give you just enough warning to prepare for the next turn, which means I’d be listening and looking ahead instead of thinking about how many minutes are left in the workout. It’s a nice change from a racing game where you’re mostly staring at the car in front of you.

I like that a stage can feel tense without needing a story to explain why. The road can get rough, the weather can make things harder to read, and there’s a real difference between taking a turn cleanly and arriving at it with too much speed and hoping the car sorts it out. I’d probably make a few bad calls anyway. The game gives you plenty of road to do that on.

There are other modes, including rallycross, where you’re racing other cars on a track rather than chasing a time through a stage. That sounds like a nice change of pace, but I think the single car rally stages suit me better because I can focus on the road without also worrying about somebody trying to pass. I already have enough going on with the trees.

The career side has more menus and decisions than I want in the middle of a workout, especially when you’re moving between events and dealing with the condition of your car. I wouldn’t call it a deal breaker, because you can handle that between stages, but I’d pick an event before getting on the treadmill and avoid turning every pause into a little management meeting. I don’t need another reason to stand still.

There’s a lot to learn about driving well here, and I think that’s appealing if you like slowly getting better at something. I don’t know how quickly the cars will start to feel predictable, though, and I’d expect the early stages to ask for more concentration than a casual arcade racer. That’s a good trade if you want the game to hold your attention.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The car is moving quickly, the turns arrive fast, and your steering inputs need to be deliberate, so I wouldn’t treat this as a game to pair with a hard treadmill run. It has full controller support, which helps, but being able to hold a controller isn’t the same as being able to drive a precise line while your hands are bouncing around. I’d keep the belt slow.

A bike seems more manageable because your upper body can stay steadier while you work the controller, although I’d still start at an easy effort. You’re making constant small corrections, listening to pace notes, and trying not to send the car wide, so this isn’t the sort of game where you can look away for a bit and expect everything to be fine. The ditch is always available.

I’d use the difficulty and driving assists to make the first events less punishing, then adjust once I know how much attention I can give it while exercising. I’d also pick a single player rally event and pause between stages if I needed to settle down or deal with the car. A long rally session can be split into smaller pieces, which fits a workout better than trying to get through everything in one go.

Rallycross is the mode I’d skip while running. The close racing and quick reactions sound like a lot when you’re also trying to keep a steady stride, and a mistake can make an already busy race feel much busier. I might try it on a bike once I’m comfortable, but I wouldn’t make it the starting point.

I don’t know why I’m more comfortable with one car on a narrow road than several cars beside me, but I am. Maybe it’s because the road is already giving me enough to pay attention to. For a treadmill, this is a slow pace and short stages kind of game.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimums point to an older gaming computer rather than a machine that needs a new, powerful graphics card, so I’d check the graphics card and processor before worrying about anything fancy. The 100 GB storage requirement is the part I’d notice first, since that’s a lot of room for a game you might be squeezing in beside several others.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

DiRT Rally 2.0 makes sense for you if you want a focused racing game and you’re willing to keep your workout easy enough to pay attention. I’d try the rally stages first, use the assists, and save the busier racing for when you’re not moving.
