Take Lonely Mountains: Downhill on a steady workout, not a sprint, because I think the narrow trails will keep your attention without making every second feel like a crisis. You’re riding downhill, trying to stay upright, and sometimes that’s plenty to think about.

## Gameplay

I like that the game gets to the riding quickly. You’re picking your way down a mountain through forests, narrow trails and wild rivers, and there isn’t a long story or a pile of dialogue asking you to sit still before you get moving. That’s useful when you’re already on a treadmill and would rather not stand there listening to somebody explain a map.

The appeal, for me, is the little negotiation between going fast and making it around the next turn. You can race and jump, but a narrow trail makes me think I’d ease off more than I wanted to, and then immediately wish I’d kept some speed. It has a nice way of making a small bit of trail feel like a decision. Not a very big decision, which is probably for the best while exercising.

Crashing is part of the deal, and I think that’s what gives the descents their tension. You can try to shave time off a run, but one bad turn can send you back to a checkpoint, so I’d expect the game to keep pulling my attention back to the screen. There’s a little bit of trial and error in that, and I’m not sure I’d enjoy repeating the same stretch when I was already tired, but checkpoints keep a mistake from becoming a whole mountain sized problem.

I don’t know that I’d want to treat every run like a race against the clock. The game seems more appealing when I’m learning a route and trying to get down it without turning my bike into a very small pile of parts. If you like improving a time, there’s a reason to keep going, but I’d probably let myself take the safer line when I’m working out. I have enough trouble coordinating my feet without adding a personal grudge against a tree.

There’s a good amount of focus here, but not much waiting around. I’d give the gameplay a 3 because the actual riding is the point, and it keeps asking you to make small choices instead of pausing for long stretches. It’s a simple setup that has room to keep you busy.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Expect to look at the screen a lot. The trails are narrow, and the mix of steering, speed and avoiding a crash asks for more timing than a game where you can wander around while nothing urgent happens. I think I’d be fine with it on a stationary bike at a comfortable pace, where my hands can stay settled, but I’d lower the pace on a treadmill before trying to thread through a tight section.

Full controller support is a real plus here, since you can keep your hands on one controller and make small adjustments instead of reaching for a keyboard. I’d start on an easier route and focus on finishing the descent, rather than chasing a fast time right away. If you find yourself leaning your whole body to get around a corner, that might be the point to slow the workout down a little. The bike is not going to lean with you.

Crashes could be the part that gets annoying while moving. You can get sent back to a checkpoint, which means you’re not necessarily losing the whole run, but I’d still expect a few moments where I want to stop pedaling for a second and concentrate. I don’t know how much I’d enjoy repeating a tricky bit at a hard running pace, so I’d save the more demanding attempts for a slower workout.

I’d skip the urge to race every descent until you know how the controls feel with your exercise setup. On a bike, that probably means keeping the resistance and speed at a level where you can still make careful inputs. On a treadmill, I’d favor a relaxed walking pace or an easy run, because needing a sudden turn of attention while your feet are moving is not my idea of a fun surprise.

The game doesn’t require competitive reactions against other people, which helps, but it does reward attention and timing. I’d call it workable rather than effortless for exercise. You’ll be playing the game, not just glancing at it now and then.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

A fairly modest gaming computer should be enough, though I wouldn’t assume integrated graphics will handle it well just because the game takes up little storage. I’d check the graphics card first, since the listed minimum calls for a dedicated card from an older generation. It isn’t asking you to build a machine that needs its own workout.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is a good choice if you want an active game that keeps your eyes and hands involved, and I’d suggest starting on a bike or at an easy treadmill pace while you learn the trails. If your workout needs a game you can mostly ignore, this probably isn’t it.
