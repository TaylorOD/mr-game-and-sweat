Pool is a surprisingly reasonable game to put next to a treadmill, because each shot gives you a little pause to find your aim, but it can also make a workout feel like a very slow trip around one table. I’d start against the computer and leave the matches with real players for when you’re sitting down.

## Gameplay

The appeal here is that pool gives you a clear little problem to solve, and then it lets you take a breath before the next one. You look at the table, choose a shot, and try to send the cue ball where you want it, which gives your brain something to chew on without needing you to react every second. I think that’s a nice change from games that treat any moment you aren’t pressing a button as a personal failure.

There are eight types of pool, plus team play, so there’s room to change things up if one version starts feeling like the same table and the same decision over and over. I like the idea of that variety, though I wouldn’t expect every mode to be equally good company during a workout. Some will probably ask you to plan more carefully, and if you’re already trying to keep your pace steady, a complicated shot can turn into a small meeting with yourself.

The computer opponent is where I’d begin. You can take your time lining things up, and you aren’t making another person wait while you try to figure out whether the cue ball is going to stop where you expect. That pause between shots is useful, but it’s also why I wouldn’t call the game a constant distraction. You might spend a bit staring at the table and deciding what to do, which is better than getting interrupted by a cut scene, but not exactly a workout playlist with pockets.

Playing against people around the world sounds like a good way to find more variety, and team play gives you another reason to try a different format. I’d save those options for after the workout, though, because other people are waiting on your turn and I don’t want the feeling of being watched to make every shot take longer. I don’t know how much pressure the online matches add, but even a friendly game can make you care more about the result than the treadmill.

Pool is also a game where a bad decision can leave you a difficult next shot, so there’s some satisfaction in thinking ahead without needing a whole strategy guide open beside you. I’d probably enjoy working through the different kinds of matches, but I can also picture myself looking at a table for a long time and then hitting the cue ball with the confidence of a man who has learned nothing. That’s still a pretty calm way to spend a few minutes.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, the biggest question is whether your hands can make a careful aim while your body is moving, because pool gives you time to line up the shot but still asks you to be precise. I’d keep the pace easy and play against the computer, where a slow turn doesn’t put anyone else in the position of waiting for me to stop wobbling. That seems workable, not automatic.

A bike feels a little more forgiving for this kind of game, since you can keep pedaling while you focus on the table, but I don’t think it turns pool into a great workout by itself. The match has quiet stretches between shots, and I’d want to set a pace before starting rather than let every difficult angle become a reason to stop moving. The table can wait. Your legs are the part you brought along for a reason.

I can’t confirm controller support from the details here, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming you can put the keyboard and mouse away. If your setup lets you aim and take a shot with a controller, that would make it much more comfortable to play while moving, though precise aiming may still feel fiddly. I’d test the controls while seated first, because discovering that you have to reach for a mouse every turn is not a great treadmill feature.

Online matches are the part I’d skip during exercise. You can play real people, and I’d rather not combine a moving belt with trying to make a careful shot on someone else’s schedule, especially when a missed ball might leave you thinking about it for the next minute. Against the computer, you can let the match move at your speed, and that’s a much better fit.

I also wouldn’t pick this for a hard run or a fast bike session. It’s calm enough for easy movement, but it asks you to look and aim rather than letting you mash a button whenever your hands happen to be in the right place. If you’re in the mood for a steady, low pressure workout, give it a try. If you want the game to keep you busy every second, this table has a lot of empty space.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimums suggest this should run on a very modest computer, and nothing in the game description points to demanding visuals or a powerful graphics card. I’d check that the computer can launch it and that your preferred input method works before setting up a workout around it. It doesn’t sound like you need to build a computer around the pool table.

## Conclusion and Score

Real Pool 3D - Poolians makes sense for someone who wants an easygoing game to pair with a gentle treadmill walk or bike ride, especially if playing against the computer is enough. I’d pass if you need fast action or confirmed controller support before buying.
