The drone view is what makes this an odd workout game in a good way, because you’re steering through a race from the aircraft’s perspective instead of watching it from behind. I think you’d stay busy, but I’d start with freestyle rather than trying to race cleanly while your legs are moving.

## Gameplay

Liftoff: FPV Drone Racing sounds like it understands what’s fun about flying a tiny drone: the speed, the close calls, and the part where you thread through a gap you probably shouldn’t have aimed for. That first person view should make even a simple flight feel like you’re making a lot of small corrections, which is great when you want something to hold your attention. It also means there’s less room to zone out and pedal on autopilot.

Racing and freestyle seem like two pretty different moods, and I’d probably be happier starting with freestyle. Racing gives you a reason to care about clean lines and getting through the course efficiently, while freestyle sounds like it leaves more room to mess around and learn how the controls respond. I’m not sure how quickly the flying starts to feel natural, but I’d expect a lot of early crashes to be part of the process. Tiny aircraft, large confidence.

The simulator angle is appealing because this kind of flying looks like it depends on precise control, and I’d rather learn that in a game than find out what a real drone costs after meeting a tree. The community side is also a nice idea for people who want to keep improving or see how other pilots fly. I don’t know how much time you’ll spend setting things up before you’re comfortable, though, and that matters when you’re hoping to play a little during a workout instead of taking a course in drone piloting.

For me, the appeal is less “racing game I can half pay attention to” and more “small technical challenge that keeps my brain in the room.” That can make a workout go by faster, especially if you like learning a tricky control style, but the learning itself is probably the slow part. I’d give yourself room to be bad at it before expecting every flight to feel smooth.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, I’d expect the drone’s quick changes in direction to be the part that takes real focus. The first person view and fine steering mean your thumbs are doing more than holding a direction while you jog, and a rough input could send your carefully aimed flight somewhere less useful. I’d keep the pace easy enough that you can look at the screen without bouncing around too much. This is not the time to prove anything to a treadmill.

A bike seems a little more forgiving because you can keep your upper body steadier while you work the controller, but I still wouldn’t treat this like a relaxed cruise game. Racing asks for careful steering, and freestyle is probably more suitable for a casual workout because you can focus on flying rather than feeling like every small mistake ruined a run. I’d start there and see how much attention the controls take before increasing the pace.

You need a game controller or a radio controller to play, so this isn’t a keyboard and mouse situation. I’d use whichever controller feels predictable in your hands, and I’d avoid trying to learn the controls at a hard running pace. The game is asking for smooth thumb movements while your body is doing its own moving, which is a coordination problem I can respect and also don’t need to make harder on purpose.

This could be a nice fit if you enjoy the idea of practicing a skill while you exercise, because the controls give you something specific to work on and the drone view keeps the screen active. If you want a game you can play loosely while your legs do most of the work, I’d pick something else. You might be able to settle into it after learning the feel, but that seems like an earned comfort rather than something you get right away.

## Computer Requirements

You’ll need a fairly recent, capable graphics card, so this isn’t aimed at a computer that only handles light games. I’d check the graphics card first, since the minimum calls for a GeForce RTX 2060 or Radeon RX Vega 56 and integrated Intel graphics aren’t supported. That’s a lot of computer for a virtual drone.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Liftoff: FPV Drone Racing makes sense for you if learning precise drone flight sounds like a fun challenge and you’re comfortable giving it your attention during a workout. I’d skip it if you want a game that’s easy to play while running hard, but for an easy treadmill pace or a steadier bike session, freestyle seems like the place to begin.
