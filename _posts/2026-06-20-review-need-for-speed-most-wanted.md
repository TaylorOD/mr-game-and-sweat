The open streets and constant police chases make this a much better workout game than a race menu followed by a loading screen. I’d start with the races and save the serious cop escapes for when my hands are cooperating.

## Gameplay

I think Need for Speed Most Wanted works because it keeps giving you a reason to move, and then it gives you another one before you’ve had time to get bored. You can jump into races, chase shortcuts, and use nitrous to get past rivals, so there’s usually something happening besides waiting for the next event to begin. That’s a lot more useful on a treadmill than a long story scene where you’re standing there trying to remember if you’re supposed to press anything.

The open world is the part I’d enjoy most. You can head toward a race, find a jump, or take a road that seems better suited to the car you’re driving, and that freedom makes the game feel less like you’re following a checklist. It also means I could get distracted by a shortcut and turn a quick trip into a small tour of the city. I’ve done that in real life too, except usually with less nitrous.

Switching cars is a nice way to keep the driving from getting stale, and the focus on different cars gives you a reason to try another one instead of sticking with the first vehicle that feels comfortable. I like that the game doesn’t make every bit of progress depend on sitting through a lot of story. There are rivals to beat and cops to get away from, and that’s enough to keep the action moving.

The police chases are fun, but they can make the whole thing feel more demanding than a casual race. You’re trying to keep your speed up while watching for traffic and looking for somewhere to go, and I can imagine getting a little too invested in escaping when I’m supposed to be paying attention to my pace. I don’t know that I’d want every workout to be a frantic chase. A race is plenty.

The jumps and shortcuts add some good messiness to the driving, because you’re often deciding whether the faster looking route is actually one you can handle. I’d rather have that than a race where I’m just holding a line around the same corners, though I’d probably miss a few opportunities while my attention wandered. It’s fine. There are more roads.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d use a controller and keep the workout at a pace where I can make small steering corrections without gripping it like I’m trying to bend it. The driving doesn’t ask you to move your body around, which is good, but it does ask your thumbs to do quite a bit of work. Controller support is there, and the game’s open world gives you room to drive around between races, but I wouldn’t confuse that with a slow, low attention game.

On a treadmill, I’d start with races rather than police pursuits, and I’d avoid pushing the pace while learning a new car or route. The races still have traffic and turns to deal with, and the police can close in quickly enough that a missed turn can turn into a scramble. That’s the part where I think my running would get worse because I’d be busy trying to save the car. I can only keep track of so many emergencies.

A stationary bike seems a little more forgiving for the faster sections, since your hands aren’t moving with each step, but I’d still keep the effort easy when cops are involved. It’s not that the game is impossible to play while exercising, it’s that it gives you plenty of reasons to care about what’s happening right now. I don’t know if I’d choose a pursuit when I was already tired. That feels like adding a second job.

I’d skip any attempt to make every session about winning, too. You can drive around, switch cars, and pick a race when you feel ready, which makes it easier to match the game to the workout instead of the other way around. If a race starts taking all your attention, ease off the exercise or take a break. The cars can wait.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older computer with a dedicated graphics card, not something that needs a recent high end gaming setup. I’d check the graphics card first, because the listed requirement calls for DirectX 10.1 support and 512 MB of graphics memory. It’s not asking for much by today’s standards, but integrated graphics aren’t a sure thing.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is an easy game to suggest if you want racing to keep your mind busy while you work out, especially if you like open streets more than a long story. I’d skip the chases when you want an easy session, and pick something else if you need a game that barely asks for your attention.
