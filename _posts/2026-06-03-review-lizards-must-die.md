I think LIZARDS MUST DIE could keep a treadmill workout from getting dull, because it sends you straight into a fight against hordes of lizards instead of asking you to spend the whole time reading about them. I’d start on a bike or an easy treadmill pace, though, because fighting while your hands are bouncing around takes more attention than the premise makes it sound.

## Gameplay

The pitch is wonderfully direct: choose a blessed hero, fight the lizards and other unholy creatures, and defend the holy places of the Ancestors. I like that the game has a clear thing for you to do, and the Ancient Rus setting gives the green plague a little more personality than another parade of generic monsters. It’s not exactly a subtle setup. That’s fine.

Hordes are a good fit for a workout game because there’s usually something in front of you to focus on, and I’d expect the steady stream of enemies to make the minutes pass faster than they would in a game that keeps stopping to explain its world. I don’t know how much variety the fighting has from one encounter to the next, though, so I’d be watching for whether each group feels like a new problem or just more lizards arriving for their shift.

The hero choice gives you at least a small decision before the fighting starts, which is nice, but I wouldn’t spend half a workout trying to find the perfect character. Pick the one whose style feels comfortable, get into the action, and see if it holds your attention. I can lose plenty of time comparing options that probably won’t matter much once everything is trying to get at me.

There’s a potentially funny contrast in being asked to save the world while also trying to maintain a sensible walking pace, but the game’s simple goal is a real advantage here. I’d rather have a clear fight than a long stretch of story that makes me stop moving to pay attention. I don’t know whether the action stays varied enough to carry a long session, so I’d treat it as a game to try in shorter workouts first.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The hordes sound like they should give you something to do without needing to study a map or remember a complicated plan, which is good when your legs are doing their own thing. But action combat still asks you to aim your attention at what’s coming in, and I’d expect that to get harder if you’re running quickly or trying to turn your whole body into a metronome. An easy pace makes more sense.

I’d use a bike for a more intense session, or keep the treadmill slow enough that the controller isn’t bobbing around in my hands. The fights seem like the part to focus on, so I wouldn’t pair them with a pace where I need to react instantly just to keep my footing. It’s a little less exciting than charging into the green plague at full speed, but I imagine it’s more comfortable.

Controller support isn’t confirmed in the information here, so I’d check that before buying it specifically for exercise. If it works with a controller, that would make it much easier to use than reaching for a keyboard while moving. I’d also make sure the controls feel manageable while holding the controller steady, because fighting a horde and fighting the buttons at the same time sounds like a lot of work.

I’d skip trying to turn this into a hard interval workout until I knew how demanding the combat feels. Start with a gentle pace, and if you’re spending more attention on the lizards than on keeping your stride comfortable, slow down. There’s no reason to make saving Hyperborea a balance exercise too.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This needs a fairly capable older gaming graphics card, so it’s not a safe bet for a basic laptop with integrated graphics. I’d check the graphics card first, since the minimum asks for the sort of dedicated GPU that has already made it through a few hardware generations.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

LIZARDS MUST DIE sounds like a reasonable choice if you want straightforward monster fighting to fill an easy workout, and I’d skip it if you need confirmed controller support or a game that barely asks for quick reactions. Check your controls first, keep the pace comfortable, and see how the combat feels.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
