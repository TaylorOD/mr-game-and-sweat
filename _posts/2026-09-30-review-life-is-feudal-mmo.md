Abella sounds like a place where you should start at an easy pace, because surviving, building a community, and dealing with other players all ask for more attention than a steady jog can spare. I’d try it on a relaxed walk or bike ride first, not during the part of a workout where you’re trying to go fast.

## Gameplay

The appeal here is that your progress seems tied to a whole place and the people in it, rather than a string of quick matches, and I think that could keep you interested for a long time if you enjoy the slow work of survival and building. The economy and diplomacy give the world more to care about than just getting through the next fight. I like the idea of a medieval game where getting along with people can matter as much as being good at a weapon, though I also know that sounds easier than doing it with strangers.

The store description is pretty clear about the trade being made. This is meant to be hardcore, with survival, role playing, community building, and a bustling economy all taking up space. I’d expect a fair amount of gathering and planning between the moments where something urgent happens, so it probably won’t keep your attention in the same constant way an action game does. Sometimes a game where you’re making slow progress is exactly what you want. Sometimes you look up and realize you’ve been thinking about supplies instead of moving your legs.

Building a community is the part that interests me most, because it gives your efforts a point beyond improving your own character. You’re trying to make something work in a shared world, and that can make even small progress feel useful. It can also mean other people affect the pace and direction of your plans, which I think is exciting if you like multiplayer games and a little exhausting if you were hoping to quietly do your own thing.

There’s a lot here to learn, and the Early Access label makes me a little cautious about treating it like a finished, neatly arranged experience. I don’t know how smooth every part of that experience is, and the store description doesn’t give me enough to make claims about specific tasks or encounters. What I can say is that the premise sounds like a longer commitment, not something I’d boot up for a quick ten minute distraction before getting off the treadmill.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d keep the workout easy enough that you can pay attention to the world and the people in it, because a massively multiplayer game doesn’t wait in quite the same way a solo game can. If something social or time sensitive is happening, you might end up focused on that instead of your pace. That’s not a flaw in the game, but it does mean I wouldn’t pair it with a hard run and expect both activities to get your best effort.

The page doesn’t tell me that controller support is available, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming you can pick up a controller and have everything feel natural. I’d check the actual controls before setting up a treadmill or bike, and I’d be especially careful about whether character movement and interaction feel manageable while you’re bouncing around. An MMO with survival and diplomacy already asks you to keep track of a lot, and adding awkward controls would be like trying to carry groceries while opening the front door with your elbow.

A bike seems like the easier place to start, not because the game is automatically a bike game, but because you can slow your pedaling or coast while you’re concentrating. On a treadmill, I’d stay at a walking pace and avoid treating this like background entertainment. I don’t know how demanding its moment to moment controls will feel for you, so I’d give it a short test at a pace where stopping is easy.

I’d also skip the idea of using it for a workout where you need constant intensity. The communal and economic parts sound like they’ll reward attention and patience, and I think that’s a nice change from games that throw something at you every few seconds. It just means there may be stretches where your hands are occupied and your legs are doing less than you planned.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum graphics card is an older model, so you don’t need a recent high end gaming computer just to meet the listed requirements. I’d check your graphics card and available storage first, since the game asks for 50 GB and still needs a broadband connection.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Life is Feudal: MMO sounds best for you if the idea of slowly building a place in a shared medieval world sounds more appealing than constant action. I’d give it a look if you’re comfortable with a patient, attention heavy workout game, but I’d check the controls before buying it for exercise.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
