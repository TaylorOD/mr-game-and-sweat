Driving through the Olympic Exclusion Zone sounds like a pretty good treadmill workout, right up until a strange roadside mannequin makes you turn around while you’re trying to keep your feet moving. I’d keep the pace easy and let the station wagon do most of the exciting work.

## Gameplay

Pacific Drive has a lovely setup for a game about worrying over a car. You head into the Zone, look for parts, and try to get back to the garage with the wagon in decent enough shape to go out again, and I think that simple loop gives your brain something useful to chew on while you exercise. You’re not just driving from one place to another. You’re keeping track of fuel, damage, and whatever odd thing has decided to interfere with the car.

That car is what makes the whole thing work for me. Fixing it and adding upgrades gives the scavenging a purpose, so a trip for supplies feels like it matters instead of like I’m wandering around because a map told me to. The wagon also starts to feel like a collection of small problems you’re responsible for, which is maybe not the most relaxing way to take a drive, but it does keep you paying attention.

The Tourists are a good example of why the Zone is more interesting than a regular empty road. They look like wooden mannequins, and you can’t treat them like ordinary scenery without consequences. Other anomalies can mess with the car in ways that make you change what you’re doing, and I like that the game’s weirdness is tied to practical decisions. It’s a little like trying to get groceries while the parking lot keeps changing its rules.

There are stretches where you stop to search an area, pick through supplies, or deal with a car that needs attention, and those parts slow the game down. Radio conversations and story details add more to follow, too. I don’t know that I’d want every workout to be a long run through menus and repairs, but the garage is a natural place to take a breather, and you can decide how much extra scavenging feels worth it. Sometimes the answer is “enough to keep the wheels on.”

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The most likely treadmill problem is the steering, not the speed. You’ll be driving while your body is moving, and when the road or an anomaly makes you react quickly, I think your thumb may be doing a little more work than it would on a relaxed couch session. Full controller support is a big help, but it doesn’t make every turn gentle.

I’d keep the treadmill at an easy pace, especially on a first trip into the Zone, and I’d avoid treating every side road like a personal invitation to collect one more piece of scrap. You can make a run more demanding by rushing and scavenging everywhere, but I’d rather get home with the car intact than try to loot a shed while my feet are on autopilot. The shed can stay there.

A bike could be more comfortable for the driving sections because you’re seated and don’t have to match the game’s steering with a bouncing step. It still asks you to look around and make decisions on foot, though, and a few surprises can pull your attention away from pedaling. I’d use a comfortable pace and pause between trips if the repair and inventory work starts to feel like too much to manage while moving.

There’s also a difference between the game’s tension and the kind of tension that makes exercise unpleasant. A storm closing in while the car is damaged can make you want to rush, and I don’t know if that’s the feeling I’d choose for a hard treadmill workout. For an easy session, the pressure is part of the fun. For a faster workout, I’d probably play something that lets me react without steering a station wagon through a hostile forest.

## Computer Requirements

A GTX 1060 is the minimum graphics card, so this isn’t a game I’d expect to run happily on basic integrated graphics. I’d check the graphics card first, then make sure the computer has enough memory, because the listed minimum is 16 GB. It doesn’t ask for a top of the line machine, but you do need a real gaming card.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Pacific Drive is worth a look if you enjoy survival games where a vehicle becomes part of the plan, and I’d play it at an easy workout pace rather than asking it to share the belt with a hard run. The driving and scavenging are a good fit, though the fiddly stretches and sudden threats need some attention. I’d leave it for another time if you want a game you can mostly play on autopilot.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
