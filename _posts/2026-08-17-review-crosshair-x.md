A crosshair that follows you onto another shooter sounds useful, but Crosshair X by itself is not much of a workout game. I’d treat it as a tool for a shooter you already enjoy, not something to launch when you want a game to keep you moving.

## Gameplay

The thing I like about Crosshair X is that it has one clear job, and it doesn’t pretend to be more than that. It puts a custom crosshair over a game, including games that don’t have one built in, and you can choose from community designs or make your own. That could be handy if the shooter you’re playing leaves you guessing where the middle of the screen is. It’s a small fix for a specific annoyance.

But I can’t review the shooting as if it happens inside Crosshair X, because it doesn’t. There are no enemies here to keep you busy and no game loop to pull your attention away from your legs. The game underneath supplies all of that, and it could be a calm single player shooter or a tense match where your attention is completely gone. That’s a pretty important distinction.

I think the custom crosshair part is more useful than the name makes it sound, especially if you already know you dislike the aiming marker in a particular game, or don’t have one at all. Having thousands of community designs gives you options without requiring you to design one from scratch, and the designer gives you a way to make your own if none of those fit. I’d probably start with a community design and only tinker if the current one actually bothers me. There’s no need to turn a small preference into a weekend project.

For exercise, the lack of standalone gameplay means you need to bring your own reason to keep playing. The overlay might make aiming feel clearer, but it doesn’t make a shooter less demanding or make a slow game more active. I don’t know that a clearer aiming point would make a shooter more fun for me on a run, but it could remove one little bit of visual uncertainty. That’s a fairly modest job.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The shooter underneath matters much more than Crosshair X does. With a controller friendly game that gives you time to line up shots, I can imagine this working on a bike or a steady treadmill pace, because you could keep your hands on the controller and let the crosshair do its one useful thing. If you’re in a fast match where people are appearing from every direction, though, you’re still trying to aim while your hands are moving with your steps. The overlay can’t make that part easier.

I’d choose a slower single player shooter or a relaxed mode, and I’d skip competitive matches while moving. That’s not because this app changes how the shooter plays, it’s because precision aiming and bouncing hands don’t really get along. There are people who can make tiny corrections while jogging, and I’m sure that looks very controlled. I’d expect to be aiming at the general neighborhood.

The controller question also belongs to the game you pair with it. Crosshair X is an overlay, not a shooter with its own controller gameplay, so it doesn’t add controller support if the underlying game doesn’t have it. If you already play a particular shooter with a controller, I’d test that setup at a comfortable pace before trying to make it your hard workout game. I don’t know how much the crosshair would help with that game until I saw how it looks over the action.

On a bike, I’d expect the setup to feel more manageable than on a treadmill if your hands can stay steady, but that depends on the bike and the shooter, not on some special bike mode in Crosshair X. On either machine, I’d keep the first try simple and change the crosshair only if it’s actually hard to see. Otherwise you can end up spending the warmup adjusting an aim marker and not playing anything. That’s not a terrible use of a warmup, I guess.

## Computer Requirements

The store page only lists Windows 10 as a minimum, with no graphics requirement, so the overlay itself doesn’t appear to ask for a powerful computer. You’ll still need a machine that can run the shooter you’re putting it over, and that game will likely be the part that decides how much computer you need. Crosshair X isn’t where I’d start worrying about the graphics card.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Crosshair X makes sense if you already play shooters and want a custom crosshair, but I wouldn’t buy it expecting a game to exercise with. Pair it with a slower, controller friendly shooter if you want to use it while moving, and leave the fast competitive matches for when you’re sitting down.
