A GT3 race looks like a good way to make a treadmill run go faster, but Assetto Corsa Competizione asks for enough careful steering that I’d save the serious races for when you’re sitting still. A relaxed practice session might work, though, and the cars are fun enough that I’d give it a try.

## Gameplay

I think the racing is the reason to play this, not a story or a bunch of side activities trying to keep you busy between races. You’re in GT3 cars, chasing other drivers around official circuits, and the appeal is in getting through corners cleanly and gradually figuring out how much speed you can carry. That gives you something to pay attention to almost all the time. There isn’t much time to wonder what the game wants from you.

The simulation focus is a real part of the appeal, but it does mean you have to care about your inputs. A corner you take badly can cost you speed down the next straight, and then you’re trying to make up ground instead of just enjoying the scenery. I’m not sure that sounds relaxing to everyone, but I like that the cars ask you to be deliberate. Sometimes I’d like to be less deliberate and just put my foot down.

There are official drivers, teams, cars and circuits, which makes the championship setting feel more grounded than a made up collection of races. You can also set up races around what you feel like doing, rather than treating the whole game like one long career you have to complete. That matters to me because I’d rather do a short race or practice session than spend a workout sorting through menus and planning an entire racing season.

The race weekend structure can still ask you to do some prep before the part where you’re actually racing. Practice and qualifying are useful if you want to learn a track and get a better starting position, but I’d skip them when I just wanted a quick session. There are setups to think about too, and I don’t know how much most people want to fiddle with those before a run. I’d start with a car that feels manageable and leave the tiny adjustments for another time.

There’s a lot here for someone who wants to get better at racing, but I wouldn’t call it a casual game in the way a kart racer can be. That focus makes the races engaging, and it also means a mistake can stay on your mind for a while after you’ve made it. I’d want to race again. That’s enough.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The hard part is keeping the steering calm while your body is moving, because this game rewards small corrections and punishes turning a corner into a guess. A controller works, which is good, but the precision you want from a steering wheel doesn’t magically show up in your thumbs. I’d expect to drift wide more often while running, and then spend the next straight annoyed at myself.

For a treadmill, I’d stick to practice or an easy solo race, lower the difficulty, and avoid treating every lap like a qualification attempt. A run doesn’t need a championship fight happening at the same time. I’d also pick a track and car I already understand, so I’m not learning a braking point while trying to keep my feet in rhythm. There’s enough going on without adding a new corner to the list.

A bike seems a little more forgiving, since your upper body can stay steadier while you pedal, but I still wouldn’t choose a close race where every small steering mistake matters. You’re turning into corners, watching other cars, and trying not to run wide, and that can pull your attention away from the workout pretty quickly. I’d skip competitive online races while moving. Other people are depending on you, and I’d rather not make them part of my exercise plan.

I don’t know that I’d enjoy this much during a hard effort, because the game wants your attention right when your legs are asking for some of it back. A slow spin or easy walk is a different story. You can let a practice session be imperfect and keep going, which is probably the least stressful way to mix the two.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum asks for a dedicated graphics card around the level of an older GTX 750 or RX 460, so I wouldn’t assume an office laptop with integrated graphics is enough. I’d check the graphics card first, and make sure you have room for a 50 GB install, because racing cars apparently need a fair bit of garage space.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This makes sense for you if you want a serious racing game and can keep your workout easy while you play. For a hard run or a race where every mistake matters, I’d pick something less demanding.
