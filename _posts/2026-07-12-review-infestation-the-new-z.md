A slow jog is about the most I’d pair with Infestation: The New Z, because gathering loot sounds manageable until another group of players turns your quiet scavenging into a fight you can’t pause. I’d save the competitive matches for when I’m sitting down.

## Gameplay

The appeal here is pretty direct: survive with friends, gather loot, stash it or trade it, and decide how much trouble you’re willing to go looking for. I like that the game gives you a reason to keep moving around instead of making every session a long stretch of menus or story, and the open world sounds like it could be a decent place to settle into a steady jog. But the other players are part of the deal, and they can change the mood fast. That sounds exciting when you’re ready for it, and a little annoying when you’re trying to keep your breathing even.

I think the most treadmill friendly part would be the slower stretch of getting around and looking for loot, because you can pay attention without every moment demanding a perfect response. Then you run into a group, or head into a war against another group, and the game asks you to care about staying alive more than your pace. I don’t know how predictable those encounters feel from one session to the next, but the fact that other people are involved means I wouldn’t count on a nice calm stretch lasting very long.

The 5v5 competitive mode sounds like the clearest place to draw a line. Shorter matches are nice when you’re exercising, since you can plan around them, but competitive play tends to make every mistake feel important, and I don’t think that pairs well with a bouncing controller and a treadmill belt. I’d save that mode for after the workout. It can be good to have something that asks for your full attention, just not when your feet are keeping time too.

There’s a lot to like about the mix of open world survival and direct player fights, especially if you’re playing with friends and enjoy the risk of losing progress. But I’m not sure I’d want every workout to depend on whether the people I meet are having a normal day. Sometimes you want a game that keeps you busy, and sometimes you want the virtual equivalent of a walk around the block. This sounds more like heading out without knowing if you’ll get a quiet walk.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The first thing I’d expect is that aiming and reacting to other players would take more concentration than the slower looting does. On a bike, I’d probably have an easier time keeping my body steady while I look around and line things up, though I still wouldn’t choose the competitive mode while moving. On a treadmill, quick turns and sudden fights sound like a good way to start thinking about the controller instead of the run.

I’d test the controls while sitting down before bringing this to exercise, since I can’t confirm from the available information exactly how well a controller works here. If you can get your preferred setup working, I’d still start with the less competitive open world play, ideally with friends, and keep the pace easy. I wouldn’t make my first try a 5v5 match while running. That’s a lot of pressure to put on your thumbs when your feet are already doing something.

There’s also the question of how much you care about the loot you’re carrying. The whole gather, stash and trade setup gives you a reason to take the world seriously, which is useful when you want a game to hold your attention, but it also means a mistake might feel more costly than in a simple arcade game. I’d skip the workout session if I knew I’d be too distracted by losing gear or getting pulled into a fight. It’s hard to watch your pace when you’re busy trying to recover a bad decision.

I don’t know how often the open world gives you a quiet stretch, so I’d treat a treadmill session as a low stakes experiment rather than a dependable routine. A stationary bike seems more forgiving if the action gets busy, but neither option makes the competitive matches a relaxed way to exercise.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older dedicated graphics card, so you probably don’t need a new gaming PC just to give this a try. I’d check your graphics hardware first, especially if you’re using a laptop with integrated graphics, because the listed minimum isn’t asking for a very recent card.

## Conclusion and Score

This makes more sense for someone who wants multiplayer survival and is happy to focus on the game, not for someone looking for a dependable treadmill companion. I’d try the open world at an easy pace, but I’d leave the competitive matches for the couch.
