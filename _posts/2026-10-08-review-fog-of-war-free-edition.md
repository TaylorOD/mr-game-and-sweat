I wouldn’t make Fog Of War - Free Edition my treadmill shooter, because a tactical multiplayer FPS asks you to aim carefully while other people are actively trying to beat you. The World War II setting and vehicles sound fun, but I’d save the battles for when I can give them my full attention.

## Gameplay

The tactical angle is what makes this more interesting to me than a shooter where you just run toward the noise, but it also means I’d expect to spend more time thinking about where to go and what to do. That can be a good change of pace when you want a match that feels like it has some shape to it. It’s just not the kind of game I’d pick when I want my legs to do the thinking.

The vehicles and artillery are a promising part of the pitch. I like the idea of a battlefield where what you bring to the fight can matter as much as how quickly you can point at something, and having several modes gives you room to look for a style that suits you. I can’t tell from the available details how much each mode changes the pace, though, so I wouldn’t buy it expecting one particular kind of match.

There’s also a story based on World War II, alongside ranks, titles, awards, and a career. That gives the game more to work toward than simply finishing a match, which I think can be a nice reason to come back. I don’t know how much of that career structure is meaningful moment to moment, and I’d be more interested in whether the battles themselves stay engaging than in collecting another title for my name.

The multiplayer part is the biggest reason I’d want to play it sitting down. A tactical FPS can make a small mistake matter, and I’d rather be able to pay attention to the fight than keep checking whether my feet are landing evenly. I can see this being enjoyable if you like working through a battle with other players, but it sounds like a poor place to half listen while your treadmill makes its little treadmill noise.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d expect the aiming and quick decisions to be the hard part here, especially once a match gets busy and you need to respond to what other players are doing. A bike might make it easier to keep your upper body steady than a treadmill, but I still wouldn’t want to be making careful shots while bouncing along. I can picture myself trying to line up a target and steering my legs like I’m carrying groceries in both hands.

I can’t confirm controller support from the details I have, so I wouldn’t plan a workout around a gamepad before checking it yourself. Even if the game works with one, that wouldn’t make the aiming effortless, and multiplayer means you probably can’t just pause the match when you need to sort out your stride. I’d test the controls while sitting down first. No need to learn the hard way while moving.

The different modes may give you some room to find a less demanding way to play, but I can’t safely point you to a specific mode as the easy workout option. I’d skip anything where you feel pressured to react instantly, and stick with a mode only if its pace gives you time to look, aim, and keep moving without turning the whole run into a very serious thumb exercise.

For a treadmill, I’d keep the speed low enough that your footing stays boring, and I’d stop the workout if you find yourself leaning forward to concentrate. On a bike, I’d try it only if you can keep your hands steady and you’re comfortable riding while focusing on a screen. The game sounds more suited to a focused session than a casual ride, which is a little inconvenient for a game review site about exercising while gaming.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The posted minimums are fairly modest, but they don’t name a graphics card, so that’s the first thing I’d check rather than assuming any older computer will handle a 3D shooter comfortably. I’d expect a mid range card from the last several years to be a sensible target, with the stated DirectX requirement worth checking too.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Fog Of War - Free Edition sounds like a better fit for someone who wants a focused multiplayer shooter and a reason to chase ranks than for someone trying to keep a steady workout going. I’d give it a try while seated first, and leave the treadmill for a game that’s more forgiving when your aim wanders.

### Overall Score: 1.67 out of 3
