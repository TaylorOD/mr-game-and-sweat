I think mining your way toward orbit sounds like a much better treadmill plan than staring at a progress bar, but Starship EVO also asks you to build a whole ship, and that part may have you standing still with your hands on the controller. I’d save it for an easy workout where stopping to think is allowed.

## Gameplay

The building is what makes me interested, because your ship isn’t just something you pick from a menu and forget about. You mine and craft, then build a ship of your own, so the work you put in has a visible purpose. That sounds great when you’re in the mood to make something, and a little like cleaning out a junk drawer when you came in hoping to get straight to space.

There’s a real difference between a game that gives you a ship and one that asks you to make one, and I think that difference is both the draw and the time commitment here. The building system is described as elegant, which sounds promising, but even a nice building system asks you to make decisions and line things up. I’d expect to spend stretches planning rather than reacting. That’s not bad. It’s just not constant action.

The survival progression and the dangerous galaxy give the crafting a reason to matter, which I like more than collecting materials just because a number on the screen says I should. I don’t know how quickly the game gets you from mining to exploring, so I wouldn’t promise you a fast start, but that gradual climb seems like it could make the first ship feel earned. It could also mean you’re still figuring out your next step while your workout belt keeps moving.

Space opera scale is a nice thing to aim for, but I’m more interested in whether the day to day work stays fun than whether the galaxy is huge. Mining, crafting and ship building all sound like they could hold my attention, especially if I’m deciding what I want the ship to do next. They also sound like the kind of activities where I could lose track of the workout because I’m trying to solve a construction problem. Then I’d look down and remember I was supposed to be exercising.

Early Access matters here because a game built around progression and construction can change a lot while it’s still being developed. I’d be comfortable with that if you enjoy seeing a project take shape and don’t need every part to feel finished right now. If you want a tightly paced adventure that keeps handing you the next big moment, this may ask for more patience than you want to give it. There’s a lot of room to tinker.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The first thing I’d change is my expectation of pace. Mining and crafting sound like the friendlier parts for exercise, since I’d expect to be able to take them at my own speed, while building a ship sounds like it could take more careful input than I want to manage while bouncing along. I’d put those jobs on an easy walk or a relaxed bike ride, not a hard run where every little adjustment feels like doing surgery on a bus.

Exploration sounds like the part that might pull you into the game without making you stop every few seconds to think. You’ve got a ship to build and a dangerous galaxy to explore, so there’s a nice contrast between the slow work of getting ready and the payoff of going somewhere. I think that could make a workout feel less repetitive, as long as you’re not trying to do the fiddly building at the same time as you’re trying to keep your footing.

I can’t confirm how well a controller handles the current build, so I’d check that before you plan a whole workout around it. Even with a controller, I’d expect ship construction to need more precise inputs than the parts where you’re simply traveling or gathering, and I wouldn’t want to discover that halfway through a run. Give it a try while you’re stationary first. That’s less exciting, but so is rebuilding a ship because your thumb slipped.

A bike is probably the easier place to start if you want to spend time building, since you can keep a steadier upper body while you work through a design. On a treadmill, I’d stick to the parts that don’t need careful aiming or lots of menu decisions, and I’d slow down before tackling the ship itself. I don’t know how often the game asks you to stop and plan, but I’d expect enough of that to make a brisk run feel like the wrong fit.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This asks for a dedicated graphics card around the GTX 1060 or RX 470 level, so I’d check your graphics card before worrying about anything else. It’s a reasonable mid range target, but an older laptop with integrated graphics probably isn’t where I’d start building a space fleet.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Starship EVO sounds best for you if you enjoy building toward a goal and don’t mind the workout having slower stretches. I’d try it on an easy bike ride or walk after confirming your controls work, but I’d skip it if you want fast action from beginning to end.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
