A Napoleonic musket battle is a pretty good way to forget you’re exercising, but I think Holdfast: Nations At War asks for more careful aiming than I’d want while jogging. I’d give it a try on a bike or an easy treadmill pace, not during a hard run.

## Gameplay

The slow muskets are a big part of why this game sounds fun to me, and also why I wouldn’t expect every minute to feel busy. You line up a shot, fire, and then wait through a reload while the battle carries on around you. That gives the action a strange rhythm, where a single shot can feel like your whole contribution for a little while. I kind of like that, though I can see those quiet seconds getting long if you’re hoping for constant action.

The scale helps. With over 150 players on a server, a fight can feel like a whole crowd of people trying to move in the same direction, and that’s more interesting to me than a shooter where every encounter is one person appearing around a corner. It also means you aren’t always the person making the decisive move. Sometimes you’re following the group and trying to get into a useful spot. Fair enough.

Napoleonic battles and WW1 give you two different kinds of shooting to settle into, and I think that variety matters because the musket pace probably won’t work for everyone. The Napoleonic side sounds like the more unusual option, with the waiting and careful shots doing as much work as the firing. WW1 should feel more familiar if you want a shooter with a faster rhythm. I’d be curious which one feels better after a few matches, but I wouldn’t pretend they’re the same experience with different costumes.

The multiplayer setup is the part I’d be most excited about and the part I’d want to be in the right mood for. A crowded battle can be funny and chaotic, but people are also trying to win, and I don’t know that I’d always want to spend a workout trying to follow a plan made by a group of strangers. If I wanted a game that kept my attention on a treadmill, this could do it. If I wanted something I could half watch while moving, probably not.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The controller support is full, which is a real point in its favor, but the game still asks you to aim at other people in a multiplayer fight that keeps moving without you. Your hands are already moving a little when you walk or jog, and a careful shot can turn into you gently steering the camera past the person you meant to aim at. The controller helps. It doesn’t make the aiming part automatic.

I’d start on a bike or at a very easy walking pace, and I’d try third person before settling into first person, since the game supports both. A wider view sounds useful when your head and hands aren’t perfectly still. I’d also set up somewhere I can stop safely, because there’s no good way to pause a live multiplayer battle while you catch your breath or adjust your position.

The musket reloads could actually be useful on a workout, since they give you a moment where you’re not trying to make a fast shot. But then the other side of that is the fight can move while you’re waiting, and when the next chance comes you may need to turn and aim quickly. It’s a bit like trying to do a calm chore while somebody keeps asking you to look over your shoulder. I don’t know if that balance would work for me on a run.

I’d skip the most serious matches while exercising and treat the first few rounds as practice with the controls. Full controller support makes the game playable from the couch, for sure, but this isn’t a relaxed shooter where you can take your time without consequences. On a bike, I think the steadier movement gives you a better shot at aiming well. On a treadmill, I’d keep the belt slow.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum graphics card is old and fairly modest, so you don’t need a new gaming computer just to meet the listed requirements. I’d check that your processor and internet connection are up to it before worrying about buying a fancy graphics card, because a crowded online battle needs a reliable connection too.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Holdfast: Nations At War sounds like a fun multiplayer shooter when you can give it your attention, and I’d suggest it to someone who likes big battles and doesn’t mind a slower pace between shots. For exercising, I’d choose a bike or a slow walk and leave the competitive matches for when I’m sitting down.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
