I think RISK: Global Domination can make a workout feel pleasantly busy, but it’s better for a steady walk or easy ride than for pushing your pace. You’re making decisions about territories and dice, not trying to react before the screen moves on, which is a nice change of pace.

## Gameplay

RISK works because the basic decision is easy to understand and hard to stop thinking about: do I spread out, defend what I have, or try to take another territory? I like that there’s a real reason to pay attention, but it doesn’t ask you to memorize a dozen buttons before you can start making choices.

The dice keep the game from turning into a clean little math problem. You can make what feels like the sensible attack and still get a lousy result, and then you’re left deciding whether to try again or save what you have. I don’t know why I always think the next roll will be the one that fixes things, but I do.

There are more than 100 maps, which gives the game a lot of room to change its shape without changing what makes RISK work. I’d start with a map you recognize, because learning the board and learning your opponents at the same time sounds like trying to read a menu while the waiter is standing there. The familiar maps are enough to keep me interested.

The slower pace is part of the appeal, though it can also mean you spend a while looking at the map and waiting for your turn. In a workout game, that’s better than a long cut scene, but it still isn’t constant activity. You’re using your brain more than your thumbs.

Free to play makes it easy to give it a try, and I think that matters here because the appeal depends a lot on whether you enjoy the board game’s turn by turn decisions. If you already like RISK, having lots of maps to choose from sounds great. If the idea of carefully placing armies makes you want to check your phone, more maps probably won’t change your mind.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

This is a game I’d play at a comfortable pace, not during hard intervals, because the important moments are the ones where you look over the map and decide where to attack. The turns give you time to settle your hands and get back to your rhythm, but I’d still expect to slow down a little when it’s time to pick territories. I don’t need my cardio to become a geography lesson.

I can’t confirm controller support, so I’d test your setup before getting on the treadmill rather than discovering that the input you want is awkward once you’re moving. Either way, selecting a territory and confirming an attack calls for more care than a game where you can mash one button and keep going. On a bike, I’d expect that to be easier to manage than on a treadmill, where each step can make a small target feel smaller.

Online matches also ask you to pay attention to other players’ decisions, and I think that’s where I’d be most likely to forget about my pace. You’re not dealing with fast aiming, but you can get pulled into planning a response, then your turn arrives and you’re trying to make several choices while your legs carry on without you. I’d stick to an easy workout and skip anything that makes you feel rushed.

The nice part is that you can take a moment to think instead of reacting to an enemy running straight at you. I’d keep the speed low enough that I can look at the map without wobbling around, and I’d stop or slow down if choosing a territory started taking all my attention. That’s probably the difference between a relaxing match and me leaning toward the screen like it owes me an explanation.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest, so an older computer or one with integrated graphics should be a reasonable place to start. I’d check that it can handle DirectX 11, but this isn’t the kind of game that sounds like it needs a fancy graphics card to move a map around.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Give RISK: Global Domination a try if you like turn based strategy and want a workout game that gives you time to think. It’s less suited to someone who wants constant action or plans to push hard on the treadmill, but for a steady walk or bike ride, I think it fits.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
