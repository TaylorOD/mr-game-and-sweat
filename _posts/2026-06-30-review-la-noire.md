The interviews are the reason I’d put L.A. Noire on a treadmill, because Cole Phelps can sit across from somebody who may be lying while you keep moving and the game isn’t asking you to land a perfect shot every few seconds. The driving and gunfights make it less relaxing than that sounds.

## Gameplay

I think the investigation work is interesting, but it can be slow in a way that’s either nice or a little too nice, depending on how much attention you want to give the screen. You search crime scenes for clues, then use what you’ve found when you question people, and I like that the game asks you to notice what’s going on instead of turning every case into a shooting gallery. Sometimes I’d probably be looking at someone’s face and thinking, “That’s a very confident face for a person I don’t trust.” That’s the job, I guess.

The interviews are the part I’d remember, because choosing how to respond to a suspect depends on reading their expression and deciding whether the evidence supports what they’re saying. It’s a neat idea, and it also means you can’t completely drift off and assume the game will tell you the answer. I don’t know that every expression is easy to read, so a wrong call can feel a little like getting graded on someone’s eyebrows.

There’s a lot of story between the active parts, and the pace can take its time. You’re working through a run of murders, arson cases and department trouble, and I think the slower scenes help give the detective work some weight, but they also make this a poor choice if you want constant action to hold your attention. A case can ask for a little patience. I’d want to be awake for it.

The action helps break that up, though I wouldn’t call it the reason to play. Driving from one location to another and getting into gunfights gives you something more immediate to do, but the investigation is what makes the game feel like itself. When a case has you moving between clues and conversations, I’d be curious to see what I missed, which is a pretty good way to forget that you’re exercising. Then you get back in the car.

I also think the game is better when you let it be a detective story instead of expecting every stretch to feel like a mission in an action game. The careful parts are compelling, but there are long stretches where you’re listening, watching or moving through a scene at a measured pace. If you need a game to keep your hands busy every second, this will probably feel like waiting for the next thing to happen. Sometimes that next thing takes a minute.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, the interviews and clue searches seem like the most comfortable fit, because the game gives you time to look around and think before you act. I’d keep the pace easy enough that I could focus on faces and bits of evidence, since guessing at an interview answer while bouncing around sounds like a good way to accuse the wrong person for no reason. The controller works, which is what I’d want here.

The gunfights are where I’d slow down or stop walking, because aiming while the controller and your hands are moving is not a great arrangement. Driving also asks you to steer, and I’d expect that to take more attention than the calmer investigation sections. You can play with a controller, but not every part is equally forgiving when your inputs get loose.

A bike seems a little easier for the slower sections, since you can keep pedaling while you look around or listen to dialogue, but I wouldn’t treat it as a game where you can ignore the screen for a while. The interviews still want your attention, and the evidence searches can be easy to miss if you’re just glancing over now and then. I don’t know that I’d want to pedal hard through a gunfight either. There’s no benefit to making the aiming harder.

I’d play this on an easy treadmill walk or a steady bike ride, and I’d be willing to pause the workout when a scene turns into a gunfight or a driving sequence that needs more focus. That might sound fussy, but the game’s pacing gives you natural stretches where you can settle in and stretches where you should pay attention. It’s a better fit for a controlled workout than a hard run.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The listed requirements reach back to some very old graphics cards, so you don’t need a recent high end gaming computer to start looking at this. I’d check your graphics card first, especially if you’re using integrated graphics, since the store page specifically warns that some of those may not work.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

L.A. Noire is a good choice for an easy workout if you like slow detective stories and don’t mind pausing when the action gets busy. Skip it if you need constant movement on screen to keep you engaged. The interviews are worth the time.
