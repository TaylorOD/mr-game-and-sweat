The first thing I’d do with 20 Minutes Till Dawn is set an easy pace, because the game keeps putting creatures around you and asking you to move while you aim. That’s a pretty good match for a workout, as long as you’re not trying to sprint through the busy parts.

## Gameplay

I think this works because there isn’t much waiting around. You’re moving through a growing crowd of Lovecraftian creatures, firing at them, picking up experience, and choosing upgrades as you level up. It gives your brain enough to do that the workout probably won’t be the only thing you’re thinking about, which is useful when you’re trying to get through the last stretch of a run.

The upgrade choices are where I’d expect to get pulled in. You can build up your character and weapon in different ways, and I like games where a run starts with a simple plan and then the choices keep nudging it somewhere else. I’d probably tell myself I was going to make one sensible build, then pick whatever upgrade sounded fun on the screen. That’s about my level of planning.

There are short pauses when you level up and choose an upgrade, which I think is a good fit for exercise. You get a moment to look at your options without having to stop the game entirely, and then the crowd comes back and you’re moving again. I don’t know if I’d want a game to make me read a paragraph every few minutes while I’m jogging, but these choices sound quick enough to keep the pace up.

Aiming is the part that gives it more to do than just running in circles. You need to keep moving so the creatures don’t close in, but you also have to point your fire where it matters, and later on there’s more going on around you. That sounds fun when I’m alert, though I’d expect to make some questionable decisions once my legs and thumbs are both getting tired.

The twenty minute length is a real commitment for one run, but at least it’s a clear one. I’d rather have a game tell me what kind of session I’m getting into than discover after starting that the next chunk of story is longer than I thought. There are going to be intense stretches, and I think the steady pressure is the point. I’d give it a try when I want the game to distract me, not when I’m trying to keep a precise pace.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, I’d start at a pace where I can use both sticks without feeling like I’m trying to steer a shopping cart with a loose wheel. The left stick handles movement and the right stick lets you aim, so there’s more thumb work than in a game where you only have to run forward. Controller support makes it practical, but it doesn’t make aiming disappear.

I’d look for the auto aim setting before starting, since that could take some pressure off aiming while the controller is bouncing in your hands. I’d still want to move carefully, though, because enemies can come from around you and a missed turn matters more here than it would in a game where you’re mostly watching a scene. I can’t tell you how much help auto aim will be at every point in a run, but it sounds like the right thing to try first.

The bike sounds a little more forgiving for the aiming, especially if you’re sitting upright and your hands are steady, but there’s still enough movement and quick reacting that I wouldn’t choose it for a hard interval. You don’t need to be perfect, and I like that, but when the screen fills up I’d want to pay attention instead of trying to push my pace at the same time. I’d keep the workout easy and let the game be the demanding part.

I’d pick the character and weapon before getting on, then leave the upgrade choices for the brief pauses during the run. There’s no reason to make setup more complicated than it needs to be when you’re already holding a controller. If you’re running hard enough that looking down to choose an upgrade feels like a chore, slow down or save this for a steadier session.

The main thing I’d skip is trying to play while exhausted or sprinting. Not because every mistake ends the run, but because the game asks for direction changes and aiming at the same time, and those are exactly the inputs that get sloppy when you’re bouncing around. On an easy run or a comfortable bike ride, I think it could be a fun way to keep your attention occupied.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very modest, so this should run on older or basic computers without needing a dedicated gaming graphics card. I’d check that the machine meets the basic operating system requirements, but the game’s tiny storage requirement is a good sign that it isn’t asking for much.

## Conclusion and Score

This is one I’d suggest for an easy workout where you want something active on screen, not a game you can half ignore while going all out. The aiming may take some adjusting on a moving treadmill, but the quick upgrade pauses and constant action make it a sensible one to try.
