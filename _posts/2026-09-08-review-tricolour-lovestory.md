Tricolour Lovestory sounds like a rough treadmill companion if you’re hoping to stay entertained by a story, but a pretty bad choice if you want the game to keep your feet moving. A million words is a lot to read while bouncing along, even when the story is about choices that sound like they could get dramatic.

## Gameplay

I think the scale of the script is the first thing you need to decide about, because one million words can be a lot of story even when you’re sitting comfortably, and it’s definitely a lot when you’re trying to remember what you just read over the treadmill noise. The premise gives you a few big emotional directions, from trying to get a love back to starting something new or watching everything burn, which is a fun set of possibilities to put in front of you. I’d want the choices to feel like they actually change the experience, though, and the store description doesn’t tell me how much they do. So I’m curious, but not sold.

The 100 or more original CGs sound like a nice break from reading, but still pictures aren’t going to make the words go away. I don’t know how the story is divided up or how easy it is to pause and pick things back up, so I’d treat this like a book that occasionally asks you to make a decision rather than a game that’s constantly pulling your attention forward. That can be a perfectly good way to spend time. It just isn’t much of a workout distraction unless you like reading while your body is doing something repetitive.

The love story setup is broad enough that you can probably approach it in the mood you’re in, whether that means hoping for a new start or picking a messier direction. I like that the description at least makes the player’s choice sound like the point, instead of pretending this is an action game with a couple of romantic scenes taped on. But a huge script is also a commitment, and if the writing doesn’t grab you, there’s a lot of it between you and the next interesting decision. That’s a long book with a controller attached.

I’d play this when I wanted a story and didn’t mind that the workout might turn into background movement. If the game gives you text speed or automatic text advance options, I’d adjust them until the reading feels comfortable, but I wouldn’t rush through the dialogue just to keep up with my pace. I don’t know whether the story’s quieter stretches are easy to move past, so I’d expect some sitting and reading, even if you’re technically still moving.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The story can wait for you, which is a real advantage over anything that expects a fast reaction the moment you look away. On a bike, I think that makes this much more practical, since you can keep your body moving while you read and take your time with the choices. On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace gentle and make sure the screen is easy to read without leaning forward, because trying to follow a long conversation while your head bobs around sounds like a small chore that gets old.

Controller support isn’t confirmed in the information I have, so I wouldn’t assume you can pick it up and play without checking first. Try the controls before you get on the machine, and make sure you can advance dialogue and choose an option without reaching for a mouse or keyboard. If that part works, the lack of quick action makes the game more forgiving than most things you could play while moving. If it doesn’t, reaching across for the mouse every few lines is going to feel like you’re doing a very slow office job.

I’d probably skip the treadmill if reading on a moving belt makes you lose your place, and use a stationary bike instead. A bike lets you keep your eyes on the story without worrying about each step changing where your screen is, and there’s no reason to turn a relaxed choice driven game into a balance exercise. Still, this isn’t a game that keeps you busy with constant inputs. The workout will be coming from you, mostly.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very modest, so an older basic computer should be enough, and you don’t need a new graphics card just to look at story illustrations. I’d check that the game runs on your version of Windows and that you have room for its 4 GB install, then get back to the important question of whether you want to read a million words.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This seems best for you if a long romantic story is the main reason you’re exercising and you’re comfortable checking the controls before you start. I’d pass if you need a game that actively pulls you through a workout, but for an easy ride with plenty of reading, it could fit.
