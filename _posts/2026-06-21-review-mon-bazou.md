A steady bike ride seems like a better fit for Mon Bazou than a hard treadmill run, because you can spend time working on a car or earning money without needing every input to be perfect. I’d save the night racing for when I’m not bouncing around.

## Gameplay

I think the appeal here is that your time goes into making a very rough vehicle into something you actually want to race, and that sounds more fun to me than starting with a perfect car and just being asked to drive it. You install the parts yourself, so there’s a satisfying bit of ownership in the work, but it also means you’re not always moving at racing speed. Some of your progress comes from ordinary jobs, which is either a nice change of pace or a reason to check whether your legs are still doing anything.

Cutting wood, delivering pizza, and managing a sugar shack all sound like useful ways to give yourself something to do between races. I like a game where the less glamorous work has a point, though I’d probably enjoy it more when each task feels like it’s building toward the car or garage I want. If I’m doing a delivery just to afford another part, that makes sense. If I’m doing it because I forgot what I was saving for, that’s mostly my problem.

The install your own parts idea is the part I’m most curious about, since it could make improving the car feel like a project instead of a shopping trip. It could also slow things down when I’m in the middle of a workout and would rather keep moving. I don’t know how much time each job asks from you, so I’d go in expecting a sim with pauses to think, not a race that keeps feeding you action.

The garage gives the longer term work a clear target, and I can see the appeal of slowly turning a scrappy setup into something you’re proud of. I’m not sure I’d want to spend every workout on that kind of careful tinkering, but having racing, deliveries, wood cutting, and the sugar shack to switch between should keep the routine from feeling like one long trip to the parts store. Probably.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, I’d treat the driving as the part that needs the most care, especially when you’re trying to race at night and there’s more pressure to steer cleanly. Full controller support is a real plus, but a controller doesn’t stop your hands from moving with your stride, and racing is where that little wobble could turn into a bad corner. I’d keep the pace comfortable rather than trying to turn the workout into a personal record.

The slower jobs seem much more forgiving. Wood cutting or pizza delivery sounds like a better match for steady movement than a race, because I’m not expecting the same quick corrections or close attention to every turn. I’d use those activities when I want to keep the workout going and leave the more delicate part installation for a break or the end. There’s no shame in doing garage work while standing still. The garage will remain there.

A bike would probably make the driving easier to manage, since your upper body can stay more settled while your legs keep going, but I wouldn’t call this only a bike game. On a treadmill, I think you can still get plenty out of the slower work, and the trick is not to let the racing push you into looking down at the controller every few seconds. I’d skip night racing while moving if I noticed myself getting too focused on the screen.

I also wouldn’t plan a workout around finishing a particular job, since the hands on car work and the money making jobs sound like they can break up the pace. That’s not necessarily bad. A change from driving to cutting wood may be enough to keep me interested, and if it isn’t, you can pick a different activity instead of treating the whole game like one long race.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum asks for a very old graphics card by today’s standards, so I’d check the graphics hardware first, especially if you’re using a laptop with integrated graphics. You don’t need a recent gaming machine on paper, and the one gigabyte install is not going to crowd out much else.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Mon Bazou sounds like a good choice for a relaxed bike workout or an easy treadmill session, especially if you enjoy earning your way toward a car you’ve built yourself. Skip the night races while you’re moving if they take too much focus, and give the slower jobs a try.
