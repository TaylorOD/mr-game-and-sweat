Starting an 18th century Ukrainian town sounds like calm treadmill company, but Ostriv keeps asking you to decide where people should live and work, and how the town is supposed to keep itself going. I think it fits a steady walk better than a run, and I’d keep the pace easy.

## Gameplay

What I like about Ostriv is that building a town isn’t just arranging buildings until the screen looks nice. You’re trying to make a place that functions, so where you put homes, workplaces and markets matters, and the decisions start connecting to each other. I like that the streets can grow in a less rigid way than a grid city builder, because I don’t need every village to look like I planned it with graph paper.

That flexibility also gives you more little decisions than I’d want during a hard workout. You can be thinking about where to build, then realize that the people who need to do the work also need somewhere to live, and suddenly you’re managing the town instead of paying attention to your legs. I think that’s a good kind of busy when you want something thoughtful to play, but it isn’t the kind of game that pulls you forward every second.

The production side is where I’d probably settle in. Setting up farms and workplaces, then trying to get the right goods where they need to go, gives you a clear reason to care about how the town is laid out. It’s satisfying in a practical way, like arranging a kitchen so you stop walking back and forth for the same spoon. I don’t know that I’d want to do all of that while jogging.

You’re also taking the role of governor, which means the town’s progress comes from a bunch of small choices rather than one big heroic moment. That suits Ostriv, and I can see why someone who enjoys a slow city builder would happily spend time adjusting things until the settlement feels right. It also means there can be stretches where you’re looking over the town and deciding what to change next, which is not much help if you need the game to keep your attention without asking for any thought.

Since it’s in Early Access, I’d go in expecting a game that’s still being developed, not a finished package with every edge smoothed down. That doesn’t put me off by itself, but it does make me want to know what’s currently in the game before buying it. A town can be a long project even when everything is working exactly as intended.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d use this for a slow walk or an easy ride, not a run where your hands are bouncing around and you’re trying to place buildings accurately. The game’s pace is mostly yours to set, which is a nice fit for exercising, but managing a town still takes enough attention that I wouldn’t expect to zone out and let the belt carry the whole workout.

The careful placement is what gives me pause. Roads and buildings are a big part of making the settlement feel like yours, and I’d rather place them deliberately than drop things wherever the cursor happens to land while I’m moving. You can take your time with a plan, but planning is still the activity. My calves can keep going while I think, probably, but I wouldn’t ask them to make the decisions.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information here, so I’d plan around mouse and keyboard rather than count on a gamepad. That makes the treadmill setup less convenient, since reaching for a mouse while walking is its own small coordination project. On a bike, I’d expect it to be more manageable if the controls are within reach, and I’d pause or slow down whenever I wanted to place buildings carefully.

I’d skip trying to play at a pace where you have to react quickly, because there’s no prize for making a crooked road with your thumb while your whole body is moving. A relaxed session seems much more plausible, especially if you’re comfortable stopping to work out a plan before you carry on. It’s not a game I’d pick when the exercise itself needs my attention.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest, and the graphics requirement is simply support for OpenGL 4.3, so you don’t need to start by shopping for a high end gaming computer. I’d pay more attention to how the game runs as your town grows, since the store page notes that larger settlements need more system resources. A tiny town may be easy on the machine, but it won’t stay tiny forever.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Ostriv makes sense for someone who wants a thoughtful city builder during an easy walk or bike ride, and I’d skip it for a run or a workout where you don’t want to manage a mouse. If you enjoy taking your time over a town, this could be a nice fit.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
