I’d try Dead Frontier 2 on an easy treadmill walk before I tried it while running, because stopping to aim at infected and rummage for supplies sounds like a lot to ask of a bouncing thumb. It seems better suited to a steady pace where you can pay attention without your legs filing a complaint.

## Gameplay

The scavenging is what makes Dead Frontier 2 sound appealing to me, even though I think it could also slow a workout down. You’re searching the remains of society for supplies, and that gives the horror a practical edge: I’d care about what’s around the next corner because it might be dangerous, or because I’m running short on something useful. That’s a better reason to keep exploring than a map full of little markers telling me to go places.

The infected lurking around corners fit the setup, but they’re also why I wouldn’t call this a game you can play on autopilot. I’d expect to keep checking where I’m going and be ready to react when one shows up, and that sounds engaging while I’m walking. If I’m trying to run hard, though, I can picture my attention going to the screen while my feet continue doing whatever they’ve decided to do. That’s not my favorite arrangement.

Improving your skills gives the scavenging a longer purpose, and I like that there’s progress to work toward beyond just getting through the next stretch. Trading with other players is a nice change of pace from searching buildings, but I’d probably do that part before starting a workout or after I’m done. I don’t need to be standing there comparing supplies while the treadmill waits for me to make up my mind.

The MMO part makes the world feel less like a series of isolated horror levels, which I think could be a big part of the appeal. It also means I wouldn’t count on being able to stop everything whenever I want to deal with a setting or take a drink. I don’t know how much time you’ll spend managing supplies compared with fighting infected, and that balance matters here because one of those is much easier to do while moving.

So, I’d expect a loop of exploring, watching for danger, and deciding whether the next bit of scavenging is worth the risk. That sounds like a good way to keep my mind occupied on a walk, but a slower stretch of searching could make a harder workout feel longer. I can lose focus just reading a menu at home. It happens.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The aiming is what I’d think about first, not the horror. A controller may be usable through Steam Input, but I wouldn’t assume the controls will feel as natural as a game built around a controller, especially when you need to aim at an infected while also moving. I’d sort out the controls while sitting down, then see how they feel at a walking pace before I tried anything faster.

I’d pick the treadmill for a gentle walk, or the bike if I wanted a steadier place to deal with aiming. The game sounds like it asks you to look carefully and react when infected appear, and my hands are more predictable when my whole body isn’t bouncing along. I’m not saying the bike makes the aiming easy. It just sounds like fewer moving parts, and I have enough of those already.

The online setup also makes me cautious about using it for an intense workout. Since this is an MMO, I wouldn’t plan around pausing whenever I need to adjust my position or catch my breath, and I’d rather not discover that in the middle of a tense encounter. I’d keep the pace easy and skip trading until I was off the machine.

I’d also avoid starting with a workout where I’m already tired, because that’s when I’m most likely to miss something on screen and then blame the infected for being in the place I was looking. I don’t know that this is a game I’d want during a hard run. For a careful walk, though, the searching and threat of something around the corner could keep me engaged.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older computer, not a machine with a recent high end graphics card, though the game may need to be set to Low. I’d look at the graphics card first, then make sure the computer can handle the online connection without getting in the way. It doesn’t sound especially demanding on paper.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Dead Frontier 2 sounds like a decent workout option for someone who likes survival horror and wants a slow, attentive session rather than a fast run. I’d try it on an easy walk after checking the controller setup, and I’d skip it if I wanted to play without thinking about my aim.
