This seems better suited to an easy bike ride than a treadmill, because hunting smaller fish sounds calm until you’re trying to turn toward one while your hands are bouncing. I’d give it a try when I wanted something simple to watch, not when I needed the game to do all the work of keeping me busy.

## Gameplay

The appeal is pretty plain, and I mean that as a compliment. You hunt and eat other fish, then grow into something larger, so there’s an immediate reason to keep moving and looking around. I think that gives the game a nice little pull, because even a small fish has a job to do. It’s not complicated.

I also like the scale of the goal. You’re not trying to remember a long story while running, and there aren’t pages of dialogue waiting for you to stop and read. You’re looking for something you can eat and trying not to become lunch yourself, which is a fairly clear plan even when my brain is busy counting steps. The food chain does most of the explaining.

The part I’m less sure about is how long that loop stays interesting. I can see the appeal of starting small and gradually becoming a bigger threat, but hunting fish can also become a lot of swimming around hoping the next target is the right size. I don’t know how much variety there is in the full experience, so I wouldn’t promise you that every stretch will feel different. Sometimes lunch is just lunch.

I think this could be good for an easy workout because the goal is active without asking you to keep track of a pile of systems. Still, you’re making choices about what to chase and what to avoid, and those choices are the game, so it may stop feeling relaxing if you want something you can half ignore. I’d expect to be more interested when the size difference between you and your target is obvious. That’s easy to understand at a glance.

The Early Access label is worth keeping in mind, especially if you’re expecting a polished game with a lot of carefully paced variety. The basic idea makes sense to me, and it doesn’t need a complicated explanation to be fun, but I wouldn’t treat that as a guarantee that every part is equally finished or engaging. I’d come in for the fish eating, not a deep simulation of ocean life.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, I’d start at a pace where I can still pay attention to where I’m steering, because the game depends on finding a target and getting close enough to eat it. That sounds manageable when the fish you want is nearby, but I’d expect it to take more focus when I have to turn around or avoid something larger. I’m not looking to jog into the aquarium wall.

A bike sounds a little more forgiving here, since I could keep pedaling while giving the screen a closer look, but I wouldn’t call that an automatic win. The game still asks you to steer toward prey instead of just letting the action come to you, and a missed turn could mean spending time searching again. I’d keep the resistance comfortable and the pace easy enough that I’m not trying to aim with my whole upper body.

I can’t confirm native controller support from the information I have, so I’d check that before starting a workout rather than finding out after I’m already moving. If you can get a controller working cleanly, I think the simple hunt and eat goal has a chance of feeling fine with slightly loose inputs. If the controls make you rely on precise mouse movement, that changes my answer quite a bit.

I’d skip any attempt to make this a hard run where you’re pushing pace and trying to react quickly at the same time. There’s no reason to turn fish hunting into a coordination exercise I didn’t sign up for. I’d choose an easy pace, keep the controls within comfortable reach, and stop playing when I’m spending more time wrestling the camera than looking for food.

## Computer Requirements

This looks like a modest PC game, though I’d check the graphics card first since the minimum calls for DirectX 11 and at least 2 GB of video memory. It doesn’t ask for much storage or system memory, so the graphics side is the part I’d pay attention to.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Feed and Grow: Fish seems like a reasonable choice for an easy workout if you enjoy a simple hunt and grow loop, and I’d be more comfortable trying it on a bike than while running. I’d check controller support before buying it for exercise, and skip it if you want a game that needs almost no attention.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
