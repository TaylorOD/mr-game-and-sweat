Ventura Bay gives you plenty to do between races, which sounds perfect for a workout until a police chase asks you to steer accurately while the treadmill is also moving your hands around. I’d play this on a bike first, or keep the treadmill pace easy.

## Gameplay

Need for Speed works best when it gets you from one race to the next without making you sit through too much story, and most of the time it does. You can race, drift, chase down a time, or just drive around the city, so there’s usually something to focus on besides counting minutes. That helps.

The five story paths, including Speed, Style, Build, Crew, and Outlaw, give you different kinds of events and reasons to mess with your car. I like having a bit of variety, though the story scenes can interrupt the driving just when you’ve settled into a rhythm. They’re not the sort of thing I’d want to watch while trying to keep a steady pace, and I’d treat them as a reason to slow down or take a break.

Drifting is a big part of the game’s personality, and I think it feels good when you get a turn right, but it also asks for more control than just holding the accelerator and pointing toward the next street. The wet roads and nighttime setting look great, which is a little unfortunate when a sharp corner is coming up and you’re busy looking at the reflections. The road is still there.

Police chases give the driving some urgency, and that can be fun when you’re sitting still, but I wouldn’t want every workout session to turn into a long escape because I missed a turn while pedaling. There’s a lot of city to roam through, and I like that you aren’t stuck repeating one kind of event, but the open world can also leave you driving between activities instead of doing them. Sometimes that’s a nice breather. Sometimes it’s just more road.

Building up your car gives the game a satisfying reason to keep going, though I’d be careful not to let tuning menus eat the whole workout. I don’t know that I’d want to make a dozen little adjustments when I’m already tired, and the game makes it easy to spend time thinking about the car instead of actually driving it. I’d pick a setup I enjoy, make changes when something is genuinely bothering me, and get back out there.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The biggest adjustment is steering, because most races ask for quick turns and small corrections while you’re also trying to keep your legs moving. Full controller support is a real plus, and the basic act of accelerating and steering is easy to understand, but controller support doesn’t make a tight corner any less tight. On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace comfortable and avoid trying to drive like I’m racing for a trophy.

A bike seems like the easier fit to me, since your hands can stay on the controller without your whole body bouncing around as much. I’d still expect to miss turns sometimes, especially when traffic, a race rival, and a police car all show up at once. That’s a lot of little decisions for something you’re doing while exercising.

Free roaming around Ventura Bay is probably the most relaxed way to start, and I’d pick regular races over police escapes if I wanted to keep the workout predictable. Drifting events sound like a fun change, but they need deliberate timing, so I’d save them for when I’m focused rather than when I’m trying to zone out. I can see the appeal of a dramatic chase, but I also know how quickly I’d forget to pedal while trying not to hit a barrier. Not ideal.

I don’t know whether the game’s story interruptions would bother you as much as they bother me, but I’d plan around them instead of expecting a continuous race for the whole session. Get through a scene when you’re ready, then start an event once you’re back in the mood to pay attention. The game gives you enough options to do that.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum asks for an older dedicated graphics card, so you don’t need a new gaming computer just to get started. I’d check that your graphics card has the required memory and that the computer can run a 64 bit version of Windows, because the car can wait while the settings menu catches up.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Need for Speed is a fun choice if you want a lot of driving and don’t mind pausing between events, but the sharper races and police chases make it less suited to a hard treadmill run. I’d suggest it to someone who can keep a comfortable pace and likes tuning cars as much as racing them.

### Overall Score: 2.67 out of 3
