I’d save Pacify for an easy walk or a steady bike ride, because collecting dolls while a supernatural creature is looking for you is a lot more fun when you can still steer calmly. Trying to sprint through the scary parts sounds like a good way to make the treadmill the second thing chasing you.

## Gameplay

The doll collecting gives you a simple job to focus on, and I think that works well for a horror game. You explore, look for the right dolls, and try to pacify the creature before it catches you, which means you’re usually doing something instead of waiting through long stretches of story. Then the creature turns up, and your careful little scavenger hunt becomes a different kind of activity.

I like that the game makes you care about what you pick up, rather than turning every room into a pile of things to collect without thinking. You’re trying to weaken and capture something that can also interrupt your plans, so there’s a nice push and pull between getting the task done and keeping yourself alive. I’d probably find the hiding and backtracking a little repetitive after a while, but it gives the chase some shape.

The different levels having their own stories and monsters helps, because the game isn’t asking you to spend the whole time doing the exact same haunted house routine. There are also multiple endings, which gives your choices some weight. I don’t know how much I’d want to replay every area just to see all of them, but I like that the game leaves room for more than one outcome.

Playing with friends changes the tone a lot. Up to six people can play together, and the proximity voice chat has effects, which sounds funny in the way that gets less funny once somebody starts whispering because the monster is nearby. I’d be curious whether a larger group makes the scares easier or just makes everybody talk over each other. Probably both.

The game’s horror setup is appealing because it gives you a task to do while you’re nervous, instead of asking you to sit still and absorb a long scene. Still, when you’re being hunted, there’s more pressure than I’d want from a game I picked specifically to keep moving. I think it’s a good balance at a desk. On a workout, I’d want the pace to stay gentle.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The full controller support is a big help, and most of the basic work sounds manageable with a controller in your hands. You can move around and search without needing to do tiny mouse movements, which is the sort of thing I want when my arms and legs are both busy. I’d use a controller and keep the workout easy enough that I can pay attention to where I’m going.

The chase parts are where I’d slow down. You need to react when the creature finds you, and turning to get away or finding a place to hide could be awkward while you’re bouncing on a treadmill. On a bike, the steady pedaling might make that easier, but the game still asks you to make quick decisions when you’re under pressure. I’d rather not discover that my emergency escape plan is steering into a wall.

I’d start with single player if I wanted to focus on the workout, since coordinating with a group over voice chat would take some attention away from both the game and the exercise. But co-op sounds like the more entertaining way to play, especially with the proximity effects, so I wouldn’t write it off. I’d try it on an easy ride first, and skip jogging until I know how much the chases throw me around.

There are stretches where you’re searching, and those should be comfortable at a gentle pace. Then you hear or see the monster and your brain tries to make a whole escape plan at once, which is more than I need from a run. I’d keep the pace low enough that stopping to think doesn’t become a negotiation with the treadmill.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You’ll want a gaming PC with a dedicated graphics card, rather than relying on integrated graphics, and the listed GTX 1050 or RX 580 level is the part I’d check first. That’s not demanding by current standards, but it does ask for more than a very old computer.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Pacify makes more sense for an easy workout than a hard one, and I’d suggest it to someone who likes horror and wants a task to keep them occupied between scares. If you mainly want a relaxed game that never asks for a quick reaction, this probably isn’t the one.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
