I think running a gas station could work as a workout game, because there’s always another small job waiting and you can put the controller down when you need to think. Gas Station Simulator has a lot of that, though I’d expect to spend more time managing a little pile of tasks than getting swept up in anything.

## Gameplay

The appeal, for me, is that the work keeps changing just enough to stop the station from feeling like one long checkout line. You clean and renovate the abandoned place, serve customers, refuel cars, and keep the shop stocked, and each job gives you a reason to move on to the next one. I think that kind of steady list of chores can be surprisingly good at taking your mind off a run, because you’re not waiting for a story scene to finish before you get to do something.

But the list can get busy, and that’s where the game might turn into a shift at work that you agreed to take for no clear reason. You’re trying to keep customers moving while also tending to the station, and I’d probably spend a lot of time deciding which problem to handle first. That does give the game some momentum, but it’s not the same as a game that keeps you moving with action. Sometimes you’re just thinking about what needs doing.

Renovating the gas station is a nice counterweight to the routine. Cleaning and repairing a place that starts out abandoned gives you a visible reason for all those small jobs, and I like that the station can gradually become a place you actually want to run. I don’t know if I’d care about every little upgrade equally, but having the bigger goal in the background makes restocking shelves feel a little less like you’ve been assigned inventory duty.

The customer service tasks are where I’d expect the game to ask for more attention. Refueling cars and working the shop are easy to understand, but doing them while other demands are piling up could make me stop paying attention to my pace. It’s the sort of game where I’d probably tell myself I’m just going to finish one more thing, and then there are two more things sitting there. I have been tricked by a list before.

I’d play this when I wanted a casual game with a clear routine, not when I wanted constant excitement. The small jobs give you a lot to do, and the renovation gives them a purpose, but managing the flow of customers can make the game feel more like keeping up than relaxing. That might be exactly what someone wants after a day of doing actual work. I’m not sure I’m that person every day.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d start at a comfortable pace and treat the game’s tasks like little stops along the way, because you can focus on one job at a time rather than having to react to an opponent or aim at something moving quickly. Full controller support is a big help here. Moving around the station, refueling cars, and handling the shop sounds manageable with a controller, though I’d expect aiming at small objects or navigating menus to take more focus than it would while sitting on a couch.

The part I’d watch is the rush of competing tasks. You might be serving customers, noticing the shelves need attention, and realizing the station still needs work, all while trying not to drift into the game so much that you forget you’re exercising. On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace easy enough that I could look down at the screen and make small adjustments without turning every shelf into a precision operation. It’s not a twitch game, but it can still ask you to concentrate.

A bike might feel a little more forgiving during the slower bits, since you can keep pedaling while you look around the station or decide what to do next. But I wouldn’t say you need a bike for it. The jobs are mostly deliberate, and there’s no reason to rush through the renovation just because the game has other work waiting. I’d skip trying to make every task happen at once. The station can wait a moment.

I’d also save any fiddly renovation or menu decisions for when you’re comfortable and settled into your pace, since those are the moments where I’d be most likely to stop pedaling or lose my rhythm. The actual service jobs sound like a better fit for moving along steadily. It gives you natural little chunks of play, although the game may keep handing you one more job when you were hoping to take it easy.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You’ll want a computer with a dedicated graphics card around the level of a GTX 960, so this isn’t aimed at a very old laptop running on integrated graphics. I’d check the graphics card first, then make sure the computer can run Windows 10 64 bit and has room for the game. The station needs a little space to open up.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Gas Station Simulator sounds like a decent workout companion if you enjoy steady chores, visible progress, and having a few tasks to choose between. I’d skip it if you need nonstop action to keep your mind off running, but for an easy pace and a controller in hand, I’d give it a try.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
