The Plaza in Tower Unite looks like the kind of place where you could keep moving and still get distracted by something every few minutes, which is either excellent workout design or a way to forget you were exercising. I’d start with Minigolf, not the social wandering.

## Gameplay

I think Tower Unite works best when you treat it like a collection of things to do with other people, rather than a game with one main activity you’re supposed to push through. The Plaza gives you a place to hang out, and the Game Worlds have modes like Minigolf, Ball Race, Zombie Massacre, and Virus. That variety is genuinely useful, because when one activity starts to feel like waiting for your turn, you can go do something else.

Minigolf is probably where I’d spend the most time. It gives you a clear little goal without asking you to remember a story or sit through a cutscene, and trying to judge a shot is a nice change from games where every problem is solved by running directly at it. The courses can make you pause and aim, though, so it’s not constant action. Sometimes the ball just sits there while you consider your options. It’s a golf ball.

Ball Race is a different kind of distraction, with movement and quick decisions that make it feel more like a little arcade challenge than a relaxing stroll. I like having that option when I want the game to ask more of me, but I wouldn’t want every workout session to turn into trying to make one tiny correction while my hands are bouncing around. I’d pick it when I’m alert and moving at a comfortable pace.

Zombie Massacre and Virus give you more direct action, which could be fun when you want to focus on surviving or chasing other players instead of looking around the Plaza. I don’t know that I’d want to make either one the default workout activity, because aiming and reacting can pull your attention away from your pace, and public multiplayer can change the mood depending on who’s there. Still, having a few modes that feel completely different is a big reason this game can hold your interest.

The social side is a real part of the appeal, and also the part I’d be most likely to skip when I’m trying to keep moving. Wandering around and seeing what other people are doing sounds pleasant, but it can also turn into standing still while you decide where to go next. There are no microtransactions, which I appreciate, and it’s nice that the game is built around activities rather than trying to sell you a new hat every time you turn around. I’d just go in with a plan.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A long walk through the Plaza seems like an easy way to start, but I think it could also be the easiest way to stop doing your workout without noticing. You’re free to explore, and there’s no urgent objective forcing you to keep up, so it should feel relaxed. I’d choose one activity before starting, then move to another when I’m ready, rather than expecting the game to keep me busy on its own.

Full controller support is a real plus here. You don’t have to reach for a mouse and keyboard every time you want to play, and Minigolf should be manageable on a treadmill or bike because you can take a moment to line up a shot. I’d avoid trying to play at a pace where every little movement has to be perfect, though. The holes are still asking you to aim, and a treadmill doesn’t make your thumbs any steadier.

Ball Race is the mode I’d be cautious about while moving. A fast section can ask for quick turns and timing, and that’s the sort of thing where you might start leaning toward the screen like the character can feel it. Zombie Massacre and Virus may also ask for more attention than I’d want during a harder run, especially when other players are involved. I’d save those for an easy pace or a bike session where I’m comfortable keeping my hands steady.

I’d skip the temptation to make the Plaza your whole workout, unless you’re happy with a relaxed session that has some standing around built into it. Minigolf is the sensible place to begin, and the faster modes are better as a change of pace than as the thing you play for the entire time. That’s enough variety for me.

## Computer Requirements

Tower Unite asks for a dedicated graphics card, and the store page warns that integrated graphics will have trouble, so I’d check the graphics hardware before anything else. A mid range card from the last several years should be the reasonable target, though the game’s storage needs can grow as new content arrives.

## Conclusion and Score

This makes sense for someone who wants a social game with several short activities and doesn’t mind choosing their own pace. I’d recommend starting with Minigolf, then trying the faster modes when you’re ready to pay closer attention.
