Building a vehicle out of bricks and then seeing what survives a collision sounds like a pretty good treadmill distraction, as long as you don’t expect to be constantly moving through the action. I’d treat Brick Rigs more like a creative break between stretches of jogging than a game that keeps your attention on every step.

## Gameplay

The physics sandbox is the appeal, and I think it’s a good one. You can build creations, drive them around, crash them, and see how much of your design is still in one piece afterward. That makes for a game where you can set your own level of seriousness, which is useful because my engineering instincts would probably amount to adding more bricks until the vehicle looks sturdy.

The open ended part is also where the game can lose a little momentum. I could see spending a while adjusting a creation and thinking about what to try next, and that’s fun when you want a project but not quite the same kind of distraction as a game that’s always throwing something at you. If you’re trying to keep a steady pace, a long stretch of planning could turn into a long stretch of walking while staring at a build menu.

Destruction gives all that tinkering a reason to exist, and the physics can make a simple collision feel like a decent payoff. I like that the same creation can be something you build carefully or something you make just to see what happens when it hits another object. Sometimes the right design goal is “this should probably fall apart.” That’s a plan.

Roleplay and playing with other people could give you more to do than building and crashing on your own, but I wouldn’t count on other players to make every session lively. The game’s appeal depends a lot on what you feel like making and how much you enjoy messing around with it. I don’t know how long I’d stay interested in a build if I didn’t have a clear idea for it, but I’d be happy to try a few silly designs and see where they go.

There’s enough freedom here to keep a curious player occupied, though the quiet parts are part of the deal. You aren’t being pushed along by a story or a steady stream of objectives, so you need to bring at least a little of your own plan. If you want a game that keeps you busy every second, this may feel like waiting for your own idea to arrive.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d start by building before exercising, because placing and adjusting parts sounds like the kind of work where you want your hands and eyes settled. Once you have a creation ready, driving it around and seeing what the physics do seems much easier to fit into a relaxed pace. The game gives you room to mess around, which matters when your thumb isn’t perfectly still.

I wouldn’t assume the building side is comfortable with a controller without checking how your setup handles the controls first. A game can be fine for driving and still ask you to do fiddly work when you want to make changes, and Brick Rigs has both sides. I’d keep the building for when you’re stopped, then stick with driving and casual destruction while moving. That’s probably the least annoying way to do it.

A bike seems more forgiving if you’re planning to spend time making adjustments, since you can pause your effort without having to step off a moving belt, but I wouldn’t say you need one to enjoy the game. On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace easy and avoid trying to build while jogging. If you’re in a session with friends, the unpredictable creations might hold your attention better than solo tinkering, though I can’t tell how much the group’s plans would shape the pace. It could be a lot of waiting for somebody to finish building. I’ve done enough waiting around in menus.

## Computer Requirements

You’ll want a Windows computer with a dedicated graphics card around the level of an older mid range gaming card, rather than expecting integrated graphics to do the job comfortably. I’d check the graphics card first, since the listed minimum is more demanding than a basic office computer.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Brick Rigs makes sense for you if building vehicles and causing physics driven chaos sounds fun, and you don’t mind supplying some of your own goals. I’d skip it if you need a constant stream of action or want to build while running.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
