The SP Battles sound like a pretty good way to keep your attention on a workout, because you’re trying to outlast a rival on Tokyo’s night highways instead of watching a long story unfold. I’d keep the pace comfortable, though, since a close race can make you start steering like the treadmill is the thing you’re trying to beat.

## Gameplay

The Spirit Point idea gives the races a clear purpose beyond just driving fast: you and the other driver are trying to drain each other’s points, so every duel has a little pressure built into it. I think that’s a useful fit for exercise gaming, where I want something to focus on but don’t want to stop moving every few minutes to watch people talk. It sounds like a race can stay interesting without needing a huge pile of extra explanation.

The RPG label makes me expect some progression around the rivals and your customized car, though I’m not going to pretend I know how deep that part goes. Customizing a car is a nice reason to care about the next race, and I like that the basic goal stays easy to understand. You’re trying to become the fastest, and there’s a car involved. That’s plenty to get started.

I don’t know how varied the rival duels feel over a long stretch, and that matters more here than it would in a game with a big story. Night highway racing can be a great steady rhythm, but if every encounter asks you to do the same thing in the same way, I’d probably start noticing the miles more than the game. Still, the head to head setup gives each race a small finish line to aim at, which is better than just driving around until I wonder what the point is.

A lot of racing games are easy to pick up and still give you enough to pay attention to, and this sounds like it’s in that useful middle ground. I’d expect the races to keep you busy for most of a workout, while the car customization gives you a reason to look forward to getting back on the road. There may be some slower thinking between races, but that seems manageable. I’ve sat through longer pauses waiting for a microwave.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A close SP Battle could pull your focus away from your stride, especially if you’re pushing the pace and trying to keep up with a rival at the same time. I’d use it for an easy run or a steady ride, where a little concentration is welcome but missing one input won’t feel like a disaster. If you’re doing hard intervals, I’d save the duels for the recovery part.

The full controller support is a big plus, and racing generally asks for fewer complicated inputs than a game that has you aiming all over the screen. But you’ll still need to steer and react, and I’d expect a tight race to be less forgiving of loose thumb movements than a relaxed driving game. On a bike, your hands can stay pretty settled. On a treadmill, I’d be more cautious about trying to make a tiny correction while your whole body is bouncing around.

I don’t know how much time the game asks you to spend between races, so I’d be ready to slow down or step off if the customization and progression screens become a place where you need to think carefully. That’s not a reason to skip the game, just a reason not to treat every minute as uninterrupted exercise. The highway racing is the part I’d want during the workout. The rest can wait until the belt stops.

I’d also start with the recommended low graphics setting instead of treating the settings menu like a chance to make the computer sweat too. You don’t need to prove anything to a car, and you definitely don’t need to prove anything to a graphics slider. Keep the controls on a full controller, find a pace where you can make small steering adjustments, and give it a try before using it for a harder session.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This needs a dedicated graphics card in the general range of an older entry level gaming card, not just integrated graphics, and 16 GB of memory is part of the minimum. I’d check the graphics card first, then plan on using the recommended low setting at 1080p, because this isn’t asking your computer to win the race too.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Tokyo Xtreme Racer sounds like a good choice for someone who wants racing to occupy their attention without turning exercise into a nonstop reaction test. I’d point you toward the night highway duels for a steady workout, and away from it if you need a game that lets you relax your hands completely.
