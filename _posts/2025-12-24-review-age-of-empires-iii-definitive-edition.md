I’d save Age of Empires III: Definitive Edition for a slow walk or an easy spin, because trying to jog while selecting musketeers and sending them across a map sounds like doing two fiddly jobs with one brain. The strategy is good, but it asks for more attention than I want from a harder workout.

## Gameplay

The fun is in getting an army together, deciding where it should go, and then watching a plan that seemed reasonable turn into a different plan because the enemy showed up. I think that back and forth gives the game a nice pace when you’re sitting down, since you’re not just waiting for one big attack to happen. But it does ask you to look at the map and think, and that’s not always a great fit when your feet are keeping their own schedule.

Building a town and gathering resources are the quieter parts, and I don’t mind those in a strategy game because they give you a minute to figure out what you want to do. Then you have to keep checking in on your workers and your army, and a minute turns into three small jobs you forgot you were doing. I’d probably lose track of one of them. That’s on me.

The Home City shipments are a nice idea because they let you bring in units or resources without making every decision feel like the same old queue. I like having another way to shape a match, though picking cards and managing an army adds to the amount of planning you’re doing. I don’t know why I always think I’ll remember a small setup detail once the match gets busy, but I probably wouldn’t.

There are campaigns and historical battles if you want something with a bit more direction, and co op battles give you a way to play with someone else. That variety makes it easier to find a kind of match that suits you, rather than having every session feel like the same skirmish. Still, I’d choose a single player match or a cooperative one for exercise and leave anything competitive for when I can pay closer attention. I don’t want my workout turning into a long pause to reconsider a bad decision.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d start on an easy setting, pick a single player match, and keep the workout gentle enough that I can look at the map without bouncing around. The game has stretches where you’re building up and figuring out what to do, which are manageable, but once units are moving and you’re trying to direct them, you need to be pretty deliberate. A relaxed pace makes more sense than trying to turn every match into interval training.

This is built around selecting units and clicking on parts of the map, so I’d plan on mouse and keyboard rather than assume a controller will feel natural. A controller mapping might be usable, but pointing at a specific group and telling it where to go is the kind of task that can get awkward when your hands are moving. I’m not saying it can’t be done. I’d just test the controls before starting a workout.

On a bike, I think you get a little more room to slow down and make a careful move without your whole body bouncing along with it. A treadmill still makes sense if you’re walking or doing a steady, easy jog, but I wouldn’t try to manage a complicated fight while running hard. You’re going to be watching the screen a lot, and the map does not get easier to read because you’re out of breath.

I’d skip multiplayer matches where other people are relying on you to react quickly, and I’d keep the difficulty low enough that pausing to think doesn’t make the whole match feel like a chore. There’s plenty to do without adding pressure. I’d rather play one steady match than spend the workout trying to issue perfect orders with a thumb that’s moving around.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are fairly modest, including integrated Intel HD 4400 graphics, but that’s listed for 720p rather than 4K. I’d check the graphics hardware and available storage first, since the game needs 42 GB free, which is a decent amount of room for a strategy game. The remaster doesn’t mean every computer needs to be a new gaming machine.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This makes sense for you if you enjoy strategy and can keep your workout easy while you plan and manage a match. I’d skip it for a fast run, but for a walk or steady ride, it gives you plenty to think about.
