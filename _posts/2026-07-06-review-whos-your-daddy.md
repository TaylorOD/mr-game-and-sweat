I’d play Who’s Your Daddy?! on an easy workout, not one where I’m trying to hit a pace, because keeping a baby away from dangerous household stuff sounds funny right up until I’m steering badly and the baby is doing better than me. It seems like a game where you’ll stay busy, but probably not very calm.

## Gameplay

The premise is ridiculous in a way that actually gives everyone something to do. One player is a clueless dad trying to protect the baby, and the baby is trying to get into trouble with the many ominous objects around the house. I like that the joke is also the whole job. You’re not waiting through a long story to get to it.

Playing the dad sounds like a frantic round of noticing one hazard, going to deal with it, and realizing the baby has found another one while you were busy. The baby’s job seems a lot simpler, which is probably why it appeals to the part of me that would rather cause a small problem than manage several small problems. I don’t know if that stays funny for everyone, but with friends it has the right setup for people yelling useful things too late.

The wacky physics seem like a good fit for this. A serious parenting simulator would be a very strange way to spend a workout, and this sounds like it knows that a baby moving around a house is funnier when the whole situation is a little out of control. There are over 67 household items to worry about, so you aren’t just standing in one place waiting for the other player to do something. The house sounds busy.

I think the competitive setup is doing a lot of the work here. With up to seven friends, the difference between being the person who has to prevent the disaster and the person trying to make one should give a group enough to talk about without needing a lot of rules explained first. I’d expect the best moments to come from somebody getting distracted by one obvious danger and missing the next one. That’s a pretty simple joke, but simple is fine.

I don’t know whether the chaos would stay entertaining for a long stretch if the people playing weren’t enjoying the same kind of silly competition. There doesn’t seem to be much reason to play this for a careful story or a slow, thoughtful challenge, and that’s not a complaint so much as a limit on who it’s for. If your group likes slapstick and a bit of friendly blame, the idea makes sense.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d keep the workout easy enough that I can watch the whole room, because the game asks you to react to what the other player is doing and not just move forward at a steady pace. A baby getting into trouble while you’re distracted for a second sounds funny when you’re sitting down. While moving, it sounds like a reason to let the treadmill slow down before you start trying to rescue anyone.

The dad role seems like the harder one for exercise. You’re trying to keep track of the baby and the hazards, and I’d expect to need quick attention shifts to deal with whatever is happening in the house. On a bike, where your hands can stay steadier, I’d have a better chance of keeping up. On a treadmill, I’d choose an easy pace and accept that I’m probably going to miss something.

The baby role sounds a little more forgiving, since the goal is to cause trouble rather than carefully protect the whole house, but it still means moving around and reacting to the other player. I’d give it a try with a controller only after checking that the controls work comfortably in your setup. I can’t confirm full controller support, and I wouldn’t want you finding that out once the baby is already loose.

I’d play with people who don’t mind a messy round, and I’d skip it if I wanted a quiet solo game that lets me zone out. There’s no gentle rhythm here that I can see. The other players are part of what makes it work, and they’re also part of why you’ll have to pay attention.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest, and the listed graphics options include Intel HD 4600, so you don’t need a recent high end gaming computer just to consider it. I’d check your graphics hardware first, but this is not asking you to build a computer that can also protect the baby.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is for you if your group wants silly competitive chaos and you’re willing to keep the workout easy enough to follow it. I’d skip it for a focused run or a solo session, but with friends it sounds like a good way to make household hazards somebody else’s problem for a while.
