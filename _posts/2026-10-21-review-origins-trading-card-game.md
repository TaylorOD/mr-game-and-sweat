I think Origins Trading Card Game could give a treadmill workout something to keep your brain busy, because both players act at once and you’re trying to battle for three locations. I’d keep the pace comfortable, though, because a live match sounds like it could pull your attention away from your feet pretty quickly.

## Gameplay

The simultaneous play is what makes this sound more interesting to me than a card game where you wait politely for the other person to finish. You’re making your plan while your rival is making theirs, so I’d expect each choice to feel a little less settled than it would in a turn based game. That’s good for keeping a match moving, but it also sounds like you need to pay attention instead of treating the game like something you can glance at between songs.

The locations give the match a clear thing to care about, and I like that the goal is to battle for three instead of just making one giant pile of cards and hoping it wins. With more than 100 rotating locations, I’d expect the setting to change what I want from a deck, which is a nice reason to adjust your plans instead of repeating the same match. I could also see that becoming a lot to think about if you’re trying to learn the game while keeping an eye on your running form.

Building decks around reimagined legends like Robin Hood and Mulan sounds fun in a plain, useful way. I’d rather have a roster that gives me ideas for different strategies than a pile of cards that only exists to make me open another pack. The store page says every competitive card is free, which is a big deal for a free to play game, and I like that the paid pack angle is rare, limited edition variants you can trade on Steam instead.

I don’t know how much variety the current Early Access version has beyond what the store page describes, so I wouldn’t buy into it expecting a finished game with everything settled. But the basic idea does make sense to me, and a rotating set of places could keep the deck building from turning into one solved routine. There’s a lot of room for that to work. There’s also room for me to make a deck that looks smart and then discover I misunderstood the whole plan.

I’d give the game time if you enjoy figuring out what your opponent might do while you’re deciding what to do yourself. If you mostly want action that lets you switch your brain off, I think the strategy part will feel like an extra chore, and not the kind you can leave until later.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The live competition is what I’d think about before starting a workout. Both players moving at once sounds like a good way to keep a match active, but I’d treat it as a game that needs your attention until the match ends, not something you can pause whenever your stride gets uneven. I wouldn’t start with a hard run and expect to make clever decisions at the same time. I have enough trouble deciding what to watch on television.

I can’t confirm controller support from the store details, so I’d check that before setting up the bike or treadmill. Card games can be comfortable with a controller, but I wouldn’t promise it here without knowing. If it does support one, I’d still expect the live PvP and the simultaneous choices to take more focus than a quiet solo deck builder.

For a first workout, I’d play at an easy pace and try a short session rather than planning a whole ride around it. I’d skip it on a day when you’re already tired or trying to hit a tough interval, because losing track of a match and losing track of your pace at the same time sounds like an avoidable little mess. On a bike, I’d expect the steady motion to be easier to pair with the thinking than a fast treadmill pace, but I’d still keep the effort low.

The rotating locations might help keep your attention on the game, and the deck building gives you something to consider before you start moving. I’d do that setup while you’re stopped, though. Trying to work out a new strategy once the competition is underway sounds like reading a menu while somebody is waiting for you to order.

## Computer Requirements

This looks like it should run on an older gaming computer, and the listed graphics cards are from a much older generation than today’s high end cards. I’d check the graphics card first if you’re unsure, but the storage requirement is small enough that the game isn’t asking you to clear out half your computer.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Origins Trading Card Game sounds like a reasonable choice for someone who enjoys competitive card strategy and can keep their workout easy while they play. I’d skip it for a hard run, and I’d confirm controller support before building your setup around it.
