Don’t start Kentucky Route Zero on a treadmill unless you’re planning to stop and read every few minutes, because the quiet, careful parts are the reason it works and also the reason exercise gets in the way.

## Gameplay

I think this is a beautiful game, but not because it keeps you busy every second. You follow Conway, a delivery driver looking for an address on a secret highway beneath Kentucky, and the game takes its time letting you meet the people and places along the way. The conversations are the point. You can’t really treat them like background noise and expect the same thing from it.

The writing has a strange, plainspoken quality that I really like, and the magical parts are allowed to sit beside ordinary problems like work, debt, and trying to find somewhere to go. You might be in the Bureau of Reclaimed Spaces one minute and thinking about whether a place can still belong to anyone after it’s been taken apart, and then the game moves on without underlining the thought for you. That restraint is a big part of why it works.

There isn’t combat or a rush of little tasks to keep your hands occupied. You make dialogue choices, move through locations, and spend a lot of time paying attention to what people say. I don’t know that you need to agree with every character to care about them, and I’d rather have that than a bunch of action pasted over the story. It’s a quiet game.

The acts and interludes also give it room to change shape, so it isn’t just one long conversation in a cave. Some scenes are funny in a low key way, some are sad, and some are strange enough that you may pause just to decide what you think you’re looking at. I’d be curious what someone makes of it on a first play, because it leaves a lot for you to sit with. That’s not fast.

The same patience that makes the game special can make it feel slow if you came looking for a steady stream of things to do. You can read at your own pace, but the pace is still mostly reading and thinking, and the game isn’t trying to turn that into a race. I wouldn’t cut through the dialogue just to make a workout feel more active. There’s not much game left if you do that.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is helpful, and the controls aren’t asking for quick aim or precise timing, but I still wouldn’t play this while moving. You’ll be reading dialogue, choosing what to say, and taking in small details in the scenes, and I feel like the treadmill would turn that into a constant negotiation between keeping your eyes on the game and keeping your feet where they belong. I’d probably end up slowing the belt down until it was mostly a desk with a moving floor.

A stationary bike is less awkward than a treadmill for this, but it doesn’t fix the attention problem. The game often gives you a conversation or a quiet scene where the payoff is noticing how a person says something, not just getting to the next place, and it seems like a shame to miss that because you’re checking your cadence. You could pause between scenes, but then you’re doing a start and stop workout around a story that already moves slowly.

The route map and wandering between locations might sound like easy parts to fit around exercise, but even those transitions belong to the mood of the game. I wouldn’t skip them to get to more dialogue, and I wouldn’t try to read the dialogue while jogging either. If you insist on trying it, use the controller and keep the pace gentle, but I’d expect this to work better with the machine turned off.

There’s no punishment for taking time, which is good, and it also means there’s no reason to force the game into a workout slot. I don’t know why so many games make a person feel like they need to be doing two things at once, but this one is better when you give it your full attention. The exercise can wait.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very low, so an old computer with basic graphics should be able to run it. I’d check that it can handle DirectX 9.0c, but this isn’t asking your computer to train for anything.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Kentucky Route Zero is thoughtful, odd, and worth giving your attention to, especially if you like stories that leave some space around their answers. It’s a poor workout companion, though. Play it at your desk.

### Overall Score: 1.67 out of 3
