A Wild West shooter with slow, powerful guns sounds like it should be easy to manage while you exercise, but Fistful of Frags asks you to aim carefully while other players are trying to put you down. I think it’s more fun as a game than as a treadmill companion, which is not quite the same thing.

## Gameplay

The appeal is pretty clear to me. You’re in a first person multiplayer gunfight, and the weapons have enough weight to them that landing a shot matters more than spraying at the general area and hoping the other person gets tired first. That gives the fights some character, and the Wild West setting helps it feel different from another modern shooter with the same rooms and guns. I’d be curious how long the slower pace feels good before I start wishing my revolver would hurry up.

The slow weapons are a real tradeoff, not just a detail in the description. I like the idea of a shooter where you have to take a breath and make a shot, but the game is still skill demanding and multiplayer based, so the other person may not politely wait while you find your aim. There’s a funny little mismatch in trying to play a deliberate gunfight while the person across from you is presumably treating it like a very serious afternoon.

I’d start with the less complicated shootout style of play and see how the pace feels before getting invested in anything more involved. Team fights sound like they could give you a little room to make mistakes, since you’re not the only person in the match trying to do something useful, but I wouldn’t assume that makes the aiming any less important. I don’t know how welcoming the player base is to someone figuring things out, and getting shot while you’re learning is still getting shot.

There’s no long story getting between you and the action, which is a big advantage when you’re exercising. You can focus on the match instead of trying to remember a plot detail from before you started sweating. I do think the same constant pressure that makes it engaging also means you can’t drift off for a minute and expect everything to be fine. You have to be there.

I’d play it when I want a focused shooter and not when I’m hoping the game will gently carry me through a workout. The weapons sound like the part I’d enjoy most, because they give each fight a little pause and consequence, but I can also see that pause turning into frustration when I miss and someone else doesn’t. That’s a pretty ordinary problem to have with a shooter.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A moving belt and a competitive first person shooter are not natural friends, and Fistful of Frags leans into the part where you need to aim at another person who is trying to hit you back. I’d keep the treadmill speed comfortable and use this for an easy walk, not a run where every bounce makes the crosshair feel like it has its own plans. On a bike, you can keep your upper body steadier, but the match still needs your attention.

The fact that it’s multiplayer matters more than the gun’s slow pace here. You can’t count on a pause when you want to adjust your grip or take a second to settle in, and a mistake can mean losing the fight before you’ve had a chance to recover your focus. I don’t know whether every setup will feel good with a controller, so I’d check the controls and try a low stakes match before putting the belt in motion. I’d rather discover a bad layout while standing still.

If aiming feels manageable, I’d still avoid treating this as a game where you can zone out and let your legs do the work. The duels and team fights both ask you to watch what’s happening, turn quickly, and make a shot at the right moment. That can pull your attention away from your pace, especially once you care whether you win. My thumb would probably take the match more seriously than my lungs.

For exercise, I’d choose a slow walk and skip any attempt to push the intensity while you’re still learning the controls. If you’re on a bike, an easy steady pace seems more reasonable than trying to sprint through a match, because the aiming already gives you plenty to concentrate on. This could work in small doses, but I wouldn’t pick it for the workout where you need the game to be forgiving.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimums are old enough that the graphics card should not be the first thing you worry about on a computer from the last several years. I’d check that your internet connection is steady first, since this is a multiplayer shooter and a bad connection is a pretty unhelpful teammate. The requirements are modest.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Fistful of Frags sounds like a fun free shooter if you want Wild West gunfights and can give them your full attention, but I wouldn’t choose it for a hard treadmill run. Try it at an easy pace first, and leave it for later if aiming turns into work.
