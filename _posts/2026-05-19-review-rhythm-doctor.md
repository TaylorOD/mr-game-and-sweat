The heartbeat timing is what makes Rhythm Doctor tempting for a workout, because you can get into a rhythm without steering a character across half the screen. I’d try it on an easy pace first, though, because the game wants your attention in a very particular place.

## Gameplay

I think Rhythm Doctor has a great idea and actually follows through on it. You’re helping patients by pressing in time with their heartbeats, and each patient has a rhythm you have to learn while the music gives you something to listen for. That’s much more interesting to me than a game that asks you to tap along to a beat you already know, because you have to pay attention and adjust instead of just finding a groove and staying there. It keeps you busy.

The boss viruses that interfere with your rhythm are a good fit for the game, because they make the timing feel like something you’re figuring out rather than a button you’re pressing on autopilot. I like that the music can carry you along, but then you have to notice when the game is trying to throw you off. It feels a little like trying to clap along at a concert after someone keeps changing the song. Not impossible, but I’d look strange.

There’s a nice balance between repetition and learning here. You’re practicing a rhythm, but it belongs to a particular patient, so the repeated presses don’t feel like the game is asking you to do the same chore forever. I don’t know if every player will enjoy that kind of close attention to timing, because the whole appeal depends on noticing small changes in the beat, but I think it’s a good way to make a simple action feel like a real skill.

The game’s focus is also why I wouldn’t call it a casual thing to put on while you’re thinking about something else. Even if the controls themselves are simple, you still have to listen and react to the musical cues, and a distraction at the wrong moment can turn a clean rhythm into a bit of button mashing. That’s a fair trade for a rhythm game. I’d rather have it ask me to pay attention than spend a lot of time waiting around for the next part.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, the hardest part probably won’t be working the controller. It’ll be keeping your attention on the game while your feet are making their own beat underneath it, and Rhythm Doctor gives you a lot of reasons to listen closely. I’d start at a pace where I can still pay attention to the screen and the music without feeling like I’m trying to conduct an orchestra with my knees. That’s not a skill I have.

Full controller support is a real plus, since the game’s basic idea doesn’t ask for a lot of complicated movement across the controller. A bike seems a little easier to me because your upper body can stay fairly steady while you pedal, but I’d still expect the timing to take focus, especially when a virus starts messing with the beat. You can press one button at the wrong time from a chair just fine, so I wouldn’t assume the moving belt makes that part easier.

I’d play this on a gentle workout, not the kind where I’m trying to push my pace and pay attention to every detail at once. The music could help you settle into a steady cadence, but I wouldn’t try to match every heartbeat with every step, because the game’s rhythm is the thing you need to follow and your legs are already doing their own little project. I’d keep the speed comfortable and let the game be the part that needs concentration.

And I’d give myself permission to stop playing when the timing starts feeling fussy. That’s not a knock on the game, it’s just that missing a beat because you’re moving is less fun than missing one because the rhythm surprised you. I don’t know how much a person could comfortably push the exercise while keeping up with the music, but an easy ride or walk seems reasonable. A hard run sounds like an unnecessary extra instrument.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This should run on an older computer with integrated graphics, so I wouldn’t start by shopping for a new graphics card. I’d check that the computer can run Windows 10 or later and has a little room for the install, then get on with it.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Rhythm Doctor is an easy game to suggest if you like music games and want something that keeps your hands and attention occupied during a light workout. I’d skip it for a workout where you need to focus hard on pace, but for an easy treadmill walk or bike ride, the rhythm gives you something fun to follow.

### Overall Score: 2.67 out of 3
