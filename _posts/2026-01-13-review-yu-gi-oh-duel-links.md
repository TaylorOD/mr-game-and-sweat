A Duel Links workout sounds better at a steady walk than at a hard run, because the cards wait for you but your next move can still take a little thought. I’d keep the pace easy enough to read the field without doing the tiny controller grip people do when they’re trying to look calm.

## Gameplay

Duel Links takes the Yu-Gi-Oh! card game and trims it into a faster format, which is a good fit for short stretches of exercise, but it still asks you to keep track of what your cards do and what the other player might be setting up. I like having a game that gives me a reason to pay attention without demanding constant movement. You can think between turns. The treadmill does not get a vote.

The card effects are where I’d expect my attention to drift away from my legs, especially once a duel gets busy and you’re trying to decide whether to play a card now or wait for the other player to make a move. I don’t know that every duel will turn into a big strategy session, but there’s enough reading and remembering here that I’d save it for a walk or an easy bike ride rather than trying to push my pace. You can’t really make a dramatic mistake with your feet, but you can absolutely forget what your own card says.

Duel World gives you opponents to face outside of the online duels, and that’s probably where I’d start. I like being able to work through duels without the feeling that a stranger is waiting on me to make a decision, and the computer opponents make the whole thing feel less like I’ve brought a card tournament to the gym. Auto Duel can help with some of the more routine encounters, which is nice when you want the game to take a little less of your attention. I’d still expect to check what’s happening before letting it play for me.

Playing against people online sounds fun, and it’s a big part of why a card game like this can keep its matchups from feeling too predictable. It also means you might get a turn where you need to read a card effect carefully while the other person waits, and that’s not the part of exercise gaming I’d pick when I’m already breathing hard. I’d keep online duels for a slow ride or a walk, and use Duel World when I wanted to move without treating every choice like it needed a committee.

There’s a lot to learn if you’re new to Yu-Gi-Oh!, and the smaller format helps, but it doesn’t make the cards suddenly explain themselves. I’d expect to have a few turns where I stop and wonder why the screen is doing what it’s doing. That’s on me, mostly.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The turns are a real advantage here, because nothing is chasing you while you decide what to do. I’d pick a comfortable speed, keep the screen close enough to read, and treat any duel that starts getting complicated as a reason to slow down for a minute. It’s not a game where you need to aim at something moving across the screen, which is a pretty big favor to your coordination.

I can’t confirm controller support, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming you can play hands free from a mouse and keyboard. Check your setup before you plan the workout around it. With mouse and keyboard, I’d expect a treadmill to be awkward unless you have a stable place for the controls, and even then I’d keep the pace modest. A stationary bike seems easier for the careful card reading, though you’re still reaching for controls between turns.

The online matches are the part I’d be most cautious about while moving. Even if the game gives you time to think, I don’t know how much room you’ll have to pause a match when you need to adjust your pace or look away, and I’d rather not find out halfway through someone else’s turn. Duel World is more forgiving for exercise because you can choose a less demanding opponent or let Auto Duel handle some encounters. I’d start there.

This isn’t a game I’d put on when I want to forget I’m exercising. It gives you plenty to focus on, which can make a walk go by faster, but it can also pull your eyes and attention toward the screen for long stretches. A bike at an easy effort seems like the safer bet for keeping your balance and your cards straight. I’d keep the treadmill slower.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older integrated graphics computer, so you probably don’t need a gaming PC just to get this running. I’d check that your computer can meet the Windows requirement and that the screen is at least the listed resolution, because reading cards on a tiny display sounds like a workout for the wrong part of your body.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Duel Links makes sense for a steady walk or easy bike ride if you enjoy card strategy and can play comfortably with your setup. I’d skip it for a fast treadmill session, and I’d start in Duel World before taking online matches into the gym.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
