The flooded town and the robot girl make this a game I’d save for a slow walk, not a workout where you’re trying to keep your pace up. I think the story could make the time pass, but the reading is the activity.

## Gameplay

ATRI is a visual novel, so most of what you do is read dialogue and follow the story. I like the setup of a town being slowly overtaken by the ocean and a boy meeting a mysterious robot girl, because it gives the summer setting a little urgency without turning the whole thing into a disaster movie. But if you want the game to keep your hands busy while your legs do their thing, this probably isn’t going to do that. You’ll be reading. A lot.

I think that’s fine when you actually want to spend time with the characters, and maybe not the best when you’re trying to forget you’re exercising. There’s no steady stream of combat or quick decisions to pull your attention back in, so the story has to do most of the work. If the conversations are landing for you, that can be enough. If they aren’t, the treadmill is going to feel like the more active part of the evening.

The contrast between a flooded world and an intimate summer story is what catches my attention. It’s a strange setup in a way I enjoy, and I’m curious how the characters make sense of their little corner of civilization while the ocean keeps taking more of it. I don’t know if the story will hold your attention through every quiet stretch, though. A visual novel can ask you to sit with a scene for a while, and if you’re waiting for something to happen, the belt will keep moving without much help from the game.

This is a pick for someone who wants a story first and doesn’t mind that “playing” often means advancing through dialogue. I like when a game lets a calm scene stay calm, but that’s not much use if you came here to be distracted from your pace. You may get invested in the summer and the characters. You may also find yourself reading the same sentence twice because you were checking how fast you were going.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A treadmill makes more sense here than a hard ride, mostly because you can keep your pace gentle while you read. There aren’t twitch reactions to worry about, and the game isn’t asking you to aim at anything while your hands are moving. With a controller, advancing through dialogue is simple enough that loose inputs shouldn’t turn into a crisis. That part is easy.

The focus is the part I’d think about before starting. You’ll want to look at text and follow the story, which means this isn’t the game I’d choose for intervals or for a pace where you need to watch your footing closely. I’d keep the treadmill at a walk, settle into the dialogue, and stop the workout if I started paying more attention to the screen than to where my feet were going. It’s a visual novel, not a very engaging safety system.

On a bike, I’d expect it to be easier to keep your eyes on the screen because you’re seated and not bouncing around, but that doesn’t make the story more active. If you’re comfortable reading while you pedal, that could work nicely. If reading on a screen makes you lose your place, or you want a game that gives you something to do between lines, I’d pick something else. There’s no shame in needing your entertainment to move a little faster than this.

I’d also skip treating it like a background game. You can probably keep pedaling through a conversation, but if you stop following what’s being said, you’re not getting much out of the part you bought it for. I don’t know that a higher exercise intensity would add anything here. It would mostly make me try to read faster, and I already have enough trouble doing that when nothing else is happening.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimums are very low, so an older computer with integrated graphics should be enough to run it. I’d check that the display can handle the game’s 1280 by 720 resolution, but this shouldn’t require a new gaming machine.

## Conclusion and Score

Save ATRI for an easy walk or a relaxed bike ride when you want a story more than a game demanding constant attention. I’d tell you to play it if the flooded town and the robot girl sound like the kind of summer story you want to read, but I’d skip it for a workout where you need the screen to keep you moving.
