I’d play Dinkum on an easy treadmill walk, because gathering things and building up your little town gives you something to do without needing to react every second. I’d keep the dangerous wildlife and fiddly inventory work for when I’m not bouncing around.

## Gameplay

The island gives you a lot of small jobs, and I think that’s what makes Dinkum a nice fit for a longer workout. You can forage, fish, mine, farm, or head out to gather materials for your town, and there’s usually some useful little task you can do without needing to plan your whole afternoon. It’s the kind of game where a walk can turn into “I’ll just collect a few more things” and then you’ve got enough wood to build something. That’s a good problem to have.

Building the town is what would keep me interested past the first stretch of wandering around. You’re not only collecting supplies for the sake of seeing a number go up, since expanding the town gives your island a sense of progress and gives you a reason to keep doing the daily chores. I like that the work stays pretty tangible. You need something, you go look for it, and then you come back with a slightly more useful place to live.

There’s a slower side to it, though, and it isn’t going to disappear just because you’re on a treadmill. You’ll spend time sorting through what you’ve collected and deciding what to do next, and that kind of inventory and crafting management can make the workout feel like a very active trip to the storage cupboard. I don’t know that I’d want to do the more involved planning while I’m running. Walking gives me enough spare attention to think, which is not a claim I can make about myself in every setting.

The island’s wildlife adds a little tension to the otherwise mellow pace, and I think that’s useful in small doses. Hunting and dealing with dangerous animals asks more of you than fishing or picking up supplies, so you get moments that feel more alert without the whole game turning into constant combat. I’d probably treat the dangerous parts as something to do when I’m fresh, rather than charge into them halfway through a workout and discover that my coordination has left the island too.

Playing solo also makes sense to me here, because you can work at your own pace and take a minute to decide what you’re doing without feeling like you’re holding anybody up. Friends could make gathering and town building more social, which is really cool, but I’d probably want to save that for a time when I can actually pay attention to the conversation. I can only manage so many projects at once. Some of them are walking.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A steady walk is probably where Dinkum feels most comfortable, since a lot of the ordinary island work gives you time to move around and choose what to do next. I’d use the full controller support and stick to easy tasks like collecting resources, fishing, or tending to the farm when I wanted to keep my attention on the workout. There’s a satisfying amount of low pressure wandering here, and the island doesn’t need me to be a surgeon with a thumbstick.

The moments that need more focus are pretty easy to recognize. Combat, aiming, and managing your inventory all ask for more careful inputs than simple movement around town, and I wouldn’t want to be fighting wildlife while my hands are bouncing with every step. A bike might feel steadier for those more fiddly moments, but I wouldn’t make that a rule. If you’re walking, you can just leave the tricky stuff for later.

I’d also keep the pace gentle rather than try to make this a high intensity game. There’s no need to rush through every errand, and the slower stretches are part of why it works at all as exercise entertainment. The downside is that the game can ask you to stop and think about what you need or where you’re going, so it’s not ideal if your goal is to keep your mind completely off the treadmill. Sometimes the island has a shopping list.

For a first workout, I’d stay close to town and build up the basic supplies, then save more dangerous exploration for when I’m ready to give it my full attention. I don’t know how much I’d enjoy sorting out a crowded inventory while jogging, but I’d be fine doing it while walking or cycling at a steady pace. Controller support helps a lot, but it doesn’t turn every activity into a relaxed one.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

Dinkum asks for a fairly recent entry level dedicated graphics card, so I wouldn’t count on an older laptop with integrated graphics to run it well. I’d check the graphics card first, since that’s the part most likely to decide whether your computer is ready for island life or needs to stay home.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Dinkum is a nice choice if you want a relaxed game with enough little jobs to keep a walk moving, and I’d recommend it to someone who enjoys town building and collecting resources at their own pace. Skip it for workouts where you need to keep your attention on fast combat or constant action.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
