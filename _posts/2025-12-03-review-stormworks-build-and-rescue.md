A rescue service you build yourself sounds like a great way to forget you’re exercising, but I think Stormworks: Build and Rescue asks for more careful attention than I’d want on a moving treadmill. I’d save the designing for when you’re standing still.

## Gameplay

The appeal here is that you’re not just sent out to help people, you design and create the air and sea rescue service you’ll use, then pilot it into storms to rescue people. I like that the job and the tools belong together, because you get to think about what you’d want to use before the bad weather shows up. I also know I’d spend a while fussing over the rescue vehicle instead of actually rescuing anybody. That’s probably part of the fun.

Building is the part that makes me hesitate a little. I think a game like this is at its best when the things you make lead to interesting choices, but designing and creating means you’re going to have stretches where you’re thinking carefully instead of moving around much. That could be a nice change of pace on a long workout, or it could turn into me staring at a vehicle and forgetting to pedal. I can do both of those things sitting still.

Once you’re piloting into fierce storms, the premise gets much more immediate. There’s a clear reason to keep going, and I like a rescue game where the goal is helping somebody rather than just finding another thing to shoot. Still, I expect the storm and the piloting to take concentration, and I wouldn’t count on being able to zone out and let my legs carry the workout. I’d probably be watching the screen.

I don’t know how much variety the game offers beyond the store page description, so I wouldn’t promise you a long string of different rescue situations. What I can say is that making your own service and then sending it into dangerous weather gives the basic idea a lot of room to be interesting. The slow parts sound like they come from the game’s central design process, not from a long stretch of waiting for the game to do something. That’s a meaningful difference.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d separate the building from the workout, because designing something carefully while your hands are moving sounds like a job I’d make harder than it needs to be. Build before you get on, then see how piloting feels during a very easy session. I wouldn’t start with a hard run and a storm rescue at the same time. That’s a lot of things asking for attention.

The controller question matters here, and I can’t confirm from the information I have whether controller support covers all the building and piloting you’ll want to do. I wouldn’t assume it does, and I also wouldn’t assume it doesn’t. Check the controls before you plan a workout around it. Having to reach for a keyboard or mouse while you’re moving would make this much less comfortable.

Even with a setup that works, I think the actual rescue missions will need more focus than a game that lets you make broad, imprecise inputs and keep going. You’re piloting your own vehicles through fierce storms, and the whole point is to reach people who need help, so I’d want my hands steady and my attention on the screen. On a bike, I’d expect to be more comfortable making careful inputs. On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace gentle and skip trying to do anything that feels demanding.

There’s a kind of workout game that gives you room to look away, miss a little, and catch up without much trouble. I don’t know that this is one of those, because the building sounds like it rewards sitting down and thinking, while the storm piloting sounds like it rewards paying attention. You could still make it work, especially if you’re happy to treat the exercise as light movement while you focus on the rescue. I’d want the controls sorted first.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to a modest computer, and even integrated graphics are listed as an option, so you don’t need a new gaming machine just to consider it. I’d check your graphics setup first, but the listed minimum is not asking for a high end card.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Stormworks: Build and Rescue sounds like a better fit for easy cycling than a focused treadmill run, and I’d suggest it to someone who enjoys designing vehicles as much as taking them out into rough weather. If you want a game that keeps your hands busy without asking for much thought, I’d choose something else.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
