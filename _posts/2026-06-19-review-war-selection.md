Save War Selection for an easy walk or a relaxed bike ride, because moving from a stone age tribe to an industrial nation sounds like a lot more thinking than I want to do at a hard running pace. The dozens of other players make it feel less like a quiet strategy puzzle, too.

## Gameplay

The part I like is that the big historical jump gives your decisions a direction, instead of making every match feel like you’re just collecting resources because a little number went up. You’re building a base and extracting resources while trying to keep your nation moving forward, and I think that could keep me interested through a long stretch of play. The trouble is, there’s a lot to keep track of even before warfare gets involved.

I’d probably spend a good chunk of a match deciding what to build and where to put my attention, and that’s not automatically bad. I like strategy games that make you stop and consider a choice, but on a workout that can turn into me standing there thinking while my legs continue their shift without me. It’s a little like trying to plan dinner while the stove is already on.

The procedurally generated map and the different nations sound like they could give matches some variety, and I like that the game is asking you to adapt rather than follow one fixed route from one age to the next. I don’t know how much that changes the feel from match to match, though, and I wouldn’t buy it expecting every game to be a completely new puzzle. There’s only so much you can learn about a map while also checking your breathing.

Dozens of players fighting for supremacy is exciting in the way that a large strategy game should be, but it also means I’d expect some busy stretches where a small mistake can matter. That’s a real tradeoff. If you enjoy managing a whole little economy and then watching it turn into a war, this has a clear appeal, but it isn’t the kind of game I’d pick when I want my brain to take a break.

War Selection is also in Early Access, which I’d keep in mind before spending time learning all its moving parts. I’m not saying that makes it a bad choice, because games can be worth playing while they’re still developing, but I’d want to be comfortable with the possibility that the experience changes. My tolerance for relearning a strategy game depends on how much coffee I’ve had.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A treadmill makes the decisions feel harder, not because you need to run fast, but because an RTS asks for attention at the same time your body is bouncing around. I’d keep the pace easy enough that I could look at the screen and make careful selections without feeling like I’m trying to thread a needle on a moving bus. That seems manageable for a walk. A hard run sounds like a lot.

On a bike, I think the steady position would make it easier to focus on the map and your base, especially during the quieter parts of a match. I’d still keep the effort low, because the game’s wars and the pressure of other players could pull my attention away from pedaling. The bike doesn’t make the decisions simpler. It just gives your hands a calmer place to work.

I wouldn’t assume that a controller is the comfortable way to play this. It’s an RTS built around managing a base, resources, and warfare, so I’d expect precise input to matter more than loose, forgiving button presses. If you already play strategy games with a controller, you may have a different experience, but I’d check before buying it specifically for that setup.

The multiplayer part is what would make me cautious while exercising. With other people in the match, you can’t treat every pause in your own attention like a harmless break, and I’d rather not be trying to catch up on what’s happening while my feet are moving underneath me. I don’t know how forgiving a match feels when you fall behind, so I’d start with a gentle workout and stop trying to raise the pace if the game starts asking for all your attention.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements look modest, but the 2 GB graphics requirement is what I’d check before assuming an older laptop can run it. A fairly ordinary gaming computer from the last several years should be a safer bet than a machine that only just turns on. The game doesn’t sound like it needs a space heater for a graphics card.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

War Selection sounds like a worthwhile strategy game if you want to build an economy and manage a large multiplayer war, and I’d try it at a slow workout pace rather than during a hard run. It’s probably not the one I’d choose for exercise that needs very little attention, but for an easy ride, I can see the appeal.
