I think Retrowave could work nicely for an easy bike ride, because the whole point is cruising through neon scenery on an endless highway and you don’t have to keep stopping to follow a story. On a treadmill, I’d start at a pace where steering still feels like a small job.

## Gameplay

The empty stretch of road is a big part of the appeal, and also the thing that could wear thin. Retrowave gives you neon lights, palm trees, synthwave and fast old school supercars, which is a very specific mood and a good one if you want something to look at while you move. I like that it doesn’t sound like it’s asking me to keep up with a complicated plot while I’m trying to remember how my legs work.

The driving gives your attention something to do, but I think this is more about settling into the look and feel than chasing a deep racing challenge. You’re moving down an endless highway, so there isn’t a finish line pulling you through a sequence of events. That can be relaxing, and it can also make the road feel like the same long commute with better lighting.

I’d expect the cars and the road to keep me looking ahead, especially if I’m trying to drive cleanly rather than just enjoy the scenery. The part I’m less sure about is how much the visuals alone can carry over a longer session, since a striking color palette is still the same stretch of highway after a while. Maybe you’re happy to let the synthwave do its thing, and that makes a lot of sense.

There’s no long story section here to make you stand still and wait for the next bit of driving, which is a nice fit for exercise. I don’t know how much variety the endless road can offer before it starts feeling familiar, but if you want a game that lets you get moving without first learning a pile of systems, this seems like a sensible place to start. The premise is simple.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The controller support is full, which means you can keep your hands where you need them instead of reaching for a keyboard while your feet are doing their own thing. I’d try it on a bike first if I wanted to relax into the scenery, then use a treadmill at a comfortable pace where I can still make small steering corrections without turning the whole thing into a balancing exercise.

Racing does ask more from you than a game where you can let go of the controls whenever you want, and I’d be careful about treating the road like background video once the speed picks up. If you’re running, a sudden correction can feel awkward when your hands and body are bouncing around, so I’d keep the pace easy and avoid trying to prove anything to a digital sports car. It’s not a game I’d choose for an interval workout where I’m already concentrating on going faster.

On a bike, the steady movement seems like a better match for the forward motion on screen, and the full controller support gives you a straightforward way to steer. I’d keep the effort low enough that I can pay attention to the road, because I’m not going to pretend racing and exercise take turns asking for my focus. They can share it, but only up to a point.

There’s also a nice practical difference between this and a game that keeps interrupting you with reading or long scenes. You can stay in the driving mood, and if you start getting tired, backing off the workout pace is easier than trying to manage a complicated action sequence. I’d still skip it if I wanted a completely hands off ride. The driving needs attention.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest by current standards, and the listed graphics cards are old enough that this shouldn’t demand a new gaming computer. I’d check that your machine can run a 64 bit version of Windows and has the required storage, then I’d expect the graphics side to be the easier part.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Retrowave sounds like a good choice for an easy ride or a relaxed treadmill session, especially if the neon highway look is what you want from a workout game. I’d skip it when I needed the game to do all the entertaining for me, but the straightforward driving and full controller support make it an easy one to try.

### Overall Score: 2.67 out of 3
