Save this for a slow walk, because I think SILENT HILL 2 is much better at keeping you tense than keeping your legs moving at a serious pace. The fog and the monsters are doing a lot of work, and I’d rather not be trying to jog through a nurse attack while also remembering where the treadmill buttons are.

## Gameplay

The story gives you a reason to keep looking around, and I think that works better here than a constant stream of fights would. James is searching Silent Hill for traces of his late wife, so the places you explore feel like they matter even when you’re mostly moving from one unsettling hallway to another. There’s a lot of walking, checking rooms, and trying to work out where to go next. That can be good exercise pacing, but you’ll have stretches where the game is asking for your attention in a quieter way.

The puzzles are a big part of that. I like that they give you something to think about, but it’s also the kind of thinking where you may stop pedaling or slow your walk so you can read the clue properly. There’s a difference between a game making you curious and a game making you stare at a note while your treadmill keeps going. This has some of both.

Combat gives the slow wandering a reason to feel uncomfortable. The monsters are disturbing, and deciding whether to fight or get away makes the fog feel like more than a pretty screen effect. I’d expect the nurses and mannequins to make me tense up, which is the point, but tension doesn’t always make for a smooth workout. It may make you grip the controller like it’s going to help.

I don’t know how much you want a horror game to interrupt your breathing, but this one seems pretty committed to it. The quiet parts are useful, though, because you can settle back into walking and look around before the next ugly surprise. I’d call the pacing uneven for exercise, not bad, and the atmosphere is doing enough that I’d keep going.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A slow walk is probably the sensible starting point. The full controller support means you can play without reaching for a keyboard, and the exploration doesn’t sound like it needs constant fast inputs. But once a monster is close, you have to move, aim, and react while the game is trying to make you nervous. That’s a lot to manage when your hands are bouncing along with your steps.

I’d turn the combat difficulty down before starting a workout. That should make the fighting less punishing if you miss a dodge or take a second to line up a shot, and there’s no prize for making the exercise harder just because James is having a rough day. I’d still keep the pace easy, because aiming while running sounds like a small experiment in how often I can hit the wrong button.

A bike might be easier during fights because your upper body can stay steadier, but it doesn’t make the puzzles magically simple. When you find a clue or need to work out where to go, I’d pause and take the time rather than pedal in place while reading every scrap of paper. I don’t know that I’d want the game deciding when my workout gets intense, but at least you can stop when you need to think.

There are also quieter stretches where you can just move through the fog and keep a gentle pace, and those seem like the most comfortable parts to pair with exercise. I’d avoid trying to make this a fast run. The game wants you alert, and my treadmill does not need me trying to dodge a monster and a belt at the same time.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This asks for a dedicated graphics card with some muscle, so I wouldn’t expect an older office computer to handle it comfortably. I’d check the graphics card first, since the minimum target is aimed at low or medium settings in Full HD rather than making the fog look lovely for free.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

SILENT HILL 2 makes sense for a slow walk or an easy bike session, especially if you want horror with enough exploration to fill the quieter parts. I wouldn’t choose it for a hard run, and anyone who wants uninterrupted action should look elsewhere. It’s a good fit when you’re happy to pause and think.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
