The monsters handle the fighting themselves, which sounds like a nice arrangement for a workout until you realize the game may be asking you to watch things happen instead of giving you much to do. I think DPS: Desktop Pension Squad makes more sense for an easy bike ride than a run where you need a steady stream of action.

## Gameplay

I like the basic idea of a squad of odd little monsters taking on the humans who stole the Dungeon Cores, and the fact that the battles happen on their own gives the whole thing a relaxed pace. You can check in, recruit more of the colorful cast, and put a squad together without needing to make every second a decision. That’s appealing when your attention is split between a game and not drifting off the back of a treadmill.

But an idle RPG is still an idle RPG, and the part where the monsters do their thing for you is also the part where you could start wondering if you might as well be watching a loading bar. I don’t know how much the squad building changes the moment to moment feel, so I wouldn’t go in expecting a deep tactical game. I’d be happy if picking a team gave me a small thing to think about, not a whole second job.

The premise has a little bit of silly fun to it. Humans stealing dungeon cores and then getting a monster squad sent after them is a reasonable use of everyone’s afternoon, I guess. I like that the game seems comfortable being a desktop companion rather than pretending every game needs to demand your full attention, though I’d want the recruiting and squad choices to feel like they matter enough to pull me back in.

For a workout, I’d treat this as something to keep open during a low effort session, not as the one game that’s going to make an hour disappear. There’s a difference between a game that lets you glance away and a game that gives you nothing worth glancing back at, and I can’t tell from the description how well DPS: Desktop Pension Squad lands that balance. I’d probably enjoy the hands off battles more when I’m doing something else. That’s a modest bar.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The auto battles are a real advantage here, because there’s no sign in the description that you have to aim, react quickly, or make a precise move at the exact moment a monster attacks. You can keep moving while the squad fights, and that should make it less distracting than an action RPG that keeps asking for your thumbs every few seconds. I’d try it on a relaxed ride or walk first, then see how often I actually need to interact.

The menus are the part I’d be more careful with. Recruiting monsters and building a squad sound like the moments when you’ll want to look at the screen and make a choice, so I wouldn’t plan on doing that while running hard or bouncing around on the belt. I’d handle those bits before the workout where possible, and save the run itself for letting the battles play out. I’ve made worse plans for a treadmill.

I can’t confirm controller support, so I wouldn’t buy it expecting to play from a controller without checking first. I’d have the computer’s usual inputs within reach and assume you may need to pause your movement for the squad management parts. That uncertainty keeps me from calling it effortless to use while exercising, even though the auto fighting itself sounds unusually forgiving.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum calls for an old entry level gaming card and 8 GB of memory, so you don’t need a new high end computer to give it a try. I’d check that your graphics card meets the listed GTX 750 Ti level first, because the monsters are doing the fighting, but the computer still has to show it.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

DPS: Desktop Pension Squad sounds like a reasonable companion for an easy workout if you enjoy checking in on a squad and letting its battles run without much direct input. I’d skip it if you want a game that keeps your hands busy and your attention fixed on the screen.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
