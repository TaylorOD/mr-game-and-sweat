A round of Ultimate Chicken Horse gives you bursts of jumping and running, then a short pause while everyone adds another way to make the course worse. I’d keep the treadmill at a pace where I can still land a jump on purpose, because the game does not care that my feet are moving.

## Gameplay

Party mode is the reason I’d want to play this. You and the other players build the level as you go, adding platforms and hazards, then try to reach the goal without getting taken out by the course you all made. I like that the construction is part of the competition, because you’re not just trying to beat a level, you’re also deciding how much trouble you want to cause and whether you can survive it yourself. That second part is where my planning would get optimistic.

The best decisions seem to be the small mean ones. Put a hazard somewhere that makes the obvious jump harder, then leave yourself a route that you think you can actually use, and hope the next person doesn’t add something that ruins your plan. I’d probably get distracted trying to make a clever obstacle and then remember I also have to get past it. That seems fair.

There’s a good rhythm between placing pieces and making a run for the goal, so the game isn’t asking you to stay locked in every second. The building breaks are short, and they’re part of the fun rather than a long stretch of menus or story. I don’t know how long that stays funny with a group that takes every placement seriously, but the whole setup seems to work best when everyone is willing to make a mess and then deal with it.

A saw in the way is easy to understand, which is helpful when you’re also trying to watch your feet. It’s not a game where you need to learn a huge set of moves before you can join in, but the jumping still has to be deliberate, and a badly placed hazard can turn a quick run into a series of attempts. I’d rather have that than a game asking me to read a wall of instructions while the treadmill keeps going.

The main appeal is that the other players shape what each round feels like, so the course can get funnier and more difficult as everyone adds to it. That also means the mood of the group matters. If everyone is enjoying the ridiculous deaths, great. If somebody is treating each round like a final exam, I’d probably spend more time watching them than exercising.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d play this with a controller and give myself a pace where a jump doesn’t require a last second correction. Full controller support is a big plus, and the controls are simple enough in concept that you aren’t trying to manage a keyboard while you move. But jumping through hazards still needs timing, so I wouldn’t start at a speed where every missed landing feels like the treadmill is keeping score.

The pauses while players build are useful for catching your breath or settling back into your stride, and the next attempt starts when the group is ready. That makes the game easier to fit around exercise than a match that never lets you stop. On a bike, I think the steady pedaling would make it easier to keep your upper body still while you play, but there’s no reason a treadmill can’t work if you keep the pace modest.

I’d start with Party mode and people who don’t mind a few clumsy jumps, rather than trying to turn the whole thing into a serious competition while I’m moving. The more the course fills up with hazards, the more I’d want to focus on the screen, and that’s where a treadmill can make small controller corrections annoying. I don’t know how much I’d enjoy trying to make a perfect jump while running quickly, so I wouldn’t make that the plan.

The game’s best workout fit is the back and forth between building and trying, not the idea that you can play it at full speed without paying attention. If you’re biking, you can probably settle into a comfortable pace and keep your hands on the controller. On a treadmill, slow the belt down enough that you can look at the course and make a measured jump. I’d skip any urge to prove I can play better while moving faster. I know how that would go.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to a computer with integrated graphics or an older graphics card, so this isn’t asking you to buy a new machine just to place hazards in front of your friends. I’d check that your computer meets the basic graphics requirement first, and online play needs broadband internet. The game itself should be the easy part.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is a fun choice for a workout with friends if you’re happy to trade some precision for a comfortable pace. I’d play it on an easy treadmill setting or a bike, and I’d pass if you want a game that lets you stop paying attention.
