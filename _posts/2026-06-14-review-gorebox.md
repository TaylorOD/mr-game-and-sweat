I’d try GoreBox on an easy treadmill walk, because spawning things with the Reality Crusher and seeing what breaks sounds like a decent way to keep your attention off the clock. I’d save the online multiplayer for when I can give it my full attention.

## Gameplay

The sandbox part is what makes this sound fun to me, because you get to decide what to spawn, build, and wreck instead of waiting for the game to hand you the next task. That kind of open ended play can be great when you want a little chaos during a workout, and it can also turn into standing around wondering what to do next. I know that feeling from games where the whole plan is “make your own fun,” and sometimes I’m good at it for about ten minutes.

The Reality Crusher sounds like the part I’d keep coming back to. Using one tool to mess with the world gives you a simple starting point, which matters when you’re moving and don’t want to stop to read a bunch of instructions. I’d make small goals for myself, like building something and then trying to wreck it, rather than expecting a structured campaign to keep me busy. It’s a little like deciding to clean one shelf and then seeing what else you can get away with.

I’m not sure how much variety the sandbox has beyond what the store description says, so I wouldn’t promise you endless new things to discover. The fun seems like it depends on whether you enjoy making your own little experiments, and I do, as long as the controls let me get to the funny part without a lot of fiddling. I don’t know how long that would hold my attention, but at least the game’s basic idea is clear.

GoreBox also includes GoreBox Animosity and early access to GoreBox 27, which has online multiplayer. I’d be curious to try the newer version when I’m sitting down, but multiplayer is less appealing as workout entertainment because now other people are involved and I’m likely to care about what they’re doing. I’d rather do my own nonsense without worrying that someone is waiting for me to make a decision.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d start with the sandbox and keep the session low stakes, because the game sounds better suited to loose experimenting than to anything where a fast response matters. You can make a mess, stop to decide what to do next, and get back to moving when you’re ready. That sounds manageable at a walking pace, though I’d expect the aiming and placing parts to take more focus than something with a few big buttons.

Controller support isn’t confirmed in the information I have, so I can’t promise you can pick it up and play from the couch. If the controls work with your controller, I’d still expect aiming the Reality Crusher and building things to need some careful thumb work, and bouncing on a treadmill makes careful thumb work a little less careful. I’d try it at a gentle pace first, and I wouldn’t start by trying to do anything precise while running.

The online multiplayer in GoreBox 27 is the part I’d skip while exercising. It could be fun when you’re able to focus on other players, but I don’t know how forgiving it is if you need a second to steady your hands or look away, and I wouldn’t want my workout to become a meeting where I’m the only one out of breath. The solo sandbox sounds like the safer place to see if the game fits your setup.

On a bike, I’d expect the slower pace to make aiming a little easier, but I don’t think it changes the basic tradeoff. You still have to manage the controls, and if building starts to feel fiddly, I’d go back to spawning things and causing a smaller mess. That’s probably enough to keep the game moving without turning the ride into a thumb workout.
