I think Nightingale could work for an easy workout, but I’d keep it to exploring and gathering rather than trying to fight through every encounter while the belt is moving. There’s a lot to do, and some of it asks for more attention than my legs are going to enjoy sharing.

## Gameplay

The part I like is having a reason to keep moving through the Fae Realms, because gathering materials and looking for somewhere to build gives you little goals without needing a big dramatic moment every few minutes. You’re not just standing in one spot waiting for the next fight, which helps when you want the game to hold your attention through a workout. I’d probably spend a lot of time making a place feel like mine, even if “elaborate estate” turns into a little house and some boxes.

The survival crafting side also means there’s a fair bit of stopping to check what you have and what you need, and I don’t think that’s bad on its own. It’s just a different kind of attention from an action game, where you can keep moving and let the next thing happen. Here, I’d expect to pause my forward progress to sort out the next bit of crafting, and sometimes that’s a nice break from running. Sometimes it’s me standing on the treadmill reading a list of materials.

Fights with the Bound and other monstrous enemies sound like the part most likely to pull me out of an easy pace, because even a fairly simple fight can turn into a lot of looking around and reacting. I’d want to get comfortable with how combat feels before making it my workout game, and I’d be happy to leave some of the more demanding encounters for when I’m sitting down. I don’t know how much the challenge changes from realm to realm, but I’d expect the hostile environments to make wandering around feel less like a casual stroll.

The Realm Card idea gives the exploration a specific reason to keep going, and I like that the realms feel like places you’re choosing to visit rather than one enormous stretch of land you have to cross forever. Still, crafting and travel can make a session feel stop and start, so this isn’t the game I’d choose when I want to zone out and keep a steady pace. It seems more like a game for an easy workout where you’re fine with taking a breather to think.

Early Access matters here, too. I’d go in expecting some rough edges and changes rather than treating every system as settled, and that can be exciting if you like watching a game grow. I’m a little less enthusiastic about building a long routine around something that might change under you, but that’s a personal preference. I don’t need every game to be finished before I can enjoy it.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d start with a low speed and stick to exploring, collecting and building, because those activities sound like they leave you enough room to move without making every thumb movement count. A bike seems especially reasonable for the slower stretches, but I wouldn’t call this a bike game by default. If you’re on a treadmill, I’d keep the pace comfortable and treat combat as a reason to slow down or pause.

The fights are where my attention would get divided. You may have to face enemies while moving through dangerous areas, and trying to look, aim and react while your hands are bouncing around sounds like a pretty good way to make a simple fight harder than it needs to be. I’d turn down any difficulty options that make sense, if available, and avoid picking a fight when I’m already working hard. I don’t know whether every encounter gives you a clean chance to reset, so I wouldn’t build the workout around that assumption.

I’d use a controller if the controls feel comfortable in your setup, but I’d test menus and crafting before starting a workout instead of discovering that item management is awkward while I’m moving. There’s enough crafting and inventory time here that those screens matter, even if the walking and exploring feel fine. You can get away with imprecise movement while gathering. It’s a different story when something is attacking you.

A practical way to play would be to make the workout about exploring a realm, collecting a handful of useful materials and heading back to build, then leave the more attention heavy fights for later. That gives you a goal without turning every minute into a reaction test. I’d skip the parts that make me stare at a menu for too long, too, because the belt doesn’t need to become a place where I’m trying to remember which ingredient I’m missing.

## Computer Requirements

You’ll want a gaming PC with a mid range graphics card, and the SSD requirement and storage space are worth checking before you install it. I’d look at the graphics card first, because this isn’t aimed at an older office computer that happens to have a game on it.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Nightingale seems like a decent choice for an easy workout if you enjoy exploring, gathering and building, and you’re comfortable easing off when combat needs more focus. I’d pass if you want something you can play without paying much attention to the screen.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
