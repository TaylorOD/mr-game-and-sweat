Don’t start NEEDY STREAMER OVERLOAD when you want to zone out and let the belt carry you, because reading chat and deciding what Ame should do next takes more attention than the bright screen suggests. I’d save it for a slow bike ride or an easy walk where stopping to read doesn’t turn into a whole production.

## Gameplay

The appeal here is watching a small set of choices gradually add up to something you didn’t expect, and I think that works better than it sounds. You’re helping OMGkawaiiAngel build an audience by deciding how she spends each day, including what kind of streams to do, while keeping an eye on the way her life is going. It’s a management game, but the person you’re managing is also a messy young streamer, which gives the choices more weight than just picking the option with the biggest number.

The audience growth part is easy to understand, and that’s good, because there’s already plenty to read. You choose activities, watch the numbers move, and decide whether to keep pushing toward Internet Angel fame or take a different approach. I like that the game makes success feel tied to the rest of Ame’s life instead of treating followers as the only thing that matters. It does make every day feel a little like planning a work calendar for someone who might post something alarming at midnight.

There’s a lot of text, including stream comments and messages, and I think that’s where the game gets its personality. The internet chatter can be funny, uncomfortable, or just a reminder that being watched by a growing crowd sounds exhausting. I’d want to read it rather than tap through on autopilot, though, and that means this isn’t the kind of game that fills every second of a run with action. Some choices are quick. Then the game gives you another chunk to read.

The multiple endings make experimenting appealing, because you can try different ways of steering Ame’s daily life and see where that leaves her. I don’t know that every stretch between those outcomes feels equally lively, since a lot of the play is still choosing from menus and checking what changed, but I’d be curious to see how the different decisions build on each other. It has a clear reason to keep going, even if that reason sometimes involves sitting still and thinking about a fictional person’s schedule.

As a game, I think it’s interesting and has a point of view. As something to keep you moving, it’s a little too willing to hand you a screen full of words and wait.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A stationary bike seems like the easier place to play, since your hands can stay on the controller and your eyes can stay on the text without you also having to manage each step. Even then, I’d keep the pace easy. When a stream comment catches your attention or you’re deciding what to do with the next day, you’re going to look at the screen instead of thinking about your legs.

On a treadmill, I’d keep the speed low enough that reading doesn’t become a balance exercise. There isn’t a fast action sequence waiting to punish you for a slow button press, which is a big point in its favor, and the game’s menu based rhythm gives you time to make choices. But a busy comment feed can pull your attention in, and I’d rather not pretend that reading little messages while jogging is somehow a great coordination drill.

Controller navigation fits the game’s pace, since you’re mainly moving through options and reading what happens next. I’d use one instead of reaching for a mouse while moving, and I’d give myself permission to pause whenever I wanted to read something properly. The game can wait. Your workout can take a minute too.

I wouldn’t choose this for a hard run where you’re trying to stay locked into a pace, because the story and choices are the part you’re here for, and skipping past them would leave you with a lot of clicking and not much else. On an easy session, though, the lack of twitch controls makes it workable. You just need to be okay with stretches where the exercise is happening beside the game rather than because of it.

The listed minimum asks for a fairly recent Core i5 processor, but it doesn’t call for a recent, powerful graphics card. I’d check that your computer can meet the processor requirement first, since that’s the part that sounds more demanding than this very text forward game looks.

## Conclusion and Score

Save it for an easy workout where you’re happy to read, pause, and make choices instead of chasing constant action. I’d point visual novel and management game fans toward it, but I wouldn’t pick it for a run where the game needs to keep your attention on the belt.
