Match three puzzles sound much easier to fit around a run than a game that asks you to react to everything at once, and Mirror 2: Project X has that going for it. I’d treat the story as a slower workout companion, not something to start when you’re trying to keep a hard pace.

## Gameplay

The mix of parallel worlds, story choices and match three games is an odd setup, but I think it gives you a reason to pay attention without asking for constant precision. You can think about a move, make it, and then catch up with the story at your own speed. That’s a lot more treadmill friendly than a game where one missed input turns a small mistake into the rest of your evening.

I also like that the game ties your choices to the fate of the story. Choices give you something to consider beyond clearing the next board, which could make the narrative feel connected to what you’re doing instead of sitting beside it. The tradeoff is that thinking through a choice is not exactly exercise for your legs. If I was moving at a steady pace, I’d be happy to slow down a little for a decision, but I wouldn’t want every stretch of the game to turn into reading and thinking while the belt keeps going.

The match three part is the piece I’d expect to carry the actual play, and that sounds promising for a workout. You have a clear task in front of you, rather than a screen full of tiny things to track, and I don’t need to be an especially clever strategist to understand what kind of decision I’m making. I’d probably keep the pace easy enough that I could look at the board properly. I have made enough questionable decisions while sitting still.

I don’t know how much of the game is spent in story scenes compared with the puzzles, and that matters here. A little story between rounds could make the game feel less repetitive, but long stretches where you’re mostly reading would pull me out of the exercise part. The store description makes the story and choices sound important, so I’d expect some slower sections and wouldn’t start this when I needed a game to keep me busy every second.

Early Access is another reason I’d keep expectations measured. The basic idea has a lot I can see working, but I’d want to know how much of the story and game is available before getting invested in the choices. I like a game that gives me a reason to come back, but I don’t want to plan a whole workout around a chapter that isn’t there yet.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The pace of a match three game should be forgiving enough for a treadmill or bike, as long as the board doesn’t demand fast, exact inputs. I’d start with an easy pace and give myself room to stop and make a move without trying to set a running record at the same time. This is the kind of game where I’d rather finish a workout feeling like I played it than spend the whole time staring down at the screen.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information provided, so I wouldn’t assume that a controller will work before checking. That’s important because reaching for a mouse and keyboard while moving is a much less comfortable plan, especially on a treadmill where your hands are already doing a little balancing act. If a controller works, I’d use it. If it doesn’t, I’d play seated instead of trying to improvise a desk setup while jogging.

The choices and story are probably where I’d pause or ease off the pace. There’s no shame in treating a workout game like a game sometimes, and I’d rather take a moment to read than pretend I can focus on a story decision while bouncing along. The match three sections seem like the part I’d play while keeping a steady pace, and the slower parts are where I’d catch my breath. Not every screen needs to be cardio.

On a bike, I’d expect the slower parts to be easier to manage because you can keep pedaling while looking at the screen, but the game doesn’t sound so demanding that a treadmill is automatically out. I’d skip trying to make it a high intensity session, though. This is more for a comfortable pace where a move can take a second.

## Computer Requirements

You’ll want a Windows computer with a dedicated graphics card around the level of a GTX 1050, so this is not built for integrated graphics or a very old machine. I’d check the graphics card first, since that’s the part most likely to separate a capable computer from one that needs an upgrade for this.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Mirror 2: Project X sounds best for someone who wants a story and match three puzzles during an easy workout, and who’s comfortable checking controller support before buying. I wouldn’t choose it for a fast run, but the basic idea makes sense for a relaxed session.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
