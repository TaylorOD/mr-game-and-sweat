The long rally stages in WRC 7 make this feel like a proper workout game, but I’d start on a bike before trying to steer through Finland while jogging. There’s always another corner coming, and that keeps you paying attention.

## Gameplay

I think WRC 7 is at its best when you’re trying to keep a car on a narrow road instead of racing a pack of cars around a track, because every surface asks you to adjust and the pace notes keep the next turn in your head. Dirt, snow, and tarmac all have their own feel, and I like that the game expects you to drive the road you’re on rather than just hold the accelerator down. You’re busy, which is useful when you’re exercising. It’s not a quiet game.

The 2017 cars are powerful, and the more demanding physics mean that a small mistake can turn into a lot of corrections. That’s exciting when you’re focused, but it can also feel like trying to thread a grocery cart through a crowded aisle while somebody keeps changing where the aisle goes. I’d give myself room to learn the handling before raising the difficulty. The road is already doing enough.

There are 52 Special Stages across 13 countries, so the game has a lot of different roads to keep the driving from feeling identical. The longer races sound like a good fit if you want something that occupies a whole workout, though I’d probably choose shorter stages when I’m not sure how much attention I have in me. A long stage can make time pass quickly. It can also make a small mistake feel like it followed you around for a while.

I don’t know that I’d care much about the eSports mode while exercising, since competition can turn a decent run into me leaning toward the screen and forgetting what my feet are doing. The regular rally driving is the part I’d come back for. You’re always reading the road and trying to keep your speed under control, and there’s very little of the waiting around that makes some racing games feel like a menu with occasional cars.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, the hard part isn’t remembering to press buttons, it’s getting a steady steering input while your hands and body are moving. WRC 7 has full controller support, which is a good start, but rally driving asks for quick corrections and careful braking, and the car can punish you for turning a little too far. I’d expect to make more mistakes while jogging than while sitting still. That’s not a dealbreaker, but it changes what I’d play.

A bike seems like the easier place to start, especially at a pace where you can keep your hands settled and still pay attention to the road. I’d use a controller, pick a forgiving difficulty, and avoid turning the session into a competition with the clock. I don’t know how much the game lets you soften the driving with assists, so I wouldn’t build the plan around a particular setting. If the handling feels twitchy, slow down before trying to fix it with your thumbs.

The long stages are appealing because you don’t have to stop and choose something new every few minutes, but they also mean you can’t just zone out when you get comfortable. A corner can arrive while you’re thinking about your stride, and then you’re correcting the car instead of relaxing into the workout. I’d skip the eSports mode on the treadmill and treat the stages as the main event. The car will still find a tree if you stop paying attention.

This is one of those games where you’ll probably want to keep the workout easy enough that the driving gets most of your focus. That sounds a little backwards, but I’d rather finish a stage feeling like I was actually steering than spend the whole time trying to rescue a car from the edge of the road. Walking might suit it better than running if you want to use a treadmill. A bike gives your hands a calmer job.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimum points to an older, modest gaming computer rather than a machine that needs a new high end graphics card, so I’d check the graphics card first and make sure the system is 64 bit. It needs 19 GB of storage, which is a little space to clear out before you get to the road.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

WRC 7 is a fun choice if you want focused racing during an easy bike ride, or a careful treadmill walk, and the range of stages gives you plenty of road to work through. I’d skip it for a hard run where precise steering sounds like one task too many.
