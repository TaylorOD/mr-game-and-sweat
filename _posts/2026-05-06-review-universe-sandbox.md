Watching a planet head toward another one could keep you occupied on a treadmill, but Universe Sandbox is more of a slow space experiment than a game that keeps your hands busy every second. I think that can work for a steady workout, as long as you’re happy to spend some of it watching the universe do its thing.

## Gameplay

The appeal, for me, is being able to change something enormous and then see what follows, because making planets collide or boiling away oceans is a lot more interesting than staring at a list of numbers. You can use science to bend gravity, fire space lasers, and customize your universe, which gives you room to follow your own curiosity instead of chasing a mission marker. I like games that let you poke at a system and see what happens, and this one seems built for that. It also sounds like the kind of game where I’d set something in motion, decide I should understand why it happened, and then look up from the screen much later.

That open endedness comes with a slower pace, though, and I think that’s a meaningful trade. You’re not constantly reacting to enemies or trying to get through the next section, so it may not hold your attention just because the game asks you to keep moving. You have to bring a question of your own, like what happens when two planets collide or what you can do to a planet’s oceans, and then enjoy watching the result. If you need a clear goal every few minutes, you might end up feeling like the universe is waiting for you to come up with the activity.

I don’t know how quickly the different tools make sense to a new player, and I’d expect some trial and error before the simulation feels easy to steer. That’s not necessarily a bad way to spend time, but on a workout it means there could be stretches where you’re focused on figuring out what to change instead of getting pulled along by the game. The fact that it’s in Early Access also makes me a little cautious about treating it as a finished, carefully paced experience. I’d go in for the sandbox, not because I expected it to hand me a tidy set of things to complete.

There’s something funny about having the power to reshape a universe and still needing to stop and think about what you want to do next. I mean that as a compliment. If you already like space and physics, the scale could make even a simple experiment feel worth watching, and if you don’t, the game may not give you enough structure to turn curiosity into momentum.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The waiting is probably what makes this more workable during exercise than a lot of simulation games. You can set up an experiment, watch it develop, and take a moment to decide what you want to alter next, so I wouldn’t expect the same constant thumb work you get from an action game. I’d try it at a comfortable pace rather than treating it like something that needs to match every step. A planet taking its time is not going to care if you’re jogging.

The interface still matters, because adjusting a simulation is different from steering a character with a couple of simple inputs. I can’t confirm controller support from the information here, so I’d check that before buying it with a gamepad only in mind; until then, I’d plan for a mouse and keyboard. On a treadmill, reaching for a mouse and clicking around could make the whole setup fussy, especially when you’re moving. On a bike, I’d expect the same pauses and decisions to be easier to fit around a steady pace, but I wouldn’t assume it turns into a hands free game.

I’d skip trying to make every experiment happen while you’re moving. Set up the next change when you’re comfortable, then watch for a while, and pause if you need to take your attention back to the belt or pedals. I don’t know that the game will keep a person entertained through a whole workout without some interest in the science itself, but it does seem forgiving of gaps in your attention. It’s not asking you to hit a perfect shot while your hand bounces around.

## Computer Requirements

I’d look at the graphics card first, because the minimum calls for dedicated video hardware and warns that integrated graphics can still cause problems even when the listed memory requirement is met. A fairly recent mid range graphics card should be the sensible target, rather than assuming any laptop that can run other casual games will be fine. Space is not the part that’s going to fill up your universe.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Universe Sandbox sounds like a thoughtful, curious game for someone who likes experimenting more than being pushed from one objective to the next. I’d suggest it for a slower bike workout or an easy treadmill session if you can manage the controls comfortably, but I’d skip it if you want nonstop action or a gamepad setup you don’t have to check first.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
