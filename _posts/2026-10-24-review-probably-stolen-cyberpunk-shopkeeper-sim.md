I’d put this on an easy walk, not a run where I’m trying to keep my breathing organized, because running a questionable little shop while rent climbs sounds like enough decisions for one belt. The cyberpunk space station part is fun, but the shopkeeping is what makes me think this could work between steps.

## Gameplay

I like that the goal is practical and a little desperate: earn enough to buy out your shop before rent forces you out. That gives the crafting and trading a reason to matter, instead of asking me to collect piles of things just because a game has piles of things. Smuggling goods of questionable origin adds a nice bit of trouble to the routine, though I’d probably be the person trying to keep everything just barely legal and then getting surprised when the factions have opinions.

The faction loyalties sound like the part that could give the shop a life beyond the daily business, and I’m curious how much of that is weighing choices against one another versus just picking a side and seeing what happens. I don’t know how quickly the game puts those decisions in front of you, so I can’t tell you if they’ll interrupt the shopkeeping or give it a good rhythm. Either way, rent is a pretty effective deadline. I’ve had less motivation to finish chores than that.

Crafting and trading should give you something to focus on, but this is still a simulation and strategy game, so I’d expect periods where the interesting move is to look at your options and think for a bit. That’s not bad on its own. I like games that let you take your time, but if the choices are mostly in menus, I can see a workout turning into a slow walk while I read the fine print on a box of suspicious space goods.

There’s a good tension in deciding how far you’ll go to stay afloat, and that’s more interesting to me than simply making the shop bigger and bigger. I’d be more interested in a choice that changes who trusts me or what I can trade than in a long list of products to manage, though I don’t know how much the game leans toward each. The premise gives it a useful direction. It isn’t just running a store in a station, it’s trying to keep the store when the station is squeezing you.

I’d give it a try when you want a game that keeps your mind occupied without asking for quick reactions every few seconds. The potential drag is that thoughtful shopkeeping can turn into sitting still and sorting through options, and that’s not what I want for every workout. It sounds like a pleasant change from games that keep shouting at me through the screen.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A steady walk or an easy bike ride seems like the sensible place to start, because crafting, trading, and faction decisions sound like things you can take at your own pace. I wouldn’t begin with a hard run and assume I can make careful choices while bouncing around. My decision making gets less impressive when my whole body is doing something else.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information here, so I wouldn’t buy it specifically for a controller workout without checking first. That matters more for a shop simulator than it would for a game where you can get by with a few basic inputs, because menus and inventory decisions are likely to be part of the regular routine. I’d check the controls before getting on the belt. It’s a boring step, but so is trying to read a small menu while moving.

If you do have a setup that feels comfortable, I’d keep the pace low enough that you can stop and think without having to grab the rails, and I’d save the more involved decisions for a slower stretch. The rising rent gives the game pressure, but it doesn’t sound like pressure that comes from aiming or hitting a precise button at the exact right moment, which is a much better kind of pressure for exercise. Rent can wait for me to finish a thought.

On a bike, I’d expect the same general tradeoff, with a bit more room to focus if the controls take both hands or the decisions get dense. I wouldn’t plan on making this my fastest workout game, and I’d skip it on a day when I’m trying to do intervals or keep my attention on something else. This seems better suited to an easy session where the shop can have most of my brain.
