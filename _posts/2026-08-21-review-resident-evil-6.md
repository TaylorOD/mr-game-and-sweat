Leon and Chris make this a busy workout, but I’d play Resident Evil 6 on a bike or at a walking pace, not try to run through the firefights. There’s enough aiming and sudden nonsense to make your legs feel like they’re getting in the way.

## Gameplay

I like that the game keeps putting something in front of you to deal with, even if it can’t always decide whether it wants to be survival horror or an action movie. You’re fighting zombies, then J’avo, then some enormous thing that seems to have been invited because the last room was getting a little quiet. It keeps your attention. It also means you’re rarely settling into a calm rhythm for long.

The different campaigns give the game a lot of variety, and I think that helps when you’re playing while exercising because you’re not just doing the same kind of encounter for the whole game. Leon’s sections lean more toward horror, while Chris’s feel much more like a military action game, and Jake’s bring their own flavor of trouble. I wouldn’t say all of it works equally well, but changing the pace does keep me from mentally wandering off to think about how much laundry there probably is.

Then the game starts interrupting its own momentum with cutscenes and quick time events, and that’s where I’d get a little annoyed. You can be moving through a fight, tracking enemies and trying to keep your aim steady, and then suddenly the game wants you to react to something on screen right away. I don’t know, I’d rather have a clean stretch of fighting than be asked to do a tiny emergency button exercise while I’m already doing a different exercise.

There’s also a lot of action here for a game that’s supposed to have survival horror in the mix. I like the messy fights and the way enemies can keep you moving around the room, but I wouldn’t go in expecting long stretches of tense, quiet exploration. The game is more interested in putting you in a dramatic situation and seeing how much shooting it takes to get you out. It mostly succeeds at keeping me busy.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A stationary bike feels like the easier match, because you can keep pedaling through the combat without every step nudging your aim around. The game works with a controller, which is what you want here, but there’s still a lot of camera control and aiming, and being able to use a controller doesn’t make your thumb suddenly precise. I’d start with a comfortable pace and see how much focus the fights take.

On a treadmill, I’d keep it to walking, at least for the parts where enemies are coming at you. A slow belt might be fine during quieter stretches, but shooting while running sounds like a reliable way to aim at the ceiling and then get hit by something I was looking right at. I’d lower the difficulty if the action starts asking for more reactions than the workout can comfortably spare.

The quick time events are the part I’d be most cautious about. They can ask for fast input while you’re also trying to stay balanced and keep moving, and I don’t know that I’d want to be jogging through those scenes. If a fight gets hectic, it’s completely reasonable to pause the workout or slow down instead of treating every encounter like an audition for an action movie.

I’d play it solo for a workout, and I’d use the lower difficulty if I mainly wanted to stay moving rather than prove anything to the game. The campaigns are long enough to give you plenty to do, but the cutscenes and abrupt set pieces mean it won’t feel like a perfectly even exercise session. Some sections will have you moving and thinking, and some will have you watching a dramatic person explain something at considerable length.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are old enough that the graphics card is the first thing I’d check, though this isn’t asking for a recent, powerful gaming machine. If your computer has a very old dedicated card around the listed level or better, the requirements look manageable.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Resident Evil 6 is a decent workout game if you like action, can keep a controller steady, and don’t mind the story stopping for its dramatic business now and then. I’d suggest it for a bike ride or a careful walk, but not for a run where you want your attention somewhere else.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
