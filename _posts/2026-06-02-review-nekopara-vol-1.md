A patisserie full of catgirl conversation sounds like a better fit for an easy workout than a run where you need to watch every step. I’d save NEKOPARA Vol. 1 for a steady pace, because reading is most of what you’ll be doing.

## Gameplay

The setup has a little more going for it than the title might suggest: Kashou Minaduki leaves his family’s confectionery business to open La Soleil, and the story builds around that shop and the catgirls in his life. I like that the bakery gives the story somewhere ordinary to happen, instead of making every scene feel like it’s waiting for a big dramatic turn. It’s a small setting, though, and the game mostly wants you to spend time with its characters.

That means reading dialogue for long stretches. There isn’t much to do between conversations, so if you want a game that keeps your hands busy or gives you regular little goals, this probably won’t hold your attention. I think it can be pleasant company when you’re in the mood for a slower story, but it’s closer to sitting with a book than playing something that pulls you into action. There’s no secret bakery management game hiding in there.

The catgirl premise is clearly the point, and the game asks you to enjoy the personalities and relationships rather than chase a complicated plot. That’s a real preference thing. I can see why people would find the cast sweet and want to spend time with them, and I can also see someone getting a few scenes in and thinking, okay, I understand the general situation now. Both reactions seem fair.

I’d give this a try if you like character focused visual novels and don’t mind a story taking its time. If you’re only interested because “cat paradise” sounds like a funny game premise, I’d be a little cautious, because most of the play is still reading. I don’t know that the setting alone would keep me engaged, but the bakery gives the conversations a comfortable place to settle. It’s a quiet one.

There’s also no need to treat it like a game you have to power through. You can take the dialogue at your own pace, and I think that’s a better way to approach it than expecting it to compete with a fast game for your attention. I’d play a bit, see whether the cast works for you, and stop there if it doesn’t. Not every game needs to become a project.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The biggest workout question here is whether you can read comfortably while moving, not whether you can react quickly. The story doesn’t demand twitch timing, and there aren’t enemies or action scenes that are going to punish a small mistake in your stride. That’s helpful, but keeping your eyes on dialogue while the screen is moving around is still a pretty specific kind of multitasking.

I’d try it on a treadmill at a pace where you can read without leaning toward the screen, and I’d keep the run easy enough that you’re not breathing hard through every line. On a bike, I’d expect the steady pedaling to make it easier to focus, though you still have to look up at the screen and follow the text. I don’t know if I’d make this my choice for intervals. The characters can wait.

You don’t need quick inputs, which is the main reason I’d consider it for exercise at all. I’d check that your control setup is comfortable before you start, since having to reach for a mouse or keyboard every time you advance dialogue would get old pretty quickly. If you have an easy way to move through the text, the game’s gentle pace works in its favor. If not, the treadmill is going to feel like a very slow trip to the keyboard.

I’d also skip it for a workout where your attention needs to stay on pace or form. This is a game that asks you to read, and I’d rather follow the conversation than pretend I’m absorbing it while I’m trying to run hard. On an easy walk or relaxed ride, though, the lack of pressure is a plus. You can just keep moving.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very modest, so an older computer with basic integrated graphics should be enough to run it. I’d care more about having a screen that’s easy to read from your workout setup than about buying a gaming graphics card for this one.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Save NEKOPARA Vol. 1 for an easy workout and for days when you genuinely want a character focused story. If you need constant action to stay interested while exercising, I’d pick something else.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
