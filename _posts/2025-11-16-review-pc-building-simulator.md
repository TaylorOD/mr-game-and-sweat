PC Building Simulator is a poor fit for treadmill or bike workouts: repairing and assembling PCs involves reading job notes, navigating menus, and carefully handling components. Its calm pace avoids twitchy action, but you’ll need to keep your attention—and your hands—on the game.

# Gameplay

Building and growing a repair business gives the game a satisfying sense of progression, and working with licensed components makes diagnosing and assembling PCs appealing for hardware fans. But much of the play is careful, step-by-step interaction with menus, parts, and software screens. It can be absorbing at a desk, though the slow, deliberate tasks are more likely to demand focus than distract you from a workout.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

# Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The Steam listing has no controller support, so expect to rely on mouse and keyboard. The game’s unhurried pace means there’s little pressure to react quickly, but selecting components and performing repairs still calls for visual attention and precise input—awkward while walking or pedaling.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

# Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements include a GeForce GTX 660 with 2 GB of memory or a Radeon R9 285 with 2 GB. The store page says integrated GPUs may work but are not supported, so this isn’t a safe bet for a computer relying on integrated graphics. An older or basic dedicated graphics card should be enough to meet the stated minimum.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

# Conclusion and Score

PC Building Simulator offers a rewarding, methodical repair-and-build loop for people who enjoy computer hardware. But its mouse-driven, detail-focused play makes it better suited to sitting down than exercising: the pace is gentle, yet the game still asks you to watch and handle each task carefully.

### Overall Score: 1.33 out of 3
