I’d put Your Only Move Is HUSTLE on a treadmill for an easy walk, because a fight that gives you time to think through each move is a lot more workable than trying to land a combo while your feet are bouncing. I’d keep the pace comfortable, though, since thinking frame by frame and jogging hard at the same time sounds like a good way to forget one of those jobs.

## Gameplay

The turn based fighting idea is what makes this interesting to me. You’re not reacting to a punch at the last second, you’re slowing things down and choosing how the fight should unfold, which turns a fighting game into something closer to planning out a scene. That’s a neat way to make every move feel deliberate, and I like that the point is both outsmarting someone and making the fight look good.

The tradeoff is that I expect to spend plenty of time thinking instead of doing. Mastering a technique and working out a flashy combo sounds fun when I’m sitting down, but I can picture staring at my options for a while and trying to figure out what actually sets up the next move. I don’t know how much of that decision making will feel natural at first. I’d probably need to give it a little time.

The promise that you don’t need a training mode is appealing, because I’d rather learn by trying things than sit through a long lesson before I get to play. But “no training mode required” doesn’t mean I’ll instantly understand how to make a fight look good, and I suspect my first attempts at choreography would be less action movie and more two people negotiating over where to stand.

Fighting friends sounds like a nice fit for the game, since you can take your time and enjoy the choices instead of treating every match like a reflex contest. Going up against opponents across the world could also give you a reason to keep improving your technique, although that sounds more like something I’d want to do when I’m focused than during a tough run. There’s a lot to think about here, in a good way.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The game’s stop and think pace could work well while you’re walking or pedaling, since you aren’t being asked to make a split second decision every time something happens. I’d expect to get distracted by planning a move, but that’s still better than having to keep my eyes locked on the screen because an opponent can punish a missed input right away. You can take your time.

I would try the controls before starting a workout, and I can’t confirm full controller support from the information here. If your setup works comfortably with a controller, that would be my first choice while moving, since reaching for a keyboard and mouse seems like an unnecessary complication when the belt is already doing its thing. If not, I’d play seated rather than try to improvise.

A bike might be easier if you want to lean in and study your options, but I don’t think a treadmill is out of the question. The turn based structure should leave room to keep your steps steady while you decide what you want the next part of the fight to look like. I’d start at a pace where looking down at the screen doesn’t throw off your rhythm.

I wouldn’t use a hard workout as the first place to learn the game. There’s a difference between choosing a move carefully and getting so wrapped up in a combo that you stop paying attention to how fast you’re walking. I can see this working best for an easy session, where the thinking is part of the entertainment and not another thing to manage.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older computer with integrated graphics, so this shouldn’t call for a gaming machine. I’d check that your system is 64 bit and has a broadband connection if you want to play opponents around the world; the storage requirement is small.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is for you if you like fighting games but would rather plan a clever move than rely on quick reactions, and it could make an easy treadmill walk or bike session more engaging. I’d check your controls before exercising, and I’d skip it for a workout where you need something fast and simple.
