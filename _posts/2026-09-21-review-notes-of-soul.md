I’d treat Notes of Soul as a story focused game first, not something to put on when you want the action to keep your legs moving. The role playing setup sounds interesting, but the description makes me expect a lot of attention going to the story and its cultural details.

## Gameplay

The idea of an experience hall where you take part in a scripted story is unusual in a way I appreciate, because it sounds like the game is asking you to engage with a place and its story rather than just chase a score. You can also learn folk knowledge and encounter a culture that may be unfamiliar to you, which could make the slower parts feel like they have a reason to be there. I’d be curious to know how much of that comes through in the actual play, since the description doesn’t say what the role playing involves moment to moment.

The protagonist’s sad but warm past sounds like the part most likely to set the pace, and I think that’s where this might lose me as a workout game. When you’re jogging, reading and stopping to follow a story can feel like trying to watch a film while somebody keeps asking you to pick up the pace. That might be exactly what you want on an easy ride, but I wouldn’t count on it keeping you busy in the way an action game does.

I don’t know how much you’re expected to read, or how long the scripted sections go before you get to take part. That matters here more than the list of genres, because Action, Racing, Strategy and RPG all sound like different games, and the description doesn’t explain how those labels fit together. I wouldn’t buy it expecting a racing game just because Racing appears in the genre list. That’s a broad label.

There’s something appealing about a game built around folk knowledge and an unusual setting, and I’m glad it’s trying for that instead of dressing up another familiar adventure. At the same time, I’d want the role playing to give you room to do more than sit through the story, especially if you’re looking for something that can hold your attention while your body is doing the repetitive part. It could be a good fit when you’re happy to take the exercise slowly. I’d be less sure on a run where you need the game to keep moving.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The scripted role playing sounds better suited to a relaxed bike ride than a faster run, mostly because I’d expect to spend some of my attention following the story rather than reacting quickly. That’s an expectation, not a confirmed description of the controls, and I don’t want to pretend I know more about the moment to moment play than the store page says. If the role playing asks for careful choices, I’d keep the pace comfortable and avoid trying to turn this into a hard workout.

Controller support isn’t confirmed in the information I have, so I can’t tell you to plug in a controller and expect it to work. I’d check that before planning a treadmill session, because reaching for a keyboard or mouse while you’re moving is not my idea of a clever little challenge. It’s mostly just a way to make both activities more annoying. If you can use a controller, I’d still test the controls while stationary first.

I don’t know whether the online side means you need to coordinate with other people, or whether you can take part in the story at your own pace. The game is listed as massively multiplayer and requires broadband internet, so I’d be cautious about starting it during a workout until you know what the social parts ask of you. A slow bike ride seems like the safer first try, where you can keep your hands steady and give the story some attention.

If you’re looking for something that fills every minute with movement, this probably isn’t the one I’d reach for. The description points toward story and role playing, and those can be nice company during easy exercise, but they can also turn into a lot of looking and thinking while the treadmill keeps doing its thing. I’d start gently and see whether the actual pace suits you.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum graphics card is old enough that this doesn’t look like a game that demands a new gaming computer, and an older desktop with a dedicated graphics card may be enough. I’d check that your system meets the listed graphics and operating system requirements, and make sure you have the required storage and internet connection. The graphics card is the part I’d look at first.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Notes of Soul sounds worth a look if you want a story led role playing experience and you’re comfortable exercising at an easy pace. For a workout that needs constant action, quick reactions or confirmed controller support, I’d wait until you know more about how it plays.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
