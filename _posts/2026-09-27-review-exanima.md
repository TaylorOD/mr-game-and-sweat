Exanima would make me slow the treadmill down before I tried to swing a sword at anything, because the combat is all awkward weight and timing instead of quick button presses. I think it’s a great game to pay attention to, but it asks for the kind of attention that can make you forget you’re exercising.

## Gameplay

The combat is the reason I’d play Exanima, and also the reason I’d be careful about recommending it. Your character moves and swings with a physical, slightly clumsy feel, so a fight isn’t just a matter of picking an attack and watching it happen. You’re trying to get your footing, manage distance, and make a weapon connect in a way that doesn’t leave you open. When it works, it feels like you’ve actually figured something out. When it doesn’t, I think I’d blame the floor for a minute.

Exploring the dark, low fantasy world gives those fights room to breathe, but it also means the pace can get pretty slow. You’re moving through places, looking for clues and trying to understand what’s going on, and I like that the mystery isn’t served up in a neat little speech every few minutes. I also wouldn’t want to do that kind of careful looking while I’m trying to keep a steady jog. You can end up staring at a corner like it’s going to explain itself.

Arena mode gives you another way to spend time with the combat, and I’d start there if I wanted to learn how the fighting feels before getting invested in the exploration. I don’t know how quickly I’d get comfortable with the movement, since the physics make even ordinary actions feel a little unpredictable, but practicing fights without also worrying about the next piece of the mystery seems sensible. It’s a lot like trying to learn a sport while somebody keeps changing the shape of the ball.

There’s a real appeal to how much weight the game gives its actions. You’re not just breezing from encounter to encounter, and a badly judged swing can turn into a whole mess of recovering your balance and trying to get out of danger. That can be absorbing when I’m sitting down and focused. During a workout, I’d expect some of the careful exploration to feel like a break, and some of the combat to make me stop pedaling so I can keep my character upright.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The treadmill is where I’d be most cautious. Exanima asks for precise movement and deliberate attacks, and that’s a rough combination with your hands bouncing along while you run. I’d turn the belt down to a walking pace, or take it off the treadmill entirely, rather than try to move quickly and react well at the same time. I don’t know how much of a fight I’d manage before my attention went fully to the screen.

A bike seems more workable, especially if you can sit upright and keep your hands steady. I’d still expect to slow down or coast through a fight, because the combat is not the kind where you can mash a button and hope your character sorts it out. The game’s pacing does give you time to look around between encounters, which could fit a relaxed ride, but I’d be ready to stop pedaling when an enemy gets close. My legs can handle a little waiting.

I can’t confirm that a controller setup will feel comfortable here, so I’d check the current controls before buying it specifically for exercise. The combat’s directional movement and timing are the part I’d be concerned about, not whether the screen is busy enough to hold your attention. If you play on a treadmill, I’d keep the pace low and take breaks from running when fights start. If you want to keep a steady, fast workout going, this probably isn’t the one.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimums are modest, and the game names Intel graphics as an option, so you don’t need a high end gaming computer just to consider it. I’d check that your graphics hardware supports OpenGL 4.6, since that’s the part of the requirements most likely to matter on an older computer. Ten gigabytes of storage is not asking you to rearrange the whole room.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Exanima is for you if you want slow, unusual combat and a dark world to poke around in, and you’re willing to give it your full attention. I’d play it on a bike at an easy pace, but I wouldn’t choose it for a run where I’m trying to keep moving the whole time.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
