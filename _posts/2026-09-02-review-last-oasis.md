I’d save Last Oasis for a workout where you’re comfortable with the game asking for your attention, because gathering wood for a walking machine sounds pleasantly steady until somebody wants the ground you’re standing on. The travel could be nice treadmill time, but I wouldn’t treat the whole thing like a relaxed ride.

## Gameplay

The idea of a survival MMO built around wooden walking machines is genuinely cool, and I like that getting somewhere is part of the activity instead of just a loading screen between fights. You’re moving to new lands because resources run out, which gives scavenging and trading an actual reason to happen. I think I’d enjoy that sense of having to keep going, though it also sounds like a game that can turn a simple plan into a lot of chores.

The resource pressure is what makes me hesitate. If supplies deplete fast, then a stretch of travel and gathering isn’t automatically a calm stretch where you can ignore the screen, because you’re trying to stay ahead of the next shortage and whatever other players are doing. That tension might be the whole appeal, and for somebody who likes survival games it probably works. I’m less sure it’s the kind of pressure I’d want on every workout.

Clans and territory give the world a reason to have other people in it, but they also make the game feel less predictable than a single player adventure. A sword fight or a raid can be exciting when you’re sitting still and ready to react, and less fun when you’re trying to keep your steps even while the situation gets worse. I don’t know how much of the game you can spend just traveling or trading without getting pulled into conflict, so I wouldn’t count on it as a quiet gathering game.

There’s a lot here that I’d want to learn before I could relax into it, from keeping resources stocked to understanding how best to use your walker. That can be fun if learning a survival game is part of your evening, but it’s still attention you’re not giving your pace. I’d start with the basic goal of keeping supplied and moving, rather than trying to become a clan leader on day one. That sounds like enough.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Travel and scavenging are the parts I’d aim for during an easier workout, because they sound more forgiving than fighting over territory. The trouble is that Last Oasis is still a multiplayer action game, and a fight can show up when you’d rather be watching your footing or keeping a steady cadence. I can picture myself having a perfectly reasonable plan to gather supplies and then suddenly caring a lot about a sword fight. That’s not ideal treadmill planning.

I can’t confirm full controller support, so I’d check that before buying it for this setup. Even with a controller, I think the shifting attention between movement, survival decisions and other players could be a lot while running. On a bike, where your hands are usually steadier, it might be easier to manage, but the game still wants you to notice what’s happening rather than coast through every moment.

I’d keep the workout easy, and I’d avoid treating clan fights or territory as the part you need to make progress on. If you can focus on travel, trade and gathering, that seems like the least fussy way to play while moving. If the game keeps pulling you into dangerous situations, I’d stop the workout or play it sitting down instead of trying to multitask through a fight. The walker can wait.

I don’t know how much you can control the pace of those encounters, and that uncertainty matters here. A game that sometimes lets you gather quietly and sometimes demands quick decisions isn’t a dealbreaker, but it’s not a great match for a hard run either. I’d give it a try on an easy session only after confirming the controller situation.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimum asks for an older dedicated graphics card, so this doesn’t look like a game you should expect to run well on integrated graphics alone. I’d look at the graphics card first, then make sure you have room for the 40 GB install, because survival games have a way of taking up the space you were saving for something else.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Last Oasis sounds most appealing if you already enjoy survival MMOs and want a world where traveling, gathering and fighting all matter. For exercise, I’d only try it at an easy pace, after checking controller support, and I’d skip the expectation of a calm workout where nothing urgent happens.
