Keeping a whole front line organized while your feet are bouncing sounds like a lot, so I’d save Call to Arms - Gates of Hell: Ostfront for a slow bike ride or a singleplayer session where you can give the screen your attention. It looks like the kind of strategy game that can pull you in, but it also asks you to think at exactly the moment your legs are trying to think about something else.

## Gameplay

I like the idea of a realistic World War II strategy game where you command troops in historical singleplayer and can move into a dynamic campaign, PvP or PvE battles. There’s room to choose the kind of play you want instead of being pushed through one long story, and that matters when you’re deciding whether you have the focus for a full battle or just want to do a little planning. I’d start in singleplayer, because learning how a game like this expects you to manage troops is already enough to keep me occupied. I can leave the strangers out of it for now.

The strategy part sounds like the main draw, and also the part that makes this a stop and think game rather than something you can play on autopilot. You’re commanding troops, not just steering one character toward the next objective, so I’d expect to spend time considering what to do before committing to it, and then watching to see whether the plan works. That can be great when you’re settled in and paying attention. While I’m jogging, the same pause to look at the situation could turn into me staring at the screen like I’ve forgotten why I opened the fridge.

I think the dynamic campaign is probably the most appealing place to spend time, since it gives you a way to return to the game without every session needing to feel like a separate match. PvP sounds like a much harder fit for exercise, because other players won’t necessarily wait while you look over what’s happening, and I’d rather not turn a workout into a meeting where everyone else has already moved on. PvE could be more comfortable if you want battles without that pressure. I’d still pick the mode based on how much attention you have that day.

There’s a lot here for someone who likes commanding a battle and making choices that have consequences, and the realistic approach is likely to be a plus for that person. I’m less convinced it’s a great game to put on when you want a simple distraction and a steady pace, since the interesting decisions are also the ones that ask you to concentrate. I’d play it when I actually want to think about a strategy game. That’s a pretty specific mood.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The movement you want from your legs is steady, but the movement this game asks from your attention is not always steady, and I think that mismatch matters more than the fact it’s a strategy game. When the screen asks you to check the battle and make a decision, I’d expect my hands to get less precise if I’m running, especially if I’m trying to look down at a treadmill display too. I can picture myself missing the useful information and then blaming my legs, which are innocent.

I’d try it on a stationary bike first, at an easy pace, and keep the workout simple enough that I can focus on the battle. That feels like a more practical way to handle a game where I expect to be moving between planning and action, instead of trying to hold a careful pace on a treadmill while also paying attention to several troops. If the controls feel awkward with a controller, that would make the bike suggestion less useful, so I’d check the control setup before buying it specifically for controller play. I can’t confirm controller support from the information here.

For exercise, I’d skip PvP and stick with historical singleplayer or the dynamic campaign, where I can put the game down when I need to sort out what I’m doing. I wouldn’t start a fast workout and assume I’ll keep up with the battle, because this doesn’t sound like a game built around forgiving, simple inputs. It may be fine once you know the controls, but I’d give it a try at a gentle pace before treating it like a regular treadmill game.

There’s a nice kind of focus in a strategy game when you can take your time, and that could make a calm bike session more engaging than just watching a screen. On a treadmill, I think I’d spend too much of the workout trying to keep my eyes and hands steady, and not enough of it enjoying the game. Some games are better when the belt isn’t moving.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This needs a dedicated graphics card around the GTX 1070 or RX 5600 level, so I’d check your graphics card before anything else. The 16 GB of memory and 80 GB of storage are also worth making room for, and an SSD is recommended for the best experience.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This makes sense for you if you want a serious strategy game and are happy to exercise at a pace that leaves room to think. I’d choose a stationary bike, start in singleplayer, and skip PvP during a workout.
