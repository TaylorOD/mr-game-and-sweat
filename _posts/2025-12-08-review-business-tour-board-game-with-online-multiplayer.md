A property trading game is a much better treadmill companion than another game asking you to aim at something while your whole body is bouncing. Business Tour gives you time to think, though I’d expect the other players to make that time feel a little less like a workout break.

## Gameplay

The appeal here is that the game has room for a plan, and a plan is something I can manage while moving, at least until I start treating a property decision like it needs a committee meeting. You’re playing a multiplayer tabletop game built around making your own monopoly, so the fun is in watching a small decision turn into a bigger one, not in reacting quickly. That sounds like a good fit for someone who likes strategy but doesn’t want to spend a run reading a novel.

The online part is probably what makes the game more interesting than playing the same board game alone, and also what could make it drag. Other people bring their own plans and their own pace, which is nice when a match is lively and less nice when everyone takes a while to choose. I don’t know how consistently quick the matches feel in practice, so I’d go in expecting some waiting rather than assuming every minute will have you making a move.

Daily tasks give you a reason to come back, which is useful in a free to play game, but I wouldn’t let a checklist decide whether your workout was fun. I’d rather pick a match because I want to play than turn a relaxed ride into a shift at the task factory. Still, having a small goal can help when you’re getting on the treadmill and don’t want to spend five minutes deciding what to do.

Playing with friends sounds like the easiest way to get the best out of it. You can take the board game seriously, or you can make a questionable choice and have someone you know ask what you were thinking, which is most of the appeal of tabletop games anyway. Competing against players around the world gives you more people to play with, but I’d expect that side to feel more competitive and less forgiving of a distracted turn.

I think the game will keep your attention in short stretches, then hand you some quieter moments while the rest of the table acts. That’s a good balance for a workout if you like thinking between bursts of activity, but it might not be enough if you need constant action to forget you’re exercising. I’d give it a try if you already enjoy board games. It isn’t trying to be a sprint.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The controls should be manageable while you’re moving because the game supports a controller and the tabletop setup doesn’t ask you to make fast, precise movements. I’d use a steady walking pace on the treadmill or an easy effort on the bike, then keep the controller somewhere you can reach without leaning around. There’s no point making a property decision and nearly stepping off the belt at the same time.

The part I’d pay attention to is how much you care about the match. Online multiplayer can make a slow decision feel urgent even when the game itself isn’t demanding quick hands, and I know I can turn a casual game into a serious little job without anyone asking me to. If that starts happening, lower the pace or play with friends who won’t mind you taking a moment to settle back into the workout.

I’d probably skip it for a hard run. You want enough attention left to follow what the other players are doing, and I don’t think the game is going to distract you from your legs as completely as an action game would. On a bike, I’d expect the slower rhythm to fit more naturally, but a treadmill walk should work too. It’s a board game, not a reflex contest.

Full controller support is a real plus here, and the turn based feel gives you room to adjust your grip or take a breath between decisions. I don’t know whether every match will give you a comfortable pause, since other players set some of the pace, but the basic idea is much friendlier to exercise than a game that needs constant aiming. Keep the workout easy enough that you can still think.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This looks like a game that should run on an older computer, and I’d check that your system can connect to the internet before worrying about graphics. The requirements don’t point to a demanding visual setup, so this probably won’t be the reason you need a new machine.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Business Tour makes sense for someone who wants a casual strategy game during a steady walk or ride, especially if they have friends to play with. Skip it if you need constant action to stay engaged, but if choosing your next move sounds like a decent way to pass a workout, give it a try.
