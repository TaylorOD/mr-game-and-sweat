I think Isonzo can work for a workout, but I’d keep the pace easy because trying to line up a bolt action shot while your hands are bouncing is a very specific way to miss a person who is standing still. The Alpine setting is lovely, which is a nice thing to look at while you’re catching your breath.

## Gameplay

The Offensive mode is the reason I’d play this one. You push through a section of the Italian Front, try to take objectives, and then move on to the next stretch if your side manages it, so there’s usually a clear thing to focus on instead of a long story scene asking you to sit still. The fighting can get busy fast, with attackers coming through a valley and defenders trying to keep them there. It has a good sense of direction.

A lot of the appeal is that the rifle fights aren’t just about pointing at a shape and holding the trigger. You need to aim, make a shot count, and take cover while people with machine guns and artillery are making the whole area feel less like a pleasant hike. I like that the rifles make you slow down a little, though that can be frustrating when you’re trying to get back to the action and there’s a long sightline between you and the next objective.

The class choices give you a few different jobs, which is nice if the standard rifle pace starts to feel samey. I’d probably start with Rifleman and get used to the maps before picking a role that asks you to think about more than shooting. There’s a lot happening around the objectives, and I don’t know that I’d want to learn every class while also trying to keep a steady workout going.

Some stretches are less exciting than the firefights, because you can be moving toward the front and waiting for the next push to form. I think that’s part of what makes the battles feel like a team effort, but it also means the game doesn’t keep you busy every second. Sometimes the most useful thing you can do is get into position and wait, which is a very reasonable part of a war game and not my favorite thing to do while the belt is moving.

The setting does a lot for me. Northern Italy has rocky slopes, narrow valleys, and towns that make the fighting feel different from one open field after another, and the scenery is good enough that I’d probably notice it even while trying not to get shot. I don’t know how often I’d stop to admire a mountain when people are firing at me, but it’s there.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

This is a game where a small controller wobble can turn a careful shot into a shot at the wall, so I’d start with a slow walk or an easy bike pace rather than trying to push hard. Controller support is available, which gives you a workable setup, but aiming still asks for more control than a game where you can just steer toward the next thing.

I’d adjust the controller sensitivity until small thumb movements don’t send the sight racing past a target, then stick to a class and play style that lets me take cover and pick my moments. The rifle fights give you a little room to breathe between shots, but enemies can appear while you’re lining up, and that’s when keeping the workout pace and keeping the team alive start competing for the same bit of attention.

Online matches don’t wait for you to stop pedaling or steady your hands, so I’d avoid a hard run and treat each match as a stretch where I need to pay attention. There are also moments where you’re moving back toward the fight after getting taken out, and I’d use those to settle into a comfortable pace instead of trying to sprint through the dull parts. I would not play this while doing an all out interval. My thumbs aren’t that organized.

The bike may be a little easier for aiming because your upper body can stay steadier, but I wouldn’t call it an automatic bike game. You still need to look around and react to people shooting at you, and a fast turn at the wrong moment can cost you a fight. For either setup, I’d play at a pace where you can still make a deliberate shot, and pause between matches if you need a breather.

## Computer Requirements

This needs a dedicated graphics card around the level of a GTX 970 or Radeon R9 290, so it’s not something I’d expect to run happily on basic integrated graphics. I’d check the graphics card first, then make sure there’s room for the 30 GB install, because the mountain views need a little space.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

I’d suggest Isonzo to someone who wants a focused multiplayer shooter and is willing to keep their workout pace modest while aiming. It’s a harder fit for an intense treadmill run, but an easy walk or steady ride gives the fights room to work.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
