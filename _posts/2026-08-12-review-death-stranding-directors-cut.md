The delivery routes in DEATH STRANDING DIRECTOR’S CUT could make a treadmill workout feel surprisingly natural, because Sam is walking across a broken America for much of the game, but I’d keep the speed easy enough that a rocky slope doesn’t become a balance exercise for both of you. I’d play it for the strange world and the quiet stretches, not for a fast moving action game.

## Gameplay

I think this is a good game with an awkward fit for people who want something happening every minute. A lot of the appeal comes from planning a route, loading up Sam with cargo, and then watching the terrain turn a simple delivery into a little problem you have to solve. That can be absorbing in the same way as figuring out how to carry too many grocery bags in one trip, except the groceries are important to the future of humanity.

The walking itself has more going on than it sounds like it should. You pay attention to slopes and uneven ground, and keeping your cargo balanced matters, so I think the quiet sections would give your hands something to do without demanding constant, sharp reactions. But that calm can stretch out, and if your idea of exercise gaming is a steady stream of enemies or action, you may start wondering if Sam could just take the bus.

Then the game pulls you into its story, and I don’t know that the long cinematic stretches would work for everyone trying to keep moving. There are plenty of scenes where the game wants your attention more than your legs, and it can be a little odd to be jogging while watching a serious conversation play out. I’d probably lower the treadmill speed or pause when a scene starts, rather than pretend I can follow the plot and watch my footing at the same time.

The world and the delivery premise are the parts I like most. Connecting isolated survivors gives the long walks a reason to exist, and the game’s version of a ruined America is strange enough that the next trip doesn’t feel like walking around the same empty field again. There’s also combat with threats like BTs and MULEs, but I’d call those interruptions, not the reason to pick this up.

The DIRECTOR’S CUT adds things like a buddy bot and a firing range, which give you other ways to engage with the game beyond simply carrying cargo over hills. I’d be curious about the new tools, but I wouldn’t expect every added activity to be equally useful as workout play. A firing range sounds like a nice change of pace, though it also sounds like a fine place to stop walking for a while.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The steady pace is a real advantage here, as long as you don’t crank the belt up and then ask your thumbs to steer Sam over rocks, avoid danger, and keep every box upright. Full controller support makes the game practical to use while moving, and most of the time you’re not being forced into twitchy inputs. I think an easy treadmill pace is the better match, because watching the ground in the game and keeping your own feet settled are both part of the deal.

On a bike, the slow stretches should be comfortable, and there’s less concern about matching your stride to what’s happening on screen. The tradeoff is that the game’s traversal can ask for more attention than a bike ride where you mostly want to zone out. When Sam is struggling over rough ground, you’ll likely need to look at the screen and make small corrections, and that’s not ideal if your legs are already working hard.

I’d change the difficulty or accessibility settings if the challenge starts making every delivery feel like a careful balancing act, and I’d take the slower routes on days when I want the game to share attention with the workout. I’d also pause for long story scenes or use them as a reason to slow down, because there’s no medal for being confused about who’s talking while you’re trying to steer around a cliff. The action is manageable when it comes, but I wouldn’t pick a difficult fight as the part of the game I want to handle mid sprint.

The game also has moments where you need to stop and think about the route or cargo, so it won’t keep your brain busy in a simple, even way. That’s a plus if you like a workout that comes and goes in intensity, and a minus if you want a rhythm game that keeps the same beat. I’d give it a try on a relaxed session first, before deciding whether the story pauses are a dealbreaker.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You don’t need a high end gaming computer, but this does ask for a dedicated graphics card around the level of an older GTX 1050 or RX 560, so integrated graphics probably aren’t the plan. I’d check that graphics card requirement first, and make sure you have room for the 80 GB install, which is a lot of space for a game about delivering things.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is for you if slow exploration and a weird, serious story sound like good treadmill company, and you’re willing to pause or ease off when the game asks for your full attention. If you want constant action while you work out, I’d pick something else.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
