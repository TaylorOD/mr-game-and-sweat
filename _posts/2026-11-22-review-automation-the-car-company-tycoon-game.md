You can spend a workout designing an engine instead of steering a car, which is either a pretty good deal or a very strange way to spend your cardio. I think Automation - The Car Company Tycoon Game is interesting, but it asks for enough focused menu time that I’d save it for a slow walk or an easy ride.

## Gameplay

The engine design is the part I’d want to poke at first, because there’s something genuinely fun about making choices that affect how a car turns out instead of just picking a color and calling it engineering. You’re balancing the technical side against the business side, and I like that the game cares about both. A brilliant design that doesn’t make sense for the company you’re trying to build isn’t automatically a win, which gives the decisions some weight.

There’s a lot to think through, though, and that’s both the point and the thing that makes this a hard game to recommend for every workout. You’re making plans, looking at the results, and then changing your approach, so I don’t think it keeps your attention in the same easy way a racing game does. Some stretches are going to be you staring at a design choice and trying to work out what it means for the next one. It can make the treadmill feel very long.

The car design tools sound like the sort of thing I could get absorbed in, especially if you enjoy getting specific about how a car should be built. I don’t know how much detail you personally want from a game, but I think there’s a real audience for having room to make a car your own and then seeing if those choices make sense as a business. I’m also a little wary of being handed a lot of options when I’m already trying to remember to keep walking. I have enough trouble deciding what to eat when I’m hungry.

Automation is in Early Access, so I’d go in expecting a game that’s still being worked on rather than a finished, perfectly smooth experience. That’s not a reason to avoid it by itself, and games about building a company can grow in interesting ways over time, but it does mean I’d want to know what’s currently in the game before buying it. Looking at screenshots or a current feature list first seems reasonable. I wouldn’t want you to buy it expecting a simple car builder and then find out you’ve signed up to manage an automotive empire.

What I like is that the game has a clear idea of what it wants you to care about. You aren’t just making something that looks good, you’re trying to make smart engineering and business decisions and see whether they add up. I think that’s a much more interesting hook than a game that only lets you decorate cars, but it also means you have to enjoy thinking through the tradeoffs. If you mostly want to drive, this isn’t going to scratch that itch.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, I’d treat this as a game for walking rather than running, because the design and business decisions are likely to have you looking closely at menus and stopping to think. The nice thing is that the game isn’t built around split second reactions, so you aren’t trying to steer through traffic while your hands bounce around. The less nice thing is that your attention is doing a lot of work even when the game isn’t asking you to move quickly.

A bike sounds a little easier to manage, mostly because you can keep your legs moving while you take a moment to read or make a choice. I still wouldn’t expect to fly through the game while exercising, and I’d be prepared to pause or slow down when I’m trying to understand a design decision. This is not the one I’d pick for a day when I want the game to carry me along. You have to bring some focus to it.

I can’t confirm full controller support, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming you can put the keyboard and mouse away. The kind of work the game asks you to do seems much more suited to careful menu selection than loose controller inputs, and reaching for controls while you’re moving could get annoying fast. I’d check the current control options before setting it up on a treadmill. That’s a small bit of homework, but at least it’s the useful kind.

If you do give it a try while exercising, I’d make the workout forgiving and avoid trying to push the pace while you’re learning the game. You’re making engineering and company decisions, not reacting to a rival car, so there’s no reason to hurry through it. I don’t know that I’d use it for a hard run, but for a relaxed walk where you’re happy to pause and read, it could be a pretty unusual way to pass the time.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are fairly modest, and the store page says it can run on integrated graphics, though it also warns that performance may be poor in some situations at the lowest settings. I’d check the graphics chip first and leave room for the game to run less smoothly than you’d like. At least you aren’t looking at a machine built to launch cars into orbit.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is for you if designing engines and making business decisions sounds like a pleasant way to spend time, and you don’t mind a game that asks you to stop and think. I’d play it while walking slowly, but I wouldn’t choose it for a run or a workout where I want to stay focused on moving.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
