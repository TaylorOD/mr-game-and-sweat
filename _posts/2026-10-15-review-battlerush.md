BattleRush sounds like a game I’d save for a steady workout, because building a defense while trying to beat other players seems like a lot to ask of your thumbs once the treadmill starts bouncing. The destructible environment sounds fun, but I wouldn’t count on a multiplayer match waiting while you sort yourself out.

## Gameplay

The idea of a battlefield you can reshape is appealing, and I like that the description gives you something to do besides just charging at the other side. You can build a defense, create an army, and try to crush the enemy, which sounds like it could keep you making decisions instead of staring at a long story scene. I’d expect that to be more engaging than a game that asks you to jog through a hallway until somebody finally shows up.

There’s a tradeoff, though, because building defenses and putting an army together both sound like they could pull you into planning mode. I don’t know how much time the game spends on those choices, and I wouldn’t want to pretend I know the pace from a short description, but even a handful of pauses to think can make a workout feel like you’re waiting for a meeting to start. That may be exactly what you want on a slower ride. It’s less ideal if you’re trying to keep your attention on moving.

The total destruction is the part I’d be curious about. I like when the space around a fight can change, because it gives you a reason to pay attention to where you are instead of repeating the same approach, but I also wonder whether it makes the battlefield harder to read when you’re already moving. I don’t know how much of the environment can be destroyed or how that plays out, so I’d treat that as a promising idea rather than a guaranteed workout bonus.

Since it’s multiplayer action, I’d expect other players to keep the pressure up, and that’s probably the strongest reason to try it if you want a game that can hold your attention. There’s no big story description here to suggest long stretches of reading or cut scenes getting between you and the action. Still, the planning sounds like it could create slower patches, and I’d rather have those than a game that never lets me think for a second. Just not while I’m trying to walk a straight line.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d start with a bike rather than a treadmill, mostly because a multiplayer fight where you’re building defenses and managing an army sounds like it may ask for more careful input than I’d want while my steps are moving my hands around. That’s an expectation, not a report from playing it. You may be more coordinated than I am, but I’d rather not learn how steady my aim is by finding out at the same time as another player.

Controller support isn’t confirmed in the details I have, so I wouldn’t assume it’ll work smoothly with a controller. I’d check that before setting up your workout, and if you can’t verify it, try the game sitting down first. Having to reach for a keyboard while you’re on a treadmill sounds like a small decision that could turn into a very stupid one.

Even with a controller, I think the multiplayer part makes this a tougher fit than a game that waits for you. You can’t count on stopping whenever you need to adjust your pace, and the action could ask you to react quickly while you’re also trying to keep a steady cadence. I’d keep the workout easy and choose a bike if you have one available, since it’s easier to stay stable while making more precise movements. If the game turns out to be calmer than I expect, great. I’m not going to plan around that.

I’d also skip any attempt to make this a hard run. The building and army choices sound like they could take focus, and combining that with a fast pace seems like a good way to spend your workout concentrating on the wrong thing. Give it a try at an easy pace first, and see whether the controls feel comfortable before you commit to a longer session. There’s no reason to turn a free game into an obstacle course for your wrists.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older computer, and you don’t need a recent gaming machine just to get started. I’d look at the graphics card first, since the listed minimum is an old dedicated card rather than integrated graphics, but the overall bar is modest.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Save BattleRush for a low effort bike session if you’re interested in multiplayer action and the idea of changing the battlefield sounds appealing. I’d skip it for a treadmill workout unless you’ve confirmed the controls work for you and you’re comfortable keeping your pace easy.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
