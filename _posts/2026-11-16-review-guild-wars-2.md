I’d take Guild Wars 2 out into the open world for an easy treadmill session, because the events and roaming enemies give you something to do without needing every minute to be a boss fight. I’d leave competitive PvP for when I’m sitting down, since a bouncing thumb and a teammate depending on you seem like a poor combination.

## Gameplay

The open world is the part I’d point you toward first. You can wander into events as they happen, help with whatever is going on, then carry on exploring, and that loose structure suits exercise better than a game that keeps stopping to explain why you’re allowed to move again. Some events are busier than others, but there’s usually something nearby to pull your attention back in.

Combat has more going on than standing still and tapping one button, which I like, though it also means I wouldn’t treat it as background noise. You have weapon skills, profession choices, and dodges to think about, and enemy attacks can ask you to react rather than just keep swinging. I think that gives a walk some nice focus, but on a harder run I’d probably be pressing buttons with the confidence of someone trying to work a remote in the dark.

There’s a lot to learn across the professions, and changing weapons can change what your skills do, so character customization feels like it can keep you busy for a while. That’s cool if you enjoy trying things and figuring out what fits you. It’s less appealing if you want to start playing without looking at menus, because the game gives you plenty of choices before you’ve had a chance to decide whether you like the first ones.

The story and the personal story missions bring more talking and stopping than the open world does. I wouldn’t call that a deal breaker, but I’d save those missions for a slower workout or a time when you’re happy to pause and pay attention. I don’t know, I have a hard time listening carefully while also trying to remember which button is my dodge.

Gathering and exploring can make the quieter stretches feel worthwhile, and the large maps give you room to set a modest goal instead of chasing a whole quest chain. I’d pick one area and follow whatever catches my eye, rather than trying to clear every marker like I’m finally being graded on errands. That approach feels like it fits the game better anyway.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d start with open world events, not structured PvP or World versus World. Those modes can involve other players and quick decisions, and I don’t want to be the person who misses a dodge because my feet have started setting the pace. The open world lets you take a breath between fights, which is a much nicer arrangement for moving and playing at the same time.

Guild Wars 2 doesn’t have native controller support, so you shouldn’t expect to connect a controller and have everything ready to go. You can set up a controller mapping through Steam Input, but that means deciding how to handle the many skills, menus, and interactions, and I’d want to test that while seated before trusting it on a treadmill. There are a lot of buttons. It’s not impossible, but it’s a little project.

If you’re using a keyboard and mouse, a bike is probably the easier fit, since your hands can stay more steady while you steer and choose skills. On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace easy and stick to exploring or simpler events, where a delayed input doesn’t immediately turn into a scramble. I’d avoid trying to handle complicated menus while moving, because I already take long enough to find things when I’m sitting still.

I’d also be comfortable skipping a fight and moving on instead of treating every enemy as a personal challenge. Some combat needs more attention than an easy workout can give it, and there’s no reason to turn a relaxed session into a thumb workout with a treadmill attached. I don’t know how enjoyable the controller setup would feel for everyone, but I’d only count on it after setting it up myself.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum calls for an older dedicated graphics card, so I’d check your graphics hardware before assuming a basic laptop will run it well. You don’t need a recent high end gaming computer by these requirements, but this isn’t aiming at integrated graphics either. The 70 GB of storage is also worth checking before you start installing.

## Conclusion and Score

Guild Wars 2 makes sense for you if you want a large world to explore at a relaxed pace and don’t mind sorting out your own controller mapping. I’d skip it for a workout if you need plug and play controls or want to focus on competitive fights while moving.
