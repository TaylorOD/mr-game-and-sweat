A head to head duel sounds like a nice way to keep your brain busy while you exercise, but Paunch asks you to focus on another person who is trying to hit you, and that is a lot to manage while your body is bouncing around. I’d save it for a stationary bike at an easy pace, not a treadmill run.

## Gameplay

Paunch’s one on one online duels are a simple idea, and I think that simplicity is part of the appeal. You don’t have a story to read through or a lot of separate systems to learn before you can fight someone, so the game gets straight to the part where you’re trying to outplay another person. That’s a nice change when you’ve got limited attention to spare.

The flip side is that the whole experience rests on those duels staying fun. There isn’t much room in this format for a quiet stretch where you can catch your breath and pay half attention, because your opponent is the activity. I’d expect a good match to pull me in, and a one sided one to feel like I’m just waiting for it to be over. That’s a pretty narrow range.

I like that a duel gives you a clear goal without making you keep track of a bunch of story details, and I can see the appeal of trying to read another person rather than repeat the same fight against a computer. But I don’t know how much variety the game can get out of that one on one setup. It might be enough for someone who likes learning an opponent’s habits, but I’d probably want more than the same kind of confrontation if I were settling in for a longer session.

There’s also the online part, which makes the experience depend on having another person available and willing to fight. That can be exciting, because a human opponent is harder to predict than a routine encounter, but it also means you can’t really decide to pause the duel whenever your workout needs a moment. I’d rather have that tension on the couch.

The presentation and premise have a very direct, no fuss quality that I enjoy, even if it doesn’t make me think Paunch is going to keep every player entertained for long. It’s the kind of game where the basic idea is clear before you’ve spent much time with it, and from there it has to earn your attention with the match itself. Some people will love that. I’d want more to do.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The thing I’d worry about first is not the exercise, it’s the other person. In a one on one fight, you’re reacting to somebody who can change what they’re doing at any moment, and I’d expect myself to lean toward the screen and care more about winning than keeping a steady pace. That’s not really the workout I’m trying to get.

On a treadmill, I’d skip it. Even if you keep the pace low, a duel is still a bad place to be making quick adjustments with your hands while your feet are moving underneath you. The game asks for attention at exactly the time I’d want my workout to be boring and predictable. I don’t know whether I’d make it through a match without slowing down to concentrate, and then I’ve basically turned the treadmill into a chair with a moving belt.

A stationary bike is less awkward, but I’d only try it at a gentle pace where I can sit steadily and keep my hands on the controls without wobbling around. I’d also be ready to stop pedaling during a close exchange, which is not a great plan if the point is to keep exercising. It’s manageable in theory. In practice, I’d probably get invested in the fight.

The requirements say keyboard and mouse are required, with a controller optional, so I wouldn’t assume you can comfortably play with only a controller. That matters here because reaching for a mouse or keyboard while exercising is more fiddly than it sounds, and Paunch doesn’t seem like the kind of game where I’d want to take my time lining things up. If you’re going to try it, set up the controls before you start moving and keep the effort easy.

I’d skip this for a serious run or a hard cycling session. If you’re already comfortable on a stationary bike and you’re willing to treat the game as the main event for a while, give it a try, but don’t expect it to be a relaxed background game. It wants your attention.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

Paunch asks for a recent, fairly strong graphics card, so this isn’t one I’d expect to run well on an older laptop or integrated graphics. I’d check your GPU first, since the minimum calls for an RTX 2060 or RX 6700 XT class card, and that’s a bigger ask than the small download size suggests. The install itself is only 3 GB, which is the easiest part.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Paunch makes the most sense for you if you want a direct online duel and have a PC that meets the requirements. For exercising, I’d keep it to an easy stationary bike session, and I’d pass on playing it while running.
