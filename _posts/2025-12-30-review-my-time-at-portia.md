Portia’s workshop gives you something useful to fuss over while you’re moving, so I’d try it on an easy treadmill pace before asking it to carry a harder workout. There’s always another commission or little workshop improvement waiting, though a surprising amount of that means standing still to read a menu.

## Gameplay

The workshop is the part that would keep me coming back, because making something for a commission gives all the gathering and crafting a reason to happen. You’re not just collecting rocks because a game has rocks. You’re trying to get the neglected workshop into better shape, and I like having a small practical goal to work toward while the town carries on around you.

The trouble is that “make this thing” can turn into a lot of checking what you have, checking what you need, and then going out to find the missing piece. I think that can be satisfying in the low pressure way of getting one chore done before starting another, but it does mean the game regularly asks you to stop moving and think. I’d probably enjoy that on a walk or an easy ride more than during a run where I’m trying to hold a steady pace.

Growing crops and raising animals give the workshop a quieter side, and I like that Portia isn’t only asking you to make equipment and run around collecting materials. You can spend time making the place feel more like yours, and the residents give those errands a little more personality than a list of jobs would have on its own. I don’t know that every conversation is going to be something I want to read while exercising, but it’s nice to have the option to slow down and get to know the town.

The abandoned ruins bring a different kind of attention, since exploring and fighting don’t have the same relaxed pace as working in the workshop. I’d be more careful there, especially if I were already tired and my hands weren’t staying exactly where I wanted them. The game has more than one speed, which is useful, but it also means you can accidentally go from calmly making things to trying to react to an enemy while your feet are doing their own job.

I’d expect the best stretches to be the ones where I’ve got a commission in mind and can gather what I need without worrying about every conversation or every scrap of material. The slower stretches aren’t necessarily bad, but there are enough menus and small decisions that I wouldn’t call this a game that keeps you busy every second. Sometimes the exciting plan is checking the workshop.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Portia seems like a game where you can set your own pace, and that matters more to me than having constant action. Full controller support is a good start, and most of the workshop work sounds like it gives you room to make small mistakes without immediately turning the whole session into a disaster. I’d try it while walking or biking first, then see how much attention I had left for the more involved bits.

On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace modest when I was looking through menus or reading what a resident needs, because even a calm game can make you stare down at the controller longer than you meant to. You can keep a commission in mind and head out to collect materials, but I wouldn’t treat every trip across town as a reason to speed up. The workshop will still be there when you’re done jogging. It’s patient.

Combat and exploring ruins are where I’d ease off. You may need to move and react more deliberately there, and I don’t think trying to keep a running rhythm while watching an enemy is the nicest use of either your attention or your thumbs. I’d save those sections for a slower pace, and if the treadmill makes aiming movement feel awkward, I’d go back to town and do some workshop jobs instead.

On a bike, I’d expect the slower crafting and gathering to fit comfortably, but I’d still watch for stretches where I’m reading instead of pedaling with any regularity. The controller helps, and the game isn’t built around quick online matches or constant twitch reactions, but it isn’t an automatic exercise game either. I’d choose a comfortable pace and let the town set the mood a little.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This should run on an older gaming computer, but I wouldn’t assume that any basic office machine will handle it well, since the minimum calls for a dedicated graphics card around the GTX 660 level. I’d check the graphics card first, then make sure the computer has enough memory to meet the requirement. It’s not asking for a brand new machine.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Portia looks like a pleasant fit for an easy workout if you enjoy crafting toward small goals and don’t mind pausing to read or plan. I’d skip it for a hard run, but I’d give it a try on a walk or a steady bike ride.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
