Put Mini Motorways on the screen for an easy run, because laying roads and watching traffic pile up gives you something to think about without asking you to react every second. I’d use a controller and let the little city get messy for a while.

## Gameplay

I think Mini Motorways is a great fit for exercise because the decisions are small at first, and then the city grows until you’re looking at a road you made ten minutes ago and wondering why you trusted it. You connect homes to destinations, adjust the layout as more cars show up, and try to keep traffic moving. It’s a simple idea that keeps giving you something to fix. Which is more than I can say for some of my actual plans.

The fun is that you’re never really done designing the map. A road that worked when there were a few cars can turn into a long queue once the city grows, so you have to decide whether to move roads around or spend a limited upgrade on a motorway or another tool. I like that there isn’t one clever layout that solves everything. You’re making a series of compromises, and sometimes the sensible choice is to stop improving one part of town and go deal with the new mess.

There’s a steady shift from calm little planning exercise to staring at a cluster of roads and trying to understand how you got there. I think that’s exciting, but it can also pull your attention away from the workout, especially once several routes need help at once. You can pause and take a moment to rethink things, which makes the pressure feel manageable instead of frantic. The cars are patient. The city is not particularly organized.

I’d play for a short stretch of exercise and see how much attention the current map is taking, rather than expecting to zone out for a whole session. When the layout is working, you can watch the traffic and make one small adjustment now and then. When the city starts growing in every direction, you’re back to planning, and I don’t know that there’s a way to avoid that without letting the roads become a bowl of noodles.

The game’s simple look helps. You can read the roads and destinations at a glance, so you’re not squinting at tiny details while moving, and the gradual pressure makes it easy to stay interested without needing a story or a bunch of cut scenes. I’d tell you to give it a try if you like making a plan and then revising it when reality gets involved. That part is familiar.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

This should work on both a treadmill and a bike, and the full controller support takes away the most annoying question before you start. You’re placing and changing roads, not aiming at something that’s trying to run away from you, so a little wobble in your hands shouldn’t ruin a whole city. I’d still keep the controller somewhere comfortable, because reaching across your body to place a road sounds like a small thing until you’re doing it repeatedly.

I’d start at a comfortable pace and use pause whenever I wanted to redraw a busy area or figure out where a motorway would help. That lets you separate the planning from the watching, rather than trying to make a careful decision while your legs and the traffic are both doing their thing. I’d probably avoid speeding the game up while I’m still getting used to exercising with it. The cars can get busy without any help from me.

On a bike, I think the little bursts of decision making would fit pretty naturally, since you can keep pedaling while you look over the map. On a treadmill, I’d be a bit more careful during the late stages, when there are more routes to follow and a quick redesign can turn into several decisions in a row. You can always pause, though, and the game isn’t going to punish you for taking a breath. I appreciate that.

I don’t know how much I’d enjoy trying to make a perfect city while exercising, because I can get too invested in getting a layout right and forget what I’m supposed to be doing. But you don’t need to play perfectly for this to work, and the lack of twitchy action makes it much easier to keep your feet moving. If you want something that asks for occasional focus rather than constant reflexes, this makes sense.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimum requirements are modest, and the game should be comfortable on an older computer or one using integrated graphics. I’d check that your computer can run Windows 7 SP1 or newer and has the required DirectX support, but you don’t need a powerful gaming machine for a road map.

## Conclusion and Score

Mini Motorways is an easy recommendation for you if you like planning, tinkering, and watching a small problem gradually become a larger one. I’d skip it if you want a workout game that lets you switch your brain off completely, but otherwise it’s a very practical way to keep your legs moving while your imaginary city gets complicated.
