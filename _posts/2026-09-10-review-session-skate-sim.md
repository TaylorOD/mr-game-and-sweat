I’d pick a mellow route through Session: Skate Sim on an easy workout, because the fun is in setting up a line and trying to land it, not in keeping your hands perfectly still. On a run where I’m already bouncing around, I’d probably spend a lot of time watching my skater fall down.

## Gameplay

The dual stick controls are a genuinely interesting idea, and I think they make landing a trick feel like something you did rather than something you selected from a menu. Each stick controls a foot, so there’s a lot to learn before the board does what you pictured, and that awkward early stretch could either be the appeal or the reason you put it down. I like that it asks you to learn the movement instead of handing you a button that says “do skateboarding.”

A lot of the game is about finding a spot, trying a line, and adjusting when your trick doesn’t come together. That gives you a nice little goal to focus on without a long story stopping you every few minutes, but I think the repeated attempts could get slow if you’re not in the mood to concentrate. It’s a bit like practicing the same turn in a parking lot, except the parking lot has stairs.

The real life skate spots and the ability to customize your skater and gear give you plenty to poke around in, and that part seems especially good if you like skateboarding as a whole culture and not just a way to get to the next level. Missions give you some direction when free skating starts to feel like wandering around looking for a curb you like. I’d start with whatever tasks teach the controls, then switch to free skate once I wanted less structure.

The realism is also where I’d expect to have a mixed time. A trick that looks simple can take actual practice with the sticks, which makes pulling it off feel earned, but it also means this probably isn’t the game I’d choose when I want to turn my brain off after work. I don’t know if I’d want every trick to feel like a small coordination project, but I respect that the game is committed to it.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is a good start, and Session doesn’t sound like a game where you need to aim at something moving across the screen every half second. Still, its controls ask for precise stick movement and timing, and that’s the part that makes me hesitate about playing while running. Your feet are already doing their own thing, and the game would like your thumbs to do something very specific too.

I’d use free skate and take the pressure off trying to land a complicated line. Pick a spot with room to experiment, try a few simple tricks, and treat falling as part of the activity instead of something you need to correct immediately. That sounds more workable on a bike, where your upper body can stay steadier, than on a treadmill where every step adds a little wobble.

Missions could be a nice way to give a workout some shape, but I’d save the ones that demand a particular trick or a clean sequence for when I’m sitting down. I can picture trying to repeat one exact move, getting close, correcting the sticks, and then realizing I’m leaning toward the screen like the controller has a better view. It probably does not.

The slower pace helps, though. You can take your time setting up a line, and there isn’t a competing player forcing you to react right now, so you’re not dealing with the kind of pressure that makes a treadmill session feel like a bad decision. I’d keep the workout easy, use a controller, and stop chasing the trick once it starts taking more focus than the exercise. That’s enough of a plan.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimum graphics card is old, so this doesn’t look like a game that demands a recent high end GPU, though the minimum processor is more serious than I’d expect from the visuals. I’d pay attention to frame rate first, since the store page says a higher frame rate makes for a better experience, and lower graphics settings are the suggested fix if performance is low.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Session: Skate Sim seems like a good workout companion if you want a focused, patient skateboarding game and you’re happy practicing the controls instead of rushing through a run. I’d give it a try on a bike or an easy treadmill session, but I’d save the more demanding tricks for the couch.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
