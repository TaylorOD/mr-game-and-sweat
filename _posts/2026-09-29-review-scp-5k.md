Aiming down a dark corridor while your legs are moving sounds like a bad trade, and SCP: 5K is exactly the kind of shooter I’d want to play at a pace where I can stop and line things up. I’d try it on an easy treadmill walk or a steady bike ride, not during a hard run.

## Gameplay

The tactical horror part works for me better than the conspiracy setup by itself, because the slow, careful movement gives the shooting some weight instead of making every encounter feel like a hallway full of targets. You’re fighting against the SCP Foundation after it turns on humanity, and I like that the premise gives the danger a human side as well as the horror side. I’d be more interested in a tense stretch where I’m trying to make a safe decision than in a long explanation of why the organization did it.

There’s a deliberate pace here, and that’s both what I want from it and what could make me put it down for a while. You’re not just sprinting from one fight to the next, so I’d expect stretches where you’re checking the space ahead and deciding how careful to be. That helps the tension, but if you’re looking for constant action to keep your brain busy while exercising, some of that quieter movement may feel like the game is waiting for you to remember where you put your attention.

I think the singleplayer option is useful for this kind of game, because I’d rather take my time than feel like I’m slowing down a group while I figure out what’s happening. Co-op could make the tactical side more fun if you’ve got someone who enjoys moving carefully too, although I’d probably be a less useful teammate while bouncing along on a treadmill. I’m not sure I’d want to make someone wait while I adjust my pace and try to aim at the same time.

The Early Access label matters here. The project is still working toward a full release, so I’d go in expecting an unfinished game rather than a complete horror campaign with every rough edge already sanded down. That’s reasonable for a project in progress, but it does make it harder to recommend as the one game you buy for a whole exercise routine. I’d give it a try if the tactical horror idea is what you want, and keep expectations modest.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The biggest adjustment I’d make is lowering the speed, because careful aiming and a moving body are not a combination I’d trust at a workout pace. On a treadmill, I’d keep it to a walk and expect to slow down or stop when a situation needs more precision. You can do plenty of walking while the game asks you to move cautiously, but I wouldn’t treat every encounter like it’s background entertainment.

A bike sounds a little easier to manage, mostly because your hands can stay steadier while you pedal. I’d still choose a relaxed pace, since the horror and tactical shooting are going to pull your focus toward the screen, and aiming while your legs are doing a hard effort is not where I’d want to test my coordination. I have enough confidence in my thumbs to know when they’re about to make a small problem larger.

I’d set up the controls before getting on the equipment and make sure aiming, movement and reloading feel comfortable. If controller play is available in your setup, that’s what I’d use, but a controller doesn’t make this a simple button pressing game. The shooting still asks you to be deliberate, and a loose thumbstick adjustment can matter more here than it would in a game where you’re mostly running around.

There’s no reason to turn the workout into a challenge mode. I’d skip trying to keep a fast pace through the tense sections, and I’d be comfortable pausing or stepping off when I need to sort out what’s happening. That might sound less efficient than playing straight through, but I think a game that makes you pay attention should get the attention. You can always start pedaling again.

## Computer Requirements

You’ll need a dedicated graphics card, and integrated graphics are specifically not expected to perform well, so I’d check the graphics card before anything else. The requirements are subject to change during Early Access, which is one more reason I wouldn’t assume a computer that just barely runs it will stay comfortable.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

SCP: 5K makes sense for someone who wants tactical horror and can keep the workout easy while they aim carefully. I’d pass if you need nonstop action or want to play hard enough that the game can fade into the background.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
