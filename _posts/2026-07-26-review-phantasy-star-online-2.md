For a workout, I’d take Phantasy Star Online 2 out into Halpha and spend my time fighting, not trying to follow every bit of the MMO around it. The action can keep you busy, but the menus and online errands are going to ask for more attention than your legs do.

## Gameplay

The combat is the reason I’d keep this installed. You’re running around open areas, attacking enemies with your weapons and abilities, and moving between groups of creatures instead of standing in one place waiting for a turn. That gives your brain something useful to do while you exercise, and the basic rhythm of getting to the next fight makes sense pretty quickly.

PSE bursts are a good example of the game working in your favor here. You’re fighting in a combat sector, enemies keep showing up, and the whole thing can turn into a stretch where you’re focused on the action instead of checking how much longer you have left on the treadmill. I think that’s a better workout fit than stopping every few minutes to read story dialogue. There are still plenty of MMO bits around it.

The world also has urgent quests and large boss fights, which sound like the exciting part until you remember that other players are involved and the boss isn’t going to wait while you sort out your pace. I’d be happy to jump into those when I’m feeling alert, but I wouldn’t make them the plan for a tired day. I don’t know that every big fight will feel equally comfortable while moving, especially when you’re dodging and trying to keep the enemy in view.

Character creation is a lot of fun if you enjoy spending time making your character look exactly right, and the creative spaces give you another reason to hang around when you’re not fighting. I like that there’s more to do than repeat combat forever, but I’d keep that stuff for when the belt is stopped. There are only so many ways I can adjust a character while trying not to drift toward the edge.

There’s a fair amount to learn across weapons, abilities, quests, and the various menus, and I’d expect to spend some workout time doing less fighting than I hoped. That isn’t a dealbreaker for an MMO, but it does make this less immediately pick up and play than an action game that just puts enemies in front of you. I’d start with the combat and leave the sorting out for later.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The regular fights should be manageable if you keep the pace comfortable, because you can move around and attack without needing to land every input perfectly. I’d use an XInput controller, since that’s what the listed requirements support, and I’d avoid turning a workout into a serious boss attempt before I knew how the camera and dodging felt. A small adjustment to camera sensitivity may help, if the game’s settings suit you, but I wouldn’t spend half the workout tuning it.

On a treadmill, the quick turns and dodges are the part I’d watch. You can be moving forward in the game and suddenly need to react to an attack or a group coming at you from another direction, and that’s when a controller starts feeling a little less precise than it does on the couch. I’d keep the speed easy enough that you can look at the screen, and I’d skip the fights that need your full attention if you’re already working hard.

A bike gives you a little more room to focus on aiming and keeping track of enemies, which is useful in boss fights and urgent quests, but it still doesn’t solve the menus or the online nature of the game. I wouldn’t count on pausing in the middle of an activity to catch your breath or read something. Some parts are just going to be better when you’re not exercising.

The open world is where I’d do most of my workout play, since you can move between fights at your own pace and you’re not forced to keep up with a tight story sequence. Character setup and creative spaces are easy skips while you’re moving. You can get back to them after.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum graphics card is old enough that this doesn’t look like a game demanding a recent high end PC, though the 103 GB of storage is a bigger ask than I’d expect from an older card. I’d check that you have room for it and that your computer can meet the listed requirements, and keep in mind that the store page calls for Windows 10 even though Microsoft no longer supports it.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Phantasy Star Online 2 makes sense for you if you want an action MMO to chip away at during easier workouts, especially if the idea of roaming around Halpha and joining other players sounds fun. I’d skip it if you want a game that’s instantly simple to control or easy to pause whenever your workout gets demanding.
