I’d start with the EMS role on an easy ride, because the open world gives you somewhere to go without turning every minute into a sprint. Police work and fires sound more exciting, but they also seem like the easiest way to start steering with your whole upper body.

## Gameplay

Flashing Lights sounds better to me as a relaxed emergency services sandbox than as a game you need to play perfectly. You can choose police, firefighting, or EMS, and that gives the same town a few different reasons to keep moving through it. I like the idea of deciding what kind of shift I’m in the mood for, though I’d probably want the game to get me into a call fairly quickly instead of spending a workout figuring out what I’m meant to do.

Police work has the clearest built in tension, because even a routine call can turn into something where you’re watching traffic and trying to react. That could pull my attention away from the treadmill in a useful way, but I’d be less interested in a pursuit that asks me to make careful turns while my hands are bouncing around. I can see that being fun from a chair. I’d rather not learn how good my steering is while also learning how close the wall is.

Firefighting and EMS seem like the more appealing choices when I want a slower session. Responding to a fire or a medical call gives you a reason to drive across the map, and the work at the scene makes the game feel like more than driving around waiting for something to happen. I don’t know how much variety each role keeps up over a longer stretch, so I’d go in expecting a simulator that gets a lot of its mileage from the situation you make for yourself. That can be plenty.

The up to 10 player co-op is probably where the game has the most room to turn into a good evening, since you can split up across the services and make a little role play out of it. It also means the fun depends on the people you’re playing with, which is a generous way of saying your friend who insists every call needs a dramatic backstory may become part of the evening. Player made mods give it another way to stay fresh, and I like that the game leaves room for people to make their own fun instead of pretending the same shift will stay exciting forever.

Solo play sounds more uneven to me. You have the freedom to choose a service and explore, but a quiet stretch in an open world can feel long when there isn’t a call pulling you along. I’d try the roles that interest me, and if the game starts feeling like I’m driving between things I don’t care about, I’d switch services rather than force myself to finish a shift. There’s no need to make exercise feel like a second job.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The driving is the part I’d plan around first, because an emergency vehicle asks for more attention than a slow sightseeing drive, especially when you’re trying to arrive quickly. A bike seems like the easier place to handle that, since I’d be less likely to bob around while steering, but I could see a treadmill working fine during calmer calls and travel between them. I’d keep the pace comfortable. Nobody needs me trying to outrun a fire truck with my legs.

A controller is the setup I’d reach for, and I’d check that the controls feel comfortable before starting a workout. Moving around the open world should be manageable with loose inputs, but police situations and urgent responses could ask for more focus, and I wouldn’t want to be figuring out a fiddly control while trying to keep my footing. I’d save anything that feels like a chase for a slower pace, or just skip it while exercising.

The nice thing is that the game doesn’t sound like it needs you to be in a constant fight. There are calmer stretches where you can travel to a call or play a more relaxed shift, and that suits a workout better than a game that punishes you for looking away for one second. I’d probably pick EMS or firefighting first, then move into police work when I wanted to pay closer attention. The workout can set the mood.

I don’t know that I’d choose multiplayer for a moving session unless everyone was happy to keep things casual, because coordinating with other people can pull you into the game more than you planned. For solo play, I’d keep the pace easy and treat the urgent moments as a reason to slow down, not as a personal challenge to drive perfectly while jogging. That seems like the sensible arrangement.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum asks for an older dedicated graphics card, so this doesn’t look like a game that needs a new high end PC just to get going. I’d check that your computer has a graphics card around that level first, since the minimum is more demanding than basic integrated graphics.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is a better fit for a relaxed ride than a hard run, and I’d give it a try if you like emergency services role play or have people to play co-op with. If you want constant action that keeps your hands busy every second, I’d pick something else.
