Save the treadmill for a slower day, because Squad 44 sounds like the kind of match where your attention belongs on the battlefield, not on keeping your feet in the right place. I’d be more comfortable trying it on a stationary bike, and even then I’d keep the effort easy.

## Gameplay

I like the idea of a tactical World War II shooter where the scale is big enough that you’re working with a squad instead of treating every fight like a quick arcade match. The immense battlefields and realistic weapons sound like they could make each small decision matter, which is much more interesting to me than running around collecting points for doing the same thing over and over. The trade is that realism can mean a lot of time getting into position before anything happens. I’d need to be in the mood for that.

The multiplayer focus also makes it a harder game to pick up casually than a shooter with a short solo mission. You’re joining a frontline with other people, and the store page strongly advises using a microphone, so I’d expect communication to be part of the experience rather than something you can ignore and still get the same result. That can make a match feel coordinated and tense, which is cool. It can also turn a workout into me trying to understand what someone said while a battlefield is happening.

I think the vehicles are appealing in theory, because they give the huge maps a different scale and make the battles feel like more than infantry moving between cover. I’d want to learn the basics before I tried to take responsibility for anything complicated, though. I can picture myself spending the whole time worrying that I’m doing the wrong job, which is not exactly a relaxing way to get my steps in.

There’s a lot here for someone who enjoys tactical multiplayer and is willing to learn how its fights work, and I wouldn’t mistake the slower pace for a lack of action. The action just sounds like it asks you to pay attention in a particular way. I don’t know that I’d want that every time I exercise, but I can see the appeal of a match where the whole team has to pull in the same direction.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The battlefield is the reason I’d be careful about playing while moving. Squad 44 is a multiplayer shooter built around realism, and aiming at distance while your body is bouncing is a rough combination, even before you add quick reactions and teammates depending on you. You could end up easing off the workout every time the match gets tense, then missing the part where you were supposed to be exercising. I would.

A stationary bike sounds more manageable because you can keep your upper body steady, but I’d still start at a pace where aiming and talking don’t become a second job. The microphone recommendation matters here, since I’d want to be able to communicate without leaning around or fussing with equipment while I’m riding. I’d save harder efforts for something that doesn’t ask me to line up shots at the same time.

I also wouldn’t count on a controller just because the game might be playable with one. The store information doesn’t confirm controller support, so I’d check before buying it for a workout setup, and I wouldn’t try to figure out controls halfway through a live match. If the only comfortable setup for you is a controller, that uncertainty matters. I don’t know how much I’d enjoy sorting it out while teammates are waiting for me.

I’d skip this for running workouts where you’re pushing the pace or doing intervals, since multiplayer gives you no clean moment to stop and reset when your focus slips. A very easy bike session is the one setup I’d be willing to try, with the expectation that I might need to pause the exercise plan and concentrate on the game. That’s a slightly odd trade, but at least it’s an honest one.

## Computer Requirements

This needs a fairly capable gaming PC, not an older office computer you happen to have plugged into a monitor. I’d look at the graphics card first, since the minimum calls for a GTX 1080 or RX 6600 class card, and the game also asks for a lot of storage. The virtual battlefield takes up room.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Squad 44 makes sense for you if you want a demanding team shooter and already enjoy the slower, more careful side of multiplayer. For exercise, I’d only try it on an easy stationary bike after checking your controls, and I’d leave the treadmill out of it.
