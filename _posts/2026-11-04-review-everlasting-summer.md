Everlasting Summer is easy to keep open during a relaxed bike ride because most of the game asks you to read and make occasional choices. On a treadmill, that same reading can make a fast pace awkward, so this is better for steady exercise than intervals.

## Gameplay

Everlasting Summer is a visual novel, so its main activity is reading dialogue and choosing what the protagonist does next. Semyon is an ordinary young man who falls asleep on a bus and wakes up at a Soviet pioneer camp, surrounded by teenagers and a summer setting that makes little sense to him. The mystery of how he got there gives the story something to chew on beyond the camp’s daily routines.

Your choices shape which characters Semyon spends time with and which story route opens up. There are several characters to get to know, including Lena, Slavyа, Alisa, and Ulyana. Some choices feel small in the moment, but they can steer the story toward a different relationship and ending. That gives the game a reason to replay, especially if you want to see what changes when Semyon makes different decisions.

The pace is the big tradeoff. There are long stretches of dialogue and narration, with illustrated backgrounds and character portraits doing most of the visual work. The writing can pull you into the camp’s odd mix of everyday life and unanswered questions, but it is still a lot of reading. You are not constantly making decisions, and the game will sometimes spend a while setting up a scene before asking what you think. If you want a game that keeps your hands busy and your eyes darting around, this is not it. If you like following characters and piecing together a strange situation, the slower pace is part of the appeal.

The menus offer the usual visual novel tools, including saving and loading, and the ability to move through dialogue at your own pace. Those features make it easier to pause for a drink or pick up a route again later. Still, the core loop remains read, choose, read some more. That means the story can be engaging, but the gameplay itself does not provide much activity to distract you from a workout.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

This is forgiving game input by design. There are no combat encounters, timed button presses, or quick reactions to punish you for wobbling. Most of the time, you can keep a comfortable rhythm and only need to focus when a choice appears. A mistake is more likely to send Semyon toward a different story path than cause a sudden game over.

The catch is that you need to read. That is manageable on a stationary bike or during a slow treadmill walk, where you can glance at the screen without losing your balance. It is less convenient at a brisk running pace, especially when a choice has several options and you want to think through what each one might mean. You can pause and take your time, though the stop and start rhythm may interrupt a workout.

The game is designed around simple menu and dialogue inputs rather than precision control. If you have a controller set up for menu navigation, the slow pace should suit it well. If your setup relies on a mouse, reaching for it while exercising is less comfortable. Either way, this is a much better fit for relaxed exercise than for a workout where you want to keep your eyes forward and your hands still.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very low: a Pentium 4 at 1.5 GHz or an Athlon XP, 512 MB of RAM, DirectX 9.0c compatible graphics, and 877 MB of storage. The listed operating systems range from Windows XP through Windows 11. This is a simple visual novel, not a game asking your computer to render a huge world or a pile of effects.

A modern computer with integrated graphics should be more than enough. Even an older desktop or laptop that struggles with newer games has a good chance of running it. The small storage requirement is also friendly if you are keeping a workout computer free of large installs.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Everlasting Summer works best when you want a quiet game to accompany easy exercise. Its branching character routes and central mystery give you a reason to stay with the story, and its slow, forgiving input makes it much less demanding than an action game. The downside is exactly what the genre promises: a lot of reading, with long stretches where you mostly wait for the next choice.

Pick it for a relaxed bike session or a gentle treadmill pace, not for a workout that needs constant focus on the room around you. It is free to play, runs on very modest hardware, and has enough routes to encourage another pass through the camp. Just be ready to read more than you press buttons.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
