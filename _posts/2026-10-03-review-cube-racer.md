Building obstacles between races sounds like a much better treadmill idea than staring at a track and waiting for other people to finish, but then you have to race through the mess everyone made. I think Cube Racer could keep you busy, though the multiplayer part might make you forget you’re exercising.

## Gameplay

The building phase is the part that makes me curious. You and the other players get to put obstacles on the map before the next race, so each round has a little bit of planning and then a reason to care whether your plan worked. I like games where people make the next part harder for each other, and this sounds like the sort of idea that could turn a perfectly ordinary race into a crowded hallway. Maybe not a very polite hallway.

The round structure also sounds useful for exercise because you’re not stuck in one long race with no natural stopping point. There’s a break between rounds to build obstacles, then everybody has to deal with what’s there. I’d expect those pauses to be the slow part, and I don’t know how long they last, but at least they’re part of the game rather than a story scene you can’t get around.

I’m less sure how much control you get over what you build or how much time there is to think about it, so I wouldn’t promise that the game keeps you moving every second. If the building is quick, great, you’re back to racing. If it asks you to fuss over the map for a while, that’s a little like stopping your run to decide where to put a traffic cone, which is not why I got on the treadmill.

The racing itself sounds like the part that could carry the game. You’re competing to reach the finish while navigating obstacles that other players placed, and I think that gives each round a reason to pay attention without needing a big story attached. I don’t know how chaotic the tracks get, so I’m not going to pretend I know what a typical race feels like. The idea is fun, though.

For me, the multiplayer angle is both the appeal and the part I’d watch. Other people help make the obstacles feel less predictable, but competing with them can turn a casual race into a little personal grudge against a cube. I’d probably enjoy that more than racing the same fixed course over and over, as long as the rounds move along.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The build phase might be where you want to ease off a little, because that’s the moment when your attention shifts from racing to placing obstacles. I’d keep the treadmill at a pace where I can look at the screen and make a choice without wobbling around, then treat the race as the more active part. You’re still doing the workout, even if the game briefly asks you to be an architect.

The multiplayer race is where I’d be cautious about running hard. Losing a race because you were taking it easy is fine, but if you’re trying to beat several other players, it’s easy to lean forward and start making every turn feel urgent. I’d rather keep a comfortable pace than turn a casual game into a second job with a finish line.

A stationary bike seems a little more forgiving for this kind of play, since you can keep your upper body steady while you focus on getting through the obstacles. I’d still start with an easy effort and see how much attention the racing asks for. If you find yourself staring at the screen and forgetting to pedal, lower the effort or take a breather between rounds. That’s not a failure. It’s a cube race.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information here, so check that before you buy it for exercise. I wouldn’t want to discover that I need to reach for a keyboard while I’m already trying to stay in a race, and I can’t tell you how the controls feel from the store description alone. That uncertainty matters more here than it would in a game where you can stop and take your time.

I’d skip any attempt to make this a hard running workout until you know how demanding the races feel and whether the controller setup works for you. Easy jogging or relaxed cycling is the sensible first try, with the option to stop moving during the building phase if you need to concentrate. The game can wait for you to decide how ambitious you want to be.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very modest, so an older Windows computer with basic graphics should be enough to give it a try. I’d check that the machine meets the listed DirectX requirement first, but this doesn’t look like a game asking your computer to train for the race too.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Cube Racer sounds like a clever little multiplayer game for easy exercise, especially on a bike, but I’d confirm controller support before buying it for the treadmill. If you like making a race harder for the people you’re racing, give it a try at a comfortable pace.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
