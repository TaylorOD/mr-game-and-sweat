I’d put 觅长生 on an easy treadmill walk, not a hard run, because building a cultivation life sounds like the sort of thing that will have me reading and making choices instead of watching my feet. On a bike, I’d keep the pace comfortable and let the breakthrough plans take as long as they take.

## Gameplay

The slow growth is the point, and I think that makes 觅长生 more appealing than a game that keeps throwing emergencies at you. You begin with very little and build your cultivation over time, with breakthroughs as the big milestones, so there’s a longer goal to come back to instead of a stream of tiny tasks. I like that kind of progress when the game gives you room to decide what matters, although I can also see myself spending a suspicious amount of exercise time deciding what to do next.

The open world gives you stories, chances to meet and interact with characters, and places to explore, including secret realms. That sounds like a nice mix for a workout because you can have a longer-term plan and still get pulled toward something new, but I wouldn’t expect every stretch to feel busy. Some of it is going to be reading, weighing a choice, and then reading some more. You may be sweating, but the character is apparently taking a moment to consider the implications.

The choices about how you treat people are a part I’d be curious about. You can be kind, or you can kill someone and take their treasure, which is a pretty wide range of approaches for one cultivation career. I’d rather a game let me choose what kind of person I’m being than pretend every decision is equally noble, but I don’t know how much those choices change the larger story. I’d still be tempted to see how far the less generous path goes, mostly because I’m curious what the game lets you get away with.

I think the risk is that the steady progression could turn into a lot of waiting for the next meaningful step, especially when you’re trying to exercise and the game asks you to absorb a story or work out a plan. There’s no action focused on constant fast reactions in the description, which is promising, but that doesn’t mean every moment will keep your attention. I’d pick it up for the world and the slow climb, not because it sounds like a reliable way to make every minute fly by.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A comfortable pace matters more here than athletic ambition, because I’d expect the reading and decisions to compete with whatever your legs are doing. On a treadmill, I’d start with a walk and keep the belt slow enough that I can look at the screen without turning every choice into an emergency. Running might work during calmer stretches, but I’d lower the pace whenever a story or decision asks me to pay attention. I don’t need to discover that I can’t read and jog at the same time by making both parts worse.

The bike seems like a decent place for longer sessions of exploring and thinking, since a small change in pace doesn’t have to throw off your footing. But I wouldn’t call it a free pass to stare at the screen for ages. If you’re choosing between helping someone and taking their belongings after killing them, maybe give your legs a steady rhythm while you decide. It’s not a quick decision for me, at least.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information I have, so I’d check that before buying it specifically for a workout setup, and I’d try the controls while seated before adding a moving belt or pedals. If you’re comfortable with the way you’ll control it, the game’s slower, choice heavy shape seems more forgiving than something that needs quick aiming or perfect timing. If the controls make you pause to hunt around, though, the whole workout is going to feel like a negotiation with a small screen.

I’d skip a fast run and any setup where you have to look away from your feet to make frequent decisions. A relaxed bike ride or easy walk sounds like a better fit, especially if you’re happy for the game to have quiet stretches. I don’t know whether every section gives you a convenient stopping point, so I’d keep the effort low enough that setting the controller down is not a production.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to a computer that can run it on integrated graphics, though the listed UHD 630 setup needs dual channel memory when using the integrated graphics. I’d check that graphics setup first, because it matters more here than having a big gaming machine.

## Conclusion and Score

This sounds like a thoughtful, slower RPG for someone who wants a long cultivation climb and choices about how to treat people, not a game to carry a fast run. I’d try it on an easy walk or a steady bike ride, after confirming the controls work for your setup.
