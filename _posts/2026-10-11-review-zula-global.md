A team based FPS like Zula Global sounds busy enough to keep your mind off the treadmill, but I’d be careful about taking a live match too seriously while your feet are moving. I’d try it at an easy pace first, because aiming and keeping track of teammates are already doing a lot.

## Gameplay

The part that makes this appealing to me is that you’re working with teammates to overthrow the other side, rather than just wandering around waiting for something to happen. A match built around fighting the enemy should give you plenty to pay attention to, and that’s useful when the treadmill starts making the minutes feel longer. I like the basic idea. There isn’t much detail in the store description about how the different modes play, though, so I wouldn’t promise you every match has the same rhythm.

The real world maps and weapons sound like a sensible direction for an FPS, because you can get a feel for the setting without having to learn a whole pile of invented lore first. I don’t need a shooter to make me read a history of its imaginary universe before I can aim at somebody. The description doesn’t name particular maps or weapons, so I can’t point you toward a specific favorite or say which ones work best for exercise.

Weekly content and daily gifts give you reasons to check back in, and that’s nice if you want a game that keeps changing around the edges. I’m a little less convinced by daily rewards as a reason to make time for a game, because a gift can start to feel like another thing you’re supposed to collect. I don’t know how much the weekly additions change the actual play, so I’d treat them as a bonus rather than the reason to choose it.

Free to play makes it easy to give Zula a try without paying up front, which is a real point in its favor. It also means I’d pay attention to whether I’m enjoying the matches themselves, not just the feeling that I’ve unlocked a little something for showing up. I can’t tell from the store description how the free to play setup handles purchases, and I wouldn’t want to guess. The basic pitch is clear enough.

I’d call the gameplay a reasonable fit for keeping you occupied, with the caveat that team play can make each fight feel more serious than a casual treadmill distraction needs to be. There’s no story or long cut scene in the description to pull you away from the action, and I think that helps. Still, if what you want is a shooter where you can drift in and out of attention, I’d look elsewhere. Your teammates are part of the experience, not decoration.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The constant aiming is what makes this awkward while you’re moving. I’d expect quick adjustments and tracking enemies to be much harder when your hands are bouncing, and a live team match doesn’t give you a tidy moment to stop and sort yourself out. That could be a good way to forget the treadmill, but maybe not the way you meant to.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information here, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming you can pick up a controller and play comfortably. If you do try it, check the controls before you start exercising and test them while standing still. I’d rather find out that aiming feels wrong beside the machine than while trying to keep my balance and help a teammate.

On a bike, your upper body may be steadier, so I’d give that a try before attempting a faster treadmill pace. Even then, this is a game where looking away at the wrong moment could matter, and I wouldn’t count on being able to coast through a fight. It’s less like a relaxed ride with a game on and more like trying to hold a conversation while someone keeps asking you to point at things quickly.

For exercise, I’d skip the competitive matches unless you’re comfortable accepting that your performance might be worse than usual. I’d keep the pace easy, start with a short test, and quit the match before you increase the intensity. I don’t know whether the game offers modes that are forgiving enough for a moving player, because the store description doesn’t say. So I’d treat it as a game to test carefully, not one I’d set up as my default workout.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest, and the listed Intel integrated graphics should put this within reach of older or basic computers. I’d check the graphics first if you’re on an older machine, but the requirements don’t make it sound like you need a gaming tower just to get started.

## Conclusion and Score

Zula Global seems worth trying if you want a free team shooter and can play while sitting still, but I wouldn’t choose it as a treadmill game unless you’re happy to keep the workout gentle and accept some missed shots. For biking, it may be a little more manageable, though I’d test the controls before counting on that.
