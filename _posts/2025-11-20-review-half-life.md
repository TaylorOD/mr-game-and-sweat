Half-Life makes a good workout game because its campaign keeps you moving through Black Mesa, but its gunfights often ask for quick, accurate aiming. A bike is the easier match for those hectic moments, while a treadmill works best when you can keep your pace steady and your hands under control.

## Gameplay

You play Gordon Freeman, a scientist whose day at Black Mesa goes very badly after a resonance cascade opens the door to an alien invasion. The story unfolds as you move through the facility, with scientists and security guards offering bits of context along the way. There are few traditional cut scenes, so you spend most of your time moving, fighting, and figuring out how to reach the next area.

That steady mix is a big reason Half-Life still works. You will blast headcrabs off scientists, pick your way past hazards, and deal with soldiers sent in to clean up the mess. The weapons have different uses, from the trusty crowbar to explosives and the shotgun, and you need to pay attention to what is coming at you. Some enemies are better handled with a quick shot, while other encounters reward finding cover or using the right weapon.

The game also regularly changes what it asks you to do. One stretch may have you searching for a way through a lab, and the next may drop you into a tense fight or a hazardous platforming section. The opening tram ride is a slow introduction, and there are quieter stretches where you explore and work out a puzzle instead of shooting. Those breaks help the pacing rather than turning the campaign into a series of endless firefights. The occasional puzzle can still make you stop and look around, which is not ideal if you want a constant stream of action, but it rarely feels like you are stuck watching a long movie.

Half-Life is good at making its world feel like a place that is falling apart around you. Alarms, explosions, panicked staff, and enemies bursting into the room give you things to react to without dragging you away from control. It keeps your attention, and the campaign has enough changes of pace to stay interesting. The multiplayer mode adds another option for fighting other players, though quick competitive matches are a different proposition from the more forgiving single player campaign.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The store page lists full controller support, which is a real help if you want to play without keeping one hand on a mouse. Still, Half-Life is a first person shooter, and its best action asks you to aim at moving enemies, turn quickly, and react when something appears at close range. A controller makes the game playable while exercising, but it does not make those moments effortless.

The campaign is a better fit than multiplayer for a workout. In single player, you can pause when you need to adjust your pace or deal with a tricky section. Fights against headcrabs and soldiers can get hectic, and platforming or narrow ledges call for more care than simply walking forward and firing. You may want to ease off the treadmill during those sections, especially when the game is asking for precise jumps or fast aiming.

A stationary bike gives you a steadier place to handle the controller and focus on the screen. On a treadmill, the same aiming and movement demands can be awkward if your stride makes your hands bounce. The campaign has enough exploration and calmer passages to make the pairing workable, but this is not a game where you can count on every encounter being relaxed. Multiplayer is a poor choice for exercise if a mistake means losing a competitive fight and you cannot pause.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimum requirements are a 500 MHz processor, 96 MB of RAM, and a 16 MB video card, along with Windows XP, a mouse, a keyboard, and an internet connection. The recommended requirements are still very modest by modern standards, at an 800 MHz processor, 128 MB of RAM, and a 32 MB or better video card.

In plain terms, Half-Life asks very little from a computer compared with modern games. The minimum video card requirement is tiny, and you do not need a recent gaming machine to meet the listed specs. The store page lists Windows XP, so the requirements are old, but the hardware demand itself is about as light as it gets.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Half-Life is a strong choice if you want a workout game with a proper campaign rather than a loop of quick matches. Its varied fights, environmental puzzles, and steady story give you plenty to focus on, and controller support makes it a practical option for playing while exercising. The catch is that aiming and sudden danger still need your attention, so do not expect to keep the same pace through every firefight.

Pick the campaign for a good mix of action and calmer sections. A bike is the more comfortable setup, but a treadmill can work if you are ready to slow down when Black Mesa gets busy.

### Overall Score: 2.67 out of 3
