I think PICO PARK could work for an easy walk, but I wouldn’t start it at a running pace, because coordinating a group through a puzzle is already enough work without also trying to keep your thumbs steady. It’s a game where everyone needs to agree on what to do, which is a pretty good way to find out who in your group thinks they’re in charge.

## Gameplay

The cooperative puzzle setup is a genuinely good fit for playing with other people, and I like that the levels make you solve things together instead of just putting a bunch of players in the same room to do separate jobs. You might be lining up to reach a ledge, moving around to get a key, or trying to get everybody through a door, and I think that small shared goal gives you something to focus on without asking you to learn a huge pile of systems first. It gets busy in a nice way.

The trouble is that “together” can mean four people making four different suggestions while nobody moves. That kind of confusion is funny when you’re sitting on a couch, but on a treadmill it could turn into a lot of looking down at the controller and trying to follow the plan at the same time. I’d play with people who can laugh when a plan goes sideways, because I don’t know how enjoyable it would be with a group that treats every missed jump like a meeting that could have been an email.

There’s a satisfying little rhythm to these kinds of puzzles, where you see the goal, try something, and realize the group needs to rearrange itself before anything works. I think that’s much more interesting than a puzzle game that asks you to stare at one screen for ages, and PICO PARK sounds like it keeps the action moving between those moments of figuring things out. The pause to think is part of it, though.

The game supports two to eight players, so it can suit a small group or a full room, but I’d be a little cautious about putting the maximum number of people together if everyone wants to call the shots. A handful of players can talk through a plan without turning every doorway into a committee, and there’s plenty of room for everybody to help. More people might still be fun. I’d just expect some friendly confusion.

I also think the appeal depends a lot on who’s playing with you. If your group enjoys trying a solution, noticing it didn’t work, and changing the plan without getting annoyed, this could be a really fun way to spend a workout. If you want a game that keeps pulling your attention forward on its own, the puzzle sections may leave you watching the screen and waiting for the group to settle on something. I don’t know how much of that would bother you until you know your crew.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d try this on a treadmill at a walk, not a jog, and I’d keep the speed low enough that I could concentrate on where my character needs to go. Controller play makes sense here, and the movement sounds simple enough that you’re not constantly aiming at tiny targets or reacting to enemies from every direction. But timing jumps and coordinating with other players are still going to take some attention, and I wouldn’t want the belt deciding how much time I get to think.

On a stationary bike, I think the slower puzzle sections would be easier to manage because your legs can keep moving while you stop to talk over a plan. That doesn’t mean I’d automatically choose a bike, since some people are perfectly comfortable walking and using a controller, but a bike gives you a little more room to look at the screen without worrying about your next step. I’d still start easy and see how much concentration the group needs.

Online play gives you a way to play together without being in the same room, which is helpful if your potential puzzle group doesn’t all live nearby. You’ll still need some way to communicate, though, because silently watching everybody move around and hoping they guessed your plan sounds like a rough strategy. I’d use voice chat if that works for your group, and I’d stop between levels if people need a minute to explain what went wrong.

I wouldn’t pick PICO PARK for a hard run or a workout where you want to zone out. The game’s cooperative decisions are the point, and if you’re tired or the group is arguing about where to stand, you may end up paying more attention to the puzzle than your workout. That’s fine for an easy session. It just isn’t background entertainment.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest enough that an older computer with integrated graphics should be able to handle it, so I’d check that your system can run a 64 bit version of Windows before worrying about buying a graphics card. It only needs a small amount of storage, which is nice because the puzzle is going to take up more room in the group chat than on your drive.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

PICO PARK seems like a fun choice for an easy workout with patient friends, especially if you want a reason to talk and solve things together. I’d skip it for intense running or solo background play, but for a relaxed walk or bike session with a group, it sounds like a good time.
