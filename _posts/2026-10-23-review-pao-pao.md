For an easy workout, I’d bring PAO PAO along and let the seaside town set the pace, because cooking and growing crops sound a lot better beside a treadmill than another game asking me to react in half a second. I think it’s more of a gentle companion than something that’ll make you forget you’re exercising.

## Gameplay

PAO PAO is built around small, homey activities, and that sounds pleasant to me, but I’d want to know how much there is to do before I made it my regular workout game. Cooking, fishing, growing crops and making a home give you a few different reasons to spend time in the town, so you’re not just staring at one task until your legs are done. I like the idea of having a place to return to. It gives the little errands somewhere to go.

Fishing is the activity I’m least sure about for a workout. I don’t know how it works in practice, and I wouldn’t want to promise it’s a calm, easy thing if it turns out to need careful timing. If it lets you take your time, I can see it being a nice change of pace between other jobs. If it asks for a lot of concentration, I’d save that for when I’m sitting down, because jogging and trying to be a patient fisherman sounds like two chores at once.

Cooking and crops feel like a better fit for a steady session, at least from the activities described. You can make progress in small pieces, and the home by the sea gives that progress a personal reason to matter beyond filling up another bar. I tend to enjoy games more when the little tasks make some kind of place feel like mine, even if my decorating choices would probably be described as “a chair goes here.”

It’s also in Early Access, which changes how I’d think about buying it. I’d go in for the cozy town and the activities that are there now, rather than assuming there’s an endless pile of new things waiting around the corner. I don’t know what the game will look like later, and I wouldn’t buy it based on an imagined future version. The current idea is appealing on its own.

Playing with up to three friends could make the chores more fun, especially if you already like the kind of game where everyone finds a little job and gets on with it. I’d probably play solo during exercise, though, because coordinating with friends while I’m trying to keep my pace sounds like a lot of conversation for a game that seems happiest when you slow down. Some people love that company. I’m a little more useful when only one thing needs my attention.

Overall, I think the game’s relaxed activities are a real draw, but they may not give you enough momentum if you need constant action to stay focused. There’s a nice variety in the premise, and the seaside home gives it a clear mood without needing a big dramatic reason to keep moving. That sounds good to me, with the usual caveat that cozy can sometimes mean I’ve been standing beside a crop longer than I meant to.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The full controller support is a big help here, and the sort of activities PAO PAO describes sound much more forgiving than anything where a missed input ruins the whole run. I’d expect it to work best at a comfortable walking pace or on a bike, where I can look around town and decide what I feel like doing next without trying to steer with the precision of a surgeon.

On a treadmill, I’d keep the session simple and avoid turning every little task into a project. Cooking or checking on crops sounds like a reasonable place to start, while fishing is the one I’d try only once I had a feel for its demands. I don’t know whether it calls for quick reactions, so I’d rather be cautious than find myself leaning toward the screen like I’m trying to read a tiny label.

A bike might be a little easier for the activities that ask you to pay attention to a screen, since your hands and eyes can stay steadier while you pedal. But I wouldn’t rule out the treadmill at an easy pace, because the game doesn’t sound built around chasing enemies or racing a clock. If you’re going for a harder run, I’d pick a task you can do without getting too invested in every button press.

I’d start solo, use the controller, and treat the town as something to visit in short stretches rather than planning a whole workout around finishing a long list. That gives you room to stop when you’re tired, and it avoids making a calm game feel like a shift at a tiny seaside restaurant.

The minimum asks for a basic dedicated graphics card, so this isn’t aimed at a computer using only integrated graphics. I’d check the graphics card first, though the listed GTX 1050 is an older, modest option rather than a high end one.

PAO PAO sounds like a pleasant choice for easy workouts and players who enjoy making a small home feel personal. I’d give it a try if that pace appeals to you, but I’d look elsewhere if you need constant action to keep your legs moving.
