Running a card shop while you exercise sounds like a better fit than trying to aim at something while the treadmill moves under you. I’d start with a relaxed pace, because the shop gives you plenty to think about without needing to turn every workout into a race.

## Gameplay

TCG Card Shop Simulator sounds like the kind of game where the small business decisions are the fun, and I think that can work really well as long as you enjoy doing similar jobs over and over. You stock booster packs, set prices, hire staff, host events, and expand the store, so there’s a clear little loop of making the shop better and then deciding what to do with the next batch of money. That’s satisfying in a practical way. You can point at a shelf and say, I did that.

Opening packs instead of putting them up for sale is the decision I’d be most interested in, because it turns shop inventory into your own card collecting. You’re choosing between what helps the store and what you want for yourself, which is a nice small conflict to have in a game about running a local card shop. I don't know how much depth there is to the collecting side, so I wouldn’t go in expecting a whole separate card game. But the temptation to keep the packs is very understandable.

The store work could also get repetitive, and I think that’s the part you need to be okay with before you buy it. Stocking shelves and setting prices sound pleasant when you’re in the mood to run a little shop, but less exciting if you want every minute to introduce something new. Hiring staff and expanding give you longer term goals, though, and hosting events gives the shop another thing to build around. You’re not just staring at booster packs forever. Probably.

I like that the basic fantasy is specific. It’s not a giant business empire or a made up chain with a hundred locations, it’s a local game store where the latest packs are on the shelves and you can decide to keep some for yourself. That makes the choices easy to understand, even if the routine might eventually feel like managing a very small pile of responsibilities. I don't know if I’d want to play it when I was looking for a big dramatic story, but I’d be curious to settle into it on a day when I wanted a game that lets me make modest plans.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

This seems much more suitable for a steady workout than a game that asks you to react quickly, and the full controller support is a real point in its favor. Stocking shelves and making shop decisions sound like tasks you can take at your own pace, rather than something where a slightly imprecise thumb movement ruins the whole afternoon. I’d try it at a walk first, then see if a very easy jog still leaves enough attention for the store. No need to turn pricing booster packs into a coordination drill.

On a bike, I’d expect the same general fit, especially at a comfortable effort where you can glance at what you’re doing without feeling like the screen is moving away from you. The shop management decisions could make a longer ride pass more easily, but I’d probably slow down when I was setting prices or deciding whether to sell packs or open them. That sounds like the point where my brain would insist on treating every little choice like it needs a meeting.

I’d skip any attempt to make the workout intense while you’re learning how the controller handles the shop. Full support means you can use a controller, which is great, but it doesn’t automatically mean every interaction will feel effortless while you’re bouncing along. If the routine starts to need more careful movement than expected, lower the pace and keep the workout easy. This is still a store simulator, not a reason to negotiate with the treadmill.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

A mid range graphics card from the last several years should be enough, and you don’t need a top end gaming computer just to open the shop. I’d look at the graphics card first, since the minimum calls for a GTX 1050 rather than integrated graphics.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is a sensible workout game for anyone who likes small business sims and card collecting more than constant action. I’d give it a try at an easy pace, but skip it if repeating shop tasks sounds like work you already have enough of.
