A famine story about a bandit escorting four girls to a demon’s feast is not what I’d put on to forget I’m exercising, but I can see it working for a slow ride when you want something to read. The treadmill is less convincing, and that’s mostly because reading takes your eyes off the belt.

## Gameplay

The premise is grim in a way that gives the story somewhere to go. A ruthless trafficker, a mute girl seeking revenge, and a deadly secret could easily feel like a pile of misery arranged for effect, but I think the unlikely bond and redemption angle give it a reason to keep moving beyond the shock of the setup. I’d want the characters to do more than make the world feel bleak. That’s the part I’d be watching.

This is a visual novel, so the main activity is reading rather than reacting. That can be a nice change from games where you’re constantly watching for an enemy or trying to land a button press, and it also means your workout might start to feel like standing in line while holding a controller. I don’t know that the story would pull me along at the same pace as a game with more frequent things to do, but I like the idea of a slower game that lets you settle in.

The famine ravaging ancient China gives the story a setting that sounds central to its mood, not just a background for the characters to walk past. I’d be interested to see how it handles the ugly parts of the premise, because a story about people being sold and delivered to a demon’s feast needs more than dark subject matter to make me care. I don’t know how well it balances the bleakness with the redemption story, so I’d go in for the writing rather than expecting a light evening.

There’s no action loop here to keep your attention busy while you exercise, and for me that matters. If I’m in a quiet stretch of a ride, I can see reading dialogue being pleasant; if I’m trying to keep a running pace, a paragraph that needs my full attention is going to feel like a small meeting I didn’t schedule. I’d play it when I’m ready to follow a story, not when I’m hoping the game will distract me from my legs.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The bike seems like the more realistic place to play this, because the game’s pace is built around taking in text and the bike lets you keep your eyes on the screen without also managing a moving belt. I’d keep the effort easy, give myself room to read, and treat it as a story session that happens to involve pedaling. That’s a pretty specific kind of workout, but it could work.

On a treadmill, I’d probably keep the speed low enough that I can look at the screen and still feel steady, and I’d stop reading if I noticed I was paying more attention to the text than my footing. That may sound like the obvious answer, but visual novels ask for exactly the thing a treadmill makes awkward: looking down at words for more than a quick glance. You can do it, I think, but it’s not the kind of game I’d choose for a brisk run.

I can’t confirm controller support from the details here, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming you can play hands-free of the mouse and keyboard. I’d check what input options it actually has before setting it up, and if you do use a controller, make sure you can comfortably advance the text without shifting around. That’s an unglamorous test, but it’s better than finding out halfway into the first conversation.

The nice part is that the game isn’t built around aiming or fast reactions, so a little wobble shouldn’t turn a story moment into a disaster. The harder part is paying attention to what you’re reading, and I’d expect the dramatic parts to hold my focus more than the easy stretches of a workout do. For me, this is a low effort bike game first, and a treadmill game only if you’re comfortable moving slowly.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to a very modest computer, including older integrated graphics, so you don’t need a gaming machine to give this a try. I’d check that your computer can run the game and has the small amount of storage it needs, then spend the rest of the setup time figuring out where the screen can sit comfortably.

## Conclusion and Score

This is for you if you want a dark, reading heavy story and have a stationary bike set up somewhere comfortable. I’d skip it for a treadmill workout where you want to run hard or keep your eyes up, but for slow pedaling and a grim story, it could fit.
