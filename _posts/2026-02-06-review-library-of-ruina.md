A treadmill run in Library Of Ruina is mostly a quiet stretch of reading followed by a turn based fight where you can take your time, and I think that works better than it sounds. You might get so invested in choosing which book to throw at somebody that the workout becomes background noise, though.

## Gameplay

This is a strange, thoughtful strategy game, and I like that it doesn’t rush you through its fights even when the story is taking its time. Guests arrive at the Library, combat breaks out, and defeated guests become books that help the Library grow. It’s a good premise, and the game keeps building on it in ways that make the fights feel connected to the story instead of like a separate activity. It is also a lot of reading. You’ve been warned.

The combat asks you to choose combat pages, assign attacks, and see how dice resolve against the other side. That means you’re making decisions about what to play and what to defend against, rather than just picking the biggest number and hoping your hands cooperate. I think that’s satisfying when you have the attention for it, but there are fights where I’d be staring at the available pages, weighing options, and realizing the treadmill had been moving the whole time. Not exactly a thrilling pace.

The Abnormality battles and the later floor realizations are where the game’s ideas get especially interesting, because they make you think about how you’ve built your librarians and what each fight is asking you to do. I like that the strategy changes as you meet different opponents, and that a fight can feel like a problem to solve instead of a pile of health bars. But if you’re just trying to keep your feet moving and let your brain take a break, this game may feel like somebody handed you a book report during gym class. I don’t know why that felt like a reasonable combination when I wrote it down, but it does.

There’s also a lot of story between the fights, and it’s not the kind of story you can safely half listen to while your legs do their own thing. The characters and the Library’s odd little world are a big part of why the game works, but the text can turn an easy workout into a long pause with a controller in your hands. I wouldn’t skip the story if you’re interested in it. I would just save it for a day when reading is part of the plan.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The turn based battles are a real advantage here because the game waits while you decide what to do, and nobody is going to punish you for taking an extra moment to read a page. A controller is usable, which helps, but selecting targets and moving through all the combat choices still asks you to pay attention. You can play while moving. I wouldn’t expect to do it on autopilot.

I’d set the difficulty and pace of the workout around the story, not the other way around. A gentle bike ride or an easy treadmill walk makes more sense to me than trying to run hard through a dense fight, because the game keeps asking you to check details and make plans. When the narration starts, I’d let myself read instead of trying to keep up with the belt and the plot at the same time. You can pause between decisions, but a story scene still wants your eyes.

Combat also has enough choices that sloppy inputs could send you into the wrong menu or leave you second guessing an attack, even though there’s no quick reaction test in the usual action game sense. I think I’d be more comfortable on a bike for the careful fights, since bouncing less would make it easier to read the screen, and I’d keep treadmill sessions to easier days. Maybe that’s just me being cautious around a game that has more buttons than my brain wants during cardio.

This is not a game I’d choose when I want my exercise to distract me from thinking. It’s a game I’d choose when I want the exercise to give me time to think about what I’m doing in the game, and those are different moods. If you enjoy strategy and don’t mind stopping between scenes, it could fit. If your workout plan is built around keeping a steady pace and looking ahead, the Library is going to ask for a lot of your attention.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum graphics card is old enough that a recent mid range gaming computer should have plenty of room to run this. I’d check that your computer has a dedicated graphics card at all before worrying about more powerful hardware, because the listed minimum isn’t asking much. The Library appears to have modest expectations of your computer.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Library Of Ruina is for you if you want a strategy game that gives you time to think and you’re happy to spend part of a workout reading. I’d play it on an easy ride or walk, but I wouldn’t pick it for a fast run or a low attention session. It takes its time, and sometimes you will too.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
