I’d keep this for an easy workout, because choosing a real place to build a survivor settlement sounds like the kind of strategy game that can pull your attention away from your pace without needing nonstop action. Then night comes and the infected arrive, which is a pretty good way to make you pay attention again.

## Gameplay

The real world map idea is the part I’m most curious about, because picking a familiar region and making it your base gives the settlement a specific place to belong instead of making it feel like a pile of buildings on a blank screen. You take charge of survivors and adapt existing buildings, and I like the practical feel of that. It sounds less like building a perfect little town and more like figuring out what you can use before things get worse. I’d probably spend too long debating which building deserves to be useful.

Then the game shifts from rebuilding to defending the zone at night, and I think that contrast gives the planning some purpose. A settlement game can turn into a lot of arranging things because the game lets you arrange things, which is how I end up organizing a drawer and then forgetting what I wanted from the drawer. Here, the infected give your choices some pressure. I don’t know how much time the game gives you between planning and defending, so I can’t promise it’s a relaxed pace the whole way through.

The mixture of strategy, simulation and action is appealing, but it also sounds like the sort of game where you need to keep a few decisions in your head at once. That can be good for an easy jog, since I’d rather have something to think about than stare at a progress bar, but I wouldn’t expect this to be an automatic game you can play while half paying attention. If the settlement needs a rethink, you’ll want to make that choice carefully. It’s not a game I’d put on when my brain is already done for the day.

Early Access makes me a little cautious, mostly because a game built around both settlement management and nighttime defense has a lot of pieces that need to work together. I’m not saying that means it’s bad, and the core idea genuinely sounds cool, but I’d want to be comfortable with the current version being a work in progress before I paid for it. If you enjoy trying strategy games while they’re still being developed, that could be part of the appeal. I’d just go in knowing it may not feel finished.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d start this on a slow walk or an easy ride, not a hard run, because choosing where to set up, adapting buildings and preparing to defend the zone all sound like decisions that deserve your eyes. You can probably keep moving while you plan, but I’d expect the infected showing up at night to make you focus more closely. That shift from slow decisions to urgent defense is interesting, and it’s also where I could see my workout getting interrupted by me leaning forward at the screen.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information I have, so I wouldn’t buy this specifically expecting a comfortable controller setup. Even if you can play with a controller, I’d expect selecting buildings and giving orders to be less forgiving while your hands are bouncing around on a treadmill. That’s an expectation, not a claim about a particular control setting, but it matters if you’re trying to exercise and not just sit on the belt holding a controller.

On a bike, I think the slower stretches of planning might work better, since you can keep pedaling while you look over the settlement, and then ease off when the night defense asks for more attention. I’d skip a fast run until you know how demanding the controls and attacks feel. I don’t know how much you can safely let the game wait while you catch your breath, so I wouldn’t count on being able to step away from a tense moment. You may end up standing on the treadmill to make one building decision. That’s not ideal.

There’s also a nice workout rhythm in the premise, at least on paper, because the rebuilding gives you a reason to keep moving through the quieter parts and the night defense gives you a reason to stay alert. But I think that only works if the game’s controls feel manageable for you while you’re in motion. I’d give it a try at a very gentle pace first, and I’d be ready to play it seated if the defense gets too fiddly.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum asks for an older dedicated graphics card, but it also wants a decent amount of memory, so I’d check your RAM before assuming an old computer can handle it. A machine around the stated graphics level should be the place to start, and this isn’t asking for a recent high end gaming PC.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This sounds like a thoughtful strategy game with a specific hook, and I’d suggest it to you if settlement planning and defending survivors both sound fun. For workouts, keep the pace gentle and don’t assume the controls will suit a moving setup.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
