Joel and Ellie make for a pretty good treadmill distraction, but I wouldn’t pick this when I want a workout that keeps my attention on the belt and nothing else. The quiet stretches are nice for settling into a pace, and then a Clicker comes around a corner and I’m suddenly gripping the controller like the treadmill is going to help me aim.

## Gameplay

The story and the action work well together, though I think the game is more uneven as a workout than it is as an adventure. You spend time exploring ruined places with Ellie, looking for supplies and trying to stay out of trouble, and then the game asks you to make a plan while people are already coming through the door. That shift keeps me interested, but it also makes the game hard to play casually while moving.

Sneaking past infected is tense in a way that suits the game, because you’re listening, watching for an opening, and trying not to waste the few resources you have. Clickers especially make a small mistake feel expensive, which is good drama and not exactly relaxing cardio. I’d probably enjoy that tension on an easy walk more than during a hard run.

The human fights are where the action gets exciting and where I’d have to give the screen more attention than I want to during a workout. Enemies move around, ammunition is limited, and aiming carefully matters, so this isn’t a game where I can just keep jogging and press the same button now and then. I would spend more time crouched behind cover than I’d like to admit.

Then you get quieter stretches where Joel and Ellie are talking, exploring, or moving through the world without an immediate fight. I like that the game lets their relationship grow in those moments, and I wouldn’t rush through them just to get back to running around with a weapon. I don’t know that I’d want every workout to stop and start with the story, but the slower scenes are part of why I care what happens to them.

There are also long story scenes, and you can’t turn every quiet minute into gameplay just because you happen to be on a treadmill. That makes the whole thing a better fit for an easy session where pausing or slowing down is fine than for a workout built around a steady pace. I’d rather take the story at its own speed than try to outrun a conversation.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A bike or a slower treadmill pace gives you more room to react when the game changes from searching a room to fighting for your life. Full controller support is a big help, and the game can be paused, so you aren’t stuck trying to aim while your legs are telling you something else. I’d still keep the pace comfortable.

The controls are manageable, but aiming at moving enemies and reacting to Clickers both ask for focus and reasonably steady hands. If I were running, I’d expect to lose some accuracy every time the belt got bouncy, and in a game where supplies are scarce that could mean a fight turns into a lot of missed shots. I don’t know if I’d want to learn the combat while also trying to hold a pace.

I’d lower the difficulty or adjust the aim assistance if the shooting starts feeling like too much work. The game has accessibility options, so you can make aiming and combat more forgiving instead of treating every encounter like a skills assessment. There’s no special honor in making a treadmill session harder than it needs to be.

I’d also use the quiet exploration and conversation stretches to settle into a rhythm, then ease off or pause when a fight starts demanding quick decisions. That probably sounds less like a consistent workout plan and more like driving through a town with too many stop signs, but it fits this game. On a bike, I’d be more comfortable making those changes without worrying about my feet.

This is not something I’d put on when I’m trying to watch a show and exercise at the same time. The story keeps pulling you in, and the combat punishes divided attention enough that I’d rather choose a pace I can maintain without thinking about it.

## Conclusion and Score

Save it for a relaxed workout where pausing is fine, and play it if you want a story driven action game that gives you plenty to pay attention to. I’d skip it for a fast run where you need the game to keep going at your pace.
