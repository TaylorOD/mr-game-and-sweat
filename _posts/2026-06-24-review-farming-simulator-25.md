A tractor moving across a field is about as close as a game gets to giving you a steady pace without asking you to aim at anything, so I think Farming Simulator 25 could work well for an easy treadmill session. I’d keep the pace comfortable, because there’s plenty to do, but most of it can wait while you get your feet sorted out.

## Gameplay

The appeal is the routine, and I mean that in a good way. You take a field through its work, send a machine across it, then watch the rows change while you decide what comes next. That gives your brain something to follow without constantly demanding a fast decision, which is useful when you’re trying to exercise and not prove that you can multitask like a person in a commercial.

I like that the farm can be a long project instead of a string of urgent little missions. You can work toward expanding the operation, take on contracts, and decide whether you want to focus on crops, animals, or the machinery that makes the whole place run. There’s a lot to keep track of, though, and menus are part of the job. I’d probably enjoy making a plan, then immediately forget which machine I meant to use.

The game adds rice and other crops to the farming mix, and that’s a nice reason to try a different kind of field work instead of doing the same crop routine forever. Weather can also affect the farm, which makes the world feel less like a perfectly behaved spreadsheet. I don’t know that I’d want every system turned into a major decision, but having a few things change the plan sounds more interesting than watching a tractor do the exact same lap all afternoon.

There’s a learning curve to all the equipment, and this isn’t the kind of game where you can pick up every vehicle and know what every attachment does right away. You can also hire helpers for some jobs, which is useful when a task starts to feel like moving a very expensive lawn mower in a straight line for a long time. I’d expect the first stretch to involve some reading and menu time, and that may slow down a workout if you’re hoping to start moving and never stop.

I think the slow pace is a feature, but it’s also why I wouldn’t call it a great match for every run. When you’re focused on managing a field or setting up a job, the time can move nicely; when you’re waiting for a task to finish, you may notice that you are waiting. That’s fine on a relaxed ride, and a little less ideal if you need the game to keep pulling your attention forward.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, I’d choose a simple job and give myself room to look at the screen without constantly correcting my footing. Driving a tractor across a field is not a reflex challenge, and the full controller support means you can play without having a keyboard balanced somewhere that will eventually slide off. I’d start with an easier pace and see how the steering feels before turning a simple chore into a driving lesson.

The controls do ask you to do more than hold one direction, though. You’ll be steering, switching equipment, lining up with a field, and handling menus, and I can see that getting awkward if you’re jogging hard enough that your hands are bouncing around. I’d save the fiddlier setup for a slow ride or a walking pace, and let a helper handle the repetitive work when the game gives you that option.

A bike seems especially comfortable for jobs where you can settle into a line and keep going, but I wouldn’t say the treadmill is out of the question. The pace of the game is forgiving, and you can take a moment to think instead of reacting to an enemy appearing behind you. Co-op could be fun if you want to work on a farm with someone else, but I’d keep the exercise part easy enough that the conversation isn’t competing with three menus and a vehicle.

The part I’d skip while moving quickly is any stretch where you’re figuring out an unfamiliar machine or trying to place equipment just right. You can do that at a low pace, and you don’t need to turn every minute of the workout into a precision task. It’s a much better fit for steady movement than a hard run where you want your eyes and hands doing something else.

## Computer Requirements

This needs a dedicated graphics card at roughly the level of a GTX 1050 Ti or RX 470, so integrated graphics aren’t the target. I’d check the graphics card first, because the rest of the minimum requirements are fairly modest and the field is where you’ll spend a lot of your time looking.  
### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Farming Simulator 25 makes sense if you enjoy slow, hands on routines and want a game that can sit beside a steady workout without constantly demanding a quick reaction. I’d give it a try on a bike or an easy treadmill pace, but I’d skip it if you need nonstop action to keep your legs moving.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
