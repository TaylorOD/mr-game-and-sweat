The forest sounds like a decent place to keep your legs moving, but BIGFOOT asks you to look around and aim at the same time, which is where my treadmill confidence starts to get a little shaky. I’d play it at an easy pace, not while trying to set a personal record.

## Gameplay

The whole idea of proving Bigfoot is real by hunting it is a good fit for a game, because it gives you a reason to search the woods instead of just wandering around until something happens. I think the searching and setting up your hunt would keep me interested, especially since you’re not only looking for the creature, you’re trying to be prepared when you find it. There’s a nice difference between “I’m going for a run” and “I’m going for a run with a plan.” My plans usually need a bit of work.

The searching is also the part I’d be most cautious about expecting to carry the whole game. You’ll be looking through the forest for clues and supplies, and I can see that being a good steady activity when you’re moving at a comfortable pace, but it sounds like there can be stretches where you’re scanning the trees and waiting for something to turn up. I don’t know whether that would feel tense or just make me stare at the same patch of woods while trying not to drift toward the edge of the treadmill. Probably depends on how often the hunt gives you something to do.

Once Bigfoot becomes the focus, the game asks for more attention than a relaxed walk through the woods. Aiming and reacting to a dangerous animal are a different kind of job from checking the scenery, and I’d expect the sudden encounters to pull your focus away from exercise. Traps and other preparation give you a way to think ahead, which I like, but thinking ahead doesn’t make the actual moment of trying to shoot something less fiddly. The forest gets less peaceful pretty quickly.

I’d be more interested in playing with other people, if you have that option available, because coordinating a hunt sounds like it could turn the slower searching into part of the fun. On your own, I think the quiet stretches will either help you settle into the game or make you wish the creature would quit hiding behind every tree. BIGFOOT is also in Early Access, so I’d keep that in mind if you want something that feels finished and predictable. It might be a fun hunt, but it’s still a work in progress.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The first thing I’d change is the pace, because you don’t want to be jogging hard while you’re searching for a target and trying to aim. A gentle walk gives you more room to look around without turning every encounter into a small argument between your feet and your thumbs. On a bike, I’d expect the slower search sections to be easier to manage, though aiming still asks you to pay attention.

I’d set up the controls before getting on the machine and make sure looking around, aiming, and firing are comfortable on your controller. I can’t confirm how well every controller setup works, so I wouldn’t make the first hunt also be a controls experiment. That’s like assembling a treadmill while already running on it, which seems like a lot of faith in your afternoon.

The sudden action is the part I’d skip while exercising hard. When you need to track Bigfoot and react quickly, loose inputs can mean missing your shot or losing control of the situation, and I don’t know how forgiving those moments are. I’d keep the pace easy enough that I can slow down or stop safely if the game gets busy. It’s not a game I’d pair with a hard interval workout.

The quieter hunting and preparation should be the better match for exercise, and I’d use those moments to settle into a steady pace. Then when the hunt gets active, I’d be ready to give the screen more attention and the treadmill less. That balance sounds workable, but it won’t suit you if you want a game that lets you tune out and keep moving without thinking. Some games let you coast. This one seems to want you looking over your shoulder.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimum points to an older, lower end computer rather than a machine built for the newest graphics card, so I’d look at your graphics hardware first and make sure it can handle the game. You’ll also need a 64 bit system, broadband internet, and room for the install, but this doesn’t sound like a game that demands a big upgrade.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

BIGFOOT sounds best for you if you like a slower hunt that can suddenly demand your attention, and you’re happy to keep your workout easy when that happens. I’d give it a try on a walk or relaxed bike ride, but I’d skip it for a hard treadmill session.
