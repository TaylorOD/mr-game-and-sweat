A workout in SpaceEngine would be less about chasing a score and more about deciding which planet to drift toward while your legs do their thing. I think that’s a pretty good trade, as long as you’re okay with a game that gives you room to get distracted by space.

## Gameplay

SpaceEngine is a planetarium you can move around in, and I like that the basic appeal doesn’t depend on winning or keeping up with anything. You can look around the known universe and explore areas generated by the game, which means you’re free to follow whatever catches your eye instead of being marched through a set of objectives. That sounds calming in a way that a lot of games aren’t. You can go have a look at a planet and not be late for anything.

The science-based approach is a big part of why I’d be interested in it. Space isn’t just a backdrop here, and I think that makes looking around feel more worthwhile than scrolling through pretty pictures. At the same time, it’s still a simulation, so I wouldn’t expect it to give you a steady stream of things to do. You’re choosing where to go and what to look at, and some of that will be exciting for you while some of it will feel like slowly searching a very large room.

There isn’t a story pushing you along, which is good for a workout but does mean the game needs your curiosity to carry it. I don’t know that I’d want to decide what to explore while trying to keep a hard running pace, because that sounds like the kind of decision I’d put off until the treadmill had already asked me to make it. On an easy session, though, picking a destination and taking your time seems like a nice fit. The universe is not in a hurry.

Procedural generation gives you places to explore beyond the mapped areas, which is a neat idea, but I’d probably spend more time appreciating the scale than trying to cover ground. There’s always somewhere else to point yourself, and that can be freeing, but it can also turn into a lot of wandering without a clear reason to stop. I think I’d enjoy that more when I wanted to browse than when I needed a game to keep pulling my attention back. It’s a very large thing to look at.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The relaxed pace is the main reason I’d try this while moving. There’s no opponent forcing you to react right now, and you can take a moment to decide where to go without losing a match or missing a jump. That should make it less stressful than a game that asks for quick, exact inputs, which is nice when your hands are bouncing around a little.

The part I’d be careful with is navigation. Exploring a huge space sounds simple until you’re trying to steer, look around, and figure out where you want to go while also running. I don’t know how comfortable the controls would feel on a controller for every part of that, so I wouldn’t assume you can leave the mouse and keyboard behind without checking first. If controller input works for your setup, I’d still try it at an easy pace before turning the treadmill up. Space has plenty of room for a slightly crooked turn.

On a bike, I think the slower pace could be especially comfortable because you can keep moving while taking your time with the view. You’re not trying to aim at a fast target, and nothing in the premise suggests that you need twitch reactions. But if you find yourself constantly stopping to use menus or make small navigation adjustments, that could get old on either machine. I’d keep the session gentle and treat exploration as the activity, not try to make this a high intensity workout with a second job attached.

I’d skip any goal you invent that makes you rush from place to place, because that would remove the thing that makes this appealing for exercise. Pick somewhere interesting, give yourself time to look around, and don’t worry about seeing as much as possible. There’s enough universe to make the treadmill feel like it has more distance to cover. A little too much, probably.

## Computer Requirements

You’ll want a reasonably capable gaming PC, not just integrated graphics, and the graphics card is the first part I’d check. The minimum calls for a card with at least 3 GB of VRAM, with the listed minimum being a GTX 1050 Ti or RX 460, so older or basic machines may have a harder time showing the universe as intended.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

SpaceEngine makes more sense for an easy workout where you want to explore at your own pace than for a session built around constant action. I’d suggest giving it a try if wandering through a science-based model of the universe sounds enjoyable to you, but skip it if you need the game to keep handing you clear goals. It asks you to bring your own curiosity.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
