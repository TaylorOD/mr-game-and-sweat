I think you can get a good workout out of HITMAN World of Assassination, but you’ll spend as much time deciding how to handle a target as moving Agent 47 around. Pick a quieter route through a level and it’s a lot more treadmill friendly.

## Gameplay

The fun here is that a target can be dealt with in a bunch of different ways, and I like having room to make a plan that’s more “walk in dressed as staff” than “run directly at the problem.” You can explore, watch how people move, and look for chances to get close without starting a fight. That gives your brain something to chew on, which is great until you realize you’ve been standing in one spot to watch a guard walk past the same doorway again. He’s committed to his route.

Mission Stories are a good place to start if all the possibilities feel like too much. They guide you toward a particular setup, so you can learn the level without having to guess which random person has the key you need. I’d use those at first and then try wandering on later runs, because the game is at its best when you know just enough to get into trouble on purpose.

There are quiet stretches, though, and they’re part of the game rather than a short pause between action scenes. You might be watching a target, waiting for a disguise to be useful, or walking around trying to work out whether the person behind that door is going to notice you. I don’t know, I think that slow pace is a big part of why the levels are fun, but it can be a weird match for a workout when you’d rather keep moving.

Getting spotted can turn a careful plan into a messy fight, and that shift is exciting when you’re focused but not what I’d want every minute of a run. The game gives you freedom, but it doesn’t mean every choice is equally easy to pull off. I’d treat a botched plan as a reason to improvise or reload, not as a sign that you need to be faster. The guards are probably better at their jobs than I’d be.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Controller support is full, which makes the basic movement and interaction easy to handle without reaching for a keyboard. The part I’d be careful with is aiming, sneaking past people, and reacting when someone notices you, because those moments want more control than a bouncing thumb always has available. On a bike, I’d expect the steady pace to make it easier to focus. On a treadmill, I’d keep the speed comfortable and avoid trying to play like I’m escaping through an airport.

I’d start with Mission Stories, play on an easier difficulty, and pick a level where you can take your time instead of trying to get through every objective quickly. If you’re getting tired, you can stop pushing forward and let the game wait while you catch your breath or sort out what you’re doing. I like that the game doesn’t demand constant action, even if sometimes you’ll be moving your legs while Agent 47 stands still beside a cupboard.

For a workout where you want continuous movement and quick reactions, this probably isn’t the one. Aiming carefully while the belt is moving could get irritating, and a sudden fight is a different job from a calm walk through a level. I’d skip trying to play stealthily at a hard running pace, and I wouldn’t choose it for a workout where you want to switch your brain off. It asks you to pay attention.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum graphics card listed is old enough that you don’t need a new high end gaming computer just to consider this, though I’d check that your machine can meet the game’s graphics requirements before buying. It also needs 60 GB of storage, which is a lot of room for a game about quietly borrowing somebody else’s clothes.

## Conclusion and Score

This is a good choice if you want a workout game that gives you time to think and doesn’t require constant button mashing. I’d play it on an easy walk or a steady bike ride, but I’d pick something else for a fast run.
