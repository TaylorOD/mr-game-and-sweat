Assassin’s Creed II’s rooftop running across Renaissance Italy can make a treadmill session feel lively, but stealth missions and story scenes bring the pace to a halt. It’s better for workouts where you can slow down or pause than for keeping a steady, hands-free rhythm.

# Gameplay

Ezio’s adventure offers plenty to engage with: exploring Florence and other Italian settings, climbing rooftops, sneaking past guards, and getting into fights. The varied missions can help distract you from exercise, especially when you’re moving through the city.

The pace isn’t constant, though. Story scenes, travel, and stealth sections interrupt the action, and sneaking successfully can demand more attention than a casual workout allows. Engaging overall, but not a nonstop action game.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

# Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The Steam store lists no controller support, while the minimum requirements mention an optional controller. Without controller support clearly listed, it’s safest to plan on using a keyboard and mouse—awkward while running or biking. Stealth and combat also involve deliberate movement and quick reactions, so loose inputs can make play frustrating.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

# Computer Requirements

The listed minimums are modest by modern standards but call for a dedicated graphics card: a 256 MB DirectX 9-compatible card, with examples including older ATI Radeon X1950 and NVIDIA GeForce 7-series cards. The minimum CPU is an Intel Core 2 Duo 1.8 GHz or AMD Athlon X2 64 2.4 GHz, with 1.5 GB of memory on Windows XP or 2 GB on Vista. These requirements point to an older or basic dedicated GPU rather than integrated graphics as the target.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

# Conclusion and Score

Assassin’s Creed II has an absorbing setting and satisfying stretches of exploration, but its cutscenes, stealth, and controller uncertainty make it a poor fit for uninterrupted exercise. It’s worth trying if you’re comfortable exercising at a flexible pace and can play from a stable setup; otherwise, save it for when you’re off the treadmill or bike.

### Overall Score: 1.67 out of 3
