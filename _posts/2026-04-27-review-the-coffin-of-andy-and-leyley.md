A story about a brother and sister practicing cannibalism is probably not what your legs need for motivation, but the slow walking and talking might fit an easy workout better than a game that expects your thumbs to keep up. I’d keep the pace comfortable, because there’s a lot to pay attention to.

## Gameplay

The premise is so unpleasant that I kind of admire how plainly the game gets on with it. Andy and Leyley witness a botched satanic ritual, and the situation turns into a strange, grim story about the two of them and the choices they make. The cannibalism isn’t just a spooky detail in the background, and I think that makes the game more interesting than a standard creepy house full of things to collect. It’s also not exactly dinner conversation.

The walking and talking setup gives you room to look around and take in the scenes, while the light puzzles break up the dialogue without turning the game into a big logic exercise. That sounds like a good mix for a workout, except the conversations are a lot of the point, and I wouldn’t want to be jogging hard while trying to follow every exchange. I can pay attention to my feet or a complicated family situation. Usually not both.

There’s a particular kind of dark comedy here that I like, where the game lets the characters be awful and uncomfortable without asking you to pretend they’re charming. I’m not sure everyone will want to spend their exercise time with people this difficult, and that’s fair, but the sibling dynamic gives the story something more specific than just “look, Satan.” It has a perspective, even when I’m not sure I want to agree with anyone in it.

The slow pace is a real part of the experience. You move through conversations, explore the setting and deal with small puzzles, and if you’re hoping for constant action, you’ll probably start checking how much longer you have left on the treadmill. I don’t know that the puzzles are enough to keep you busy on their own, but they do give you little stretches where you can concentrate on moving around instead of reading. Then the dialogue starts again.

I’d play this when the workout is easy enough that I can follow what’s going on, rather than when I’m trying to push myself. The story’s disturbing subject matter and uncomfortable humor are the draw, and they also make it a pretty specific mood. If you’re into unsettling character stories, it may be exactly the kind of odd thing you want to think about while you walk. If you just need something cheerful to get through cardio, maybe not.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

You can use a controller, and that matters here because the game isn’t built around quick aim or twitch reactions. Movement and light puzzles sound manageable while you’re walking or pedaling, and I’d expect the slow pace to be forgiving if your hands are bouncing around a little. I’d still pick an easy speed, because reading dialogue while exercising is a bit like trying to listen to someone explain a dream while you’re counting steps.

On a treadmill, I’d keep the belt slow enough that looking at the screen doesn’t become the whole workout. There are stretches where you’ll want to read and follow the conversation, so I wouldn’t treat this as a game you can safely half-watch while pushing for a personal best. You might be able to make it work on a bike at a steady pace, but it still asks for attention rather than giving you a constant stream of action to react to.

I’d also save it for a workout where stopping to think for a moment isn’t going to annoy you. The puzzles are described as light, which is reassuring, and the controller support means you don’t have to reach for a keyboard every time you want to move around. Still, the story is doing more work than the movement, so a distracted session could leave you wondering why everyone is upset and who said what. It happens.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This should run on an older computer, and I wouldn’t start by worrying about getting a powerful graphics card for it. The requirements are modest enough that a basic machine with integrated graphics is likely the first thing I’d try, though I’d still check that it can handle the game’s graphics standard.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is a strange, slow story game that could suit an easy walk or bike session if you enjoy dark humor and can keep your attention on the dialogue. I’d skip it for a hard workout or if you want something lighthearted to take your mind off exercising.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
