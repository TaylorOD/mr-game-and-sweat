Fishing for strange little sea creatures is a pretty good way to pass a treadmill workout, and DREDGE gives you a lot to do between the moments when the ocean tries to make you regret going out. I’d play it at an easy pace, because sometimes the best use of your attention is steering around a rock.

## Gameplay

I think DREDGE works because fishing is calm without being completely automatic, and there’s always another boat upgrade or distant island making me think I can just do one more trip. You catch fish, sell them, and use the money to improve your boat, which is a simple routine but a useful one. A faster boat makes travel less of a chore, and better gear lets you reach more fishing spots. The game doesn’t need to turn every fish into a life decision.

Then the sun goes down, and I start thinking I should have headed back earlier. The dark brings strange sights and dangerous things out on the water, and that shift from a quiet fishing trip to trying to get home safely is the part I like most. It’s tense, but not in the way a fast action game is tense. I’d probably be telling myself I had plenty of time right up until I didn’t.

The fishing and dredging challenges add just enough little bits of work to keep the routine from feeling like holding one button while the boat does everything. Some ask you to watch a moving marker and react at the right moment, and dredging asks you to fit objects into a limited space. I find that kind of small interruption pleasant when I’m playing at a desk, but it does mean I can’t entirely hand my brain over to the treadmill. A fish has paperwork, basically, though it’s not very much paperwork.

There’s also a story about the archipelago and the odd things happening around it, and I think it gives the trips a reason to matter beyond making the boat better. You’ll talk to people and follow their requests, but I wouldn’t buy DREDGE expecting the story to constantly pull you forward. Some of the appeal is just checking a new corner of the map and seeing what’s there, which is more my speed than being rushed through a pile of plot.

Inventory management is probably my least favorite part, because fitting fish and gear into a small grid can turn a quick stop into me rearranging everything like I’m packing a suitcase badly. It’s not hard, and I don’t think it ruins the game, but the boat’s limited space makes every trip feel like a choice about what to bring back. I’d prioritize upgrades and useful fish over trying to collect absolutely everything.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The boat moves at a pace that’s mostly easy to follow, and the game isn’t asking for constant aiming or rapid button combinations. That’s a good fit for light exercise, especially while you’re cruising between fishing spots or sitting safely at a dock. Full controller support helps, and I’d use a controller rather than trying to manage the boat with a keyboard while moving.

The part I’d be careful with is steering near rocks and handling the little timing challenges. You’ll need to make small adjustments to the boat, and fishing can ask you to react at a specific moment, so a loose hand or a bouncing thumb could mean a missed catch. It’s not a game that punishes every mistake brutally, but I wouldn’t start with a hard run and assume I can play at full attention. I’d keep the pace comfortable and give the boat a wide berth around obstacles.

At night, I think the exercise machine could make the game’s tension a little harder to manage, because you’re trying to read what’s happening on the water while also getting home before the situation gets worse. The monsters and strange sights aren’t a reason to avoid the game, but they’re the moments when I’d want to pay attention and stop trying to chase a faster pace. You can choose to head back to a dock and rest instead of squeezing another fishing trip into the dark. I’d take that option pretty often.

A bike might feel easier during those careful steering stretches, since your hands can stay steady while you pedal, but I wouldn’t say the treadmill is a bad fit. The main thing is not to set a pace that turns every turn in the boat into a tiny emergency. If I were starting a workout, I’d leave a little room to slow down, and I’d avoid making the final trip of the night while I’m distracted. There’s always another fish.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are low enough that an older graphics card should be able to run DREDGE, and the game only asks for a 720p, 30 FPS baseline. I’d check the graphics card first if you’re using an older computer, but this is not asking you to buy a new machine just to look at a foggy ocean.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

DREDGE is a good choice for an easy workout if you like quiet exploration that occasionally turns unsettling, and I’d skip it if you need nonstop action to stay engaged. Keep the pace comfortable, head back before night gets too far along, and let the boat take its time.
