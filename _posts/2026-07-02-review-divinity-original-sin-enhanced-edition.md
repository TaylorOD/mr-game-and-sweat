## Gameplay

Turn based combat makes Divinity: Original Sin - Enhanced Edition a much better workout companion than most big role playing games, because you can think through a fight without the game demanding that your hands keep up with your legs. I’d still expect to stop moving for a while when the story starts talking.

The fights have enough going on to hold your attention. You can use rain to leave the ground wet and then follow it with electricity, or set oil on fire and watch a plan get considerably less tidy. I like the idea of a fight changing because you noticed what the battlefield was made of, and I also know I’d sometimes spend a long time deciding whether to make a smart move or just hit the nearest skeleton. Both are valid, I think.

The murder investigation in Cyseal gives you a reason to look around and talk to people, but the conversations and searching slow the pace down quite a bit. There’s plenty to read, and I wouldn’t want to pretend all of that is going to keep you jogging at the same speed. You can take a walking pace while you follow the story, or treat the quiet stretches as a reason to get off the belt and stretch. The game isn’t going to hurry you along.

A lot of the appeal comes from figuring out what sort of party you want and how its different skills work together, and that means menus, equipment, and choices are part of the meal. I think that’s fun when I’m settled in, but it can feel like stopping at the grocery store every few blocks when you’re trying to exercise. The Enhanced Edition has full voiceovers, which helps, though voice acting doesn’t make every conversation short.

I don’t know that the story would keep me moving through all the slower stretches by itself, but I would want to see what happens after a fight goes sideways and the battlefield fills up with fire or poison. There’s enough interesting stuff here to come back to, even if some of that stuff involves reading a conversation while the treadmill keeps doing its job.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The fights wait for you, which is a big deal when your hands are bouncing around and you’re trying to choose an ability with a controller. Divinity has full controller support, and the turn based setup means there’s no enemy sprinting across the screen because you took a second to look at your buttons. I’d be comfortable playing it at an easy pace.

The controller doesn’t make every decision effortless, though. There are skills, equipment, and conversations to sort through, and I’d expect to slow down or stop when I was trying to compare a handful of items or read something important. I don’t know how much of the dialogue I’d absorb while running, and I already have a hard enough time remembering why I walked into a room when I’m not doing cardio.

On a bike, I’d probably be comfortable at a steady pace through combat, and I’d slow down during exploration and conversations. On a treadmill, walking seems like the better fit, with a slower jog possible during fights once you know your way around the controls. I’d avoid making the more difficult choices while pushing hard, and I’d pick a lower difficulty if I wanted the workout to be the priority. There’s no need to make an ogre fight a coordination exercise too.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest, so an older computer with a DirectX 11 compatible graphics card should have a chance of running it. I’d check that graphics support first, since the rest of the listed requirements are pretty easy to meet by today’s standards.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Divinity: Original Sin - Enhanced Edition makes sense if you want a thoughtful game that gives you time to breathe during combat, and you’re fine with reading and managing menus between the more active parts. I’d suggest walking or riding at an easy pace, and I’d skip it if you need a game that keeps your eyes and hands busy every second.

### Overall Score: 2.67 out of 3
