Keep the pace easy for this one, because Outcore: Desktop Adventure sends you out of the game and into Windows apps like Paint and Notepad to solve puzzles. I’d treat it more like a walk or gentle bike ride than something to play while you’re bouncing along at a hard pace.

## Gameplay

The desktop idea is what makes this appealing to me, and also what makes it a little hard to settle into as a workout game. You aren’t only looking at a game screen and reacting to things inside it. You’re opening familiar apps and searching your computer for files you didn’t put there, which gives the mystery a nice personal feeling. I like a game that can make Notepad seem suspicious.

The story starts with a girl appearing on your desktop, and the questions about who she is and how she got there give the puzzle solving a reason to matter. I think that setup is much more interesting than having a character explain the rules of a made up world for ten minutes, though you should expect to stop and think rather than stay in constant motion. If you need a game to keep your attention through frequent action, this probably isn’t the one.

Paint and Notepad are familiar enough that the interaction sounds approachable, but familiar doesn’t always mean effortless. I can imagine looking at something on the desktop, figuring out what it might mean, and then trying an idea in an app, which is a pleasant change from being asked to fight another batch of enemies. It also means your brain is doing some actual puzzle work. My legs might be moving while the rest of me is trying to remember where I saw a clue.

Searching through files you didn’t put there is a clever way to make the computer itself part of the mystery, and I’d be curious to see how far the game takes that idea. I don’t know how much of the experience is spent reading or piecing things together, so I wouldn’t promise you a steady stream of activity. The description points toward memories and discovery, which usually asks you to pay attention, and that can be a nice change on an easy workout. It can also mean standing on a treadmill because you forgot you were on one.

Since it’s free to play, giving it a try doesn’t ask much of your wallet, and the concept is unusual enough that I’d rather see it than hear someone explain it in a paragraph. I’d go in expecting a puzzle adventure with quieter stretches, not an action game with a story attached. That difference matters if you’re picking something specifically to keep your pace up.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A bike seems like the easier place to play this, mostly because you can keep your upper body steadier while you look between the game and Windows apps. I’d choose a light effort and leave the hard intervals for something that doesn’t ask me to inspect files or work out what a clue means. There’s a real difference between turning your legs while you think and trying to type while your whole view is bobbing around.

On a treadmill, I’d keep it to a walk unless you already know you can use your computer comfortably while moving faster. The Paint and Notepad parts sound like they’ll ask for more deliberate input than a game where you can hold one direction and let things happen, and the searching could mean shifting your attention around the screen. I wouldn’t want to guess at a tiny detail while jogging and then blame the computer for my handwriting.

Controller support isn’t confirmed in the information I have, so I wouldn’t build a workout plan around using only a controller. The desktop app idea makes keyboard and mouse interaction seem likely to matter, even if some parts might work fine with a controller. I’d set the game up at a desk or bike station where reaching the keyboard and mouse isn’t a small athletic event.

The good thing is that puzzle games generally let you take the time you need, and this one sounds like it wants you to explore rather than make split second decisions. That suits an easy ride where you can slow down your effort when a clue gets interesting. I don’t know whether every puzzle will be comfortable to handle while moving, so I’d be ready to pause or stop pedaling when you need to focus. A little less momentum is probably better than opening the wrong thing five times.

I wouldn’t choose it for a fast treadmill run, but for a relaxed bike session or a walk it could be a nice change from games that demand constant reactions. You’re not trying to aim at something while your hands bounce around, which is already a pretty low bar, but the desktop switching still asks you to pay attention.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older, modest gaming computer rather than a machine built for the newest graphics cards, and the small storage requirement shouldn’t take much room. I’d look first at whether your computer meets the graphics requirement, though this doesn’t sound like a game asking your PC to do anything especially wild.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Outcore: Desktop Adventure sounds worth trying if you like puzzles and want a quieter workout companion, especially on an easy bike ride or walk. Skip it for a fast run, and don’t count on the desktop interactions being effortless while you’re moving.
