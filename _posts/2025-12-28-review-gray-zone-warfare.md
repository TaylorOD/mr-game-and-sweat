I’d keep Gray Zone Warfare off the treadmill until you know you can use a controller comfortably, because a tactical shooter sounds like a lot to manage while your hands are bouncing around. On a bike, I’d start easy and see whether the aiming asks for more focus than the workout can spare.

## Gameplay

I think the PvE first approach gives this a more appealing workout shape than a shooter built around competing against other players, because you’re there to take on missions and explore the island rather than keep up with somebody else’s score. You can deploy solo or with a squad, and that leaves room to play at your own pace, at least in principle. The word “tactical” does make me expect a slower, more deliberate kind of action, though, and that could mean stretches where you’re thinking carefully instead of moving much. I don’t mind a little of that. I just wouldn’t want my jog to turn into standing upright while I work out what to do next.

The setting gives the missions a reason to feel connected. You’re on a quarantined island in Southeast Asia, with factions fighting and a larger mystery around the Event, and I like having that bigger question hanging over the work. It sounds more interesting than being sent from one unrelated firefight to another. I don’t know how much the story will pull me along in practice, and it’s an Early Access game, so I’d be cautious about buying it specifically for a finished, carefully paced campaign. The premise is doing its job, though.

Earning reputation for completing missions is the part I’d want to understand before I planned a workout around it, because progression can be a nice reason to stick with a game, but it can also make every outing feel like a job you’re trying to finish before you can stop. I’d be happy to do a short mission and call that a decent session, rather than expecting a tidy beginning and ending every time I hop on the treadmill. That’s probably the healthier arrangement for both of us.

There’s enough here that I’d be curious to try it, especially if you like shooters where the atmosphere and the mission give you something to think about between fights. I’m not going to pretend I know how often those fights happen or how long the quieter stretches are, since the description doesn’t tell me, and that matters a lot for exercise. A game can sound perfect for a workout and then ask you to focus on one careful decision for several minutes. I’ve done that while walking around the house, and it doesn’t exactly raise my heart rate.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The aiming is what gives me pause, because even a PvE first game still asks you to handle a tactical shooter, and aiming carefully while your body is moving is not the same as aiming from a chair. On a bike, I’d try a low effort ride first and see if I could keep my hands steady enough to enjoy it. On a treadmill, I’d walk before I tried jogging. I would not start by turning the belt up and hoping my thumbs sort themselves out.

I can’t confirm controller support from the details I have, so I’d check that before buying it for exercise. If you do play with a controller, I’d expect the deliberate pace to help, but I wouldn’t count on the game waiting for you or making precise inputs unimportant. The whole point of a tactical shooter is that choices and aim matter, and that’s a lot to ask when you’re bouncing along. Maybe your hands are steadier than mine would be.

Playing solo sounds like the option I’d look at first for a workout, since coordinating with a squad while trying to keep a steady pace sounds like one task too many. I wouldn’t assume solo means you can pause whenever you want, though, because I don’t know that from the information here. If a mission is asking for your full attention, I’d slow down or stop rather than trying to be the person who gets exercise and makes every careful shot at the same time. I only have so much coordination to spend.

This feels more promising for an easy bike session than a run, but only if the controller works for you and the aiming feels manageable. Skip the treadmill jog until you’ve tried it at a walking pace. That’s the part I’d be careful about.

## Computer Requirements

This needs a dedicated graphics card with 8 GB of video memory, so an older office computer probably won’t be enough. I’d check your graphics card first, because the minimum range includes cards like the GTX 1080 and RTX 3060, and an SSD is required too.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Gray Zone Warfare sounds like a worthwhile try for someone who wants mission based PvE and can give the aiming their attention, but I wouldn’t choose it as a dependable treadmill game. Check controller support before buying, and start with a slow ride or a walk.
