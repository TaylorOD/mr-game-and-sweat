A Japanese street racing game with wager races sounds like a pretty good way to make a treadmill session go faster, as long as you’re not trying to do your most serious driving while your feet are bouncing around. I’d save the car tweaking for when you’re standing still.

## Gameplay

I think the appeal here is that the racing has a reason to happen beyond winning another race, with local crews fighting over territory and wager races giving you something specific to care about. That could make a short session feel like it has a bit of direction, and I like the idea of choosing to explore or race instead of having every minute decided for you. I don’t know how much time the prologue spends on each of those things, so I’d go in expecting some stops along the way.

The car customization sounds like the part that could quietly eat up your workout. Detailed changes are fun when you’re in the mood to fuss over a vehicle, but I can also see myself moving things around for a while and then realizing I have been standing still with a controller. That is not a terrible way to spend an evening. It is a less useful way to get a run in.

Set between 1990 and 2009, the Japanese street racing setting has a clear identity without needing to dress itself up as some giant racing festival. Crews, territory, and wagers give the driving a little pressure, which is more interesting to me than racing just because a menu says the next event is available. I’m curious how much the crew conflict actually shapes the moment to moment play, but even as a setup, it gives the races a reason to matter.

There’s enough here to keep me interested, though I wouldn’t assume it’s all fast driving with no pauses. Open world exploration and customization make it sound like a game where you’ll sometimes take a break from racing to decide what you want to do next, and for me that’s fine on the couch but not always great mid workout. I’d probably treat the slower bits as natural water breaks. Nothing wrong with that.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is a big deal here, because I wouldn’t want to build a workout around reaching for a keyboard while trying to control a car. Still, racing asks you to steer and react with some care, and your hands aren’t exactly known for being perfectly still when you’re jogging. I’d start at a walking pace, or take it to a bike where your upper body is less likely to be bouncing around.

The open world gives you a lower pressure option between races. I’d use that time to cruise around and explore, then save wager races and crew battles for when I’m feeling steady enough to pay attention to the driving. If I tried to customize a car while moving, I’d probably end up turning a workout into a lengthy discussion with a menu. So I’d make those choices before I start.

I don’t know how demanding the races feel from one moment to the next, and that matters more than the genre label here. A race that asks for careful steering and quick corrections can pull your attention away from your stride, while cruising around sounds easier to pair with a treadmill. I’d keep the pace comfortable and be willing to pause between activities, rather than trying to turn every race into a test of coordination.

On a bike, I’d expect to have a little more control over my upper body, but I still wouldn’t push the resistance or pace so hard that the racing gets sloppy. The wager part could make it tempting to focus on the game instead of the workout, which is exactly why I’d save competitive feeling events for a steady, manageable effort. You can get the fun without needing to win every time. Probably.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimum points to an older dedicated graphics card, not a machine that needs a current high end gaming setup, so I’d look at your graphics hardware first. The target is 1080p at 60 frames per second on low or medium settings, and an SSD is recommended. This should be a reasonable game to run on a modest gaming PC.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

NIGHT-RUNNERS PROLOGUE sounds like a nice fit if you want street racing with some freedom to explore, and you’re comfortable keeping the workout pace sensible during races. I’d skip the car setup while moving and save the more demanding driving for when you feel steady. If menus and tuning tend to turn your exercise time into sitting time, this may not be the one.
