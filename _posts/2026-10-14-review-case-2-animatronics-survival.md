The animatronic versus survivor setup sounds like it could keep your attention better than a horror game that asks you to spend a long time watching and waiting. I’d be cautious about playing the online mode while running, though, because three survivors have to deal with players controlling the animatronics, and that sounds like a lot to keep track of while your hands are bouncing.

## Gameplay

The online setup is the part I can judge most clearly from the available details, and I think the split between the animatronics and the three survivors gives it a useful source of tension. You aren’t all trying to do the same thing, which is usually more interesting than making every player run around doing the same job. It also means the fun probably depends a lot on which side you’re playing and how much you enjoy being hunted or doing the hunting.

There’s a satisfying idea in letting a player become the threat, rather than making the animatronics scenery that you run away from. I like that the role sounds active, and not just like you’re walking around looking at scary things. Still, I don’t know how much variety each side gets, or how much the game asks you to learn before the match starts, so I’d keep my expectations a little measured.

The game has two modes, but the store description gives me much less to go on about the other one. That makes it hard to say whether it gives you a calmer option or just a different way to get chased around. I’d want to know more before buying it mainly for that mode, because “you’ll have to find out” is a fun line in a game description and a less useful answer when you’re trying to choose a workout game.

I think the online mode could be fun when you want other people to make the horror feel less predictable, but that same uncertainty can make it harder to settle into a steady pace. A human controlled animatronic is not going to politely keep doing the same thing while you get your breathing under control. I’m not sure how much the match gives you room to think, so I’d expect some tense stretches rather than a smooth, even rhythm.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, I’d probably keep the pace easy and use the online mode only if I was comfortable reacting while moving. The setup sounds like it could pull your focus toward the match, especially for the survivors, who have to deal with one or more players on the animatronic side. I can see myself leaning toward the screen when I should be looking where I’m going, which is usually a sign that the game is winning the attention contest.

A bike seems like the less fussy place to try it, since you can keep pedaling without needing to place each step while your attention is on the screen. That doesn’t make the game relaxed, though. If the action asks for quick decisions or precise movement, you still have to manage that with your hands, and a surprise from a player-controlled animatronic sounds like the kind of thing that makes me forget I’m supposed to be exercising. I don’t know how demanding the controls are, so I’d start with a very easy pace.

I can’t confirm controller support from the details here, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming that setup is ready to go. Check the controls before you plan a treadmill session, and if you can get it working with a controller, try a short, low effort session first. A keyboard and mouse would be a lot harder to manage while moving, and I don’t want to pretend otherwise.

I’d skip online matches when you’re tired or trying to hold a specific pace. Playing against people means the pressure can change quickly, and I’d rather not have the match decide that my legs need to sprint. This could still work for a casual bike ride if you’re fine with stopping or losing track of your pace for a moment. On a treadmill, I’d be more careful.

## Computer Requirements

You’ll need a 64 bit Windows computer with a graphics card around the level of a GTX 1050 Ti, so this isn’t aimed at a very old laptop with built in graphics. I’d check the graphics card first, then make sure you have enough memory and storage, because the game asks for a reasonable amount of both. It shouldn’t require a top of the line gaming PC.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

CASE 2: Animatronics Survival sounds like a more promising fit for a relaxed bike session than a focused treadmill run, especially if the online chase sounds fun to you. I’d hold off if controller play is important and you can’t confirm it works, since that changes the whole workout setup.
