Keep this for a slow treadmill walk, because Gwent gives you time to think between cards but still expects you to pay attention when it’s your turn. I wouldn’t start a hard run with an online match going.

## Gameplay

The best thing about Gwent is that a match makes you think about when to stop, not just which card to play, and that gives the rounds a nice little tug of war. You’re trying to win two rounds out of three, so spending a powerful card now might make the current round easier and the next one miserable. I think that’s a clever decision to build a whole card game around, even if I sometimes want to play my impressive card immediately because it looks impressive.

There’s a satisfying amount of room to make a deck feel like yours, and the Witcher setting gives the factions a clear identity without requiring you to know every bit of lore first. Monsters, Nilfgaard, Skellige, and the other factions all give you different ways to approach a match, which is exciting if you like trying strategies and a little tiring if you just wanted to get moving. I’d start with one faction and give it a few matches before opening every deck menu like I’m looking for a place to park.

Most of the match is played in turns, so it isn’t the kind of card game where you’re frantically clicking through a pile of actions. That’s good for thinking, but the card text and the choices behind each move can pull your attention away from the treadmill. I don’t know how quickly you’ll want to learn a new deck while exercising, but I’d keep the first few games simple and let the more complicated strategies wait until your legs aren’t also asking for attention.

The online PvP part gives the decisions some weight, which makes a win feel earned and a bad play feel like a small public announcement that you got greedy. I like that tension when I’m sitting down to focus on a match, but it’s also why I wouldn’t call Gwent a low effort background game. You’re not just putting cards down while the game politely gets on with itself.

There’s plenty to like if you enjoy card strategy, and the free to play price makes it easy to see whether the decisions click for you. Still, deckbuilding and learning card effects are a real chunk of the experience, so someone looking for a game that gets straight to uncomplicated action may find themselves reading more than playing. It’s a card game, and it does enjoy being one.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The turn based pace sounds almost tailor made for exercise, and some of it is: while the other player is deciding what to do, you can keep walking or pedaling without needing to react to something on screen. Then your turn arrives, you start comparing cards and trying to remember what the opponent has already played, and the workout quietly becomes a chair with moving parts. I’d use it for an easy walk, not a run where every step makes the text wobble.

A controller should make choosing cards more manageable than reaching for a mouse, but I’d test the controls before committing to a workout. The bigger issue is that these are online matches, so you can’t just pause when you want to get your pace back or take a second to read a card carefully. Nobody needs me to make a brilliant strategic decision while jogging, but the other player is still there waiting.

On a bike, I’d expect it to be easier to keep your body steady while looking at the cards, though that doesn’t make the match any less competitive. On a treadmill, even a modest bounce can turn small text into a bit of a guessing game, and guessing what a card does is a fun way to lose a round. I’d keep the speed low, use a larger screen if you have that option, and avoid starting a match when you’re already tired.

I wouldn’t skip the whole game for exercise, but I would skip ranked or otherwise high pressure matches while moving. A casual match at an easy pace is more reasonable, especially once you know your deck and don’t need to inspect every card like it came with a tiny contract. If you’re hoping to zone out, this probably isn’t the one.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older computer rather than a machine built for demanding new games, and the graphics card is the part I’d check first. I wouldn’t expect you to need a recent gaming PC just to move cards around a board.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Gwent is a thoughtful free card game that I’d suggest to someone who already likes strategy and wants a gentle workout companion. For anything faster than an easy walk or ride, I’d save the match for later.
