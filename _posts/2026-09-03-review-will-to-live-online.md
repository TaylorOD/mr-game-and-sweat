I’d save Will To Live Online for an easy bike ride, not a run where the belt is bouncing you around while you aim at mutants and other survivors. You’re trying to exercise and play a multiplayer shooter at the same time, and I think those two jobs are going to argue a little.

## Gameplay

The post apocalyptic setting gives the game a clear reason to keep you moving through its world, and I like that the threats aren’t only other players. Mutants give you something to fight without making every encounter a contest with another person, which sounds like a more relaxed way to spend a workout. I’d probably be more interested in exploring and taking fights as they come than treating every survivor as somebody I need to beat.

The other side of that setup is that a shooter asks for attention in a way a lot of exercise games don’t. You’re not just making a broad choice and letting things play out. You’re trying to aim, react, and stay alive while the world is full of things that can attack you. I think that could be a fun kind of distraction when you’re sitting still, but while you’re running it sounds like a lot of tiny corrections at exactly the point where your hands are already moving around.

The clan conflict sounds like it could give the world a reason to care about other players, and that’s a neat fit for an online RPG. I’d be curious how much of the fun comes from working with a clan, because playing alongside people can make an online game feel less like wandering around by yourself. But I wouldn’t go into this expecting a calm background activity. A struggle for survival has a way of making you pay attention, even if you were hoping to check out mentally for a few minutes.

Early Access matters here, too. I don’t know how the game will change over time, so I’d treat what’s there now as something you’re trying rather than a finished package you’re buying into. It’s free to play, which makes that a lower stakes decision, but free still costs you time, and I’d want the shooting and exploration to be enjoyable before I got invested in the bigger online parts. A free game can still take up the whole evening.

I think the basic idea has enough going for it to be worth a look if you enjoy shooters with a persistent world and other players in it. For me, though, the most appealing part for exercise would be the stretches of exploration and fighting mutants, and I can’t tell you how long those stretches last or how often the game pushes you into more demanding fights. That uncertainty makes me less confident about recommending it as a workout game than as a game to try on its own.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The aiming is what makes me cautious. Even if you’re only dealing with mutants, a shooter asks you to point carefully and respond to what’s happening, and that gets harder when your body is bouncing along with your steps. On a bike, your hands may have a steadier time, but I’d still expect combat to pull your attention away from your pace. I’d probably start at a very easy effort.

Fights with other survivors are the part I’d leave for when you’re not exercising. Other people can react to you, and I wouldn’t want to be halfway through a workout trying to make precise decisions against someone who is entirely focused on the game. I don’t know whether every encounter is intense, but I’d rather make that call from a chair than find out while my feet are doing their own thing.

Controller support isn’t something I can confirm from the information here, so I’d check that before planning a whole workout around it. I wouldn’t assume that a controller will make aiming comfortable just because the game is a shooter, either. If you do try one, I’d test it while stationary first and see whether moving and aiming feel manageable. That’s a boring first step, but it beats getting settled on the treadmill and discovering the setup is a project.

For a moving session, I’d focus on exploring and take the workout down a notch if combat starts demanding more attention. I’d skip clan conflict and fights with other survivors until you can give them your full focus, and I wouldn’t count on being able to pause an online game whenever you want. You might get a decent bike session out of the quieter parts, but I’d be careful about calling the whole game treadmill friendly. The action sounds like it wants your hands.

## Computer Requirements

This needs a dedicated graphics card around the level of a GeForce GTX 1060 or Radeon RX 580, so it’s not aimed at a computer relying on basic integrated graphics. I’d check your graphics card first, since the minimum also calls for 12 GB of memory and a fair amount of storage, and 45 GB is a decent chunk of the drive.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is worth trying if you want a free online shooter RPG and don’t mind keeping your attention on combat. For exercise, I’d only try it on an easy bike ride after checking controller support, and I’d leave survivor fights for another time.
