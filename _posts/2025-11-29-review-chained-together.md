Chained Together is a strong workout distraction because every jump can turn into a team rescue, a sudden fall, or a long climb back up. It is less friendly to a treadmill than a bike, though, since the platforming asks for careful timing right when your feet are doing their own thing.

## Gameplay

The goal is simple: climb out of Hell. The catch is that you are chained to your friends, or dealing with the chain on your own in solo play. The chain turns ordinary platforming into a shared physics problem. One player’s jump can tug everyone else, and a bad landing can leave the group dangling or send the whole party tumbling. You can use the chain to help pull each other up, but it is just as good at making a small mistake everybody’s problem.

That makes even basic stretches of platforms feel tense. You need to judge jumps, watch where your teammates are, and sometimes wait for everyone to get into position before moving on. In a group, communication matters more than raw reflexes. A confident leap from one player can be a useful boost, or the start of a spectacular group failure. Solo play keeps the climbing challenge but loses the shouting and negotiations that make cooperative runs memorable.

The game offers different ways to approach the climb, including a more forgiving Beginner mode, a standard Normal mode, and Lava mode, where rising lava puts pressure on your progress. Beginner mode is useful for learning the routes and physics without every mistake threatening to erase a long stretch of progress. Normal mode makes falls hurt more, while Lava mode adds a reason to keep moving instead of taking your time to line up every jump. Those options give you a choice between a relaxed learning run and a more demanding climb.

There is very little between you and the actual platforming. You spend your time jumping, adjusting your position, and figuring out how the chain will react, rather than sitting through long cut scenes or piles of dialogue. The climb keeps presenting new obstacles, and the possibility of losing progress makes success feel earned. It can also make a failed jump pretty painful, especially after a long stretch of careful work. That risk is the game’s main source of excitement, but it can become frustrating if your group keeps repeating the same mistake.

For a game built around trying, falling, and trying again, Chained Together does a good job keeping you occupied. The levels are the workout distraction, not a story you have to stop moving to follow. The main drawback is that a bad fall can leave you replaying familiar sections instead of making progress.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Chained Together works with a controller, which helps a lot here. Movement and jumping are straightforward, but that does not mean the game is easy to play with loose inputs. You need to judge gaps, control your position, and pay attention to the chain. A slightly mistimed jump can drag your friends into trouble, and in the harsher modes it can cost you a lot of progress.

That makes a stationary bike the better fit. You can keep your hands on the controller and focus on tricky jumps while pedaling at a comfortable pace. On a treadmill, even a walking pace can be awkward during sections that need precise timing or quick corrections. It is also a game where your attention can snap toward the screen when a fall is about to happen. If you try it on a treadmill, keep the pace easy and the route clear. This is not the platformer to pair with a hard run and hope your feet handle the details.

Lava mode is especially demanding for exercise play, since stopping to catch your breath or carefully study a jump can work against you. Beginner mode is the sensible choice for a relaxed workout session. You can focus on learning the climb without every slip turning into a major setback.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements call for a 64 bit version of Windows 8, 10, or 11, an Intel Core i5 6600 or AMD R5 1600X, 8 GB of RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1650 or equivalent. You also need 8 GB of storage. These are reasonable requirements for a modern PC, but the GTX 1650 means you should not expect the game to run well on just about any older computer with integrated graphics.

Online play has an extra requirement: a high speed internet connection and a good connection between players. That matters for a game where everyone’s position and the chain physics need to work together. A poor connection can make a cooperative climb more frustrating than it needs to be.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Chained Together is a good choice if you want a game that keeps your attention through active play and gives you a reason to care about every jump. The shared chain creates funny and stressful moments without needing much story or downtime, and the different modes let you decide how much punishment you want from a fall.

For exercise, it fits a bike better than a treadmill. Controller play is convenient, but the precise jumps and the cost of mistakes ask for more focus than a casual pedaling game. Start with Beginner mode, keep your pace manageable, and bring friends who will not blame you for every fall. They will probably have enough evidence to make a strong case.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
