I’d put Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor on a steady treadmill walk, because the dwarf does the shooting for you and your job is mostly to keep moving before the bugs surround you. On a bike, I think it’d work too, though the swarms might ask more of your hands than your legs.

## Gameplay

Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor gets the part I want from a workout game right. You’re moving almost all the time, enemies keep coming, and there isn’t a long story scene asking you to stand still while the treadmill keeps going. One dwarf against a planet full of bugs is a simple setup, and it gives you a reason to keep circling instead of checking how much time is left.

The automatic shooting makes the game feel different from an action game where you need to aim every shot, and I like that trade. You steer through the crowd, the dwarf fires, and you’re trying to get into a position where the guns can do useful work without you taking a bunch of hits. I don’t know that I’d want a game like this to demand careful aiming while I’m moving, so it’s a sensible choice.

Mining is part of the appeal, even though sometimes it means you’re walking through rock instead of running straight toward the next swarm. You collect resources and experience as you go, and upgrades give you decisions about what kind of run you’re building. I think that works well because the choices are short breaks in the action, not a long menu session where you forget what your feet are doing.

There’s a nice little push and pull between getting what you came for and getting out alive, and that makes the mission objectives feel like more than a reason to point at another group of bugs. You can get greedy and keep gathering, or decide you’ve had enough of the swarm and focus on getting through the stage. I’m probably too willing to keep picking things up when I should be moving on. That’s true in games too.

The runs can get busy, and I think that’s where the game earns its place as an action game rather than just something to have on beside a workout. You’re watching the bugs, looking for space, and trying to keep your dwarf pointed toward a useful route. When the screen fills up, I’d expect the pressure to feel exciting, but also to make me pay less attention to anything else. It gets loud on screen.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The controls sound like a good fit for moving because you don’t have to keep lining up shots, and the game has full controller support. I’d set the controller somewhere secure, keep the workout at a pace where I can steer without gripping it like I’m trying to hang on during a bus turn, and give the game a try before making it a hard run.

The movement still matters a lot, though. You’re not just walking forward while the dwarf handles everything, because avoiding a crowd and finding a path through the terrain are the decisions that keep a run from going badly. I think a steady walk or an easy ride would leave me enough attention to make those calls, while a fast run might turn the controller into a second workout for my thumbs.

A bike could be the easier choice if you want to keep your upper body still while you steer, but I wouldn’t call it automatically better. On a treadmill, the constant movement and short bursts of danger seem like they could fit together nicely, as long as you’re not trying to sprint through a crowded screen. I’d lower the intensity before I tried to play at full speed. There’s plenty happening already.

The upgrade choices are another small pause in the rhythm, and I’d take the time to read them rather than tapping through while distracted. I don’t know how much of the game’s detail I’d catch on a hard workout, especially once the enemies fill the screen, so I’d start with a comfortable pace and see whether I’m still making decisions I understand. That’s a better plan than finding out halfway through that I’ve chosen upgrades by color.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum graphics card is a GTX 1050, so this isn’t a game I’d count on running well on integrated graphics or a very old office computer. I’d check the graphics card first, and the rest of the requirements don’t look especially demanding.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor is an easy game to recommend for a steady workout, especially if you like action that keeps you moving without demanding constant aiming. I’d skip the hardest exercise pace until you know how much attention the swarms take, but this one looks like a good way to make a walk less quiet.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
