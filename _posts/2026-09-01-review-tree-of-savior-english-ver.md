A long MMO can work on a treadmill, but Tree of Savior has enough quest text and menu time that I’d treat it as a steady walking game, not something to throw on during a hard run. The colorful world is a nice change from gray fantasy towns, and I’d be happy to wander around in it.

## Gameplay

The class system gives you a lot to think about, which is fun when you’re sitting still and a little less fun when the belt is moving. You choose a character path, build up skills, and get into fights with groups of enemies, so there’s usually something to adjust or try. I like having room to shape a character, but I’d probably spend more time comparing skills than I’d want to while jogging.

Questing is a big part of the pace, and that’s where I think this game loses some of its workout energy. You move through the world, talk to characters, read what they need, then go fight or explore, and repeat. That gives you a clear thing to do, but it also means stretches where you’re mostly navigating and checking text instead of reacting. I don’t know that I’d want to keep one eye on a screen full of dialogue while trying to stay at a comfortable pace.

The fights should help keep your attention, especially when you run into tougher enemies or a boss, because then you have more reason to watch what’s happening and use your skills at the right time. Regular enemy groups sound like the more relaxed part of the loop, and I’d probably enjoy those on an easy walk. A boss fight on a treadmill is another matter. I’d rather not be trying to read an attack and remember which skill is on which button while my feet are doing their own thing.

There’s also the usual MMO business of gear, menus, and figuring out what to improve next. That’s appealing if you like planning out a character, but it can break up the movement in a way that a simpler action game doesn’t. I know I’d tell myself I was just checking one thing, then discover I was standing still in a menu thinking about equipment. This is still a game, apparently.

The fairy tale look and the search for the goddesses give the world a clear identity, and I think that helps the slower stretches feel like they’re part of a place rather than a row of identical corridors. I’m not going to pretend the visual style fixes the questing pace, though. If you like wandering around an MMO world and slowly building a character, there’s a lot here to enjoy. If you want constant action, you may end up looking for the next fight while an NPC is still talking.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d set up the gamepad controls before stepping onto the treadmill, because this is not the kind of game where I’d want to reach for a keyboard whenever a fight gets busy. Tree of Savior supports gamepad controls, which makes it much more workable for exercise, but there are still plenty of skills and menus to manage. I’d take a little time to make sure the buttons I need most are easy to reach.

Walking is where I think it fits best. You can move through the world and handle ordinary encounters at a pace where a small delay in your reaction doesn’t turn everything into a mess, and you can ease off when you need to read a quest or change equipment. On a bike, I’d expect the same kind of stop and start, just without having to worry about my feet keeping time with the belt.

I’d avoid making a hard workout out of boss fights or anything where you’re expected to react quickly, especially if you’re still learning the controls. I don’t know how much a given encounter will demand from you, so I’d start with ordinary questing and see how it feels before taking on tougher content while moving. There’s no shame in stopping to sort out a menu. The menu will still be there.

Multiplayer makes the pace less predictable, since other players and group activities can ask for more attention than wandering around alone. I’d stick to solo questing or low pressure grinding when exercising, and save activities that need careful coordination for when I’m sitting down. That way you can keep moving without feeling like you have to choose between your character and your balance.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum asks for an older dedicated graphics card, so you don’t need a recent gaming computer just to get started. I’d check the graphics card first, since the game still asks for 8 GB of memory and a broadband connection. The old card requirement is pretty manageable.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Tree of Savior makes sense for you if you want a colorful MMO to explore at a walking pace and enjoy building a character over time. It’s less suited to a fast run or a workout where you don’t want to pause and read, but I’d give it a try if the slower questing sounds relaxing.
