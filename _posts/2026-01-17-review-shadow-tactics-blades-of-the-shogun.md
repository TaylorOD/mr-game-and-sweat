A treadmill workout with Shadow Tactics means you’ll spend a lot of time watching guards and waiting for the right moment, which is either a nice break from button mashing or a very long way to walk for one distracted samurai. I think it works better when you’re ready to slow down and think than when you’re trying to keep a steady pace.

## Gameplay

This is a good stealth game, and I like that it asks you to solve a little problem instead of just reacting to the next thing that moves. You have five specialists, and their different skills give you options for getting past guards, distracting them, or taking them out. The fun is figuring out what fits the situation, then watching the plan work. Or noticing one guard you forgot about and watching it not work.

The pace is deliberately careful. You watch patrols, check who can see what, and wait for a gap, so a mission can turn into a string of small decisions rather than one long action scene. I think that’s compelling when I’m paying attention, but it also means the game can go quiet while you study the area. On a workout, that’s a chance to breathe. It’s also a chance to wonder if that guard has been walking the same six steps for the last ten minutes.

Each character’s abilities make the group feel useful in different ways, and swapping between them keeps the puzzle from being the exact same one all the way through. I’m especially into games where a plan can involve more than one person, because it’s satisfying to see a few separate actions line up. I’d probably still get too focused on making every part perfect, which is a lot of energy to spend avoiding one person who is standing near a doorway.

You can pause to plan and give commands, which makes the action easier to read than a game that expects you to perform every move at once. But there’s plenty to learn, and some situations reward careful timing and precise movement. I don’t know that I’d want this as my only workout game if I needed constant action, but if I’m fine with stretches of quiet attention, it gives me something interesting to think about.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The biggest adjustment is accepting that your workout might have a stop and start rhythm, because the game often asks you to look around, make a plan, and then carry it out. On a bike, I’d expect that slower pace to feel fairly manageable. On a treadmill, I’d keep the speed comfortable and treat the planning moments as a chance to settle into the belt rather than trying to push the pace through a tricky section.

Full controller support helps, and pausing to plan means you don’t have to do every action in a hurry. Still, selecting characters, moving them carefully, and timing actions around patrols takes more precise input than a game where you can just keep moving forward. I’d probably use a lower workout intensity and save the more involved missions for when I’m fresh, because a guard noticing you at the wrong moment is annoying even when you’re sitting down.

There’s a lot of watching and waiting, but that isn’t automatically bad for exercise. The game doesn’t need you to aim quickly at a target every few seconds, and I think that makes it less stressful to play while moving than an action game with constant fights. It does ask you to focus, though, and I wouldn’t expect to zone out and keep a perfect pace. That would be asking a lot of both of us.

I’d take the missions one section at a time, pause whenever I need to line things up, and avoid turning the treadmill up because I’m impatient to get through a patrol. You can make progress without playing like you’re racing the game. The guards are already doing enough walking.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimums point to an older computer with a dedicated graphics card, not a machine that needs to be built around the latest games. I’d check the graphics card first, since the stated minimum is an older Nvidia or AMD model with 1 GB of video memory, and this shouldn’t require a new gaming PC.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Shadow Tactics is for you if you like careful stealth and don’t mind pausing your workout rhythm to think through a plan. I’d skip it if you need constant action to keep your legs moving.
