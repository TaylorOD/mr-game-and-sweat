Minion Masters is a strong workout game for keeping your mind busy: you are constantly choosing cards and reacting to an opponent, with no long story scenes to interrupt the action. That same live competition makes it a risky treadmill pick, though, since you cannot stop a match when your footing or focus needs attention.

## Gameplay

Minion Masters mixes card battles with tower defense. You build a deck, choose a Master, then play cards during online matches to send minions, spells, and other effects into battle. Your goal is to break through the enemy side and defeat their Master. The basic idea is easy to grasp, but there is a lot to think about once both players start throwing units onto the field.

A key part of the match is controlling the bridges. Minions that hold them help you earn mana faster, which gives you more chances to play cards. That creates a useful tug of war. You can spend mana to push toward the enemy, or try to take control of the bridges and build an advantage for later. A cheap unit can be useful for contesting space, while a stronger card may punish an opponent who has spent too much mana elsewhere.

Deck building gives the game plenty of room for different approaches. The store page lists more than 200 cards, all free to collect, and their unique mechanics give you reasons to try new combinations. Your Master and card choices can support a steady push, defensive play, or a plan built around particular units and spells. In 2v2, you can bring a friend and coordinate your pressure, though you still need to keep track of what the other team is doing.

Matches are quick to get moving, with little downtime once the battle begins. You are watching the board, managing mana, and deciding where the next card will do the most good. That makes Minion Masters a good distraction from exercise in the sense that your attention stays occupied. It is not a quiet game for zoning out and letting your legs do all the work. You will be making decisions almost constantly, but the clear card based structure keeps those decisions readable.

The downside is that the action is happening in real time against other players. A poor placement or a late response can give the other side a big opening, and there is no pausing a live match to catch your breath or sort out your balance. Still, as a strategy game, it avoids the long cut scenes and slow stretches that can make other games awkward during a workout.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Minion Masters has full controller support, which is a big help when you are exercising. You can play without reaching for a keyboard and mouse, and the game’s card selection and placement are a more natural fit for a controller than a game that expects precise aiming at moving targets.

But controller support does not make the match forgiving. You are placing cards while an opponent is actively making their own moves, and timing can matter a lot. The game also does not give you a pause button for online battles. On a stationary bike, where your hands and balance are steady, that is manageable if you keep the workout at a pace that lets you focus. On a treadmill, the combination of running, controller inputs, and reacting to a live opponent is a much tougher ask. One hand twitch is not usually a disaster in a casual game. Here, it can mean losing a bridge or wasting a card at a bad moment.

I would play it at a relaxed walking pace or on a bike rather than trying to run hard through matches. If your workout needs frequent adjustments, save the serious ranked play for when you are off the machine.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest by current standards: an Intel Core i3 at 2.4 GHz or equivalent, 4 GB of memory, and a GeForce GT 240 or equivalent with at least 512 MB of video memory. It also asks for DirectX 10, a broadband internet connection, and 6 GB of storage.

The GT 240 is an old graphics card, so this is not a game that calls for a recent high end gaming computer. The online connection matters more than having a powerful graphics card, since matches are played against other people.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Minion Masters is a solid fit if you want an active strategy game that gives you things to think about from the start of a match to the finish. Deck building, bridge control, and quick card choices give it plenty of depth without burying you in reading or long scenes. The free to play setup also makes it easy to try, and the large card pool gives you room to experiment.

For exercise, the main question is how much you need to move. A bike or easy treadmill walk gives you a better chance to handle the real time matches without turning every misplaced card into a balance test. It is not a relaxed game to play at a hard running pace, but with a steady workout and a controller, it can keep your brain occupied while your legs keep going.
