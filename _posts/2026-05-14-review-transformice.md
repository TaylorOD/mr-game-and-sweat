The mice are all trying to get cheese at once, which sounds like a nice distraction from a treadmill until the Shaman starts making a route and you need to land a jump with everyone else moving. I’d keep this to an easy walk, because Transformice looks like it wants your hands and your attention at the same time.

## Gameplay

I like the basic idea more than I expected to. Dozens of mice trying to reach the cheese gives every level a little shared panic, and the Shaman might help everyone get there or make the whole attempt harder. That’s a funny amount of responsibility to hand somebody in a hat.

The physics are a big part of why I think the game could stay interesting, since a crowded platforming level with lots of mice is already hard to read and loose physics make it less predictable. You’re not just tracing the same jump over and over. At least, that’s the hope.

The Shaman part sounds like the most interesting wrinkle, but also the part I’d be least comfortable doing while exercising. Helping other mice means thinking about what they need, not only getting yourself to the cheese, and I’d probably get so focused on trying to be useful that my pace would turn into a suggestion. I don’t know whether I’d be a helpful Shaman. I would try.

Millions of levels is a lot, and I like that the game has room to keep surprising you, but that number also makes it hard to know what kind of session you’re signing up for. Some levels may be a quick dash, while others ask you to pay more attention to the route and the Shaman’s work. I’d give it a try when you want a busy little game, not when you’re hoping to zone out.

The skill trees give you something beyond the next cheese run to think about, and the hats are clearly an important reason to keep showing up even if I can’t pretend they make me better at jumping. I’m glad the game knows that dressing up a mouse is a perfectly reasonable use of your time. It’s still a game about cheese, so the priorities are clear.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The quick movement is the part that makes me hesitate. A platformer asks you to judge jumps and react when the level gets crowded, and Transformice adds other mice moving around you plus a Shaman whose choices can change how you reach the cheese. I’d be paying more attention to the screen than I want to during a harder run.

I’d play at a gentle walking pace, or on a bike where a sudden jump doesn’t change my footing, and I’d keep the workout easy enough that I can look at the game without wobbling around. I don’t know how forgiving the levels feel when your timing is a little off, but the hectic physics don’t make me think “great, let’s sprint.” That’s probably enough information.

I also wouldn’t count on controller play here. The game’s movement and Shaman work are built around direct inputs and mouse placement, so I’d expect to be using a keyboard and mouse unless you confirm a setup that works for you. That’s awkward on a treadmill, where your hands are already doing a job, and the online crowd means you can’t treat every mistake like a private practice run.

If you can choose how much responsibility you take on, I’d avoid trying to manage the Shaman role while moving fast. Getting cheese is one thing, but setting up a path for a group while the level is in motion is a lot to ask from someone whose attention is also on their feet. On a slow bike, that may feel manageable. On a hard run, I’d skip it.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are very modest, so an older computer should be enough to get started. I’d check that the game runs comfortably on your machine, but you don’t need a powerful graphics card for this one.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Transformice sounds fun if you want a busy multiplayer platformer and can keep your workout easy while playing. I’d pass on it for intense treadmill sessions, but a relaxed walk or bike ride is a reasonable place to give it a try.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
