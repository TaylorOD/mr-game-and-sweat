I’d take a steady walk through Cyrodiil before I tried running through it, because Oblivion is great at giving you something interesting to do and equally happy to leave you reading menus for a while. The world is big, and your legs may have to be patient about it.

## Gameplay

The best thing about Oblivion is that wandering off the road can be the point, and I think that works especially well when you’re exercising because the next distraction is usually somewhere nearby. You can head toward a quest, notice a ruin or a cave along the way, and suddenly the plan has changed without a big explanation stopping everything. I like games that let you set your own pace, and this one is generous about that.

The quests give you a reason to keep moving, but there’s a lot of dialogue and reading between the parts where you’re exploring or fighting. You can follow a marker and get back to doing something, but talking to people is part of the deal, and some stretches feel like being asked to pause your workout so a town can explain its local situation. The people do have a lot to say.

Combat can pull your attention back in a hurry. Melee, blocking, magic, and bows all ask for a different kind of timing or aim, and I’d expect a fight to feel less relaxed than walking around looking for the next cave. The difficulty setting gives you a way to make combat less of a chore if you’re not enjoying how hard an enemy hits, and I’d use that before I let a bandit ruin a perfectly decent run.

There are also stretches where you’re sorting through equipment, spells, and quests, and I don’t know that I’d want to stop every few minutes to inspect a new pair of boots. It’s a big role playing game, so some time spent deciding what to carry comes with the territory, but it does make the game a little stop and start. Still, you can ignore a lot of side content and go wherever you want, which keeps the experience from feeling like a long list of chores.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Walking between places seems like the natural workout pace here, because the game often gives you room to look around and steer without reacting to an enemy every few seconds. I’d use a controller and keep the difficulty comfortable, then let the quest marker do some of the navigation work. Getting lost in the woods can be fun, but it’s not always the kind of surprise your treadmill needs.

A bike could work nicely for the stretches where you’re traveling, checking the map, or just taking in the landscape, and the game’s slower pace gives you time to settle into it. On a treadmill, I’d be more careful once combat starts, since movement and aiming can get a little awkward when your hands aren’t steady. I wouldn’t want to discover that a wolf is behind me at the exact moment I’m trying to turn around.

The controller makes a big difference, and I’d expect the basic movement and menus to be manageable without reaching for a keyboard. But I’d avoid trying to do precise bow shots or complicated inventory decisions while moving quickly, because there’s no special prize for making every part of the game harder for yourself. I’d stop or slow down before sorting through gear. It’s a lot of little buttons.

I also think it’s worth treating the game like a place to spend time rather than a race to finish quests. You can explore, ride between destinations, or take a break from the main story when you feel like wandering, and that flexibility is useful when your workout pace changes. The fights and menus keep it from being a completely hands off game, though, so I’d keep the intensity low enough that I can still pay attention to what’s happening. There’s a lot happening.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This needs a fairly strong gaming computer, not an older office machine you happen to have lying around. I’d check the graphics card first, since the minimum asks for a Radeon RX 5700 or GeForce 1070 Ti, and you’ll also need an SSD with plenty of space. The 125 GB install is going to take up room even before you’ve found a single cave.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Oblivion Remastered is a good choice if you want a big fantasy world to explore at your own pace and don’t mind dialogue, menus, and occasional fights interrupting the steady movement. I’d play it on an easy walk or relaxed bike ride, and I’d skip it for workouts where you need to keep your hands and attention on the exercise.

### Overall Score: 1.67 out of 3
