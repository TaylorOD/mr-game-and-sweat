Save Alan Wake for a walk or an easy ride, because the flashlight combat asks you to aim while things are coming at you, and I wouldn’t want to do that at a full run. The story and the spooky little town of Bright Falls make it more absorbing than a game I’d put on just to fill workout time.

## Gameplay

I think the best thing about Alan Wake is that its action and story keep interrupting each other in a way that mostly works. You’re following a mystery about a Dark Presence stalking Bright Falls, then you’re shining a flashlight at something awful until it can be shot, then you’re back to wondering what’s going on. It keeps your attention. Sometimes it keeps it while you’re supposed to be exercising, too.

The flashlight combat is the part that makes this a game rather than a spooky audiobook. You aim the beam at enemies to burn away the darkness protecting them, then shoot, and I like that you have to make a little space and get your bearings instead of just holding down a button. But you’re also doing that while enemies move toward you, so it can turn into a tense stretch of aiming, dodging, and trying to remember which direction you were going. My treadmill belt would be getting very little attention.

There are manuscript pages to find, and I think they’re a nice fit for a game about slowly working out what happened. They add context and make me want to look around, but they also encourage poking around when I might rather keep moving. I’d grab them when they’re on the way and leave the rest alone. I don’t need to turn a workout into a search for every scrap of paper.

The pacing has some slow stretches, especially when the story is taking over and you’re watching instead of doing much. I don’t mind that as much here as I would in an action game that keeps promising constant fighting, because the quieter parts help build the uneasy feeling. Still, you’ll have moments where you’re mostly following the plot, and that’s not going to be everyone’s idea of a useful treadmill distraction. Sometimes it’s more television than exercise game.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Running hard and aiming at moving enemies sounds like a bad arrangement, and I’d keep this to walking unless you’re unusually steady on your feet. There are full controller support and straightforward controls, which help, but the combat still asks you to point the flashlight carefully and react when enemies close in. A controller doesn’t stop your hands from bouncing around.

A bike seems like the easier place to handle the action, since you can sit still and make small corrections with the sticks while the game gets dramatic around you. I’d still keep the resistance comfortable, because when an enemy is getting close, the natural response is to stare at the screen and forget you’re pedaling. That’s a pretty easy game to get pulled into.

The flashlight and shooting make the stressful moments feel like they need your attention, but I don’t think every part of the game has to be played with the same level of focus. I’d pause during a cut scene, take the pressure off during exploration, and save harder combat sections for a slower pace. I don’t know how much story you’d want to follow while exercising, but the game gives you enough quieter stretches to catch your breath.

I’d also play on Easy if the combat starts asking too much of you. That’s not me trying to prove anything to a horror game, and it would let you spend more time following the story and less time repeating a fight because your aim drifted while you were moving. If you want a workout where your attention stays on your legs, this isn’t it.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to a fairly old computer with a dedicated graphics card, not a machine that needs to be built for the newest games. I’d check the graphics card first, since it needs DirectX 10 support and its own memory, and I wouldn’t assume every basic integrated chip will be comfortable running it.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Alan Wake is a good choice for a steady bike ride or a slow treadmill walk if you want a story to pull you along and don’t mind pausing when the action gets busy. I’d skip it for fast running or a workout where you want to look away from the screen.

### Overall Score: 2.33 out of 3
