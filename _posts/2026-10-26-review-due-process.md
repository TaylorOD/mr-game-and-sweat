The planning phase in Due Process sounds like a nice way to ease into a workout, but the round starts and suddenly you’re in a team fight where the other people aren’t waiting for your legs to catch up. I’d save it for when you’re sitting down.

## Gameplay

I like that the planning part has a real job to do. You’re not just looking at a map until the shooting starts, you’re trying to agree on a way into a building or a way to hold it, and then you have to see if that plan survives contact with the other team. That gives each round a little shape, which I think is more interesting than running at the same objective with no thought behind it.

The weekly procedural maps are a smart fit for a game about planning, because learning one layout forever would make the whole tactical part feel like memorizing a route to the grocery store. New layouts keep the decisions from getting too comfortable. I’d be a little worried about being the person who confidently suggests the wrong hallway, but that’s a team activity, technically.

When the shooting starts, though, this is still a tactical PvP shooter, and that means every angle and every sound can matter. You’re making choices with other players, and a plan that seemed sensible a moment ago can get messy fast when another team reacts differently than you expected. I like that tension in a game, but it asks for your attention in a way that makes jogging and playing feel like two competing jobs.

I don’t know that the planning phase would give me much of a break, either, because I’d probably be trying to listen, look at the map, and remember what we just agreed to do. The nice part is that the game doesn’t sound like it’s padding things out with a long story or a stack of cut scenes. The less nice part is that the waiting is still thinking, and thinking can be tiring in its own way.

I’d play this when I wanted a team tactics game, not when I wanted something easy to half pay attention to. Good communication seems central, and I can see that being really cool when everyone’s on the same page, but it also means a quiet or distracted player can make the round harder for the whole group. I don’t know, I’d want to be fully there for it.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The round structure may look like a workout friendly setup, since there’s a planning period before the action, but I wouldn’t count on that being a reliable place to catch your breath. You’re still coordinating with a team, and the start of a round can turn into a fast aiming situation without much warning. The treadmill is going to keep moving either way.

A bike seems a little more manageable for this, mostly because you can keep your upper body steadier while aiming, but it’s not an easy game to play loosely. You’re trying to track opponents and make quick decisions, and if you’re bouncing while doing that, I’d expect to miss things. Maybe I’d turn the resistance down and treat the ride as a gentle one, rather than trying to make every match count as a hard interval.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information here, so I’d check before buying it for this setup. Even with a controller, tactical aiming and communicating with your team are going to take focus, and I’d be careful about assuming a controller makes the game comfortable to play while moving. A controller can help with keeping your hands in one place. It can’t tell the other team to stop flanking.

I’d skip this during a run where you need to keep your attention on pace or form, and I wouldn’t queue up expecting to stop whenever you want. It’s competitive multiplayer, so the match keeps moving whether you’re ready for the next push or still trying to get your feet settled. If you want something that can sit quietly in the background while you exercise, this probably isn’t it.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimums point to an older gaming PC rather than a new high end machine, and the GTX 760 or RX 570 class graphics requirement is the first thing I’d check. You’ll also need a broadband connection, which makes sense for a game built around online team matches. The SSD recommendation is worth considering, but your graphics card matters more.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Due Process sounds like a thoughtful tactical shooter for someone who wants to focus on a team and a plan, and I’d tell you to play it at a desk. For treadmill or bike gaming, I’d pick something that lets you look away without making your whole team regret it.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
