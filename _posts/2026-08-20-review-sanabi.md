That prosthetic arm is going to ask for more precision than I want to give it while running hard, so I’d start SANABI at an easy pace or on a bike. The movement looks like a great way to keep your attention, but it also looks like the kind of game where one distracted thumb sends you into a wall.

## Gameplay

The arm is the reason I’d keep playing. You use it to swing across gaps and skyscrapers, zip through bullets and traps, and get into fights, so moving through the world has a nice feeling of momentum instead of just being a series of jumps. I think that’s a great fit for exercise because you’re usually doing something, and the next grapple point gives your eyes somewhere to go besides the treadmill display.

It does ask you to be deliberate, though. The fun is in connecting movements, and the difference between a clean swing and a missed one can come down to timing. I’d enjoy that on a couch, and I’d probably still enjoy it on a treadmill, but I don’t think I’d be looking at anything else while trying to get through a tricky section. My television can wait.

The action has a good, direct shape to it. You’re a retired veteran with a prosthetic arm, fighting powerful enemies in a dystopian city, and I like that the game’s signature movement is also what makes its fights feel different from a standard run and jump action game. It isn’t asking you to memorize a complicated set of systems before you can have fun. You get to move, react, and keep moving. That makes sense to me.

There’s a story here, too, and it means the game isn’t constant motion from one end to the other. I don’t know how much of the story will pull you in, but the slower stretches are the tradeoff for having a reason to care about the city and the person moving through it. They’d be fine during an easy workout, though I’d be less pleased if I’d planned to keep my pace up through a long stretch of dialogue. You can’t swing through every conversation.

The pixel art gives the action a clear look without making me feel like I need to pick out tiny details while I’m moving. I’m not someone who needs every game to be visually elaborate, and here I’d rather have a clean view of the next gap than a lot of things competing for attention. SANABI seems to know what it wants you to watch. Usually it’s the place you’re about to miss.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d play with a controller and keep the workout easy enough that I can make small, deliberate inputs. The arm gives you a lot of movement, but that doesn’t mean the game is forgiving about where you send yourself, and quick grapples or dodges while your hands are bouncing could turn a fun run into a short lesson about coordination. I don’t know that I’d want to tackle the hardest sections while pushing my pace.

On a bike, I think the same focus issue is there, but the steadier hand position would make the precise movement a little easier to manage. That doesn’t make the game automatic, because you still need to react to traps and attacks, and boss fights are probably where I’d stop pedaling for a moment if I needed to concentrate. I can picture getting into a fight and forgetting I was supposed to be exercising. That’s not ideal, but at least it’s a game doing its job.

For the treadmill, I’d start with a slower pace and treat tricky platforming as a reason to ease off, not as a challenge to prove I can keep up with the belt. I’d also give myself permission to pause between sections if I was getting overloaded, rather than repeatedly missing a grapple because I was trying to do two precise things at once. This isn’t a game I’d put on during an all out run.

The controls are suited to a controller, and the action keeps giving you a reason to use it, which is a big plus here. I’d skip the idea of playing it while distracted or at a pace where my hands are moving around too much, because SANABI’s movement is the part I’m here for and it needs attention. On an easy workout, that’s a fair deal.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest, so I’d expect an older computer with a basic graphics card to have a reasonable shot at running it. I’d check that the machine meets the listed graphics requirement first, but this doesn’t look like a game that needs a new gaming PC just to swing through a city.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

SANABI is an easy recommendation for someone who wants a fast action platformer and is willing to keep their workout at a pace where precise controller inputs are manageable. If you need a game that lets you zone out while you pedal or run, I’d choose something less demanding.
