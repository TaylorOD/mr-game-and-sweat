I wouldn’t put a headset on and try to run through a battle royale, and STAND OUT VR makes that sound like an especially bad plan because you’re trying to survive while moving around a huge island. It could be a fun game to play before or after your workout, but I’d keep the moving belt out of it.

## Gameplay

The part I like about the premise is that it gives you several ways to deal with a fight, and they don’t all sound like variations on aiming a weapon. You can build, drive, parkour or fly, which should keep the match from feeling like you’re standing in one spot waiting for somebody to appear. I think that variety is a real point in its favor, even if I’d probably be better at the driving part than the flying part.

A battle royale also has a lot of dead air built into the format, at least compared with a match where you’re always being handed the next thing to do. You’re on a huge island, other players are trying to be the last one alive, and sometimes the important choice might be whether to go looking for trouble or stay out of it. I’d enjoy having that choice, but I can also picture spending a stretch of the match wandering around with a weapon and wondering if everybody else had a meeting I missed.

The range of activities sounds a little silly in a way I mean positively. You can eat, too, which is one of those details that makes me curious about what the game feels like minute to minute, but I don’t know how much that changes the pace or whether it’s mostly a small extra. Either way, a game that lets you drive around and then suddenly asks you to care about lunch is doing something different.

The hard part is that all those options still sit inside a competition where you’re trying not to be eliminated. That pressure can make the freedom fun, because you can choose how you approach the island, but it also makes every bad decision matter more than it would in a low stakes game. I’d want to play it when I’m ready to pay attention, not when I’m looking for something gentle to fill the middle of an easy workout.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

For a moving treadmill, this is a no from me. VR already asks you to keep track of where your body is while your eyes are seeing a different space, and then this game adds fighting, driving and parkour, which sounds like a lot to coordinate while your feet are moving on a belt. I’d rather not turn the edge of the treadmill into an opponent.

The bike isn’t an obvious workaround, either. You could sit still on a stationary bike, but you’d still be wearing a headset and trying to react to other players, and the game’s standing or room scale VR support points toward using your body and space rather than settling into a relaxed cycling position. Maybe there’s a comfortable way to set that up, but I wouldn’t count on it as an exercise game.

I also wouldn’t go into it assuming a regular gamepad will solve the problem. The store details confirm VR support, but they don’t tell me enough to promise a standard controller setup, and in a competitive game I’d want the controls to feel natural before I added exercise. I can’t tell you exactly how every interaction is handled, so I’d check the control setup before buying it.

Even apart from the headset, a battle royale is not very forgiving of distractions. You can’t pause the other players while you get your rhythm back, and having to quickly respond to someone else is exactly when I’d stop paying attention to my cadence. That sounds like a game I’d play standing in a clear space, not one I’d put on while trying to keep moving.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum graphics card is a GTX 960, so this isn’t asking for a new high end gaming computer, but it does need a dedicated card rather than basic integrated graphics. I’d check that your PC can run VR smoothly before worrying about anything else, because a stuttery headset experience sounds miserable even when you’re sitting still.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

STAND OUT VR sounds like a lively battle royale with more ways to play than just running toward the next fight, and I’d give it a try if you want a VR game to play standing in a safe space. For treadmill or bike workouts, though, I’d pick something that doesn’t ask you to fight other people while your view is inside a headset.
