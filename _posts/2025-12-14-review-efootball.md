I’d take eFootball to an easy treadmill pace or a steady bike ride, and play against the AI before I let an online match turn my workout into a very serious meeting about a misplaced pass. Soccer gives you something to watch almost constantly, which helps, but you still need your hands and attention.

## Gameplay

The appeal is pretty straightforward: you’re trying to move the ball up the field, find space, and stop the other team doing the same, and that gives your brain a steady little job while your legs do theirs. I think that works well for exercise because there’s less time spent watching a story unfold than in a lot of games, and a match has a natural end. You can get into the rhythm of watching the field and making a choice. Then the other team takes the ball and you reconsider your choices.

Passing and defending are where I’d expect the game to keep me engaged. You’re not just holding one button and waiting for a goal, because choosing who gets the ball and when to pressure the other side both matter. I like games that give you something to think about without asking you to read paragraphs while you jog, and this seems to land on the active side of that line. There’s a lot happening, though, so I’d start against the AI rather than learn the game while somebody online is trying to win.

The online play is the part that makes eFootball feel like more than a soccer game to have on in the background, since you can play with people around the world. That’s a fun idea, and other people make matches hard to predict, but I think it also makes the game less forgiving as workout company. When an opponent breaks through, you have to react right then. The treadmill is not going to provide a timeout because you were trying to decide which defender to switch to.

I’d also be aware of the menus and team setup around the matches. That kind of preparation can be enjoyable if you like thinking about how you want to play, but it’s not exercise time, and I wouldn’t count it as the part that keeps me moving. I don’t know how much of that tinkering you’ll want to do, but I’d keep it for before or after a workout. Standing on a moving belt to adjust a lineup sounds like a very specific way to forget why you started walking.

For the actual soccer, I think eFootball has a good workout shape because there’s pressure, movement, and a clear thing to focus on, without a long story stopping everything. I’d play the AI matches when I wanted to keep a relaxed pace and save online competition for when I could give it more attention. That split makes sense to me.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d use a controller and keep the workout gentle enough that my hands can still make deliberate inputs. Passing, changing players, and aiming a shot all ask for some coordination, and defending can turn into a scramble when the ball moves quickly. It’s not a game where you can fully zone out and pedal through whatever happens. You’ll be watching the field.

On a treadmill, I’d begin at a walk rather than assume I can run and play well at the same time. The camera is following a match and there are players moving in different directions, so if my stride is bouncing my hands around, I’d expect my passing and defending to get less precise. I’d rather lower the speed than turn every loose ball into a small emergency.

A stationary bike seems easier to pair with the quicker parts, since your upper body can stay steadier while you work the pedals. Still, online matches ask you to react to another person, and I wouldn’t pick one if I wanted a completely relaxed workout where I can look away whenever I feel like it. Offline against the AI gives you a more sensible place to start, and I’d change the difficulty down if I found myself concentrating so hard that I stopped noticing my legs.

The controller support is there, which is important, but having a controller doesn’t make every game comfortable while you’re moving. I’d keep the session to a pace where I can focus on the match and keep my balance, and I’d stop playing online if the competition was making me tense up. There’s no reason to defend a corner kick like it’s my job.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimum is an old dedicated graphics card, so you don’t need a recent high end gaming PC, though I wouldn’t assume integrated graphics will be enough. I’d check that your computer meets the Windows requirement and has room for a 70 GB install, because that’s a lot of storage for a game about kicking a ball around.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

eFootball makes sense for you if you want soccer to keep your attention during a steady workout, especially against the AI with the pace turned down. I’d skip online matches while running hard, but it’s free to play, so you can give it a try and see how your hands handle the passing.
