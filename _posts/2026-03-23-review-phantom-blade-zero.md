I’d save Phantom Blade Zero for a workout where you’re ready to pay attention, because a fast wuxia fight and a moving treadmill are asking for some of the same focus. On a bike, I’d be more comfortable giving it a try, but I wouldn’t expect to coast through the fights.

## Gameplay

The action is what makes this sound appealing to me, and also what makes me hesitate about exercising with it. Phantom Blade Zero puts you in the role of Soul, a warrior with sixty six days left to live, fighting assassins while trying to unravel a world ending conspiracy. That’s a fun, direct setup. It gives the fighting some urgency without asking you to memorize a history book first.

I like the wuxia angle because it gives the game a clear identity beyond “another person with a sword.” Still, the description promises exhilarating action, and I think that probably means the fights will ask more from you than holding one button and wandering forward. I’d want the combat to feel good enough that paying attention is a treat, not a second shift.

The RPG side makes me a little less certain about how smoothly it fits into a workout. I don’t know how much time the finished game spends on story or character choices, and that matters here, because stopping to read or think every few minutes can turn a run into a series of awkward pauses. A story about having sixty six days left could be a brisk push forward, or it could be a lot of standing around hearing about the deadline. We’ll see.

What I do like is that the premise gives the action a reason to keep moving. Soul’s got limited time, assassins are after him, and there’s a big conspiracy to sort out, so it sounds like the kind of game that can keep your attention without needing a sports scoreboard flashing at you. I’m less sure it’ll be easy to pick back up mid workout if you’ve taken a break, but at least the situation is easy to remember. Man with sword, not much time.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The first thing I’d change is my expectations. This does not sound like a game where you can let your hands drift around the controller and still do fine, because action built around fights with assassins probably needs you to notice what’s happening and react. I’d try it at a walking pace before taking it into a run. There’s no shame in making the workout a little less ambitious than the sword fighting.

A bike seems more forgiving to me, since you can keep your body steady while your hands do the work, but that doesn’t make the combat automatic. If an enemy forces quick decisions, I’d still want to be somewhere comfortable enough to focus on the screen. I don’t know how forgiving the game will be when you make a mistake, so I wouldn’t start a hard workout by assuming every fight can be handled with one thumb and optimism.

Full controller support is a real plus, and it means you don’t have to invent a keyboard setup while you’re already moving. I’d keep the controls close and simple, and I’d take a break from the belt or pedals for any stretch where the story asks me to sit still and pay attention. That’s not ideal for keeping a workout going, but it beats trying to process a plot while bouncing along and then blaming the plot when I miss something.

I’d also be cautious about treating every part of the game like a workout section. The action may be the reason to play, but an RPG can have slower stretches between fights, and we don’t have enough detail to know how often those happen here. If the game gives you a natural pause, use it to settle your pace or take a breather. I’d rather have a slightly less intense workout than try to win a sword fight while my feet are doing their own thing.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This needs a mid range gaming PC, not an office computer that happens to have a screen attached. I’d check your graphics card and make sure you have an SSD with plenty of space, because the minimum target is 1080p at 30 frames per second with upscaling and the install needs 145 GB. That’s a fairly large corner of your drive.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Phantom Blade Zero sounds worth playing, but I’d choose a steady bike ride or an easy treadmill walk over a workout where you’re trying to push your pace. It’s for you if you want fast fantasy action and don’t mind giving the screen your attention. I’d wait on it if you need a game that lets you zone out.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
