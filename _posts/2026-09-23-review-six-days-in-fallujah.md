I’d save Six Days in Fallujah for a walk where you can keep your attention on the screen, because clearing rooms while your legs are moving sounds like a pretty good way to forget what your feet are doing. It’s deliberate in a way that could make a workout feel focused, but I’d probably start on a bike.

## Gameplay

I think the tension is the point, and it’s also what makes this a strange match for exercise. You’re moving through a realistic depiction of urban combat, watching corners and trying not to rush into a room without thinking. That sounds engaging, and it probably is, but it’s not the kind of action game where you can pay half attention and still have a decent time. My treadmill brain tends to have a limited number of open tabs.

The close quarters give you plenty to react to, because an enemy can be waiting around the next corner or inside the next room, and I like the idea of having to slow down and make choices instead of sprinting toward a marker. I also think that careful pace could make a bike session feel less repetitive. You still have to aim and stay alert, though, so there’s a difference between a tense game and an easy game to play while your whole body is doing something else.

The game is based on true stories from a brutal battle, and that gives the setting a seriousness I’d want it to handle carefully. I wouldn’t treat the subject matter like a theme park ride, and I’d be more interested in whether the missions make you feel the weight of moving through a dangerous place than whether you can clear a building quickly. I don’t know how much that feeling holds up over a longer stretch, but the premise is at least more thoughtful than just putting targets in a room and asking you to shoot them.

It’s Early Access, so I’d go in expecting a game that’s still being developed rather than a completely finished package. That matters here, because a tactical shooter depends on its pacing and how well its encounters work, and those are exactly the things that can feel rough when they’re not quite tuned. I’d be curious to see how it develops. There’s a lot of room for this kind of game to get things right, and a lot of room for it to feel awkward.

I don’t think the slower pace is automatically a flaw. In a game about a dangerous urban battle, stopping to look before entering a room makes sense, and the pressure can keep those pauses from feeling like nothing is happening. But if you want something that fills every second with simple action, this could feel like waiting for your next chance to make a careful decision. I’d be fine with that on a focused session. I wouldn’t pick it when I was already tired.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The aiming is what I’d worry about first, because the game asks you to check angles and react to enemies at close range, and a treadmill adds a steady bounce right when you’d prefer your thumbs to be steady. Full controller support is a real plus, and you can use a controller without having to reach over to a keyboard, but that doesn’t turn precise aiming into a background activity. I’d expect to miss a few shots while my body was doing its own thing.

A stationary bike seems like the easier place to start, mostly because you can keep your upper body steadier while you pedal. I’d keep the resistance comfortable and choose a pace where I can still pay attention, rather than trying to turn every mission into an interval workout. On a treadmill, I’d walk instead of run, and I’d be willing to stop moving for a moment if a room got tense. That’s not a thrilling fitness plan, but it sounds more sensible than trying to do careful aiming while jogging.

I’d also skip this when I wanted a game I could pick up and play without concentrating. The tension comes from paying attention to where you’re moving and what might be around the next corner, so I think checking out mentally would make it less fun, not more relaxing. If you like tactical shooters and you’re happy to keep your workout gentle, it could work. If your plan is a hard run with lots of quick reactions, maybe not.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You’ll need a mid range gaming computer, not an office laptop relying on integrated graphics, and I’d check the graphics card first because the minimum calls for a GTX 1060 or Radeon 580. The processor and memory requirements are also above the bare minimum for an old machine, so this one expects a computer that can handle modern games without needing a pep talk.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This makes more sense for a careful bike session or an easy treadmill walk than for a run. I’d suggest it to someone who likes tactical shooters and wants to stay focused, but you can skip it if your workout game needs forgiving aiming and constant action.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
