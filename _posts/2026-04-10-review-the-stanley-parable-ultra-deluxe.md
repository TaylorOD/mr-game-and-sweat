Walking on a treadmill through an office while a narrator comments on your choices sounds like a strange workout, but The Stanley Parable: Ultra Deluxe is one of the easier games to keep moving through. I’d play it at an easy pace, because the interesting part is listening and deciding which door to take, not reacting quickly.

## Gameplay

The game’s best idea is that it keeps noticing what you do, and sometimes it responds in a way that makes you wonder if you’ve misunderstood the whole situation. You walk through an office, hear the narrator explain what Stanley is supposed to do, and then you can follow along or go somewhere else. I like that the game doesn’t treat every wrong turn like a failure. Sometimes ignoring the instructions is the whole point.

There’s a lot of walking between the moments where something changes, though, and those stretches can feel long if you’re waiting for the next funny or unsettling bit. The narrator is usually enough to keep me interested, but this isn’t a game that constantly asks for your attention, and I think that’s either a relief or a problem depending on how much you want to be entertained every second. Sometimes you’re just walking down another hallway. It’s an office.

The branching paths make it easy to keep exploring without needing to memorize a complicated set of rules. You can follow the narrator, make a different choice, and see how the game reacts, then start over and try another route. I don’t know how many people will want to find every possible ending, because the repetition is part of the joke and also still repetition, but I think trying a handful of different choices is great fun.

Ultra Deluxe adds more material to the original game, including a new content door and a bucket that changes parts of the experience. I think the new material works best when you already understand the game’s sense of humor, since a lot of the fun comes from the way it plays with your expectations. The narrator can be very funny, but he also spends a lot of time talking, and you have to enjoy hearing him talk. There isn’t much to do while he’s making his point, so I’d be fine with taking a break between routes.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

There’s no rush to make quick inputs, so you can keep walking or pedaling while the game takes its time explaining itself. The full controller support helps, and most of what you’re doing is moving through hallways and choosing where to go. I’d set the treadmill to a pace where looking around doesn’t feel like trying to read a street sign from a moving car.

The main thing I’d watch is the camera. You’ll be steering with one stick and looking around with another, and that takes a little attention, especially when you’re trying to line up with a door or follow a hallway. It’s not demanding, but a bouncy run could make those small adjustments annoying. On a bike, I think the steady position makes looking around easier, though you still need to keep your hands on the controller.

A slower workout is probably the right match, since the game gives you plenty of time to listen and decide whether to do what the narrator says. If you’re running hard and want something that keeps your brain busy, this may leave too much room for you to start thinking about your grocery list. I’d skip trying to rush through it and let the narration happen. You’re not being timed.

There’s also nothing here that punishes you for pausing to settle your grip or check your pace. That makes the game forgiving, but it also means your workout can start to feel like a walking tour where the guide has a lot of opinions. I’d probably enjoy that more on an easy session than during a run where I’m already working to keep my balance.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements are modest, and an older computer with a basic dedicated graphics card should be enough to run it. I’d check the graphics hardware first, but this isn’t asking you to build a gaming computer just to visit an office.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

This is a good choice for an easy workout if you like dry humor, strange detours, and games that let you stop and think. I’d pass if you need constant action, but for a relaxed treadmill walk or bike ride, Stanley has plenty to say.
