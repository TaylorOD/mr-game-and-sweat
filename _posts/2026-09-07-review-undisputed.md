If you want a workout game where you spend most of the time reacting instead of reading menus, boxing sounds promising, and Undisputed has enough going on in the ring to keep your attention. I’d start with Fight Now against the computer, not an online match where your opponent gets to decide how much focus your legs deserve.

## Gameplay

The punching is what makes this work for me, because you’re not just swinging constantly and hoping somebody gets tired first. You have to think about timing, defense, and how much energy you’re spending, so even a basic exchange can give your brain a little job while your body is doing the more repetitive one. That’s good for a workout game, though it also means this isn’t something I’d pick when I’m so tired that choosing a punch already feels like a meeting.

There’s a nice balance between watching what the other boxer is doing and trying to make your own opening, and I think that gives each fight more shape than just trading punches until one person falls over. The licensed boxers help make the matchups interesting, but I wouldn’t buy it just to look at the roster. The part you’re actually doing is the reason to play.

Fight Now seems like the easiest place to settle in, since you can get to a fight without asking the game to introduce you to a whole career first. Career mode adds more structure, which is nice if you want a reason to keep coming back, but it also means training and menus between the fights. I don’t know that I’d want all of that when I’m trying to keep a workout moving. Sometimes I just want the bell to ring.

The pacing inside a fight has enough changes in it to stop my attention from wandering, but the thinking doesn’t disappear. You’re managing your stamina and looking for chances to attack, and that can pull you into the match in a way that makes you forget you’re exercising. Which is kind of the goal. It can also make the treadmill feel like an inconvenient place to be standing.

I’d skip online play during a workout. Even if you’re comfortable with the controls, a close match asks for more concentration than I want to promise while my feet are keeping time with a belt. The computer is a much more forgiving partner, and there’s no need to turn every round into an appointment.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The rhythm of a boxing match is useful here, because you get stretches of active play broken up by brief pauses between rounds, rather than a long story scene that leaves you moving along while nothing happens. I’d still choose a slower pace on the treadmill and keep the difficulty comfortable, since trying to dodge and counter at full speed while your hands are moving on a controller sounds like a good way to lose track of both activities.

A controller is the way I’d play it, and the controls ask for more deliberate timing than a game where you can hold one button and keep going. You’re watching for an opening, deciding whether to defend or attack, and trying not to throw away all your stamina at once. That’s manageable on an easy run or a steady bike ride, but I’d expect a tough fight to take more focus than a relaxed workout.

The bike seems a little easier for the close, careful parts, mostly because you’re not also trying to keep your steps even while you react. On a treadmill I’d avoid turning the speed up just because the fight gets exciting. I know that’s easy advice to give from a chair, but a virtual boxer can wait for me to find my pace.

Career mode is probably fine if you want the extra progression, but I’d expect the training and menu stretches to interrupt the workout more than Fight Now does. You can use those pauses to catch your breath or make a small adjustment, though they might also leave you standing there waiting for the next useful thing to happen. That part sounds a bit like being ready to leave the gym and then remembering you have to put the weights away.

I’d stay away from a match that demands all of your attention, especially if you’re already pushing your pace. There’s enough timing involved that you’ll feel it when your inputs get loose, but a computer opponent and a relaxed effort should leave room for both the fight and the workout. I’d keep it easy at first.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You’ll need a fairly recent gaming graphics card, and the required SSD is the part I’d check first if your computer is short on storage. The minimum target is more than an old office laptop is likely to handle comfortably, but it doesn’t call for an especially high end machine either.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Undisputed makes sense for you if you want boxing to occupy your attention during a steady workout, and I’d point you toward Fight Now against the computer. Skip it if you want a game that lets you react without much thought, or if you’re planning to turn every treadmill session into a serious online match.
