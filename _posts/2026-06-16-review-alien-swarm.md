Alien Swarm is the sort of workout game where I’d keep my pace comfortable, because aiming at a crowd of aliens while a team is waiting on me sounds like a good way to forget I’m exercising. The shooting looks like it would keep me involved, but I wouldn’t choose it for a run where I want to pay no attention to the screen.

## Gameplay

The appeal is pretty straightforward, and I mean that in a good way. You’re looking down at a squad of marines, moving through a mission and trying to keep aliens from overwhelming you, so there’s usually something on screen that needs your attention. I think that constant pressure would make the time go by quickly. It also means you’re not going to get much of a breather just because you’re jogging.

The cooperative part is what makes the game sound more interesting than just pointing at aliens until they stop moving. You’re working with other people, and the team has different roles, including a medic and a tech, so I’d expect the group to feel better when everybody has a job. That sounds fun when people are communicating, and maybe a little messy when everyone has their own idea about where the squad should go. Four people all trying to solve the same problem is still four people.

I like that the action isn’t only about firing at whatever gets close. Missions have objectives, and things like cutting through doors or welding them shut give you a reason to look around and think about the route. The quieter moments seem like they’d be short enough to keep the pace from dragging, but I don’t know how much patience I’d have for stopping to coordinate while my feet are going. I’d want the people I’m playing with to be okay with a little silence.

There’s a certain practical satisfaction to taking a mission one problem at a time, especially when the aliens are coming from several directions and the group has to adjust. I think the top down view helps, since you can see more of what’s happening than you would in a shooter where the camera is right behind your character. Still, it’s a lot to track, and I can picture myself staring at the screen so hard that my legs would start feeling like an optional feature.

The free to play part makes it easy to give it a try, and the complete code base being available for free is an unusual extra for a game like this. I wouldn’t treat that as a reason to play it, though. The reason is the co-op shooting, and if you don’t have a group to play with, I’d be less sure it has the same pull. I don’t know whether the team coordination would feel fun or like I accidentally joined a very small meeting.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d put this on a bike before a treadmill, mostly because the aiming asks you to be steady with your hands while you’re moving a character and watching a squad at the same time. On a treadmill, every step adds a little bounce, and that’s not what I want while trying to aim at aliens and avoid losing track of the team. It could still work at a walking pace, but I’d be cautious about running.

I’d plan on using a mouse and keyboard rather than assuming a controller will work well. That’s a lot more hand movement than I want from a workout game, and it leaves me splitting my attention between the screen and the controls. If you already play this way, you might find it natural. I’d probably feel like I was trying to steer a shopping cart with one hand while sorting groceries with the other.

On a bike, I think the steadier position would help, but the actual play still needs aim and quick decisions. I wouldn’t pick the hardest setting for an exercise session, and I’d skip playing with people who expect me to react instantly every time something goes wrong. The game is co-op, so even if you’re personally happy to pause and catch your breath, other players may be keeping the mission moving.

A slower pace and a group that knows you’re exercising would make a difference. I’d tell them up front that I may miss a callout or need a moment to get my hands settled, because pretending I can do everything at once is how I end up doing nothing especially well. This is more suited to an easy ride than a hard run, and even then I’d want to be comfortable with mouse and keyboard controls.

I wouldn’t rule it out as a workout game, because the missions sound busy enough to pull your attention away from the exercise. But the same things that make it engaging, aiming, teamwork and reacting to what’s happening, make it a poor fit when you’re bouncing around. You can give it a try while walking or riding gently. I’d keep the treadmill speed down.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older PC, so I wouldn’t expect this to need a recent graphics card. I’d look at whether the computer has a compatible DirectX 9 graphics card first, but the listed hardware is modest by current standards. It sounds less demanding than the sort of machine you’d need to run a new game at high settings.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Alien Swarm sounds like a fun co-op shooter, and I’d give it a try if you have people to play with and can use mouse and keyboard comfortably. For workouts, I’d save it for an easy bike ride or a slow walk, not a run where your hands are bouncing around.
