A dragon game sounds like a good way to forget you’re jogging, and Century: Age of Ashes probably succeeds at that a little too well. I’d save it for an easy, steady workout, because chasing someone through the sky is more demanding than nudging a character down a hallway.

## Gameplay

The appeal is pretty simple, and I mean that as a compliment: you’re on a dragon, other people are on dragons, and everyone is trying to shoot each other out of the sky. There isn’t a long story or a pile of dialogue asking you to stop and pay attention, so the action should keep your brain occupied while your legs do their thing. I like that it gets to the point. There’s only so much lore I can absorb while remembering to drink water.

The different classes and dragons give you a reason to try a few approaches, which is nice because aerial combat can otherwise turn into a lot of circling and hoping your shots land. I’d expect the class choice to matter more as you learn what each one is good at, and I think that’s appealing if you like getting a little better at a game over time. I also wouldn’t make a hobby out of studying every ability before playing. You can probably pick one that sounds fun and learn the rest by getting chased around.

The modes help keep the matches from feeling like the same dogfight over and over. Carnage is the straightforward team fight, while Gates of Fire puts more emphasis on flying through a route and Spoils of War adds the job of collecting and bringing back gold. I like that those ideas give you different reasons to move through the arena instead of just pointing your dragon at the nearest target. Spoils of War does sound like a lot to keep track of when the other team is also shooting at you, but I suppose that’s the point.

I think this is the kind of action game that can hold your attention without asking you to read much or wait around for a cut scene, and that’s a pretty useful thing when you’re exercising. The trade is that the fun comes from fast fights against other players, so losing can feel less like a quiet game moment and more like being repeatedly reminded that someone else knows where they’re going. I’d probably need a few matches before I stopped flying into trouble on purpose. Probably.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace easy enough that I can still aim and turn without my hands bouncing all over the place. The game has full controller support, which is a real plus, but flying and lining up shots still asks for more precise movement than a game where you mostly steer in one direction. I’d expect the right stick to become the part that gets messy first, especially while I’m trying to keep track of another dragon circling behind me.

The bigger concern is that these are multiplayer arena battles, so you can’t count on pausing when your legs need attention or you’ve drifted into a pace you didn’t mean to pick. A match can get busy at the exact moment you’d like to look down and adjust the treadmill, and I don’t think you should make yourself choose between staying safe and helping your team. That feels like a bad little bargain.

On a bike, the steady motion might make it easier to keep your hands controlled, but I’d still treat the game as something for a relaxed ride rather than intervals. You’re tracking moving targets, aiming, and reacting to attacks, and I don’t know that I’d want to be doing all that while also trying to push hard. A casual match at a pace you can hold seems much more sensible.

I’d start with a controller and see whether the aiming feels manageable before turning the workout up. If there’s an aim sensitivity option, I’d adjust it until small stick movements don’t send the camera sailing past a target, though I wouldn’t spend the whole session in settings. And I’d skip playing when I’m planning a hard run, because the game is already providing plenty of urgency.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimum graphics card is old by current standards, so you don’t need a new high end gaming computer just to try this. I’d check the graphics card and make sure the computer can handle the required Windows version, then remember that this is an online game and needs broadband internet. It should be a much smaller commitment than buying a new dragon.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Century: Age of Ashes sounds like a fun choice for an easy workout if you enjoy quick multiplayer fights and can stay comfortable while aiming. I’d skip it for hard treadmill sessions or any ride where you need to pause often, but for a steady pace, the dragons are a pretty good distraction.
