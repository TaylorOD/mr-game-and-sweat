Planning a burglary sounds like a bad way to keep your heart rate steady, but Thief Simulator has enough quiet watching and careful picking through houses that I think it can work as a low effort workout. I’d keep the pace easy, because getting caught while your thumb is bouncing around sounds like a lot of extra excitement.

## Gameplay

The part I like is that you’re not just rushing in and grabbing everything you can reach. You gather intel, figure out how to get in, steal what you can, and sell it to buy better equipment, so there’s a nice little sense of building toward a more capable thief. I think that’s a better fit for a simulation game than making every minute a chase. You get to plan a bit.

That planning can also slow things down. Watching for an opening and deciding what’s worth taking asks you to stop and think, and I don’t think that’s automatically bad, but it means the game won’t keep your attention in the same way an action game does. Some of the time you’re doing careful work rather than having a big moment. That’s just how stealing goes, I guess.

I don’t know how much of the intel gathering will feel useful to you once you understand the routine, but I like that the game makes preparation part of the job instead of treating every house like a pile of objects waiting to be collected. The equipment gives you something to work toward, too. Buying better tools after selling stolen things is a pretty clear reason to keep going, even if the career plan is not one I’d bring up at a family dinner.

The appeal is mostly in the small decisions. Do you keep looking around, do you take another item, do you leave before you get greedy? I think those choices give the theft some tension without needing a big story to explain why I should care. Still, if you want constant movement or a lot of action, the slower stretches could feel like waiting for a bus that is technically part of the game.

There’s a quiet, methodical rhythm to it that I enjoy more in theory than I would in every mood. You get a goal, work out a way in, and try to get out with enough to make the next step easier. That’s a satisfying little loop of decisions, though I wouldn’t want every workout to turn into careful inventory management with a criminal theme.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d start with a walk on the treadmill, not a run, because this game asks you to look around and make small adjustments instead of holding one direction and letting the scenery do its thing. The slower pace suits the intel gathering and sneaking, and you can still get a decent workout without trying to aim while your whole body is moving. I would not make the belt the place where I prove anything.

It supports a controller, which makes it a much more realistic option for exercising than a game that expects you to sit at a mouse and keyboard. But controller support doesn’t make every action effortless. When you’re trying to move carefully, look around, and interact with things, a little wobble can make a precise action feel more fiddly than it would on the couch. That’s the part I’d keep an eye on.

A stationary bike may be the easier place to start if you want to play attentively, since your upper body can stay steadier while you manage the controls. I wouldn’t say the treadmill is out, though. Keep the speed comfortable and treat the slower moments as time to settle into a steady pace, rather than trying to jog through every decision like you’re late for a meeting.

I’d skip pushing the intensity when you’re sneaking or trying to get in and out cleanly. The whole point is to stay deliberate, and there’s no reason to turn a quiet game into a thumb workout where every little movement goes a bit sideways. If you want to exercise hard, this probably isn’t the one I’d pair with that. It needs some attention.

The game’s best workout fit is a steady, moderate session where you’re happy to slow down and think. That sounds less exciting than sprinting through a mission, but it also sounds like a better way to avoid fumbling around the controls while trying to be subtle.

## Computer Requirements

You don’t need a new gaming computer for this one. The listed graphics requirement is an old card, so I’d check that your computer can handle the basics before worrying about buying anything fancy. It’s asking for a computer, not a small data center.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Worth trying if you like slow, hands on games and want a workout that leaves room to think. I’d play it at a walking pace or on a bike, but I’d skip it if you want to run hard and keep your hands mostly on autopilot.
