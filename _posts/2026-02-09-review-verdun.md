Verdun sounds like a bad match for a treadmill, because trench warfare means you can spend a lot of time aiming carefully and waiting for somebody across the field to make a mistake. On a bike, I think the slower pace might work better, but the multiplayer pressure still makes this a game I’d keep for a workout where you can stop moving easily.

## Gameplay

The part I like about Verdun is that it doesn’t turn World War One into a parade of easy targets, and I think that makes the shooting feel tense in a way a lot of multiplayer shooters don’t. A rifle shot matters, and the distance between you and the other squad can make the whole fight feel like a very serious game of trying to spot somebody before they spot you. That’s interesting. It can also mean a lot of looking at a ridge.

Frontlines is the mode I’d start with, since the attack and defense setup gives the match a direction instead of leaving you to wander around and wonder what everyone else is doing. Your squad is part of the fight, and the line can move as one side pushes or gets pushed back, which gives you a reason to stay focused even when your immediate view is mostly mud and bad decisions. I’d expect the changing front to keep me more engaged than a mode that’s just a series of unrelated shootouts.

Gas attacks and artillery add some memorable pressure, and I like that they fit the setting without making every moment a big spectacle. You have to react to what’s happening around you, and that can be pretty compelling when you’re moving with the squad. But I don’t know how much patience I’d have for getting taken out by a shot I never saw, then working my way back toward the action. It’s still a multiplayer shooter.

There’s a lot to like if you want your action game to feel slower and more deliberate, and I’d rather play that than another match where everyone is flying across the screen. The tradeoff is that careful aiming takes attention, and the slower stretches aren’t always restful in the way a slow game should be. Sometimes you’re standing still because you’re choosing to be cautious, and sometimes you’re standing still because you’re trying to figure out where the other team is. Those feel different.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d be cautious about playing this while running, because aiming at a distant target while your hands are bouncing around sounds like a job I’d make harder than it needs to be. Even if you stick to the squad, you’ll still need to turn, aim, and react to people firing at you, and a small movement of the stick can be the difference between hitting someone and sending a bullet into the countryside. I’d rather not have my crosshair doing its own workout.

A bike gives you a better chance of keeping your upper body steady, so that would be my choice if you want to try it while exercising. I’d keep the resistance low enough that the game doesn’t turn into a surprise leg day, and I’d play the Frontlines mode rather than looking for a more competitive, aim focused match. If you can’t pause or leave without affecting the rest of your squad, though, I’d save the match for when you’re done exercising.

Controller support isn’t something I can confirm from the details here, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming that a controller will make the aiming comfortable. If you already have a setup that works, I’d try it on a stationary bike first and keep the controls simple, with sensitivity low enough that you’re not overcorrecting every time you reach for a target. I know I’d be tempted to turn it up after missing a shot, and that’s probably how I’d miss the next one too.

There’s also a social part to multiplayer that makes this a different kind of workout game. Your squad may be counting on you to move with them, and you can’t always decide to take a break at the exact moment your legs have had enough. I’d skip it for a hard treadmill run, where the effort is already making your hands less precise. A gentle bike ride is the more reasonable experiment.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum graphics card is old enough that you don’t need a brand new gaming computer, but this isn’t a game I’d expect to run on whatever laptop happens to be nearby. I’d check the graphics card first, and make sure your internet connection is stable since this is multiplayer only.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Verdun seems like a thoughtful multiplayer shooter for someone who enjoys slow, tense fights, and I’d suggest trying it seated before adding exercise to the mix. For a treadmill workout, I’d pass. On an easy bike ride, it might work if your controls feel steady and you’re fine with a match asking you to stay present.
