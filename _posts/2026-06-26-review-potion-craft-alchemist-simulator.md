A treadmill session with Potion Craft would be more like a slow walk through a shop than a game that keeps your hands busy every second, and I think that can be a nice change if you’re tired of chasing things around a screen. You’re making potions for customers, but a lot of the fun is stopping to think about what you want to make next.

## Gameplay

The potion making has a nice amount of hands on fuss to it, because you grind ingredients, drop them into the cauldron and steer the brew toward the effect you want. I like the idea of making a recipe yourself instead of selecting a potion from a menu, and the map gives those little choices somewhere to go. It feels a bit like trying to follow a recipe while your spoon keeps nudging the page.

Customers make the shop feel like more than a crafting screen, since they come in with requests and you decide what to sell them. I think that’s where the game gets its personality. Sometimes you’ll have the right potion ready, and sometimes you’ll have to decide whether it’s worth trying to make something new for a person who may not come back tomorrow. That’s a pretty ordinary shop problem, which is why it works.

There’s a lot of room to experiment, but the game does ask you to pay attention to the path your ingredients are taking and what you’re adding to the brew. You can’t quite mash buttons and hope the potion turns into the right thing. I don’t know if I’d want that much careful crafting in every game, but here it’s the point, and the pace gives you room to make a plan without feeling like you’ve stopped the game.

The slower shop rhythm is also the thing that could wear thin. You’re not constantly getting a new surprise, and some of the pleasure is in repeating the process with a slightly different goal. I think that’s relaxing when you want a game to settle down beside your workout, but it’s not going to distract you from every minute on the treadmill. There are stretches where you’re thinking about the recipe more than anything else.

I’d give it a try when you want a gentle game with a bit of problem solving, not when you need a constant stream of action to keep your legs moving. The shop and recipe decisions are engaging, but the game expects you to enjoy taking your time. Some days, that sounds about right.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The pace is forgiving, which is a big point in its favor for exercising. Customers aren’t rushing you through a timed challenge, and you can take a moment to look at a recipe or work out where to steer the potion. I’d expect this to be much easier to play on a bike than a fast action game, though I can see it working on a treadmill at a walking pace too.

Potion Craft supports a controller, but the crafting still involves careful movement and selecting small things on screen, so I’d expect the controls to feel less direct while you’re bouncing along. The whole point is to guide ingredients and the brew where you want them, and a slightly off input might mean you’re correcting your path instead of focusing on your stride. It’s not a disaster. It’s just a little fiddly.

I’d keep the treadmill speed comfortable and avoid trying to make a complicated new recipe while you’re jogging. Start with a potion you already understand, handle the customers at whatever pace feels easy, and save the experimenting for when you’re stopped or moving slowly. You don’t need to turn your workout into an ingredient delivery service.

On a bike, the steady pace and time to look at the screen make the shopkeeping feel more natural to me. You can still get distracted while you’re working out a recipe, though, and I genuinely don’t know that I’d want to be pedaling hard while tracing a careful route through the potion map. I’d use it for an easy ride, not a hard interval session.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum graphics card is old enough that I’d look at your graphics hardware first, especially if you’re using a laptop with integrated graphics. You don’t need a new gaming computer for this, but the listed minimum is more than the bare minimum built in graphics you might find in an older machine.

## Conclusion and Score

Potion Craft suits you if you want a calm workout game with gentle planning and a shop to manage, and I’d skip it when you need fast action to keep your attention. For an easy ride or a slow treadmill walk, it makes sense.
