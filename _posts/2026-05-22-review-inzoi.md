Watching a Zoi try to build a life while you keep moving sounds better to me than trying to run through a game that needs perfect aim, and inZOI has the kind of slow, adjustable pace that can work for a workout. I’d treat it as a low effort ride or walk, not the thing I put on when I need the game to make me forget my legs.

## Gameplay

The part I like most is that you can make the life you want to watch, from customizing characters to building houses, and then spend time seeing how that little setup turns into a story. That gives you a personal reason to keep looking at the screen, which helps when the treadmill is starting to feel like a chore you agreed to for no clear reason.

I think the emotional simulation gives the game more to work with than just arranging a nice house and admiring it, because the people in it are meant to have lives and feelings that unfold as you play. I don’t know how much of that will feel surprising over a long stretch, especially in Early Access, but I like the idea of creating a situation and letting it develop instead of needing a constant stream of action.

There’s also a lot of room to get distracted by the creative tools. You can spend time making a character look right, then decide the house needs another change, and suddenly the workout has become a quiet appointment with your own very specific design opinions. That’s not necessarily bad, but I’d go in knowing that some of your playtime may be making choices rather than watching a story move along.

I wouldn’t pick inZOI when I want a game to carry me through a hard run, because customizing and building ask you to stop and consider what you’re doing, and the simulation sounds more like something you guide at your own pace than a sequence that keeps pushing forward. That flexibility is useful on a bike, where you can ease off when you want to think, but it also means the game may not distract you much during its slower stretches. A lot depends on whether you enjoy tinkering.

As an Early Access game, it’s also a little harder to recommend without qualification. The core idea sounds appealing to me, and having tools for both characters and houses gives you things to shape, but I’d want you to be comfortable with a game that’s still being developed rather than expecting every part of the simulation to feel finished. It’s a promising place to spend a relaxed workout, not something I’d buy expecting a perfectly formed life story.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The pace is the reason I’d try this on a stationary bike first. The game is about controlling and observing lives, customizing characters, and building houses, so I’d expect plenty of moments where you can pedal steadily while deciding what you want to change. You don’t need to react to an enemy or track a moving target, which is a nice change from games that make you tense your shoulders like you’re trying to win a very small championship.

On a treadmill, I’d keep the speed comfortable and make any detailed character or house changes while walking rather than running. When you’re picking through tools or trying to place something carefully, your attention goes to the screen, and I think that could make a faster belt feel more awkward than the game itself deserves. I’d also skip treating every quiet stretch as a cue to speed up, since the game may ask for more thought than movement.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information here, so I wouldn’t buy it on the assumption that you can play comfortably with a controller. That matters a lot for this setup, because keyboard and mouse controls can be more fiddly when you’re moving, even when the game is relaxed. If you already know your setup works, the slower pace seems forgiving; if you don’t, I’d check before making this your workout game. I’d rather find that out before I’m already on the treadmill.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This needs a dedicated graphics card around the level of an RTX 2060 or Radeon RX 5600 XT, so it’s not a game I’d expect to run well on basic integrated graphics. I’d look at the graphics card first, since the minimum is a fairly serious ask for a life sim, and then make sure you have room for its 40 GB install.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

inZOI makes the most sense for you if you like shaping characters and homes and want a calm game to pair with a steady bike ride or an easy walk. I’d skip it as a workout distraction if you need constant action, or if you haven’t confirmed a comfortable way to control it.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
