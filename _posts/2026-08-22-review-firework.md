A slow ride is probably the right place for Firework, because a funeral fire and a reopened massacre case sound like they want your attention more than they want you sprinting. I’d keep the pace easy and let the mystery do its thing.

## Gameplay

Firework has a setup I like: an accidental fire at a funeral gives the police a reason to reopen an old massacre case, and Lin Lixun gets pulled into the investigation. That gives the story a human starting point instead of asking you to care about a pile of clues just because they’re clues. As the victims’ pasts come into view and the case gets stranger, I’d expect the mystery to keep me interested, even when I’m moving slowly through it.

This is a horror adventure built around exploring places, finding clues and solving puzzles, not a game that’s going to keep your hands busy every second. I think that suits the story, because poking around and deciding what a detail means is part of the appeal, but it also means there will be stretches where you’re reading or thinking instead of making quick progress. Some people love that kind of slow unraveling. I usually want the story to give me a little time to catch up, and this sounds like it will.

The horror angle also seems better suited to atmosphere than constant panic. That matters, because I don’t want a game to turn every minute into a reaction test when I’m trying to exercise. I’d rather have time to notice that a place feels wrong and wonder what happened there, even if I end up standing still to sort out a puzzle. My legs can keep working while my brain takes a minute.

There’s a small tradeoff in the premise. A case that becomes more bewildering can be compelling, but it can also turn into a lot of names and past events that I need to keep straight. I don’t know how neatly every thread comes together, so I wouldn’t promise that the story will always be easy to follow while you’re distracted by a treadmill. If you want a simple game to glance at between steps, this probably isn’t it.

I’d play it for the investigation and the mood, not for nonstop action. The story gives you a reason to keep going, but the reading and puzzle solving make it a more deliberate game than something you can half watch while you exercise. That’s fine. It’s just a different kind of workout companion.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The investigation gives you room to slow down, which is useful when you’re on a moving belt and the screen starts asking for more thought than your legs were expecting. Firework has full controller support, so you don’t need to reach for a keyboard whenever you want to move around or interact with something. I’d start with an easy pace rather than trying to turn every bit of exploration into a running challenge.

I’d expect the controller to make basic movement and puzzle interaction manageable, but reading clues while bouncing along could get old. It’s one thing to glance at a short bit of dialogue, and another to stop following the story because the words are wobbling around. If that happens, lower your pace or pause the game for the longer bits. The treadmill can wait. It’s very patient in that specific way.

A stationary bike might be more comfortable if you want to read carefully, since your upper body isn’t moving as much as it would during a run. But I wouldn’t call running out of the question. You can keep the pace gentle, take your time with clues, and avoid treating every puzzle like something you have to solve before the next minute passes. I’d skip playing it during a hard interval workout. I can barely keep a thought in order when I’m pushing myself, and this game is asking for a few thoughts.

The scary atmosphere could help pull you into the story, but it may also make you want to focus on the screen instead of your workout. I’d save the more demanding parts for when you can safely pause, and keep the treadmill pace comfortable while exploring. Nothing about the setup sounds like a competitive match where one slow reaction ruins someone else’s day. That’s a relief.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older dedicated graphics card rather than a recent, powerful gaming setup, so I wouldn’t expect the visuals to be the part that asks the most of your computer. I’d check that your machine meets the processor and memory requirements first, then give the game a try.

## Conclusion and Score

Firework sounds like a good choice for someone who wants a strange, story led mystery during an easy workout, especially on a bike or a gentle treadmill pace. If you want constant action or something you can play without reading, I’d look elsewhere.
