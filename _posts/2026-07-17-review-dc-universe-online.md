The character creator is a pretty good way to get yourself moving, because choosing your hero’s powers and movement style gives you something to care about before the missions start. I’d pick a character who can fly and take the treadmill at a pace where I can still keep track of the fights.

## Gameplay

DC Universe Online has a nice, slightly silly premise for a workout game: you’re a new hero or villain, and eventually you’re dealing with the likes of Batman, Superman, Lex Luthor and The Joker. I think that gives the missions more personality than another anonymous fantasy quest, even when the actual job is mostly going somewhere and fighting a crowd. The DC characters do a lot of the heavy lifting.

The action is what keeps it from feeling like an exercise in reading quest text. You’re moving around, using powers and chaining attacks, and that sounds like a better fit for a workout than watching long scenes unfold. There are still menus and character decisions to slow you down, though, and an online RPG has a way of turning “I’ll just pick up the next mission” into a small meeting with your gear.

I’d be curious how much the choice of powers changes the feel of a workout, because a character built around getting close to enemies asks more from you than one who can keep some distance. The powers and movement style are a personal part of the fun, and I like that you’re not just picking a costume and then waiting for the game to do the rest. I’d probably spend longer in character creation than I meant to. That happens.

The missions have enough fighting to keep your attention, but I wouldn’t expect every minute to be equally lively. There’s travel, there are conversations, and there are stretches where you’re looking for the next thing to do rather than reacting to an enemy. The game’s free to play, which makes it easy to see whether its particular mix of superhero errands and combat works for you, but I’d go in expecting a long-running MMO rather than a tightly paced action game.

I don’t know how much patience you have for menus while you’re exercising, but you’ll want some. Sorting out your character and figuring out where to go can make the game feel like a chore before the punching starts, and I’d save those decisions for when you’re stopped. Once you’re in a fight, it’s much easier to keep your legs going and let the game be the game.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The online world is the part I’d be careful with, because you can’t treat it like a single player game that politely waits while you catch your breath. A mission can be simple enough to handle at an easy pace, but fighting while other players are around means you’re sharing the moment with people who may not want you to pause and check a menu. I’d stick to solo missions when I wanted a relaxed workout.

DC Universe Online supports a controller, which makes a big difference when you’re trying to keep your hands on the game and your feet moving. Still, combat asks you to aim your attacks and react when enemies come at you, so I wouldn’t start with a fast treadmill setting or try to play while I’m already tired. I’d keep the controls simple, stay at a comfortable pace, and give the game a little more attention than I would for a turn based RPG.

Group content sounds like the wrong place to learn how your hands and legs work together. A group mission can be fun when you’re focused on it, but on a bike or treadmill I’d rather not be the person who misses a cue because I was trying to adjust my pace. PvP is also something I’d leave for when I’m not exercising. There are plenty of other things to do in a superhero game without making every mistake somebody else’s problem.

On a bike, I think the steady combat and travel could work at a moderate pace, especially if you keep the session in solo content. On a treadmill, I’d be more cautious about turning and dodging while the belt is moving, since your hands are doing more than just picking an option from a menu. I’d start easy and see how much attention the fights actually take. No need to prove anything to a group of online superheroes.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimum points to an old dedicated graphics card, so this doesn’t look like a game that needs a recent gaming PC, though I’d check the graphics hardware before assuming a laptop’s integrated graphics will handle it. You’ll also need broadband internet and a decent chunk of storage, because a superhero MMO still needs room for all its superhero business.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

DC Universe Online makes sense if you want action and superhero missions to keep your workout occupied, and you’re happy to take breaks for menus or save group play for later. I’d give it a try on an easy pace first and see if the combat feels manageable.
