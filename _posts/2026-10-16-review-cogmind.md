I’d put Cogmind on a steady walk, not a hard run, because deciding which robot parts to keep sounds like the sort of thing that can make you forget your pace for a minute. It’s turn based, so at least the game isn’t asking you to aim while your whole body is bouncing.

## Gameplay

The robot you build out of components you find or salvage is the part that makes this sound more interesting to me than a standard dungeon crawl. Your equipment isn’t just a set of numbers to improve forever, it’s the actual collection of pieces keeping your little machine useful, and I think that gives each decision a nice amount of weight. Finding something useful feels good, but I’d also expect to spend a fair bit of time wondering whether it’s useful enough to carry around.

Turn based combat is a good fit for that kind of game, because you can stop and think instead of trying to make a decision before an enemy gets to you. That also means you may spend a while thinking. On a workout, I can see that being either a nice mental distraction or me staring at one choice until the treadmill starts to feel like a very patient coworker.

You can fight, sneak, hack, or fly your way through the world, and I like that those sound like different plans rather than little bonuses on top of fighting. I’d be more interested in a game that lets me reconsider the whole approach than one that keeps handing me bigger weapons and asking if I’d like to use them. I don’t know how often each option pays off in practice, but being able to try a less direct route gives the decisions some personality.

The world is described as living and breathing, which sounds promising for a roguelike, though I’d be going in for the choices more than for a story that carries me along. I don’t know that I’d want to learn every system during a fast workout, and this has enough tactical thinking that I’d probably want to take it slowly at first. That’s not a mark against the game so much as a reason not to expect it to do all the work of keeping my attention for me.

There’s also the Early Access label, so I’d be comfortable with the game still being a work in progress rather than treating it as a finished, carefully polished package. The basic idea already sounds like it has a clear reason to exist, though, and that matters more to me than a big list of features. I’d give it a try if the prospect of adapting your robot as you go sounds fun, and skip it if you want a story game that keeps moving without much input from you.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The turn based pace is the best reason to try this while exercising. You can pause between decisions, so there’s no immediate pressure to make a precise move while your legs are moving, and I’d expect that to work better than a game built around fast reactions. Still, the choices are the game, so if you’re trying to do a hard interval, you may end up choosing between paying attention to your pace and deciding what to do with a robot part.

I’d start with an easy workout and give yourself room to stop moving when you need to read or think. On a bike, keeping your upper body settled may make it easier to focus on the screen, while walking gives you a simpler way to adjust your pace when the tactical decisions start taking over. Neither one magically makes a complicated game simple, unfortunately.

The store information doesn’t establish controller support, so I wouldn’t buy it on the assumption that you can play with a controller. I’d check the control options before setting it up beside exercise equipment, and I’d skip a workout session if the controls make you lean forward and hunt for keys every few seconds. That would get old before the robot did.

This seems better for an easy, steady session than for anything where you’re trying to push hard and keep a rhythm. If you like taking your time with tactical choices, that’s workable. If you need a game to keep the action moving while you mostly concentrate on exercising, you may spend more time considering your next move than you meant to.

## Computer Requirements

Cogmind has very modest minimum requirements, including graphics described as “Anything,” so an older computer or one using integrated graphics should be enough. I’d check that your system can run it at all before worrying about the graphics card, because this isn’t asking your computer to render a giant sci-fi spectacle.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Cogmind sounds like a thoughtful workout companion for an easy pace, especially if you enjoy making tactical choices and changing your approach as you go. I’d skip it for a fast run or if you need confirmed controller support before you commit.
