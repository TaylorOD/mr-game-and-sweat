I think Black Myth: Wukong sounds like a good action game and a pretty awkward exercise game, which is an annoying combination when you want both things at once. It adapts Journey to the West and puts you in the role of Sun Wukong, the Monkey King, with fast combat and a story rooted in Chinese mythology. That gives you plenty to pay attention to, and I’d rather play it sitting down than try to keep my feet and thumbs organized together.

The pacing sounds like it helps, at least. There aren’t many slow stretches or long cut scenes breaking up the action, so you’re not stuck watching the screen while your workout goes on without you. That’s useful, but it also means the game keeps asking you to pay attention. There’s only so much room in my brain for that.

## Gameplay

The combat is the reason I’d want to play, and also the reason I wouldn’t put it on during a hard run. Quick attacks, dodges, and timing matter, so it sounds like a game where being a little late can turn a good plan into a bad one. That’s fun when the game has your full attention. While you’re bouncing on a treadmill, I’d expect those small timing windows to feel a lot smaller.

The game’s world and mythology give the fighting something to sit inside, which I like more than combat that feels like a string of unrelated arenas. You’re playing as a figure people already know, but the story gives you room to care about what’s happening beyond the next fight. I’d want to follow that story without trying to read the screen between steps. Maybe I’m just not good at multitasking, but it sounds like the kind of game that makes you forget the workout is there.

There are fewer long pauses than I’d want for exercise, though. In a slower game, you can settle into a rhythm while you walk around or wait for the next thing to happen, and here the action seems to come back quickly. That keeps the game moving, which is great when you’re sitting down and a little inconvenient when you’re trying to keep your pace steady. I can see why that’s a plus for players who want constant action. I’d probably want a different game on a tough workout.

The overall combat sounds rewarding enough that I wouldn’t write it off just because it takes concentration. Skillful timing gives you something to learn, and getting better at that kind of fighting can be a good reason to keep going. I just wouldn’t expect to make much progress while splitting my attention. On an easy session, maybe. When I’m tired, probably not.

### Category Score: 2.5 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A controller is the right way to play this, and Black Myth: Wukong supports one, so at least you don’t have to invent a setup around a keyboard. The trouble is what your hands have to do once the fighting starts. You need to attack, dodge, and pay attention to timing, and that’s a lot to ask from thumbs that are moving around with the rest of you. I’d be more comfortable on a stationary bike, where my upper body can stay still, but even there I’d keep the effort low.

On a treadmill, I’d expect a more frustrating mix. The game keeps the action coming, and the combat asks you to react, so there isn’t much room to look away or let your attention drift. You might be able to walk at an easy pace and play, but running while trying to time dodges sounds like a good way to make both activities worse. I’d pick a simple workout and keep the pace comfortable. If the game starts demanding too much, I’d stop the workout before I started trying to win a boss fight with my whole body moving.

The bike is a little more workable, but I still wouldn’t call this an easy game to pair with exercise. You can keep your hands on the controller and your body steadier than you can on a treadmill, which should make the quick movements less awkward. But the important part is still watching the screen and reacting at the right time. I’d probably use it for a gentle ride, not an interval workout where I’m already trying to think about how hard I’m pushing.

For people who like to focus on one thing at a time, this is probably a game to play after the workout. That sounds obvious, but the game’s steady pace makes it tempting to treat it like something you can play in the background, and I don’t think that’s what the combat is for. If you’ve played demanding action games while exercising and know you can keep your rhythm, you may be fine. I’d still start slow and see how much attention the fights take.

### Category Score: 1.5 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The visuals ask a fair amount from your computer, so this doesn’t sound like a game for an older or lower powered system. You’ll want a capable graphics card and processor to get the rich presentation and smooth play the game is built around. I wouldn’t buy it assuming every computer can handle it, and checking the requirements first is a more pleasant use of a minute than troubleshooting later.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

I’d tell someone to play Black Myth: Wukong if they want a demanding action game with a story drawn from Chinese mythology. For exercise, I’d only recommend trying it on a gentle bike ride or an easy walk, and I’d be ready to save it for afterward.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3

At the end of the day, this sounds like a game worth playing, but not one I’d choose because I wanted to work out. The combat and pacing are appealing on their own, while the quick reactions make it tough to pair with a moving treadmill or bike. If you want the game first, give it a try.
