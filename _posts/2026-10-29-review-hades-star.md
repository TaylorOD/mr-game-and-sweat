Save this for a walk where you’re happy to check in between stretches, not a run where you need both hands doing the same thing. Hades’ Star has you tending a corner of a galaxy shared with thousands of other players, and the slow pace is nice until you realize a lot of the exercise is standing there waiting for your space ships to do their jobs.

## Gameplay

I like the idea of a strategy game that doesn’t need me to make a decision every few seconds, and Hades’ Star mostly gives you room to think. You expand your star system, build it up, and send ships out to mine and move resources, with the larger galaxy carrying on around you. That steady little cycle feels good when I want something to manage without a whole lot of urgency. It’s not exactly a gripping use of a spaceship, but it does the job.

The waiting is part of the deal, though. Ships travel, resources accumulate, and you come back to check what’s ready, so there are stretches where you’re looking at the galaxy and deciding there isn’t much to do just yet. I think that could be a nice rhythm during a workout if you’re comfortable with a game that comes in small visits. If you want the screen to keep demanding your attention, it may feel like you’re supervising a loading bar with planets.

The shared galaxy gives the game a reason to feel bigger than your own little system, and I’m interested in that. There are cooperative Red Star missions and competitive options like Blue Stars, which make the game feel less like a solitary space spreadsheet. I’d probably spend more time in the cooperative side, because coordinating with other players sounds fun without making every choice feel like a personal referendum on my ability to run a space program.

That more active play can pull you out of a workout, though. In a Red Star mission, you’re making choices with other players and managing ships in a limited window, and I can see myself leaning closer to the screen instead of keeping an easy pace. Blue Star competition sounds even more like something I’d save for when I’m sitting still. I don’t need a galaxy-wide audience for my thumb to start making bad decisions.

For the most part, the game seems to reward checking in and planning more than constant action, and that’s a reasonable trade. I don’t know if that would hold my attention through a long session, since the quiet gaps might start to feel like I’m waiting for the treadmill to finish more than I’m playing. But as a game you dip into during a workout, it has a sensible pace.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

On a treadmill, I’d keep the pace easy enough that I can look down and make deliberate choices without trying to steer my whole body with one hand. Hades’ Star doesn’t sound like it asks for quick reactions all the time, which is a big plus, but moving around a strategy interface while your hands are bouncing is still a little like trying to sort coins while someone gently shakes the table.

I can’t vouch for controller support, so I’d plan around mouse and keyboard rather than buying it expecting a controller to work. That setup is fine at a desk, but it’s awkward on a treadmill unless you have a stable place for the mouse and can keep your hands steady. On a bike, you may have an easier time keeping your upper body still, though I’d still want the screen and input devices positioned where I’m not twisting around to reach them.

The main system management and slower ship movement seem like the parts I’d play while moving, because there’s room to stop and think before committing. I’d leave the more involved multiplayer activity for when I’m not trying to keep a steady pace, especially if other players are depending on my timing. I’d rather be the person who replies late than the person who sends a ship somewhere odd because the treadmill moved my hand.

If you’re comfortable with a low effort workout and a game that lets you check in, make a few choices, then let things play out, I think it could fit. If you want to keep both hands on a controller or need a game to occupy you every second, this is going to feel more like managing a tiny control room. The tiny control room does have a lot of planets.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This should run on an older computer, and the minimum requirements are modest enough that the graphics card probably won’t be the thing keeping you out of the galaxy. I’d check that your system can run the game and get online reliably, since this is built around a shared galaxy rather than an offline campaign. Your machine does not need to look like it’s preparing to launch a satellite.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Hades’ Star is a thoughtful, slow paced strategy game I’d suggest for an easy workout and a player who likes checking in over time. I’d skip it for a fast run or a setup that depends on comfortable controller play.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
