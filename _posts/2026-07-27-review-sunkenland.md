A treadmill walk through a flooded city sounds good to me, but I’d keep the pace easy because Sunkenland has you scavenging underwater and then trying to hold onto what you brought back. It’s the kind of game where I’d want my hands steady more than my legs moving fast.

## Gameplay

The part I like is that the water changes what a supply run feels like. You’re not just wandering around looking for materials, you’re going into sunken buildings and trying to bring useful things back, and that gives a simple scavenging trip a little more tension. I think that could keep your attention during a workout without demanding that every second be a fight.

Building a base from modular pieces is appealing, too, because it gives all that gathering somewhere to go. I’d probably enjoy slowly making a place that feels like it can hold together, even though survival crafting can turn into a lot of sorting and crafting menus when you’d rather be moving. You can build something nice and still spend a while deciding where the next bit of material should go. I have done worse with my time.

The NPC clan invasions make the whole setup feel less peaceful than a sightseeing trip through a sunken city. You’re collecting resources and territory, then you have to think about defending what you’ve built, and I think that gives the gathering a purpose beyond filling up an inventory. I’d be more interested in that than in collecting materials just because the game has materials to collect.

There’s a lot to keep track of here, though, between scavenging, crafting, base building and defense, and that can make the game feel like a series of small jobs rather than one smooth adventure. I don’t know how much time you’ll spend in menus versus out exploring, so I’d expect some stop and think stretches. That’s not automatically bad, but it does make Sunkenland less of a hands off workout game than the watery setting might suggest.

Early Access also makes me a little cautious. A survival game can be a great place to tinker, but you’re buying into something that is still being developed, and I’d want the current version to have enough there for the time I’m putting in. If you already like building a base and making supply runs, that sounds like a reasonable trade. If you want a tight action game that gets to the next exciting moment quickly, I’d probably look elsewhere.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d treat this as a walking game first, not something I’d try to play at a hard running pace. The underwater scavenging sounds like it could be calm enough for an easy walk, but hauling your attention between exploring, gathering and watching for clan invasions means you’ll want to be able to react without bouncing around. I think you’d notice pretty quickly if your pace was making the controls harder.

On a bike, I’d expect it to be easier to keep your upper body steady while you look around and aim, though the base building could still pull you into menus and make you stop paying attention to your cadence. The treadmill has the opposite appeal: walking around while you explore the flooded city sounds like a good match, until an invasion makes you focus on defending your base. I’d keep the speed low enough that the game doesn’t turn into a hand coordination exercise.

I’d check that your controller setup works before planning a workout around it. I can’t confirm native controller support from the facts I have, and the game sounds like it asks for more precise input during combat than during gathering, so I wouldn’t assume every part will feel comfortable while you’re moving. If the controls are working well, I’d save the base building and inventory decisions for a slower pace, and use the easier stretches of scavenging for the steadier part of your workout.

You can also choose not to make every session a full workout. I’d be fine doing the more complicated building and defense at an easy pace, then letting the less demanding exploration carry the exercise. I don’t know if that split will suit everyone, but it seems more sensible than trying to turn every invasion into a cardio interval. I’d rather finish the session than impress a video game.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to a very old graphics card, so you don’t need a recent gaming machine just to meet the listed baseline. I’d check your graphics hardware first, but this is not asking you to bring a very expensive computer to a water apocalypse.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Sunkenland sounds best for someone who enjoys gathering, building a base and dealing with the occasional invasion, and I’d suggest playing it at a walking pace or an easy spin. If you want quick action with very little time spent planning, this probably isn’t the one.
