Claiming a title like Shogun or Highland King sounds like a fun way to spend a workout, but March of Empires is better for checking in between exercise than for keeping your attention on the belt. I think the waiting and map management would leave me watching the screen more than moving my legs.

## Gameplay

The idea of building a castle and sending an army toward territorial conquest is appealing, and I like that the goal isn’t to win a quick match and start over. You’re building toward something in a shared world, so even small decisions can feel like they matter. The trouble is that this kind of strategy game can make progress feel like a row of timers with a castle parked behind them.

There’s a lot of managing to do, and that can be pleasant when you want a game to occupy your hands while you think about something else. But I feel like the menus, resources and army decisions ask for a kind of attention that comes in little bursts, then leave you waiting for the next thing to finish. It’s a bit like trying to cook dinner while checking whether the oven has made any progress.

The title choices give the whole thing a fun bit of flavor. Shogun, Northern Tsar and Desert Sultan all sound like somebody at work gave me a very serious hat and asked me to run a country. I’d be curious how much that role changes the feel of play, but I wouldn’t expect the title itself to make the day to day management less repetitive.

I also like the idea of a realm where other players are part of the picture, because a conquest feels more meaningful when there are real people on the map. The flip side is that a persistent multiplayer world means the game is asking you to keep an eye on things instead of neatly finishing a round and putting it away. I don’t know how much of the game feels urgent at any given moment, but constant war sounds like a lot to keep track of while your feet are doing something else.

I’d play this when I wanted a strategy game to check in on, not when I needed the game to carry a whole workout. The castle building and larger goal have an easy appeal, but long stretches of waiting and menu work are a big part of the deal.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d keep the pace comfortable and treat this more like a game for an easy walk or steady bike ride than a run where you’re bouncing around. Moving armies and managing your castle both sound like jobs that reward looking closely at the screen, and I don’t want to be squinting at a map while trying to remember where my feet go.

I can’t confirm native controller support, so I wouldn’t buy it expecting a controller to make everything easy. The game’s castle and realm management look much more suited to mouse driven menus, and if you’re exercising with a controller, it’s worth checking what input setup actually works on your computer before you commit. I’d rather you find that out at home than halfway through a workout with one hand holding a controller and the other trying to steer a mouse.

The shared world is the part that makes me hesitate most. You aren’t just waiting for a match to end, and a multiplayer strategy game about ongoing territorial conflict seems like a poor fit for stopping whenever you want to adjust your pace or drink some water. Maybe you can play in short check ins, but I wouldn’t want the game nudging me to make decisions right when I’m trying to keep a steady rhythm.

On a bike, I think the stationary position would make the map and menus less awkward, especially if you can keep the screen close and reach your controls comfortably. On a treadmill, I’d stick to walking and avoid anything that makes you lean forward or focus on a small detail for too long. It’s not impossible to pair with exercise, but I’d make the exercise the main event and let the kingdom wait a little.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to a very modest computer, including an old graphics card, so you probably don’t need a gaming tower to give it a try. I’d check that your internet connection is steady first, since a shared online strategy game is less useful when the realm keeps disappearing.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

March of Empires makes sense for someone who likes slow kingdom management and wants a game to check between other things. I wouldn’t choose it for a run, and I’d only try it on a bike or an easy walk after confirming the controls work the way you need. If the appeal is building a castle over time, there’s something here.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
