Taking ATLYSS onto a treadmill sounds better than it sounds on paper, because wandering through its surreal world and picking up quests seems like the kind of RPG business you can do while moving. The creature fights are where I’d slow the belt down a little.

## Gameplay

I think ATLYSS has the right basic shape for an exercise game, since you can spend time exploring and progressing a character without every minute needing to be a test of your reflexes. The odd world gives you something to look at besides a progress bar, and customizing a character gives you a small reason to keep going when the run starts to feel long. I like that the game can be played alone, too, because not every workout needs to become a group plan.

The action is the part that makes me hesitate to call it an easy treadmill game. You’re fighting creatures, and I’d expect those moments to ask more of your hands than simply walking from one place to another, especially when you’re also trying to stay steady on the belt. I’d probably keep the pace comfortable and treat combat as a reason to focus, not as something I could do without looking.

Quests are a mixed deal for me in an RPG. They give the world a little direction, and I like having a reason to go somewhere instead of wandering until I forget what I was doing, but reading what you’re meant to do can pull your eyes off the game and your attention away from the workout. I don’t know how much of that would slow me down here, so I’d be ready to pause the belt when I needed to sort out the next step.

There’s also the option to play online, including fighting with or against friends, and that sounds like it could make the game livelier if you already have somebody who wants to join. I’d start solo, though, because learning an action RPG while keeping an eye on a moving belt is already enough coordination for me. The belt doesn’t need to know my plans.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support matters a lot here, and it makes ATLYSS a reasonable game to try while exercising rather than a game you’d have to wrestle into working. I’d still use a controller layout that feels comfortable in your hands before you start moving, since fumbling for buttons is less charming when the floor is moving under you.

On a treadmill, I’d begin with a walk or an easy jog and do the questing and exploration at that pace. When a fight starts, I’d lower the intensity if I needed to, because action combat and bouncing hands are not a pairing I’d trust without trying it first. If you find yourself missing attacks or losing track of what’s happening, take the hint and slow down. It’s not a competition.

A bike seems a little easier to manage during the fights, since your upper body can stay more settled while you work the controller, but I wouldn’t call it effortless there either. The game still asks you to react to creatures, and I’d skip online play if I knew I’d get too invested in keeping up with another person. There’s no shame in making the exercise part the thing you’re there for.

I’d treat menus and quest text as natural places to stop pedaling or ease off the treadmill, rather than trying to read everything at workout speed. I don’t know whether the online side would make every session feel different, but the single player option means you can set your own pace and keep the game from turning into a social appointment. That works for me.

## Computer Requirements

The listed minimums are very modest, so I’d expect this to be approachable on an older or budget computer, rather than something that calls for a new gaming machine. The requirements don’t name a graphics card, so I’d check how your particular computer handles the visuals before planning a long workout around it.

## Conclusion and Score

ATLYSS sounds like a nice fit for easy treadmill walks or steady bike sessions, especially if you enjoy exploring and building up a character over time. I’d skip it for hard intervals or any workout where you want your hands and attention completely free.
