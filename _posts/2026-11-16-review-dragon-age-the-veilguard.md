The companion conversations in Dragon Age: The Veilguard make this a better workout game than I expected, but I’d save some of the longer story scenes for when you’re off the treadmill. Combat keeps you busy, and the game has plenty of quiet stretches too.

## Gameplay

I like that fights ask you to watch what enemies are doing instead of just mashing one attack, and you can bring two companions whose abilities add some useful strategy. There’s a nice little rhythm to picking a target, using an ability, and getting back into the fight, although it can turn into a lot of menus if you’re trying to think through every choice. I’d keep the plan simple while exercising. Nobody needs a build consultation halfway through a jog.

The combat feels good when you’re dodging, parrying, and landing attacks, and there are enough different enemies and abilities to keep that from feeling identical all the time. But it’s still an action RPG, so some fights ask for quick reactions and a bit of attention, and I wouldn’t treat it like a game you can safely play while your brain is doing something else. I’d probably pick a straightforward setup and let the companions do their thing.

Then the story slows down for conversations, companion quests, and cut scenes, and those can be a real change of pace after a fight. I enjoy having characters like Bellara and Emmrich around, and I like that their stories give you a reason to care about the people you’re traveling with, but there’s a lot of talking between the action. I don’t know that I’d want every conversation while I’m trying to keep a steady pace. Some of them can wait.

There’s exploration and side content too, which gives you a reason to wander around instead of charging from one fight to the next. That’s nice when I want a lower effort stretch, though the mix of walking, dialogue, and combat can make it hard to predict what the next few minutes will ask of me. If I’m short on time, I’d focus on the main path and leave the extra reading for later. The map can wait.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d start on the Storyteller difficulty, especially if I were running, because I want to spend more attention on the workout and less on whether I timed a dodge perfectly. The full controller support is a big help, and the single player setup means there’s no match clock or teammate waiting while you stop to catch your breath. I’d still expect to miss a few attacks when the belt is moving. That’s fine.

On a bike, I think the combat would be easier to manage because your hands can stay steadier while you aim and use abilities. On a treadmill, I’d lean on lock on and melee attacks where I could, and I’d avoid trying to make every fight look clever. The ability menus and targeting are manageable, but they’re still more work than holding forward and pressing one button. I don’t know how much careful aiming I’d want to do at a jogging pace.

The conversations and cut scenes give you chances to relax your hands, but they also mean you may be staring at the screen without doing much in the game for a while. I’d save the longer companion talks and any reading for when I’m not exercising, then pick up a quest when I’m ready to pay attention again. It’s not a game that keeps the action going every minute, and I’d rather know that going in.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This needs a dedicated graphics card, but the minimum is around an older GTX 970 or GTX 1650 level rather than a recent high end card. I’d check that first, and make sure you have room for the 100 GB install, because that’s a lot of space to hand over to one game.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Dragon Age: The Veilguard makes sense for a steady bike ride or an easier treadmill session, especially if you enjoy story and character conversations between fights. I’d skip it for workouts where you want nonstop action or need to keep your eyes mostly away from the screen.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
