A missile warning and a cloud bank are a lot more interesting than counting the minutes on a treadmill, so I think ACE COMBAT 7 can make a workout go by quickly. I’d start on easy with the novice controls, though, because trying to make every turn look like a movie while your hands are bouncing is a little ambitious.

## Gameplay

The flying is the reason I’d play this. You’re constantly turning toward another target, lining up a shot, or trying to get out of the way, and that gives your attention somewhere useful to go besides wondering how much longer the run is. It’s an action game wearing a flight simulation jacket, but it doesn’t ask you to study a pile of aviation terms before you can enjoy the planes.

The campaign’s missions are a nice change of pace from games that make you stop every few minutes to sort through menus. You get briefings and story scenes between missions, and I think the flying is strong enough that I’m happy to sit through some setup for it. The story can also spend a while explaining who’s upset with whom, and I don’t know that I’d remember all of it while exercising. I’d pay attention when it’s moving, then let the names go when it isn’t.

Combat keeps you making small decisions without turning every second into a math problem. You’re choosing what to chase and when to fire, and the clouds can make a clear shot less clear, which is a neat way to make the sky feel like it matters rather than just being a nice background. There are also different special weapons to use, so I’d try a few and stick with the one that makes a mission feel manageable instead of trying to pick the perfect option every time.

There’s a lot to like in the way the game lets you throw a plane around, but I wouldn’t call it a relaxed flight game. Enemies move, objectives can change, and sometimes the thing you were lining up is no longer where you expected it to be. I’d play the campaign on easy if I wanted a steady workout rather than a second job in the clouds.

The multiplayer is there if you want other pilots involved, but it’s the campaign I’d choose while exercising. Other players give you less room to lose track of what’s happening, and I’d rather be a little distracted by an objective marker than by somebody who knows exactly where I am. The single player missions are plenty.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

I’d expect the treadmill to make the most demanding parts feel demanding for a reason. You’re aiming with the controller while the plane is turning, and targets can move across the screen quickly, so a hard run could turn a clean shot into me steering past the enemy and then correcting too much. On a bike, I think the smaller upper body movement would make that easier, but the game still wants attention.

Full controller support is a big help, and the novice controls are where I’d start. I’d also choose easy difficulty and keep the campaign offline, because the point is to stay moving, not to turn one missed shot into a long stretch of frustration. The standard controls may give you more control over the plane, but I’d save them for when I’m sitting down and can pay attention to every turn.

Some missions will suit a workout better than others. When the objective is clear and there are enemies in front of you, the action gives you a steady thing to focus on; when the game is asking you to locate a target or make a careful approach, I can see myself slowing down to read the screen. I don’t know how much that would bother me on a bike, but on a treadmill I’d rather keep the pace comfortable and let the game have my attention.

The story scenes are a built in chance to breathe, which I don’t mind, although you may find yourself standing around waiting for the next sortie instead of getting much done in the game. I’d use those stretches to settle into a comfortable pace rather than trying to make the whole workout match the mission. It’s not a bad trade.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum asks for a GTX 750 Ti, so this isn’t a game I’d count on running well with integrated graphics. A mid range graphics card from the last several years should be a more comfortable place to start, and I’d check that before buying because clouds and aircraft are a lot less impressive when the frame rate is struggling.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

ACE COMBAT 7 is for you if you want a busy, action focused workout and don’t mind some story breaks between missions. I’d skip it for a hard treadmill session where precise aiming sounds like one more chore, but on an easy pace or a bike I think the campaign could be a good time.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
