Take Slime Rancher 2 on a steady walk or an easy bike ride, because scooping up colorful slimes and bringing them home gives your hands something to do without demanding perfect timing. I’d keep the pace relaxed, though, since looking around while the screen bobs along can get a little busy.

## Gameplay

The ranching loop is simple in a way I think works really well for this kind of game: collect slimes, feed them, gather the plorts they produce, then use those to build up your ranch and get farther around Rainbow Island. It gives you a small reason to keep moving, and the next little job is usually easy to understand. I like that the game lets you make progress without needing to treat every minute like an important mission.

The Vacpack is a good fit for the loop, because sucking up slimes and food feels active without requiring fancy input. You do need to look around and aim, but you aren’t lining up tiny targets while the game counts down. I think the slimes being so easy to spot helps too. Most of the time, you’re gathering things that are happily taking up a lot of the screen.

Then there’s the ranch itself, where you can set up corrals and gardens and decide what to do with the space you have. I can see that being a nice little break between exploring, but also the sort of thing that eats a few minutes while you decide where everything should go. I don’t know why placing a garden can make me suddenly feel like I need a better plan for my whole life, but I’d probably put it somewhere and move on.

Exploring Rainbow Island is what keeps this from feeling like a ranch chores simulator. New slimes and resources give you a reason to wander, and finding a Gordo that needs feeding is a clear, slightly ridiculous goal that doesn’t ask you to read a wall of lore first. Some stretches are slower than others, especially when you’re figuring out where to go next, but those bits are easy to take at your own pace.

The Tarr are the part I’d take more seriously. They can appear when slimes get into plorts they shouldn’t eat, and that can turn a peaceful bit of ranch maintenance into a quick scramble. I don’t know how much that would bother you, but I’d rather keep my ranch fairly orderly than try to manage a tiny slime emergency while also jogging.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is a big plus here, and the controls sound like a reasonable match for playing while moving. You can collect things with the Vacpack, walk around, and take your time deciding what to bring back. I’d use a controller and keep the treadmill at a pace where I can still look around without turning every bend into a small negotiation.

The camera is the part that asks for more care. You’ll be moving through a three dimensional space, aiming the Vacpack, and keeping track of where you’re headed, so a little screen bounce could make exploring feel less comfortable than it does sitting still. I’d expect a bike to make aiming and looking around easier, mostly because your hands and view can stay steadier. It’s not a requirement, just a practical difference.

I’d also avoid rushing when Tarr show up or when I’m trying to move through unfamiliar areas. There’s no reason to turn every trip into a race, and the game seems more enjoyable when you can stop, look, and decide what you want to collect. If something gets hectic, I’d slow down or pause rather than trying to be precise while my legs are doing their own thing.

The nice thing is that the game doesn’t sound like it’s constantly pushing you into high pressure action. You can spend time at the ranch, head out to explore, or just collect things for a while, and that flexibility matters when your workout pace changes. I’d be comfortable recommending it for steady movement, with the small warning that the first person camera may take a little adjustment.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum calls for a dedicated graphics card around the level of an older GTX 960 or Radeon R9 280, so I wouldn’t count on integrated graphics being enough. I’d check the graphics card first, because the rest of the requirements are fairly ordinary and the slimes can wait while your computer catches up.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Slime Rancher 2 is an easy recommendation for a relaxed workout, especially if you like exploring and tending a small ranch more than chasing constant action. I’d skip it if you need a game that keeps your eyes forward and your hands mostly still.
