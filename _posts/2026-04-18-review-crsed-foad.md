The first thing I’d tell you is not to take a treadmill run into a serious match of CRSED: F.O.A.D., because the game asks you to look for danger and react fast while your feet are doing their own thing. It can make a workout go by, but I’d treat it as a risky distraction, not a relaxed shooter.

## Gameplay

CRSED: F.O.A.D. has a silly and pretty appealing contrast going on: you’re trying to survive a last player standing shootout with realistic guns, while lethal mystic forces and superpowers are part of the mix. I like that the supernatural stuff gives the fights a different flavor, because another game where everyone just finds a gun and shoots each other can get samey. Here, you’re never quite sure what kind of nonsense is about to enter the fight.

The battle royale structure is good at keeping me interested, but it also means some of your time is spent getting ready for a fight that may not happen right away. You’re looking around, trying to get equipped, and listening for other players, and I think that quiet stretch can be great on a slow bike ride, where you’re happy to keep moving without needing to make a choice every second. On a treadmill, the quiet can turn into me staring at the screen and forgetting I’m supposed to be exercising. It happens.

Once the shooting starts, there’s a lot more to keep track of. You’re aiming, checking what’s around you, and trying not to get caught out by someone who has a better position or a supernatural trick you weren’t expecting. That’s a fun kind of mess when I can give it my attention, and I like that the game isn’t pretending to be a calm little walk through the woods. The woods are not calm.

I’m a little less sold on the pressure that comes with a last player standing match. Getting eliminated can mean the match is over, and there isn’t a story scene or a slower side activity to give you a breather. I don’t know that I’d enjoy that much on a tired run, because my brain would be doing the same thing as my legs, which is trying to get through the next minute.

The free to play part makes it easy to give it a try without committing money first, and that’s useful when you’re not sure this particular mix is for you. I’d go in expecting a slightly strange battle royale rather than a polished workout game built around easy breaks. The mystic forces and superpowers sound fun, but they don’t make the basic pressure of being hunted disappear.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is a good start, but it doesn’t make the aiming part any less wobbly when you’re moving. I’d probably try it on a bike first, with the resistance low enough that I can still steer carefully, and I’d leave the treadmill for a day when I’m walking rather than trying to run. Your thumb needs to make small decisions while the match keeps moving, and those are not always easy to line up with your steps.

There’s a practical question with any last player standing shooter, which is how much attention you’re willing to give it when you can’t pause the match and sort yourself out. If you’re still looking for gear or moving between fights, you can keep pedaling and take it a little easier, but getting into a close fight is when I’d expect my whole brain to leave the workout. You might end up staring down an opponent while the treadmill politely continues without you.

I’d skip trying to play it during a hard run, and I wouldn’t count on the game to give you regular, convenient breaks. It could work for a gentle ride if you’re okay with losing a match because your aim wandered, but that’s a pretty large part of the game to accept. I don’t know, I’d want the exercise to be the thing I’m doing and the match to be the distraction, and CRSED may reverse those priorities.

The controller makes it playable, not automatically comfortable. If you want action that keeps demanding quick reactions, you’ll get that here, and if you want something forgiving when your hands are bouncing around, I’d look elsewhere.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

You don’t need a new high end gaming computer for the listed minimum, and the graphics requirement points to an older dedicated card being enough to start. I’d look at the graphics card first, because the store page specifically warns that some newer numbered Nvidia cards are slower than the older model it names, which is an oddly specific thing to learn before breakfast.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

CRSED: F.O.A.D. is worth trying if you like messy battle royale fights and want a free game to put on while you exercise, but I’d save it for a gentle bike ride or an easy walk. If you want to stay focused on your workout, the matches may ask for more attention than you want to give.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
