I wouldn’t take a hard run into a 400 player War of Rights battle, because this is a shooter where aiming and staying with your team matter more than keeping a steady pace. An easy bike ride sounds more realistic, and even then I’d expect to miss a few shots.

## Gameplay

War of Rights sounds like a great idea for people who want a slower, team focused shooter, and I think that’s also where it can get in the way of a workout. You’re fighting in massive battles set during the Maryland Campaign of 1862, and that scale gives the game a sense of purpose that a small match can’t really copy. You’re not just trying to win your own little duel, you’re part of a much larger group. That’s cool. It also means you can’t always set your own pace.

The historical focus is a real reason to play, rather than just a coat of paint over another shooter. I like the idea of working in a line, taking aim, and having the fight depend on people acting together instead of one person sprinting around like they’ve had three coffees. But the game asks you to pay attention to the battle and your teammates, and that takes a different kind of focus from a game where you can glance away whenever you need to.

I don’t know how much of the battle you’ll spend in direct combat compared with moving into position or waiting for the next push, and that uncertainty matters here. Slow stretches can be fine when you’re learning the field or catching your breath, but they’re less fun if you’re standing around with a controller while the treadmill keeps moving. I’d expect the big battles to have moments where you’re watching for an opening, and that can be a long time to keep your eyes locked on the screen.

The first person shooting is also more deliberate than a game built around constant close range action, which I think is a good fit for the historical setting and a mixed bag for playing while exercising. Aiming at opposing soldiers asks for care, and a shot that takes a moment to line up is a shot that can pull your attention away from your stride. I would probably enjoy that kind of tension sitting down. My legs might have a different opinion.

There’s enough here to make me curious, especially if you like multiplayer games where the team effort is the point. I’m less sure it’s going to keep you busy every minute, and a workout game has to compete with the part of your brain that’s already asking how long you’ve been moving. Maybe that makes sense for a calm ride. It’s a tougher sell on a run.

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

A bike is the option I’d try first, because your upper body can stay steadier while you aim and you can ease off the pedals without having to stop the match. The battles are multiplayer, so you can’t pause when you need to adjust your pace or look away for a second. That’s a bigger problem than it would be in a single player shooter, where the whole army can wait while you find your water bottle.

On a treadmill, each footfall adds a little movement just when you’re trying to make careful shots, and I don’t think that combination sounds forgiving. War of Rights is about teamplay and large battles, so missing a shot or losing track of the fight could affect more than your personal score. I wouldn’t want my teammates relying on me to hit something while my hands are bobbing along with the belt. I’m probably not helping the line.

I can’t tell from the details here whether controller support is available, so I wouldn’t buy it assuming that it is. Even with a controller, the careful aiming and live multiplayer would still make this a focus heavy game, and I’d want to sit down before joining a battle where other people are counting on me. I don’t know that a controller would make the aiming feel relaxed enough to change my recommendation.

If you already play on a bike and you’re comfortable with the controls, I’d give it a try at an easy pace, but I’d skip hard intervals and anything that has you breathing too heavily to concentrate. For most treadmill sessions, I’d choose a game that lets you pause or miss a moment without affecting a whole team. This one doesn’t sound like that.

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to an older but still dedicated graphics card, not integrated graphics, so I’d check your graphics card before buying. You don’t need a brand new high end machine by the listed minimum, but this isn’t the game to assume any old laptop can handle.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

War of Rights sounds worth trying if you want historically grounded team battles and you’re happy to focus on the match, but I wouldn’t make it a go to treadmill game. An easy bike ride is the more plausible fit, especially if you already know the controls, and people looking for something forgiving while they exercise should probably look elsewhere.
