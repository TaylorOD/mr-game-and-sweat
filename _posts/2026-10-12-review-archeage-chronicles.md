Auroria makes this sound like a world you’d want to wander around in, but I’d wait to see how much of that wandering is interrupted by fights before I put it on a treadmill. With full controller support, at least you can start with the right equipment in your hands.

## Gameplay

The mix of action, adventure, and massively multiplayer role playing could work well for exercise, because a big world gives you things to pay attention to while you move. I like the idea of having Auroria to explore, and the store page puts a lot of weight on its lore and action, but it doesn’t tell me how much time you spend doing either one. That makes it hard to promise you’ll stay busy instead of standing still while the game explains itself.

Early Access makes me a little more cautious. It can be a good way to get into a game while it’s still growing, but it also means the pace and amount of content may change, and I wouldn’t plan a whole workout around a particular rhythm yet. I’ve bought into the idea of a huge world before and then discovered that “huge” mostly meant a lot of ground between the interesting parts. Maybe Auroria handles that differently. I can’t tell from this description.

The action is what might make this more engaging than a slow, lore heavy role playing game, though “action” doesn’t tell you how demanding the fights are or how often they happen. If you’re making quick decisions and moving around a lot, that could keep your attention on the game. If you’re reading through a long stretch of story, you might find yourself looking at the treadmill display instead. I don’t know which one is more typical here, and that’s important for a game you want to use during a workout.

For now, I’d expect a game with enough going on to be interesting, but not enough detail in the listing to know whether the slower stretches are easy to live with. The massively multiplayer part also means the experience could depend on what you choose to do and how the game develops in Early Access. That flexibility sounds appealing, but I’d want to try a short session before deciding it’s a dependable workout game. A little test run is reasonable.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Full controller support is a real plus here, and I’d start by lowering any settings that make the action more demanding, if the game offers them. I can’t confirm what those settings are, so I wouldn’t pretend there’s a magic accessibility option waiting in a menu. The useful thing is simply that you can play with a controller rather than needing to reach for a keyboard while you’re moving.

On a treadmill, I’d be more comfortable exploring than trying to play anything that asks for precise, fast inputs. The store page doesn’t say how combat works, so I’d treat the action as the part to be careful with, not assume it’s gentle just because the game is an RPG. If fights feel busy, I’d slow the belt down or pause the workout for them. I know that sounds less exciting than trying to do everything at once, but I’m not looking to turn a controller into a small coordination exam.

A bike may be easier to pair with the game if you want to give the action your full attention, because your hands can stay on the controller without the same bouncing you get while running. Still, that depends on how much aiming or quick movement the actual combat asks of you, and I don’t know that from the information here. I’d begin with a comfortable pace and be ready to take a break from pedaling during anything that needs more concentration.

The thing I’d skip is any activity that makes you feel like you have to keep up with other players while you’re exercising, unless you already know the game gives you room to take things slowly. I’m not saying that’s how ArcheAge Chronicles works. I just wouldn’t assume a massively multiplayer game will wait for you because your legs are busy. Check how it feels in a short, easy session first.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This needs a dedicated graphics card in the range of an RTX 2060 SUPER or RX 6600, and the target is 30 frames per second at 1080p on low settings with upscaling. I’d check the graphics card and make sure you have an SSD with the required storage before downloading, because this isn’t a game for an older office computer that has been quietly doing its best.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

I’d give ArcheAge Chronicles a cautious try if you like large role playing worlds and want an action game to explore on a bike or at an easy treadmill pace. If you need reliable, predictable workout pacing, I’d wait until there’s more detail about how the fights and slower sections actually play.

### Overall Score: 2 out of 3
