I’d save Stoneshard for a slow walk or an easy bike ride, because a mercenary who can die from one bad decision is already asking for more attention than I can give a hard workout. The turn based pace helps, but it doesn’t make the choices feel casual.

## Gameplay

I like that Stoneshard makes survival part of the job instead of treating every trip into a dungeon like a quick stop for treasure. You’re managing hunger and injuries while deciding how far to push into places that can cost you progress, and that gives even a basic choice some weight. I’d probably get invested in that push and pull, then notice I’d stopped paying attention to my pace. That’s a decent game problem to have.

The danger sounds like a real part of the appeal, but it also means the game asks you to think before you act, which isn’t always what I want when I’m trying to exercise. Turn based combat gives you room to consider your next move, and you’re not being rushed by a timer, but I can imagine taking a long pause to decide whether I’m prepared to risk one more room. I’ve spent plenty of time making a simple choice take longer than it needs to. Stoneshard may be good for that.

Procedurally generated dungeons should help keep trips from feeling identical, and learning new skills gives you something to work toward beyond just getting through the next fight. I’d be curious how much the changing layouts keep me on my toes, because I like having to adjust, but I don’t want every workout to turn into a careful study session. There’s a lot to hold in your head here. My brain is not always open for business.

The caravan gives the mercenary life a bit of structure outside the dungeons, and I like the idea of having a home base to return to after a rough outing. Still, the hunger, injuries, skills and risk of losing progress all add up to a game that sounds more like something I’d settle into than something I’d casually tap through while jogging. That’s not a knock on it. I just wouldn’t expect the game to do the work of keeping me distracted from the effort.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

Turn based combat is a pretty good starting point for exercise, since you can take a moment to think instead of trying to land a perfectly timed move while your hands are bouncing around. But Stoneshard’s consequences make that pause feel important. When a mistake could mean death and lost progress, I’d want my attention on the game, not split between a screen and a treadmill belt.

I can’t confirm controller support from the information here, so I’d check before buying it for a controller setup. I’d also plan to play seated or at a very easy pace until you know the controls feel comfortable, because needing to manage a dangerous fight while holding a controller and moving is not the kind of multitasking I trust myself with. I can barely keep a grocery list in my head when I’m looking for the right aisle.

The part I’d most want to skip during a workout is anything that turns into extended planning about whether to press farther into a dungeon or head back injured. That sounds like a fun decision when you’re settled in, and a slightly annoying one when you’re trying to keep moving and your legs are asking for a vote. I don’t know how often those decisions come up, but the survival systems suggest they’re part of the regular rhythm.

A bike might be easier than a treadmill here if your hands stay steadier while you pedal, but I wouldn’t call it an automatic fit for either. If you’ve confirmed the controls work for your setup, I’d give it a try on an easy session and stop before you reach the point where every choice feels urgent. This game has enough ways to punish a careless decision without asking your body to help.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

The minimum requirements point to a pretty modest computer, with an older graphics card or integrated graphics likely being enough to start. I’d check that your system can handle the game comfortably, but you probably don’t need to build a machine around it.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Stoneshard sounds like a thoughtful, tense RPG that I’d recommend for a relaxed workout, not a hard run. If you like careful decisions and can confirm your controls work, give it a try.
