I’d use the idle fighting as a reason to keep moving, not expect this game to occupy every second of a run. Your heroes keep fighting while you’re away, which is considerate of somebody who has to keep one hand near the treadmill controls.

## Gameplay

The idle setup is what makes Legends of IdleOn interesting for exercise, because you can send your heroes off to fight, check what they brought back, and spend the loot on upgrades without having to be alert every second. I like that rhythm more than a game that keeps asking for my full attention, though it can also mean you’re watching progress happen instead of doing much yourself. Sometimes that’s exactly what a workout needs. Sometimes you’re just standing there looking at a number go up.

There’s a lot of progression spread across different heroes, classes, and skills, and I think that gives you a nice reason to come back to the game without needing to remember one big complicated plan. Unlocking classes such as Elemental Wizard or Cannon Pirate sounds fun, and skills like Farming, Sailing, and Construction give the game more to tinker with than a single line of combat upgrades. I’d probably enjoy picking one little thing to improve and letting the rest wait. This is not a game I’d want to turn into a second job.

The idle part is also a little misleading in a useful way. Your heroes keep fighting while you’re gone, but there’s still a game here about deciding where to spend the loot and what to work on next, and I think those choices will take more attention than the fighting itself. I don’t know that I’d want to make every upgrade decision while I’m breathing hard, so I’d handle the bigger choices before a workout and keep the small check ins for the moving part.

The MMO label may make it sound like you need to be constantly available to other players, but the store description puts the focus on developing your own heroes and skills, and that sounds much more relaxed to me. Free to play also means you can give it a try without making the game a whole financial decision first, which is a pretty nice deal. The game’s long term progression could keep you interested, but if you want a steady stream of action, I think this will feel slow.

### Category Score: 2 out of 3

## Treadmill and Bike Compatibility

The heroes doing their thing while you’re away is a good fit for a workout, because the game doesn’t sound like it needs you to react to every fight at once. You can let the action carry on, then check in when you’re ready to make a choice, and that’s a more sensible setup than trying to steer a character through a dangerous room while your arms are bouncing. I’d keep the pace comfortable, though, since the game’s upgrades and skill choices still ask you to look at the screen and think.

I can’t confirm native controller support, so I wouldn’t count on being able to play from the handlebars or treadmill console without testing your setup first. A lot of the appeal here is choosing upgrades and managing your heroes, and that kind of interaction can be awkward if you’re trying to click tiny spots while moving. I’d test the controls while standing still, and if you need a mouse, I’d make sure it has a stable place to sit before starting. Nobody needs to discover their desk arrangement is bad halfway through a workout.

On a bike, I think the stop and start pace could work well if you can keep your hands steady enough to use the controls. On a treadmill, I’d stick to an easy walk rather than trying to run hard and handle menus at the same time, because I don’t know about you, but I don’t need a bunch of small upgrade icons becoming a coordination exercise. Set up your next goal before you start, then check the game between stretches of walking or pedaling.

I’d skip trying to micromanage everything while you’re exercising. Pick a simple thing to work toward, let the heroes fight, and save the complicated planning for when you’re not moving. The game’s patient pace is the point here, and it’s nice when a game doesn’t make you feel behind for taking a breath.

### Category Score: 1 out of 3

## Computer Requirements

This should run on an older Windows computer with basic graphics, so you don’t need a gaming machine just to watch your heroes keep fighting. I’d look at the graphics hardware first if your computer is very old, but the listed requirements are about as modest as they get.

### Category Score: 3 out of 3

## Conclusion and Score

Legends of IdleOn is for you if you want a slow, ongoing game to check between stretches of a workout, not a constant stream of action. I’d give it a try, but I’d confirm your controls before planning a treadmill session around it.
